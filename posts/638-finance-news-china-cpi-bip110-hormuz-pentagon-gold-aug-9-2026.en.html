⚡ Aug 9 Finance Roundup: China July CPI Rises Just 0.5% Y/Y, Missing Estimates; Bitcoin's BIP-110 Fork Lands; Hormuz Jolts Oil Markets; Pentagon Urges Faster Weapons Production

Several threads moved in parallel across global markets early Sunday: China's National Bureau of Statistics reported July CPI up 0.5% year over year, well below the 0.80% expected, with a 0.1% month-over-month decline; Bitcoin's BIP-110 fork officially landed at block 961,632, with the main chain already 18 blocks ahead of the minority chain and developers urgently urging client upgrades; Hormuz tensions kept squeezing energy markets — an ADNOC tanker was hit by a missile and Iraq's oil exports plunged 75%; the Iran war has drained U.S. defense stockpiles, and the Pentagon is urging defense firms to accelerate weapons production; and in China, gold jewelry prices returned to 1,300 yuan per gram. Here is a source-verified deep dive.

1. China July CPI Rises 0.5% Y/Y, Missing Estimates; PPI Up 3.5%

Data released by the National Bureau of Statistics at 09:30 Beijing time show July CPI rose 0.5% year over year, versus 0.80% expected and 1.00% prior, and fell 0.1% month over month. Urban prices rose 0.5% and rural prices 0.4%; food prices fell 1.5% while non-food prices rose 0.9%; consumer goods rose 0.2% and services rose 0.7%. For January-July on average, consumer prices were up 0.9% from a year earlier.

By component, food, tobacco and alcohol prices fell 0.8% year over year, shaving about 0.22 percentage points off CPI. Within food, meat prices fell 6.0% (pork down 13.3%), dairy fell 1.5% and fresh fruit fell 1.1%, while egg prices rose 14.4%. On the month, meat prices rose 1.9% (pork +4.1%), fresh vegetables +1.3%, fresh fruit -3.8%. Across the other seven categories, six rose year over year: other goods & services +5.8%, healthcare +2.9%, clothing +1.4%, education/culture/recreation +1.3%, household goods & services +0.8%, transport & communication +0.4%, while housing fell 0.3%.

Factory-gate prices kept climbing: July PPI rose 3.5% year over year and fell 0.7% month over month, while producer purchase prices rose 5.5% Y/Y. For January-July, PPI averaged +1.8%. Producer goods prices rose 4.8% Y/Y — mining industry prices surged 16.4%, raw materials +6.1% — while consumer goods fell 0.8%. Among purchase prices, nonferrous metals & wire jumped 19.0%, fuel & power and chemical raw materials both +9.3%. The "soft CPI + hot PPI" mix means downstream demand is weak while upstream cost pressure persists, squeezing margins and re-opening expectations for more policy easing.

2. Bitcoin's BIP-110 Fork Lands: Main Chain 18 Blocks Ahead

In crypto, the long-simmering Bitcoin BIP-110 fork officially materialized at block height 961,632. According to Bitcoin.com News, BIP-110-aligned mining pool Roughnecks managed to mine blocks 961,632 and 961,633 on the minority chain after the split, while the dominant chain has already advanced to 961,651 — 18 blocks ahead of the splintered chain, which is falling further and further behind.

At the same height, Bitcoin's mining difficulty adjusted to 127.48 trillion, a brutal level for the minority chain's thin hashrate. Bitcoin Knots creator Luke Dashjr posted an urgent notice on X: "If you haven't upgraded to Bitcoin Knots 29.3.knots20260508 (or 29.4) yet, you are now vulnerable to double-spending attacks aided by [Foundry and Antpool]," adding in bold: "Upgrade ASAP to avoid accepting counterfeit bitcoins." Dathon Ohm, the anonymous architect behind BIP-110, insisted "the Bitcoin network is currently under attack from hostile miners." BIP-110 is a proposed one-year change limiting non-financial data on Bitcoin, and it has fueled a bitter debate over how Bitcoin's block space should be used. Judging by current hashrate distribution, the main-chain consensus is firmly ahead, and the fork looks set to fade into a shrinking minority network.

3. Hormuz Jolts Energy Markets: ADNOC Tanker Hit, Iraq Exports Down 75%

The biggest variable in energy markets remains the Strait of Hormuz. Jin10's Aug 9 Middle East tracking shows an ADNOC vessel was hit by a missile while transiting the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday morning; the UAE Foreign Ministry strongly condemned the attack and said the situation is under control. Another vessel was attacked and caught fire east of Oman.

The cost of the closure is now visible in the data: the Hormuz shutdown has cut Iraq's oil exports by 75%; the U.S. military says it has forced 53 merchant ships to change course; and Fitch warns Kuwait's oil exports face sustained pressure due to their dependence on the Strait. Diplomatically, Iran's Revolutionary Guard spokesman said reopening the Strait depends on the U.S. fully accepting Iran's conditions, and is separate from the Iran-Oman talks; U.S. Vice President Vance said Iran has told Washington it has no plan to charge transit fees, describing the conflict as in its "mid-game." Iran's foreign minister said Tehran is working with Oman to define a temporary transit route and is close to a Strait-management agreement — but "close to a deal" and "actually navigable" are still separated by enormous uncertainty, keeping oil prices and inflation expectations highly sensitive.

4. Iran War Drains Defense Stockpiles: Pentagon Urges Faster Weapons Production

On the defense and fiscal front, multiple U.S. media reported Sunday that the Iran war has sharply depleted U.S. defense stockpiles, and the Pentagon is urging defense firms to accelerate weapons production. Months of conflict have consumed significant stocks of precision-guided munitions and air-defense systems, and restocking pressure is now rippling into the defense supply chain. Combined with the Senate's 86-11 passage of a Russia energy-sanctions bill (authorizing tariffs of up to 100% on buyers of Russian energy), Washington is pressing on defense and energy tracks at once, making defense spending and the military-industrial complex a new focus for markets. For investors, sustained geopolitical conflict means "defense spending expansion + energy supply disruption" may both become part of the second-half macro narrative.

5. China Gold Jewelry Prices Return to 1,300 Yuan per Gram

Gold markets remain strong: China gold jewelry prices have returned to 1,300 yuan per gram. With global geopolitical risk rising and last week's unexpectedly negative U.S. nonfarm payrolls dragging rate-hike odds lower, gold keeps climbing — international prices already broke above $4,400/oz on Friday, lifting domestic jewelry prices with them. The 1,300 yuan milestone reflects both safe-haven inflows and a test of end-consumer demand. Industry watchers generally believe that if Hormuz tensions and Fed policy paths keep pushing gold higher, "buy gold to store value" and "high prices dampen consumption" will coexist, likely widening profit divergence across the gold value chain (miners, branded retailers, and recycling channels).

6. What to Watch

Over the next 24 hours, keep an eye on: how Asian markets price China's CPI/PPI at Monday's open — the below-forecast CPI may strengthen easing expectations, supporting bonds and growth sectors; whether the block gap between Bitcoin's main chain and the BIP-110 minority chain keeps widening, and whether exchanges list fork tokens; whether a temporary Hormuz transit route actually materializes, which will decide whether the crude risk premium stays or fades; and whether U.S. defense restocking orders lift defense stocks. Overall, "soft Chinese inflation + persistent geopolitical conflict + strong gold" are the three threads running through markets right now, and volatility is likely to stay elevated.
