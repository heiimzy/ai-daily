⚡ July 31 Global Financial Roundup: US Q2 GDP Misses at 1.5%, Apple and Amazon Deliver Mixed After-Hours Results, Microsoft Soars 13.8% Leading Tech Rebound, Yen Surges 2.68% as BOJ Decision Looms

📉 US Q2 GDP Grows Just 1.5%, Missing Expectations

The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis released the advance estimate for Q2 2026 GDP on Thursday, showing the economy grew at an annualized rate of just 1.5% — well below the 2.0-2.1% consensus and down from 2.1% in Q1.

Consumer spending was the largest contributor, adding 2.12 percentage points. Nonresidential fixed investment added 1.20 pp, driven by continued strong AI infrastructure investment. However, the private inventory change (-0.67 pp), net exports (-1.01 pp), and government spending (-0.14 pp) all weighed on growth.

The PCE price index rose 5.1% year-over-year (vs. 4.6% in Q1), while core PCE (excluding food and energy) eased to 3.4% (vs. 4.4% in Q1), indicating continued disinflation in core measures. Personal income rose $54.9B (+0.2%) in June, personal spending rose $65.2B (+0.3%), and the personal saving rate fell to 2.7%.

"Slower growth alongside inflation still running above target puts the Fed in a tough spot — cutting rates too soon risks reigniting prices while holding too long could weigh further on activity," noted the ABA Office of the Chief Economist.

📈 Wall Street Rebounds: Nasdaq Surges 2.85%, Microsoft Jumps 13.8%

U.S. stocks staged a powerful rebound on Thursday (July 30) after Wednesday's Fed-driven selloff. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite surged 697.29 points (+2.85%) to 25,140.23. The S&P 500 gained 125.36 points (+1.71%) to 7,441.51, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 638.20 points (+1.24%) to 52,232.34.

Microsoft (MSFT) was the standout, surging 13.83% to $444.54. The company reported Q4 revenue of $90.01 billion (+18% YoY), with Azure cloud services growing 43% and surpassing $100 billion in annual revenue for the first time. Microsoft 365 Copilot paid seats exceeded 30 million, validating AI commercialization at scale.

Chip stocks exploded higher: Lam Research surged 19%, Micron Technology jumped 14.6%, Intel rallied 13%, and Applied Materials gained 13.8%, as the semiconductor sector bounced sharply.

Meta Platforms (META) bucked the trend, plunging 8.5%. While Q2 revenue of $60.8B (+28%) was in line, net income fell 14% YoY to $15.85B, and free cash flow collapsed 91% to just $784M. The company raised its full-year capex outlook to $130-145 billion, triggering concerns about AI investment ROI.

"Microsoft and Meta produced divergent reactions, showing that AI spending is being judged company by company," said Sameer Samana, head of global equities at Wells Fargo Investment Institute.

🍏📦 Apple and Amazon Deliver After-Hours Results

Apple (AAPL): Revenue $109.4B Sets June Quarter Record, Tim Cook's Farewell Call

Apple reported fiscal Q3 2026 results after the close Thursday: revenue of $109.42 billion (+16% YoY) beat the $108.65B consensus, and EPS of $2.02 (+29% YoY) beat the $1.89 estimate — marking the eighth consecutive EPS beat. This was Tim Cook's final earnings call as CEO before John Ternus takes over on September 1.

Key highlights: iPhone revenue of $54.25B (+22% YoY) beat estimates; Mac revenue of $10.35B significantly beat the $8.74B consensus; Services revenue of $30.74B slightly missed the $31.22B estimate. Greater China revenue surged 22% to $18.82B. Gross margin hit 50.1%, boosted by approximately 2 percentage points from tariff rebates.

Apple shares fell 4% in extended trading as Services revenue missed. Cook told CNBC that memory prices are "choking" and "not good for the consumer," noting the company is exploring alternatives. Apple maintained its capital-light AI strategy with just $3.4B in quarterly capex.

Amazon (AMZN): Revenue $200.6B Crushes Estimates, AWS Growth Hits 28%

Amazon delivered a massive beat on all fronts: Q2 revenue of $200.6 billion (above $197.0B expected), EPS of $5.75 (far above $1.82 expected), AWS revenue of $42.23B (above $40.57B expected, +28% YoY — fastest growth in 15 quarters), and operating income of $27.46B (above $23.61B expected).

Amazon shares surged 6% after hours. AWS maintained a 37.7% operating margin, and its custom chip business (Trainium, Graviton, Nitro) surpassed a $20 billion revenue run rate. However, Amazon's planned 2026 capex of ~$200 billion raised eyebrows, with trailing twelve-month free cash flow down 95% to $1.2B.

"AWS growth, like Microsoft's Azure, confirms that AI cloud demand is real and accelerating — not a bubble," analysts noted.

🏛️ 30-Year Treasury Yield Hits 5.24% — 19-Year High

Bond markets continued to grapple with the aftermath of Wednesday's hawkish Fed decision (3 dissenters voted to hike). The 30-year Treasury yield surged to 5.24%, the highest since 2007. The 10-year yield rose 8 basis points to 4.704%, and the 2-year yield climbed 5 basis points to 4.283%.

Markets now price a ~56% probability of a September rate hike. "Short- and intermediate-maturity bonds continue to offer more attractive yields relative to potential interest rate risks in the long end of the curve," said Wells Fargo's Samana.

💱 Yen Surges 2.68% as BOJ Decision Takes Center Stage

Currency markets saw significant volatility: USD/JPY plunged 2.68% to 159.03, the largest single-day drop since December 2022. Intervention rumors swirled, and all eyes are on the Bank of Japan's rate decision due Friday (July 31). The BOJ is widely expected to hold at 1.00%, but Governor Kazuo Ueda's press conference and the quarterly Outlook Report will be closely scrutinized for signals on future tightening.

The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) fell 0.80% to 100.08, weighed down by the disappointing GDP data. EUR/USD rose 0.45% to 1.152.

The BOJ hiked to 1.00% in June (highest in 31 years). A Bloomberg survey shows 40% of economists expect the next hike in October and 50% in December. With Japan importing virtually all its energy and over half its food, the weak yen continues to fuel imported inflation.

🥇 Gold Returns Above $4,100, Oil Edges Lower

Gold rallied 1.1% to $4,109.94/oz, benefiting from a weaker dollar. Gold futures rose 1.9% to $4,108.30. Markets interpreted the softer inflation data as reducing the urgency for aggressive rate hikes.

Oil prices edged lower: Brent crude fell 1.21% to $89.64/barrel, and WTI slipped 0.30% to $84.21. Reports of a Saudi-led Red Sea maritime security coalition eased some supply concerns. However, geopolitical risks persist as the fallout from a Russian missile entering Polish airspace continues to develop.
