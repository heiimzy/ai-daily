⚡ Aug 3 World Update: Ukraine's 635-Drone Swarm Strikes Deep into Russia, Russia Hits Mykolaiv Port, New Explosion Near Hormuz

Just as the United States and Iran prepared to restart talks over the Strait of Hormuz and markets hoped for a partial de-escalation in the Middle East, the Russia-Ukraine war on the other side of Eurasia surged again. Ukrainian forces launched one of their largest long-range drone assaults of the conflict against military and energy targets inside Russia, while Moscow said it struck Ukraine's southern port of Mykolaiv the same day. Meanwhile, the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported that a tanker heard an explosion near the entrance to the Strait of Hormuz, adding another layer of uncertainty to the already strained global energy corridor. Here is the roundup.

I. Ukraine's 635-drone swarm hits deep into Russia: casualties and damage reported

According to Russia's defence ministry, Ukraine launched a large-scale drone attack overnight during the weekend, with Russian air defences intercepting 635 Ukrainian drones through the night. Even so, several regional Russian governors reported fatalities and infrastructure damage. Ukraine's military said the overnight operation targeted Russia's military and energy networks, including the Engels strategic airbase and the Saratov oil refinery in the Saratov region, and claimed it ignited fires at an oil depot in the Kaluga region.

The drones penetrated hundreds to more than a thousand kilometres into Russian territory. In the Udmurtia region, acting governor Olga Abramova said a Ukrainian drone struck a civilian vehicle, killing three people more than 1,300 km from the front line; in the Saratov region, a residential building in Engels was hit, killing two; in Belgorod, near the Ukrainian border, acting governor Alexander Shuvayev said separate attacks killed four people, including a 13-year-old girl.

"This is a concentrated strike by Kyiv against Russia's war economy — from military airfields and refineries to the e-commerce warehouses that underpin consumer life, all are on the target list." — analysts note Ukraine is broadening its long-range campaign beyond purely military objectives.

Notably, a Wildberries warehouse in the Samara region, around 800 km from Ukraine's most advanced positions, was hit and caught fire; acting governor Vyacheslav Fedorische averted said there were no casualties and the fire was being contained. Ukraine has attacked about a dozen Wildberries facilities since July 18, aiming to disrupt an e-commerce giant Kyiv says helps prop up President Vladimir Putin's war machine. Now in the fifth year of the conflict, both sides keep expanding long-range strikes far beyond the battlefield.

II. Russia strikes Mykolaiv port, claims two military-cargo vessels and a Black Sea supply ship hit

On the same day, Russia's defence ministry announced its forces struck infrastructure in the southern Ukrainian port city of Mykolaiv, hitting two vessels carrying military cargo inside the port as well as a vessel carrying military supplies in the Black Sea. Moscow said the operation was meant to sever Ukrainian logistics links by sea and land.

In recent days, Ukrainian air-force officials and military observers had warned of an increase in Russian strikes on Black Sea ports and grain-transport vessels, affecting Ukrainian exports and imports. The ongoing offensive and defensive struggle around Black Sea shipping lanes adds fresh uncertainty to global food security and maritime trade.

III. Explosion near a tanker off Hormuz: fresh spark in the talks window

In the energy-rich Gulf region, meanwhile, the waters were not calm. The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported that a tanker heard an explosion in close vicinity to the vessel at around 4:37 am local time on Monday (August 3), about 20 nautical miles northeast its position at the entrance to the Strait of Hormuz. The agency said both the vessel and crew were safe and an investigation was underway.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi has said negotiations with Oman on the future management of the Strait of Hormuz are "in their final stages," with plans for a new route that is "neither the northern nor the southern" corridor, respecting both sides' sovereign rights and safeguarding national security. Yet Tehran insists the new-route talks are a separate discussion from whether the strait reopens or remains closed.

"Negotiations are advancing, but every maritime incident is a reminder: the military and security risks around the Strait of Hormuz remain far from resolved."

With Trump saying new talks with Iran would begin Monday afternoon, likely via intermediaries, after holding off on a planned strike, any incident at sea gets amplified. Markets are weighing the two scenarios of "a deal" vs. "no deal" in real time. About a fifth of the world's oil and LNG transits the strait, so any ripple is quickly fed into global energy prices.

IV. Summary: the fragile balance of a multi-front war

In sum, Monday (August 3) presented a strikingly mixed picture in international affairs: in the Middle East, US-Iran talks restarting and a seemingly converging deal between Iran and Oman suggested fading risk, while in Eastern Europe, Russia and Ukraine keep trading high-frequency long-range strikes — from Ukrainian drones hitting Russian e-commerce warehouses to Russian strikes on Ukrainian ports — and a sudden explosion reported near the Hormuz entrance underscored how fragile the world's security corridors remain. The international community will keep watching this afternoon's US-Iran talks and the next moves on the Ukrainian battlefield.
