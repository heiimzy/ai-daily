⚡ Aug 2 Finance Update: PBOC Vows to Stabilize Capital Markets, ColdCard Wallets Lose $86.7M in Bitcoin, Global Central Banks Buy Gold at Four-Year High

August 2, 2026 brought three major signals from the financial world. China's central bank held its 2026 second-half work conference, pledging to continue an appropriately loose monetary policy and to leverage the two capital-market support tools to stabilize and bolster market confidence; in crypto, a massive security breach hit Coinkite's ColdCard hardware wallets — hackers systematically drained 1,367 Bitcoin worth roughly $86.7 million by exploiting a firmware random-number flaw; and the World Gold Council reported that global central banks added a net 289 tonnes of gold in Q2, up 62% year-on-year to a four-year high, underscoring gold's enduring strategic role in official reserves.

PBOC H2 Work Conference: Loose Policy Stance Intact, Capital-Market Tools in Focus

The People's Bank of China (PBOC) convened its 2026 second-half work conference on August 1, with Governor Pan Gongsheng attending and addressing the meeting, according to reports from the central bank, CLS and Sina Finance on August 2. The conference noted that a series of monetary and financial policies rolled out in 2026 had supported stable economic growth: by end-June, aggregate social financing grew 7.4% year-on-year and broad money supply (M2) grew 8.0%, while the RMB exchange rate fluctuated in both directions.

For the second half, the conference pledged to continue implementing an appropriately loose monetary policy: deploying and fine-tuning monetary policy tools as needed, keeping liquidity ample, guiding financial institutions to improve the balance of credit supply, aligning the growth of social financing and money supply with economic growth and price-level targets, standardizing credit-market conduct, reducing intermediate financing costs, keeping overall social financing costs low, and maintaining the RMB exchange rate basically stable at an adaptive and balanced level.

"Leverage the role of the two capital-market support monetary policy tools to stabilize and strengthen confidence in the capital markets." — PBOC 2026 H2 Work Conference

On risk prevention, the conference called for continued support for resolving local government financing platform debt risks, advancing market-based transformation of platforms, strengthening macroprudential management and the financial stability safeguard system, and making full use of the two capital-market support tools to correct and block the accumulation of financial market risks. It also outlined plans to build a high-quality "Sci-Tech Board" in the bond market, steadily promote high-level opening of financial markets, support the development of Shanghai and Hong Kong as international financial centers, and improve digital RMB cross-border infrastructure. The conference stressed stepping up counter-cyclical adjustment and expanding domestic demand while promptly designing practical incremental policies. Market analysts see this as further cementing the policy floor for A-shares following the Politburo's supportive tone.

ColdCard Wallets Systematically Drained: 1,367 BTC Stolen via Firmware Randomness Flaw

A major security incident has shaken the crypto market. According to reports from 10jqka and IT Home on August 2, a large-scale draining operation against Coinkite ColdCard Bitcoin hardware wallets has run through at least three waves of attacks, with hackers stealing 1,367 BTC from 4,585 addresses — worth approximately $86.7 million (about RMB 587 million). Galaxy Research published the third-wave results on August 1: 207.73 BTC were moved between 12:23 UTC on July 31 and 06:42 UTC on August 1, involving 1,912 addresses; the first wave occurred on July 30 at 01:10 UTC, lasting 41 minutes and draining 1,082.65 BTC from 1,195 addresses; the second wave on July 31 took 71.16 BTC.

The root cause is a severe randomness defect in firmware released by Coinkite on March 17, 2021. The firmware set MICROPY_HW_ENABLE_RNG to 0, but the libngu crypto library only checked whether the macro exists, not whether it was enabled — so mnemonic generation silently fell back to MicroPython's Yasmarang software pseudo-random number generator, seeded only by the chip's unique ID and timer registers. Affected Coldcard Mk3 devices generated mnemonics with only about 40 bits of entropy, far below the standard 128 bits; newer Mk4, Q and Mk5 models, despite a Secure Element, still left a candidate key space of roughly 4 billion combinations, crackable with a GPU cluster. The attacks required no physical contact — attackers generated all possible mnemonics offline, derived addresses, and matched them against the public UTXO dataset.

Coinkite released an emergency firmware update on July 31, and CEO Rodolfo Novak publicly apologized, saying the company would take full responsibility for losses caused by the vulnerability. Experts stress, however, that updating firmware alone does not eliminate the risk — users must regenerate their mnemonics with the new firmware and migrate their funds, because seeds generated with weak randomness cannot be fixed by an upgrade. Binance founder Changpeng Zhao (CZ) weighed in, warning "nothing is 100% secure" and advising users to spread funds across multiple wallets. Notably, Bitcoin's price took the news in stride, trading in a narrow $62,700–$63,400 range, with the market treating it as an isolated security event rather than systemic risk.

Q2 Central Bank Gold Buying Surges 62%: Record $380B in H1 Gold Demand

The World Gold Council's Q2 2026 Gold Demand Trends report, released July 30 and highlighted by Chinese state media on August 2, shows that global central banks and other official institutions added a net 289 tonnes of gold in Q2, up 62% year-on-year — the highest quarterly total in four years. After a marked slowdown in Q1, central bank buying recovered strongly across several countries in Q2, underscoring gold's enduring importance in official reserves even as prices eased from record highs.

The report shows total Q2 gold demand was flat year-on-year at 1,269 tonnes, bringing H1 demand to 2,522 tonnes, up 2%, with a record value of roughly $380 billion. According to the WGC's Central Bank Gold Reserves Survey, 45% of respondent central banks intend to increase their own gold reserves over the next 12 months, and 89% expect global official gold reserves to keep rising. Jia Shuchang, head of research for Asia Pacific (ex-India) and deputy director of industry expansion for China at the WGC, said the key drivers are reserve diversification needs and rising geopolitical risk accelerating the move to diversify away from dollar assets.

In contrast to central bank buying, gold ETFs recorded net outflows of about 45 tonnes in Q2, driven by weaker prices and upward revisions to inflation and interest-rate expectations, particularly in North America; jewellery demand fell to its lowest since the pandemic. Chen Yanbing, senior strategy analyst at China Asset Management, noted that central bank buying is a "slow variable" for prices — it won't push gold up immediately, but it underpins the pricing floor in the medium-to-long term, and investors should learn from central banks' allocation mindset rather than treat it as a short-term buy signal. Looking ahead, the WGC expects central bank purchases to remain strong but slightly below 2025 levels, with investment demand the primary growth driver in the second half.
