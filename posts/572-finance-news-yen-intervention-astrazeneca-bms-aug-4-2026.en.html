⚡ Aug 4 Finance Update: US-Japan Stage First Joint Yen Intervention Since 2011, AstraZeneca-BMS $400B Merger Talks Rock Pharma, US Stocks Rise at Midday but Chip Stocks Slump

Global markets were re-priced on Monday (August 3) along three threads — cooling geopolitics, coordinated policy intervention and a mega-merger. Resumed US-Iran talks pushed oil lower and European stocks to a one-month high, while Japan's Finance Ministry and the US Treasury confirmed a rare joint yen-buying operation, the first since 2011. In pharma, reports of a roughly $400 billion AstraZeneca–Bristol Myers Squibb combination sent AZN shares down about 9% in London. On Wall Street, the three major indexes were higher at midday, but chip stocks led declines again, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index down more than 3%. Here is this roundup.

1. US-Japan Joint Yen Intervention: First Since 2011, Yen Jumps More Than 6 in Two Days

Japan's Finance Ministry confirmed Monday that it carried out a coordinated yen-buying intervention with the US Treasury last Friday (July 31) to counter "excessive volatility and disorderly movements in the Japanese yen in recent months." It marks the first joint currency intervention by the US and Japan since 2011 and the first US participation in yen-buying since 1998. The yen hit 163.73 per dollar on Thursday — near a four-decade low — before surging to 157.57 on Friday after the intervention, and it held around 157.70 on Monday. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama said Japan "will not hesitate to conduct further coordinated interventions in the future" and remains in close communication with the US Treasury.

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confirmed the action, saying "Friday's coordinated foreign exchange actions countered disorderly yen movements," and added that the Treasury "will not hesitate to participate in further joint intervention." Notably, Japan also announced plans to use the Federal Reserve's FIMA repo facility — borrowing short-term dollars against its Treasury holdings as collateral rather than selling the bonds outright to fund intervention. Analysts say the signal may be bigger than the intervention itself: Bessent has repeatedly backed a stronger yen and urged the Bank of Japan to keep raising rates, and markets now treat a further BOJ hike in September as highly likely to put a more durable floor under the currency.

2. AstraZeneca-BMS $400B Merger Talks: AZN Tumbles 9% in London

According to a Sunday report in the Financial Times, AstraZeneca and Bristol Myers Squibb have held talks over several months on a potential combination. If completed, the merged company would be worth close to $400 billion, ranking among the largest pharmaceutical tie-ups ever and the world's biggest drugmaker by revenue. Entering Monday, the two companies had a combined market capitalization of nearly $400 billion — AstraZeneca at about $264 billion and Bristol Myers at about $133 billion. Reuters, citing a source, said the deal would carry regulatory risk under US antitrust scrutiny in the Trump administration.

Market reaction was sharply divided: AstraZeneca's London-listed shares closed about 9% lower, their biggest one-day drop since 2020, weighing on the FTSE 100, which was roughly flat; Bristol Myers shares rose as much as 6% in US premarket trading. Jefferies analysts said they were "a bit perplexed," adding: "if there is one company that doesn't need financial engineering, it's AZ." BMO called the deal "unlikely," citing antitrust hurdles and business overlap. Analysts note AstraZeneca completed a direct NYSE listing earlier this year, with the US accounting for 42% of its first-half revenue — a deal would push it closer to the US market. If it materialized, the combined oncology portfolio would be the industry's deepest, with combined annual sales potentially surpassing $100 billion.

3. US Stocks Rise at Midday but Chip Stocks Slump: SOX Down More Than 3%

Supported by falling oil and dovish Fed commentary, US stocks moved higher at Monday's midday: the S&P 500 rose 0.84% to a two-week high, the Dow gained 1.21% to a 3.5-week high and the Nasdaq 100 added 0.47%, while the 10-year Treasury yield eased about 5 basis points to 4.68%. Brent crude was down about 4.7% at $83.83 a barrel at midday, easing fears of runaway inflation. New York Fed President John Williams sounded dovish, saying rates are "well positioned" and inflation should ease in the second half, supporting both stocks and bonds.

The tape, however, was sharply divided: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell more than 3%, with Seagate down more than 6%, Western Digital and ARM down more than 5%, and Micron and Marvell down more than 4%; Micron was also pressured by a Reuters report that Chinese memory maker CXMT is considering a second fab in Beijing. South Korea's KOSPI — which had soared 17.9% on Friday, its best day on record, as Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each jumped more than 25% — fell 5.1% on Monday. In contrast, the oil slump lifted airlines, with American Airlines and United Airlines up more than 5% and Norwegian Cruise Line up more than 4%; software led the advance, with Atlassian and ServiceNow up more than 6% and Microsoft up more than 5% to lead the Dow.

4. Earnings Storm Week Begins: SpaceX's First Post-IPO Report, AMD Due Tuesday

This week marks an earnings- and data-heavy stretch on Wall Street: SpaceX will report its first quarterly results since going public, with investors focused on Starlink profitability and AI-related capital spending (the stock has been hovering below its IPO price); Palantir reports after Monday's close and AMD follows Tuesday after the bell — AMD is up 194% over the past 12 months and its print is seen as a key test for the second tier of AI chip names; SanDisk and Western Digital also report. In individual movers, Marriott fell more than 5% after Q2 revenue of $7.07 billion missed estimates and it issued soft Q3 guidance; GameStop dropped more than 11% after announcing plans to exchange about $1.4 billion of convertible notes for common stock; Atkore surged 28% on a roughly $3.8 billion acquisition; and Ferguson gained more than 9% ahead of joining the S&P 500 on Wednesday (August 5). CME FedWatch data shows the market pricing about a 63% chance of a 25-basis-point Fed rate hike in September.

With geopolitics, currencies and earnings converging, the US July nonfarm payrolls report on Friday is the next key catalyst, while SpaceX and AMD results will test whether the "AI narrative" can support current valuations — this promises to be another volatile week.
