⚡ Aug 3 Tech Update: PKU Builds the World's First Millisecond Neurodynamics Chip, Robotis Open-Sources Its Humanoid Software, Shoucheng Holdings Backs Ion-Trap Quantum Computing

Monday's general tech roundup runs from the "brain" all the way down to the compute substrate. A Peking University team, working with the Chinese Academy of Sciences, built the world's first millisecond-level neurodynamics system chip based on phase-change memristors, dramatically lowering the hardware bar for brain-computer interfaces and early brain-disease screening. Korea's Robotis became the first company in the country to fully open-source the entire software of its humanoid robot "AI Sapiens," creating the industry's first fully open humanoid software platform. And Shoucheng Holdings' fund made a strategic investment in Yaozheng Quantum — China's only QCCD ion-trap quantum computing company — extending its portfolio from embodied AI to quantum computing. Here is this roundup.

1. PKU Team Builds the World's First Millisecond Neurodynamics Chip: 2.12 ms Per Step, Paving the Way for Brain-Computer Interfaces

In news dated August 3, a team led by Prof. Yang Yuchao of Peking University's School of Integrated Circuits, together with the Chinese Academy of Sciences, has developed the world's first millisecond-level neurodynamics system chip based on phase-change memristors, compressing the single-step computation time of neurodynamics hardware systems to 2.12 milliseconds — achieving brain-like high-speed, high-precision computation. The breakthrough opens an entirely new technical path for brain-computer interfaces (BCIs), early screening of brain diseases and personalized diagnostics.

The human brain is itself one of the most complex dynamical systems. Real-time decoding of neural states for BCIs, and high-fidelity reconstruction of the cerebral cortex from medical imaging, both depend on fast modeling of continuous neuromorphic and dynamic processes. The researchers combined neural networks with differential equations to build neurodynamics systems that reconstruct accurate 3D brain structures — but that path has long been bottlenecked by the "memory wall": massive amounts of data shuttling between memory and processors, driving up latency and energy consumption.

The Yang team took a different route, proposing a "controlled in-memory computing" paradigm based on phase-change memristors, allowing data to be processed in place without being moved. Compared with today's mainstream advanced chips, the new chip delivers a major jump in computational performance with significantly lower power consumption, meeting the stringent requirements of high-fidelity brain-structure modeling. Looking ahead, future BCIs must not only read neural signals but also understand brain states in real time, predict the evolution of neural dynamics and perform closed-loop regulation accordingly. Running at millisecond speeds, the chip could provide personalized, dynamic and explainable brain-state models for true intelligent interaction; in medicine, it could power early screening of brain diseases, disease-progression monitoring and personalized therapeutic intervention.

2. Korea's Robotis Fully Open-Sources Its Humanoid 'AI Sapiens' Software: From Sim2Real to Drive Code, Total Transparency

According to Seoul Economic Daily on August 3, Robotis (108490.KQ) has become the first company in Korea to fully release the entire software of its humanoid robot "AI Sapiens," aiming to build an ecosystem that lets anyone develop humanoids quickly and easily.

The released materials include "Sim2Real" (simulation-to-reality transfer) tools and reinforcement learning workflows, widely considered the greatest challenge in humanoid development. Robotis automated the process of seamlessly transferring a robot's walking ability — refined through repeated training in Nvidia's simulation environment — to the real world without errors, dramatically lowering the development barrier. Until now, the humanoid industry has offered only simple application programming interfaces (APIs) that let users issue manufacturer-permitted commands, while keeping the core control code — how a robot actually maintains balance and walks — strictly secret. Robotis broke with that practice, releasing the entire driving code so users can examine the robot's internal control principles, modify the code for their own research or purposes, and apply it to the robot immediately. Combined with a license carrying minimal restrictions on commercial use, users do not even need to sign a separate contract with Robotis when developing commercial products based on the released code.

The full open-sourcing is expected to breathe new life into Korea's robot ecosystem. Combining Robotis's Dynamixel actuator technology with the newly released software, university labs, startups and ordinary developers can easily build and operate high-performance humanoids that walk and run. Robotis said the move is a response to "the offensive by Chinese-made humanoids backed by enormous capital and volume." Starting with the full release of its software, Robotis plans to sequentially open hardware areas as well, including mechanical design blueprints and electronic circuit materials, with the ultimate goal of a fully open platform where users can build and modify humanoids from the ground up. "The Chinese pursuit in the global robot market is fierce, but we hold a clear advantage in the fundamental openness of our platform and the overwhelming quality of our actuators," said Robotis CEO Kim Byoung-soo. "We will create a virtuous cycle in which technology verified at research sites around the world returns to the platform, enabling Korea to lead the global humanoid ecosystem."

3. Shoucheng Holdings Invests in Yaozheng Quantum: Extending From Embodied AI to QCCD Ion-Trap Quantum Computing

According to Gelonghui on August 3, Shoucheng Holdings (0697.HK) announced that a fund under the group has completed a strategic investment in Hefei Yaozheng Quantum Technology Co., Ltd., a leading ion-trap quantum computing company in China. With this move, Shoucheng Holdings extends its frontier-tech portfolio from embodied AI and robotics — the "physical AI" core track — upward into quantum computing, a potentially disruptive computing infrastructure.

Founded in 2022, Yaozheng Quantum is currently the only quantum computing company in China on the QCCD (quantum charge-coupled device) ion-trap technology route. In 2024, it delivered a complete ion-trap quantum computer and became the first to break through quantum volume (QV) 32. The investment follows Shoucheng Holdings' stakes in dozens of robotics supply-chain companies including Unitree, Galbot and Songyan Dynamics, marking another key move in the physical AI direction.

The industrial logic: quantum computing could break through the physical limits of AI model training and inference from the compute substrate, forming deep "intelligence + compute" synergy with embodied AI — embodied intelligence needs massive real-time compute for perception and decision-making, while quantum computing may provide new forms of compute in combinatorial optimization and large-scale parallel simulation. Shoucheng Holdings is accelerating the build-out of a three-dimensional frontier-tech portfolio spanning "physical-world intelligent agents" and "quantum-grade compute power," cementing its position in future-industry investing.
