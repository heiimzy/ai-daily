⚡ Aug 4 Tech Update: Palantir Beats With $1.85B Q2 But Stock Dips 2% After Hours, AI Giants Rally While Chips Slide, Nvidia Unveils Jetson T3000

In the early hours of August 4 Beijing time, Wall Street's Monday session officially closed: the S&P 500 jumped 1.57% toward record highs as AI giants Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Alphabet celebrated, while chip stocks extended their pullback. After the close, AI software leader Palantir delivered a beat-and-raise Q2 report — yet still faced a classic "sell the news" reaction. Meanwhile, Nvidia introduced the Jetson T3000/T2000 modules for robotics and edge AI along with the 4-billion-parameter Cosmos 3 Edge world model. Here is this roundup.

1. Palantir Q2: Revenue Hits $1.85B (Up 81%), Guidance Raised, Stock Still Dips ~2% After Hours

After Monday's close (early Aug 4 Beijing time), Palantir reported second-quarter 2026 results: revenue of $1.85 billion, up 81.3% year over year (from $1.02 billion a year ago) and ahead of the $1.7-1.81 billion consensus; adjusted EPS of $0.35 crushed the $0.28 estimate, up 105.9% YoY. It was Palantir's 12th consecutive quarter of revenue beats: government revenue rose 63.9% to $1.18 billion and commercial revenue more than doubled (+123.3%) to $0.67 billion, with customer count reaching 428 (+37.2%). CEO Alex Karp said on the call that the quarter "was a testament to our deepening relationships with both commercial and government clients," adding that the company is "still in the early innings of the AI revolution."

Forward guidance was strong too: Palantir guided Q3 revenue of $1.95-2.0 billion versus the $1.9 billion consensus, and raised full-year guidance to $7.6-7.8 billion, implying roughly 70% growth. CFO Dave Glazer cited a "flywheel effect" as each AIP deployment shortens the next sales cycle. Despite the beat-and-raise, shares still fell about 2% in after-hours trading — the stock closed Monday at $125.65 (up 2.1% on the day) but remains roughly 40% below its November 2025 all-time high of $207.52, as valuation concerns around ~40x revenue persist. The pattern of "beat every quarter, sell every time" continues.

2. Monday's Close: AI Giants Party While Chips Slide — CoreWeave Surges 15%

US markets closed sharply higher on Monday, August 3, lifted by President Trump's decision to call off strikes on Iran and a resulting plunge in oil prices that eased inflation fears: the S&P 500 rose 1.57% to 7,607.47, just shy of its June record of 7,620.90; the Dow gained 1.36% to 53,197.77 and the Nasdaq 100 rose 1.67% to 28,747. The AI giants were the biggest winners: Meta surged 6.37%, Microsoft rose 5.10% to $488.41, Amazon gained 5.09% to $285.39 (hitting a record intraday high near $287), Alphabet added 4.1%-4.7% and Nvidia rose 3.20% to $207.18. Amazon's 15.3% post-earnings surge on Friday (its best day since 2012) helped vindicate hyperscaler AI capex, a theme CNBC's Jim Cramer and others framed as "long overdue validation" of data-center spending.

In stark contrast, chip stocks extended their slide: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell more than 3% intraday, with Micron, Texas Instruments, Seagate, Western Digital (-5.7%), Arm (-5.7%), Marvell and NXP down 4%-6% as doubts about the durability of AI hardware demand lingered. Notably, AI-compute rental stock CoreWeave surged 14.97% to $82.51, extending Thursday's 23.5% bounce, with Oracle up 5.6% and Snowflake up 6% as the "neocloud" complex staged a violent rebound. WTI crude plunged 6.55% to below $80 a barrel and the 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.68%, giving growth stocks a double tailwind.

3. Nvidia Launches Jetson T3000/T2000 and Cosmos 3 Edge: World Models Move to the Edge

Nvidia expanded its Jetson Thor platform on August 3 with two new modules for humanoid robotics and edge AI: Jetson T3000 delivers 865 FP4 TFLOPS of AI compute in roughly half the size and power of the flagship T5000, pairing a Blackwell GPU with an 8-core Neoverse Arm CPU, 32GB of LPDDR5X memory and 25GbE connectivity; the functional-safety-oriented IGX T3000 runs the full NVIDIA Halos robotics safety stack. The entry-level Jetson T2000 brings 400 FP4 TFLOPS and 16GB of memory to visual AI agents, autonomous mobile robots and industrial manipulators. Nvidia's edge AI platform now spans from 70 TOPS to 2,000 TFLOPS.

On the software side, Nvidia released Cosmos 3 Edge, a 4-billion-parameter world model that lets embodied systems see, reason and predict actions in real time on-device. It ranks first among 4B-parameter models on the VANTAGE-Bench vision benchmark, delivers 15Hz real-time control on Jetson Thor and generates 32 actions per inference; the model is live on Hugging Face. New "Jetson agent skills" use AI agents to auto-optimize memory usage — UBTech, Agile Robots and Connect Tech have already cut memory consumption by up to 15GB. Partners building on Thor include 1X, Agile Robots, Amazon Robotics, Boston Dynamics, FANUC, Hitachi and Techman Robot. The T3000/T2000 modules are scheduled to ship in Q1 2027.

From Palantir's "beat-and-raise yet sell-off," to the sharp divergence between AI software and chip stocks, to Nvidia cramming a world model onto a robot motherboard — the message is clear: the AI narrative is shifting from "stacking compute" to "show me profits and real deployments." Whoever commercializes the inference-cost and edge-deployment loop first will earn the pricing power in the next phase.
