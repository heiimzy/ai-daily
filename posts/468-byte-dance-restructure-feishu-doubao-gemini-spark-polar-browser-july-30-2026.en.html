1. ByteDance Launches Landmark ToB Restructuring: Feishu Products Merge into Doubao, GTM into Volcano Engine

On the morning of July 30, ByteDance sent an internal memo announcing a sweeping AI business reorganization — the largest transformation of its ToB operations since the company adopted its business unit structure in 2021.

Under the restructuring plan, Feishu's (Lark's) product team will merge with Doubao's product team to form a new Doubao product organization, led by Doubao head Zhao Qi, with Feishu head Xie Xin reporting to Zhao. On the GTM (go-to-market) side, Feishu's GTM team will integrate with Volcano Engine to create a new ToB GTM organization called the "Creativity Service Platform," responsible for all MaaS and SaaS cloud service marketing, sales, and customer support.

The restructuring reveals ByteDance's strategic priorities. According to Caixin, ByteDance's AI model business has reached an annualized revenue run rate (ARR) of $4 billion as of July — surpassing the combined ARR of all other Chinese AI model companies. Doubao processes over 180 trillion daily token calls, Volcano Engine holds a significant share of the public cloud MaaS market, and Feishu's commercialization is accelerating — more than 90% of new Feishu customers in Q2 2026 also purchased Feishu AI products.

"This is the inevitable consolidation needed for an enterprise-grade AI agent — combining model, compute, data, tools, and entry point into one unified system. ByteDance is weaving Doubao's AI capabilities, Feishu's office scenarios, and Volcano Engine's cloud channels into a single rope." — Industry analyst

This move mirrors similar consolidations across China's tech giants. In the past month, Alibaba merged three Agent products into "Qianwen Office" under DingTalk; Tencent integrated QClaw into WorkBuddy; and Huawei's Xiaoyi has been the unified AI entry point for HarmonyOS from day one. According to Analysys Q2 2026 data, China's 17 major desktop AI office agents reached 60 million monthly visits in June, with Tencent WorkBuddy leading at 20.97 million and ByteDance's Trae ranking second at 12.79 million — ByteDance clearly cannot afford to fall behind in this AI entry-point race.

2. Google Launches Gemini Spark — A 24/7 Personal AI Agent That Works While You Sleep

Google rolled out Gemini Spark across multiple countries from July 29 to 30 — a 24/7 personal AI agent that marks a major evolution from reactive Q&A assistants to proactive autonomous executors.

Powered by Google's Gemini 3.6 Flash model, Gemini Spark runs on Google's cloud infrastructure even when the user's laptop is closed or phone is locked. It natively connects with Gmail, Google Docs, Google Sheets, and other Workspace tools with zero setup. For example, it can automatically extract flight or hotel booking confirmation emails and add them to a travel itinerary spreadsheet, scan Gmail for subscription renewals and recurring payments, or draft email replies and save them to the drafts folder.

Critical tasks such as sending emails or making payments require the user's prior approval. Gemini Spark is available to Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers, initially launching in South Korea, India, and Australia with Korean and English support.

A Google Korea official stated: "Gemini Spark will handle cumbersome digital tasks on behalf of users according to their instructions, helping them focus fully on what matters most." The product directly competes with Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg's preview of upcoming "personal AI agents" during Meta's recent earnings call — the AI agent battlefield is shifting from conversational chatbots to persistent autonomous execution.

3. Ex-Perplexity Engineer Launches Polar AI Browser — Automating Knowledge Work, Not Search

On July 29, Kevin Zhang (also known as Kevin Jiang), a former engineer at Perplexity who worked on the Comet browser, launched Polar AI Browser with a $5.7 million seed round led by Madrona. Polar targets not mass consumers but knowledge workers — sales, recruiting, marketing, research, and business operations professionals.

Polar's core thesis: AI browsers shouldn't be "smarter search engines" but "tools that actually do your work." Users can assign AI agents tasks based on their open browser tabs, save frequently used prompts, schedule recurring workflows, and let the AI autonomously handle cross-app data transfers, spreadsheet management, email processing, and other repetitive knowledge tasks — all without writing a single line of code.

"The first wave of AI browsers aimed to change default search for users and have them chat with tabs for doing tasks like travel booking. But consumers don't book a flight or make a reservation every day or every week. There wasn't a strong pull for mass consumers. That's why our AI browser is focused on knowledge work — where browser agents are actually valuable." — Kevin Zhang, CEO of Polar

Polar follows a freemium model: free users receive a limited daily AI credit allowance, while power users can subscribe for $20/month. Most early customers use Chrome as their primary browser and launch Polar specifically when automation is needed — a dual-browser strategy that reflects Polar's focused positioning as a dedicated automation tool rather than an all-purpose browser.

Polar's launch validates a broader shift in the AI browser market. OpenAI's Atlas has been discontinued, The Browser Company's Dia has pivoted toward productivity, and startups like Strawberry, Browser Use, and Aside are all building around AI agents rather than conversational interfaces. The next chapter of AI browsing won't be about smarter search — it will be about software that quietly completes work in the background.
