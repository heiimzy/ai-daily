TSMC Accelerates 1.4nm Fab Construction: First Fab by April 2027, Mass Production as Early as Mid-2028

TSMC is accelerating its advanced-node capacity buildout. According to a July 30 report from Taiwan's Commercial Times, construction of the foundry giant's 1.4nm fabs at the Taichung Phase 2 expansion is picking up pace, with the first fab (FAB) expected to be completed before April 2027 (republished by ITHome at 15:49 CST on Aug 1).

The report says contracts for the project have been progressively awarded, the first-stage piling works are largely done, and work is moving ahead on utility buildings (CUP) and office facilities. The project plans four fabs in total, with the first two already contracted and now in the steel-structure construction phase. Supply-chain sources estimate that if the current pace holds, the first fab could be finished before April 2027, with trial production as early as the beginning of Q3 2027. After equipment installation and line validation, mass production could follow by mid-2028 if all goes smoothly.

  • 1.4nm is the key node after 2nm (N2), keeping TSMC at the leading edge of advanced manufacturing;
  • The Taichung Phase 2 site is a major production base in central Taiwan, and the expansion will significantly lift advanced-node capacity;
  • With AI chip demand exploding, advanced-node capacity has become the strategic battleground for every major foundry.

TSMC is already pushing capacity in Arizona and Kumamoto, Japan, and the accelerated Taichung 1.4nm build shows a multi-front strategy in the advanced-node race. As the 2028 production window approaches, the fight for the 1.4nm node will only intensify.

Musk and Altman Rarely Agree: Humanity Is Already in the AI Singularity Era

Two of AI's most prominent figures have found rare common ground. According to tech outlet thenews, Elon Musk and Sam Altman both say humanity has entered the "AI singularity" era (republished by ITHome at 15:28 CST on Aug 1).

Altman, speaking on the Relentless podcast on July 25, said: "We're basically in the singularity era now." He also predicted AI can already automate roughly 30-40% of the work humans currently perform, calling AI the driver of "the biggest economic change since the Industrial Revolution." Musk followed with a post on X on July 31: "AI has surpassed humans in many ways. We are in the singularity."

"AI has surpassed humans in many ways. We are in the singularity." — Elon Musk, X, July 31, 2026

The "AI singularity" is a hypothesis popularized by futurist Ray Kurzweil in 2005, describing a point where AI's self-improvement outpaces humanity's ability to monitor or control it. Notably, this isn't the first time tech leaders have raised the concept: Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis called AI "the prototype of a singularity" in May, and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang declared in March that AGI has already arrived. When two long-time AI rivals like Musk and Altman line up on the same question, markets may need to reset their expectations for how fast AI is moving.

Denza Z9S Coming: Second-Gen Blade Battery Plus Flash Charging, Promising a New Global EV Range Record

BYD's premium brand Denza is about to unveil a heavyweight new model. Denza general manager Li Hui teased today that the Z9S will hold its pre-sale launch at 19:30 on Aug 3, featuring the second-generation blade battery and flash charging technology, with the launch set to reveal a new global range record for production EVs (ITHome, 16:04 CST, Aug 1).

The Denza Z9GT, launched in March, already carries the second-generation blade battery with an optional 122.496 kWh pack and a CLTC range of 1,036 km — the world's best for a production EV. Expectations that the Z9S will push range even further have electrified the market. The Z9S measures 5,090mm long, 1,980mm wide and 1,490mm high, with a 3,025mm wheelbase. Powertrain options include a rear single-motor version and a tri-motor all-wheel-drive variant using Denza's "Yi San Fang" system — the single-motor version delivers 370 kW, while the tri-motor AWD combines for a peak 890 kW.

  • The second-gen blade battery is one of BYD's core technologies, balancing high energy density with safety;
  • "Yi San Fang" tri-motor tech, already deployed on the Z9GT, enables more agile handling;
  • If the production Z9S beats the Z9GT's 1,036 km, it will set a new range benchmark for production EVs.

As the NEV market enters a "range arms race," Denza's global-range ambition for the Z9S is both a showcase of BYD's technology and a signal of a renewed push upmarket.

Chery Tops 200,000 Monthly Exports: A First for a Chinese Automaker

Chinese automakers' global push just hit a new milestone. Chery Group said on Aug 1 that it sold 276,820 vehicles in July 2026, up 23.3% year over year, including 202,533 exports — up 70.1% — making it the first Chinese automaker to exceed 200,000 exports in a single month (ITHome, 15:53 CST, Aug 1).

In the same month, the group sold 129,067 new-energy vehicles, up 97.5% year over year, as NEV penetration keeps climbing. Chery Automobile (the listed entity) sold 261,876 vehicles in July, up 24.1%, and 1,536,952 vehicles cumulatively in January-July, up 10.3%. Group-wide, cumulative January-July sales reached 1,634,353 vehicles, up 10.1%, including cumulative exports of 1,146,350 (up 71.2%) and NEV sales of 604,305 (up 42.3%).

  • Export growth of 70.1% far outpaces total sales growth, making overseas markets Chery's core growth engine;
  • NEV sales nearly doubled year over year at +97.5%, accelerating its electrification shift;
  • The export record comes despite trade barriers such as EU tariffs, underscoring the rising global competitiveness of Chinese automakers.

Chery's export breakthrough is not an isolated case. Chinese automakers including BYD, Chery and SAIC are expanding aggressively across Southeast Asia, the Middle East and Latin America. Crossing 200,000 monthly exports marks a historic shift for China's auto industry — from "bringing in" to "going global."

Meta Smart Glasses Privacy Storm: UK Blogger Investigated, Apple Reportedly Delays AI Glasses

Smart glasses are becoming the new battleground for tech giants — and a growing privacy headache. According to a BBC report on July 31, TikTok blogger Scott Margerison is under investigation by Scottish police after using Meta smart glasses to film strangers in public and posting the videos online (ITHome, 15:20 CST, Aug 1).

Margerison recorded multiple videos with Meta smart glasses and posted them on his social accounts. He approached strangers unprompted, insulting people, making sexualized remarks toward young women, and clashing with a Sikh shopkeeper. Scottish police confirmed they had received complaints about the videos and are investigating. Reports say Margerison allegedly made sexualized comments about two teenagers' appearance before wearing the glasses to record conversations with the teens and their parents; in another video he told a British-born Sikh shopkeeper that Europe's diversity was "forcefully imposed."

"Smart glasses embed tiny cameras, letting wearers film their surroundings and passersby at any time. Safety mechanisms have not stopped more and more people from secretly recording others." — ITHome report

Meta says its products light up an LED indicator when recording, and the recording function auto-disables if the indicator appears blocked or tampered with. But that protection hasn't fully curbed abuse. The wider fallout: Bloomberg previously reported that mounting privacy concerns led Apple to delay the launch of its AI smart glasses. With Google, Samsung, Apple and Meta all racing into the category, balancing "convenience" against "privacy" will be the question this emerging product class must answer.
