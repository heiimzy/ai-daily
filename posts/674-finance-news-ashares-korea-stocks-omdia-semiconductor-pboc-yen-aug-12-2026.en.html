Global equities and the semiconductor supply chain delivered a barrage of headlines on August 12 afternoon: China's A-share indices rose across the board at the midday close, with the ChiNext up 1.73% and half-day turnover at RMB 1.39 trillion while compute-hardware stocks led gains; South Korea's KOSPI climbed more than 5% intraday, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both up over 8%, triggering the SIDECAR mechanism that paused program buying; research firm Omdia raised its 2026 global semiconductor revenue growth forecast to 94.1%, with memory ICs set to capture over half of industry revenue; China's central bank skipped reverse-repo operations for a second straight day; and USD/JPY briefly touched 159.43, the highest since the US-Japan joint intervention. 1. China A-Shares Rise Across the Board at Midday as ChiNext Gains 1.73%

According to WallstreetCN (published 11:30, Aug 12), China's A-share indices all closed higher at the midday break: the Shanghai Composite settled at 3946.51, up 0.32%; the Shenzhen Component rose 1.25% to 14438.07; and the ChiNext gained 1.73% to 3610.65. The CSI 300 added 0.65%, the STAR 50 gained 2.09%, the CSI 500 rose 1.18% and the CSI 1000 gained 1.37%. On the tape, compute-hardware names rallied broadly — Raytron, Yongding and Tongding Interconnect hit daily limit; semiconductor chip names rose with Xiandao Jidian up for a fourth day in five and Zhichun Tech hitting limit-up; and innovative drugs kept buzzing as Baihua Pharmaceutical extended its streak to seven boards. On the downside, oil/gas and coal slipped, with Xindazhou A and Shouhua Gas down over 5%. More than 3,600 stocks across the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing boards were in the green, with half-day turnover reaching RMB 1.39 trillion. After lunch, with fiber optics, lithography and memory chips leading sector gains, the ChiNext extended its advance to 2% (WallstreetCN, published 13:02, Aug 12), and the A-share optical-components segment kept climbing, with GZTech and China Optics both up over 10%.

2. South Korea's KOSPI Climbs Over 5% Intraday; Samsung and SK Hynix Both Surge Over 8%

According to WallstreetCN (published 10:58-11:17, Aug 12), South Korea's KOSPI index widened its intraday gains to more than 5%, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both rising over 8%, prompting Korea Exchange to activate the SIDECAR mechanism, temporarily suspending program buying in KOSPI. SIDECAR is a circuit-breaker-style safeguard Korea uses to pause program trading when the index swings sharply. The rally in the two semiconductor giants reflects expectations of strong AI-driven memory demand and improving industry conditions; the KOSPI's volume-driven surge that tripped the breaker also points to aggressive buying by foreign and domestic funds. Across the Asia-Pacific session, Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 0.1% and the Topix gained 0.3%, while Indonesian and Vietnamese benchmarks also firmed, keeping regional sentiment constructive.

3. Omdia Raises 2026 Global Semiconductor Growth to 94.1%; Memory to Capture Over Half

According to WallstreetCN (published 12:45, Aug 12) citing Omdia's latest forecast, because AI demand continues to outpace global supply capacity and is driving strong growth in the DRAM and NAND markets, Omdia has upgraded its 2026 global semiconductor revenue growth forecast to 94.1% year over year, and expects memory IC revenue to account for more than 50% of total global semiconductor revenue in 2026. Omdia also noted that AI demand now exceeds the industry's current chip manufacturing and packaging capacity, and that bottlenecks in high-bandwidth memory (HBM), advanced packaging and advanced-node capacity are likely to persist at least until 2027. This view underpins the strength in A-share compute-hardware names and Korea's memory duo, and suggests the global semiconductor supply-demand squeeze will not ease soon.

4. PBOC Skips Open-Market Reverse Repos for a Second Straight Day

According to WallstreetCN (published 12:30, Aug 12) key-news summary, China's central bank conducted zero reverse-repo operations for a second consecutive day. Reverse repos are the central bank's routine tool for managing short-term liquidity in the banking system; two straight days of zero operations is usually read as a sign that interbank liquidity is comfortably ample and no extra injection is needed to keep funding stable. Given recent sessions, money-market sentiment has stayed calm and short-end funding rates moved little. Investors should watch whether the PBOC resumes operations around tax periods or quarter-end deadlines to gauge whether its neutral liquidity posture is holding.

5. USD/JPY Rises to 159.43, the Highest Since US-Japan Joint Intervention

According to WallstreetCN (published 10:45, Aug 12), USD/JPY briefly climbed to 159.43, the highest level since the coordinated intervention by US and Japanese authorities. The yen's renewed weakness after that intervention reflects the market's continued pricing of still-wide US-Japan rate differentials and expectations of a relatively cautious pace of Bank of Japan rate hikes. USD/JPY's climb also shapes regional capital flows and export-sector earnings expectations, making it one of the most-watched variables in the FX market right now. Investors should keep an eye on subsequent BOJ official comments and whether the yen's rapid depreciation again invites official action.
