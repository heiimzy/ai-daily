⚡ FOMC Day 2: Maximum Uncertainty in Years — 38% Rate Hike Odds, Coca-Cola Beats and Raises Guidance, Boeing Loss Narrows But Misses

1. FOMC Day 2: The Warsh Fed's "Stress Test"

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) enters the second day of its July 28-29 policy meeting on Tuesday, marking Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh's second rate-setting session since his May confirmation — and arguably the most uncertain policy window in years.

According to the CME FedWatch Tool, market pricing for a 25-basis-point rate hike this week has surged from approximately 10% two weeks ago to 34-38%, while the probability of a hold stands at about 62%. Akshay Singal, global head of US short-term interest rates trading at Citigroup, noted that market pricing divergence around this decision is "the largest since September 2024." On Kalshi, traders assign a 68% probability to at least one rate hike occurring by year-end.

Citadel Bets on a Surprise Hike vs. 104 Economists Expect No Change

The core of the uncertainty stems from Warsh's decisive break from predecessor Jerome Powell's "forward guidance" playbook. Warsh has dramatically shortened the FOMC statement, declined to signal rate paths in multiple public appearances, and refused to pre-commit to any policy stance — leaving markets unable to handicap the outcome.

According to Bloomberg, Frank Flight, head of macro strategy at Citadel Securities, wrote in a research report that the Fed could surprise markets with a 25-basis-point hike this week, a move that would solidify Chairman Warsh's anti-inflation credibility and "decisively end the era of forward guidance." Flight argued that hiking now would carry more impact than waiting until September by fundamentally reshaping expectations of how the Fed responds to inflation.

"We doubt Warsh would face widespread opposition if he argued for tightening. The final decision could go either way, but we think the Fed is more likely to raise rates by 25 basis points on Wednesday than to stand pat." — Wrightson ICAP analysts

However, all 104 economists surveyed by Reuters in mid-July expect rates to remain unchanged this week, with 78 of them believing the current rate level will be maintained through December. Goldman Sachs chief US economist David Mericle believes that following softer June inflation data, most voting members are unlikely to push for a hike this week, though at least one dissenting vote in favor of a hike is expected.

The Inflation-Energy Nexus Driving Hike Odds

The key variable driving rate hike expectations higher comes from the energy market. Middle East tensions escalated again this month, with Brent crude briefly breaking above $100 per barrel and posting a monthly gain of over 20%. Although oil prices pulled back after the US suspended airstrikes on Iran Monday, the risk of Houthi rebels threatening Red Sea exports has not been resolved. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack has pointed to energy and AI costs pushing up inflation as factors forcing the Fed to consider rate hikes.

Goldman Sachs data shows inflation pressures are spreading beyond energy into healthcare, financial services, and other sectors. With a normalized index where the 1990-2019 average equals 0 and the 2022 peak equals 10, the current inflation pressure index has climbed to 6. On the other hand, June CPI unexpectedly fell to 3.5% (from 4.2% in May) and core CPI eased to 2.6%, giving the Fed data to support a "wait and see" approach. Evercore ISI noted that raising rates immediately after June's improved inflation data "would look strange."

2. Coca-Cola Q2 Beats Estimates, Raises Full-Year Guidance

Before Tuesday's opening bell, Coca-Cola (KO) reported second-quarter 2026 results that beat expectations across the board and raised its full-year guidance.

Key highlights:

  • EPS: GAAP EPS of $1.03, up 16% YoY; comparable EPS (non-GAAP) of $0.97, up 11% YoY, beating the $0.93 consensus estimate
  • Revenue: Net revenues of $13.4 billion, up 7% YoY; organic revenues (non-GAAP) grew 6%, above the prior guidance range of 4%-5%
  • Operating margin: GAAP operating margin of 34.9% (vs 34.1% in Q2 2025); comparable operating margin of 35.6% (vs 34.7%)
  • Global volume: Unit case volume grew 5%, driven by 4% concentrate sales growth and 2% price/mix growth

Coca-Cola raised its full-year 2026 guidance: organic revenue growth now expected at approximately 5% (up from 4%-5%), and comparable EPS growth of 9%-10% (up from 8%-9%). CEO Henrique Braun commented: "We delivered another strong quarter by staying close to the changing needs of our consumers and customers."

Coca-Cola shares traded approximately 1.5% higher in pre-market trading on the results and raised guidance.

3. Boeing Q2: Loss Narrows but Misses Expectations, Record $715B Backlog

Also reporting before the bell Tuesday, Boeing (BA) posted second-quarter results that showed meaningful improvement year-over-year but fell short of market expectations.

Key metrics:

  • Revenue: $24.56 billion, up 8% YoY, driven by 171 commercial aircraft deliveries (+14% YoY)
  • GAAP loss per share: ($0.67), improving from ($0.92) in Q2 2025
  • Core loss per share (non-GAAP): ($0.76), missing the consensus estimate of ($0.27)
  • Operating cash flow: $1.36 billion, up 501% from $227 million a year ago
  • Free cash flow: $631 million, compared to ($200 million) a year ago
  • Total backlog: A record $715 billion, including over 6,200 commercial airplanes

A key milestone: The FAA restored Boeing's authority to self-certify airworthiness for all newly built 737 MAX and 787 jets on July 20, removing a critical operational bottleneck. CEO Kelly Ortberg stated: "Our operations are more stable and key certification programs remain on plan. While there is more work ahead in the second half of the year, the momentum we are building continues to move Boeing in the right direction."

Boeing shares declined approximately 3% in pre-market trading as the wider-than-expected loss disappointed investors.

4. US Pre-Market: Nasdaq Continues to Slide, Dow Strengthens

US stock futures were mixed Tuesday morning, continuing Monday's sector rotation pattern:

  • Nasdaq 100 futures: Down approximately 0.8%-1%, as semiconductor stocks remained under pressure. Nvidia edged lower in pre-market as concerns over AI "circular financing" persisted
  • S&P 500 futures: Nearly flat, down approximately 0.1%
  • Dow Jones Industrial Average futures: Up approximately 0.3%-0.6%, benefiting from falling oil prices and strength in consumer staples

The rotation out of mega-cap tech into broader markets was already evident Monday: the S&P 500 Equal Weight Index gained 0.72%, while the market-cap-weighted S&P 500 rose just 0.02% — a spread of 1.43 percentage points, among the largest in recent months. Advancing stocks outnumbered decliners 1.6-to-1, indicating broadening bullish participation.

This is "Super Week" for earnings, with roughly one-third of S&P 500 companies reporting. Beyond Coca-Cola and Boeing, today's post-market reports include Visa (V), Ford (F), Teradyne (TER), and others. Wednesday through Thursday, the four mega-cap tech giants — Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon — will report, with markets focused intently on AI capital expenditure guidance.

5. Oil Continues to Slide, Treasury Yields Ease

Against the backdrop of the US-Iran pause in hostilities, international oil prices extended Monday's sharp declines. In Asian trading Tuesday:

  • Brent crude: Fell another 1.4% to approximately $87.15/barrel, after plunging 8.7% on Monday
  • WTI crude: Declined 1.3% to approximately $81.70/barrel, after Monday's 7.5% drop

Treasury yields eased alongside oil. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at approximately 4.64%, down from last week's high of 4.72%. The 2-year yield also edged lower as markets adjusted positions ahead of the FOMC decision.

The US Dollar Index traded in a narrow range around 101.50 as markets remained cautious before the Fed. If the FOMC surprises with a hike, the dollar could break through the 101.50-101.80 resistance zone; if it holds steady but delivers a hawkish statement, the dollar may also find support.

6. Economic Data and Outlook

Tuesday's US economic data calendar includes:

  • Advance goods trade balance (June)
  • Advance retail and wholesale inventories (June)
  • S&P/Case-Shiller 20-city home price index (May)
  • Conference Board Consumer Confidence (July)

Economists expect the consumer confidence index to reflect the impact of elevated oil prices, interest rates, and labor market conditions on US household expectations.

All eyes are now on Wednesday, July 29: the FOMC rate decision at 2:00 PM ET, followed by Chairman Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET. In a communication framework without forward guidance, every word from Warsh will be scrutinized for signals about the September meeting and the broader rate path.
