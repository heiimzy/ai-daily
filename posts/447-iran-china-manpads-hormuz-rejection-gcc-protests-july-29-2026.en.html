⚡ July 29 World Affairs Quadruple: Iran to Receive 400 Chinese MANPADS Within Weeks, Hormuz Talks Hit Deadlock, Kuwait Mediates as Saudi Oil Sites Struck, Washington Erupts Against Netanyahu

I. Exclusive: Iran to Receive 400 Chinese Shoulder-Fired Air Defense Systems Within Weeks

Iran is set to receive a first shipment of up to 400 Chinese-made shoulder-fired air defense missile launchers within weeks, three sources familiar with the deal told Reuters on July 29, as the country rebuilds its defenses amid the ongoing war with the United States and Israel.

The purchase, valued at $60-70 million, represents one of Tehran's largest-known efforts to strengthen its short-range air defenses since the war began in late February. The conflict has exposed critical gaps in Iran's ability to protect military sites and strategic infrastructure from repeated U.S. airstrikes.

The contract covers the purchase of between 300 and 400 man-portable air defense systems (MANPADS), including Chinese-made QW-12 and FN-16 missiles. The deal was signed with Zhongqing Baoshang International Investment, a Hong Kong-based company that sources said was acting as an intermediary between Iranian officials and the Chinese defense manufacturer.

Under the agreed delivery plan, shipments will initially be flown from Urumqi in western China, then transited through Pakistan to Iran. A European security source confirmed his country's authorities were aware of multiple contracts under discussion involving QW-series MANPADS sales to Iran, including QW-12, QW-18, and QW-19 systems.

The QW-12 is an upgraded variant of the QW-2, featuring a mid-infrared dual-band seeker and a contact/proximity composite fuze, with an effective range of 500-6,000 meters and altitude reach of up to 5,000 meters. It is designed to engage low-flying aircraft, helicopters, drones, and cruise missiles, with demonstrated anti-decoy capabilities that Chinese experts describe as world-class.

The FN-16 is an infrared-guided system with similar range specifications. Defense analysts say that while the QW-12 is less capable than newer variants like the QW-18 and QW-19, these systems can still provide an effective layer of short-range protection against drones and low-flying targets.

The delivery of hundreds of MANPADS would significantly expand Iran's short-range air defense inventory and underscore deepening military ties between Tehran and Beijing. U.S. investigators are also examining whether a Chinese-made MANPADS was responsible for shooting down a U.S. F-15E Strike Eagle over southwestern Iran last month, NBC News previously reported.

II. Hormuz Stalemate: Iran Rejects Oman's 50-50 Proposal, Warns Strait Will Remain Closed

Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi told state television on July 28 that Tehran has formally rejected Oman's proposal for an equal division of transit routes in the Strait of Hormuz, warning that the waterway will remain closed unless its counterproposal is accepted.

Oman had submitted a Gulf-backed joint management plan based on the Strait of Malacca model, under which ships would pay voluntary fees to fund navigation management, environmental protection, and search-and-rescue operations. The proposal aimed to prevent Iran from exercising sole control over the strategic waterway.

However, Gharibabadi presented Iran's counterproposal: Tehran demands full control over one direction of traffic through the strait, plus partial control over the opposite route passing through Omani waters. He also declared that Iran no longer recognizes the International Maritime Organization's Traffic Separation Scheme (TSS), arguing that the channel lies mostly within Omani waters and compromises Iran's security needs.

"If the Strait of Hormuz returns to its pre-war status, our success in the war will not be complete," Gharibabadi said, emphasizing that the strait represents a "new defense capacity" now integral to Iran's national security. He warned that any European warship approaching the strait would be considered a "legitimate target."

Iran also rejected any third-party involvement in mine clearance operations, insisting that de-mining rights must remain exclusively with Tehran. Gharibabadi added that Iran has "no plans to negotiate with the US," affirming that talks are proceeding only with Oman.

Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) foreign ministers convened a video conference on Tuesday, chaired by Omani Foreign Minister Badr Al Busaidi, to discuss reducing hostilities and restoring freedom of navigation in the waterway that carried one-fifth of global energy supplies before the war.

III. Kuwait Condemns Attacks on Saudi Oil Infrastructure, Engages Qatar in Emergency Diplomacy

Kuwait's Foreign Ministry on July 29 issued a strong condemnation of attacks on oil infrastructure in Saudi Arabia's Eastern Province by Iran-aligned armed groups, describing them as "blatant aggression" and a "flagrant violation" of Saudi sovereignty.

The statement said the attacks reflect the "insistence on escalating tensions" by Iran-aligned groups despite Riyadh's "sincere efforts" to de-escalate. Kuwait reaffirmed its "absolute solidarity" with Saudi Arabia and backed all measures the kingdom takes to protect its security and resources.

In a parallel diplomatic track, Kuwaiti Foreign Minister Sheikh Jarrah Jaber Al-Ahmad Al-Sabah held a phone call with Qatari Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani to discuss the broader regional security situation. The two ministers also explored measures to safeguard freedom of navigation in strategic waterways critical to global energy supplies.

Saudi air defenses have intercepted drones launched from Iraqi territory by Iran-backed militias for a second consecutive day, targeting petroleum facilities in the kingdom's Eastern Province. Saudi Arabia has confirmed conducting retaliatory strikes against Iran-aligned targets in Iraq in coordination with U.S. Central Command.

IV. Washington Erupts: Thousands Protest Netanyahu, Storm Hotel Lobby — 49% of Americans Favor Arrest

As Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu held closed-door talks with President Donald Trump at the White House, Washington, D.C. witnessed massive protests demanding his arrest under the International Criminal Court's warrant.

Hundreds of demonstrators marched toward the White House in heavy rain, chanting "arrest Netanyahu" and calling on U.S. authorities to enforce the ICC arrest warrant issued in November 2024. The warrant charges Netanyahu and former Defense Minister Yoav Gallant with war crimes and crimes against humanity — including starvation as a method of warfare, murder, persecution, and other inhumane acts — committed during Israel's genocidal war on Gaza.

The protests escalated dramatically in the evening when a group of activists breached security at the Four Seasons Hotel in Georgetown, where Netanyahu was dining. Video footage verified by multiple news agencies shows protesters storming the lobby and attempting to reach the restaurant before being forcibly removed by security personnel. Chants of "Bibi, Bibi, you can't hide... you're committing genocide" echoed through the hotel.

Public opinion in the United States has shifted markedly against the Israeli leader. An Economist/YouGov poll published ahead of the visit found that 49% of U.S. adults believe the country should arrest Netanyahu — nearly double the 27% who oppose it. This includes 68% of Democrats, 55% of independents, and even 23% of 2024 Trump voters.

The controversy has also ensnared European ICC member states. Netanyahu's flight to Washington traversed the airspace of multiple ICC signatories — including France, Italy, and Greece — none of which acted on their obligation under the Rome Statute to arrest him.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, whose office recently explored legal avenues to arrest Netanyahu if he visits the city for the UN General Assembly, labeled the Israeli prime minister a "war criminal, the architect of a horrific genocide against the Palestinian people." Netanyahu dismissed the remarks as "hate speech."
