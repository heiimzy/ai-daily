⚡ Aug 6 World Briefing: FIFA Backs Infantino as Carney Curses, Trump Hits El-Sayed, Uganda Captain Killed

Over the past hour or two, three major stories dominated the international stage: FIFA held an emergency meeting in Rabat, Morocco, where Gianni Infantino apologized for his collapsed World Cup privatization plan but retained the backing of the executive board — while Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney publicly cursed and said he has lost faith in the FIFA president; in the United States, Donald Trump attacked Michigan Senate primary winner Abdul El-Sayed, repeatedly calling him a "hater of Jews"; and in Africa, 27-year-old Ugandan football captain David Owori was beaten to death by suspected robbers outside his home in Kampala, prompting nationwide mourning. Here is this round's world briefing.

1. FIFA emergency meeting: Infantino apologizes but stays, Carney curses

According to Al Jazeera's August 6 report, FIFA's executive board backed president Gianni Infantino after an emergency meeting in Rabat, Morocco — but the fallout from his failed plan to sell stakes in the World Cup to private investors is far from over. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, whose country co-hosted this year's World Cup, was captured on video cursing Infantino and saying he has lost faith in him.

The crisis stems from FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE): Infantino proposed selling stakes in tournaments including the men's and women's World Cups to private investors through a new subsidiary, offering each member association $40m (£30m) to back the deal. After The Times broke the story on 28 July, UEFA said at the weekend that it had lost confidence in Infantino, calling the proposal a "shabby, back room, opaque deal"; FIFA secretary general Mattias Grafstrom wrote in an internal memo on Tuesday that the situation was "a sad and reproachable series of events."

Yet after Wednesday's meeting, Grafstrom and the management board "reaffirmed their full support" for Infantino, and the two men sent a signed letter to FIFA's vice-presidents, council and 211 member associations, "sincerely apologizing" for their errors and committing that they would "not happen again." FIFA also denied a Times report that Infantino had promised Morocco the 2030 World Cup final in exchange for support. Infantino, first elected in 2016, has led FIFA for a decade — this is one of the most serious trust crises of his tenure, with skepticism now spreading from football circles into politics.

2. Trump attacks El-Sayed as a "hater of Jews" after Michigan win

According to Al Jazeera's August 6 report, US President Donald Trump attacked Michigan Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed, repeatedly calling him a "hater of Jews" and "a hater of Israel." El-Sayed won the Democratic nomination on Tuesday, defeating a well-funded opponent in what was widely seen as a showdown between the left and the pro-Israel establishment.

BBC analysis notes the victory sent tremors through Democratic Party leadership: El-Sayed, the child of Egyptian immigrants and a former Michigan health official, ran on a left-wing platform backed by Senators Bernie Sanders and Elizabeth Warren and Congresswoman Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez. His opponent, Congresswoman Haley Stevens, had the backing of Washington's Democratic establishment and $32m from AIPAC, with outside groups favoring her by a nine-to-one margin. El-Sayed's campaign refrain — "money out of politics, money in your pocket" — helped him overcome the spending gap. Trump had earlier also called him a "communist."

The race matters far beyond Michigan: it is a battleground state, and a Republican has not won its Senate seat since 1994. In November, El-Sayed will face Republican Mike Rogers. A left-wing victory would energize Democratic hopes of retaking the Senate and offer a blueprint for the party's left in 2028; a defeat could badly damage the party's midterm prospects.

3. Uganda football captain David Owori killed in street attack, aged 27

According to a BBC report on August 6, David Owori, the captain of Ugandan record champions SC Villa, was beaten to death by suspected robbers outside his home in Kampala at the age of 27, triggering national mourning and fresh anger over violent crime. Police said a group of attackers struck Owori with paving stones on Tuesday night as he approached his home in the Makindye suburb, stealing his mobile phone and other belongings and leaving him unconscious; he died of his injuries on Wednesday.

Owori, a right-back and midfielder popularly known as "Colgate," captained SC Villa, the record champions of the Uganda Premier League, and represented the national team, the Cranes, in the 2021 Africa Cup of Nations qualifying campaign. He returned to SC Villa in 2023 and helped the club win its first league title in 20 years in 2024 — a 17th top-flight crown — earning a contract extension until January 2027. "We have lost more than a player — we have lost a leader, brother and friend," SC Villa said in a statement.

Uganda's parliament observed a minute of silence on Wednesday and called for a swift investigation, while social media erupted in anger over violent crime in Kampala. Notably, another Ugandan sports star, rugby international Sydney Gongodyo, was fatally attacked by a mob in Kampala just weeks earlier — the two tragedies have thrust the East African nation's public security crisis into the spotlight.
