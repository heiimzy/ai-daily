⚡ Aug 3 Tech Update: South Korea Breaks Ground on National AI Computing Center, Microsoft's Project Perception Enters Public Preview

Monday afternoon brought two landmark moments for global AI infrastructure and security. In East Asia, South Korea officially broke ground on its National AI Computing Center in Haenam County, moving a public-private project worth roughly KRW 2.5 trillion into full construction with a target of 15,000 GPUs by 2028. Across the Pacific, Microsoft's Project Perception went into public preview as scheduled, deploying red, blue and green AI security agents to reshape the "AI against AI" defense paradigm. Here is the roundup.

I. Groundbreaking for Korea's National AI Computing Center: KRW 2.5 trillion, 15,000 GPUs by 2028

South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) held a groundbreaking ceremony on Monday afternoon at the Solarido Data Center Park in Haenam County, Gwangju-Jeonnam, marking the start of full-scale construction. The National AI Computing Center is the core infrastructure of the government's "AI Highway" initiative, with a total investment of about KRW 2.5 trillion (approximately USD 1.8 billion). The facility covers a 48,996-square-meter site with a total floor area of 16,978 square meters across two above-ground floors, housing a computing building, operations building and ancillary structures.

Under the plan, the center will establish roughly 15,000 advanced GPUs by 2028, expanding to 50,000 GPUs by 2030. It will provide AI R&D and service environments for industry, academia and research institutes, and include an R&D zone supporting the design, prototyping and performance verification of domestically made AI semiconductors — moving "sovereign AI" from demonstrations to on-site infrastructure.

"Through the National AI Computing Center, the government and the private sector will work together to secure advanced GPUs and revitalize domestic AI semiconductors, contributing to full-stack AI competitiveness and aiming to become an AI infrastructure hub in Asia." — an MSIT official at the groundbreaking ceremony.

II. Public-private KOACC: Samsung SDS consortium leads, Naver Cloud and Kakao join in

Construction and operation of the project are exclusively handled by KOACC (Korea AI Computing Center Co., Ltd.), a public-private special purpose company established in June 2026 with investment from MSIT, the National Growth Fund under the Financial Services Commission, and the Samsung SDS consortium. Initial capital totals about KRW 400 billion (approximately USD 280 million), with private investors contributing 71% and the public sector 29%.

The consortium is stacked with heavyweights: Samsung SDS is the largest shareholder with a KRW 120 billion investment for a 30% stake, Naver Cloud holds 26.1% through its KRW 104.4 billion investment, and other participants include Samsung C&T, Kakao, Samsung Electronics, KT, Clush, Jeonnam Development Corp and the Gwangju-Jeonnam Integrated Special City. Ahn Jung-tae, former vice president and CFO of Samsung SDS, serves as CEO. KOACC also plans to raise up to KRW 2 trillion or more in loans through policy financing and project financing.

Notably, before the center is completed in 2028, KOACC plans to deploy some computing resources as early as 2027 at Samsung SDS data centers to respond to urgent AI compute demand. For power, the company has applied to Korea Electric Power Corporation for electricity service and will build capacity in phases from 40MW to a total of 80MW.

III. Microsoft Project Perception enters public preview: red, blue and green AI agents

Meanwhile, Microsoft confirmed that its agentic security system Project Perception entered public preview on August 3. Built for a world where AI-powered attacks move at machine speed, Perception coordinates three classes of AI agents in a continuous loop: red team agents that hunt for attack paths before attackers exploit them, blue team agents that investigate and determine which risks are meaningful, and green team agents that fix and harden defenses — all while keeping humans in control of critical decisions.

Perception launches inside Microsoft Defender and will extend across Microsoft Security over time. Pricing is consumption-based, metered in Security Compute Units, so scanning breadth becomes a budget line rather than a licensing tier. Microsoft draws a clear line between Perception — "AI that acts" — and Security Copilot — "AI that assists."

"Attackers are using AI to lower the cost and scale of attacks, so defenders also need to sense, reason and act at machine speed." — Hayete Gallot, EVP of Microsoft Security.

IV. MAI-Cyber-1-Flash: 96% on CyberGym, 50% cost savings

The core milestone behind Perception is MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, Microsoft's first in-house cybersecurity model. Descended from the MAI-Thinking-1 line, it sits inside MDASH, Microsoft's multi-agent vulnerability identification and remediation harness with 100+ agents, absorbing up to about 90% of routine vulnerability work and escalating only the hardest tenth of tasks to larger models such as GPT-5.4.

Microsoft reports that the MDASH + MAI-Cyber-1-Flash configuration scores 96% on CyberGym — a UC Berkeley benchmark of 1,507 real vulnerabilities across 188 open-source projects drawn from Google's OSS-Fuzz corpus — about 12 points above Anthropic's Mythos. The company also claims roughly 50% cost savings versus its current MDASH configuration (GPT-5.4 + 5.4 mini + 5.3 Codex). During a private preview that began in May, the pipeline reportedly surfaced 16 new vulnerabilities, including four critical remote-code-execution flaws in the Windows networking and authentication stack. These are vendor-reported figures, yet to be independently verified in real customer environments.

V. Summary: compute infrastructure and AI security accelerate in tandem

Together, the two threads of Monday afternoon sketch both sides of the AI industry's evolution: South Korea is betting national will on compute infrastructure and domestic AI chips, turning "compute sovereignty" into a USD 1.8 billion physical project; Microsoft is using agentic security to move AI security from "assisting humans" to "acting for humans." One builds the supply-side foundation of compute; the other builds the trust foundation for AI deployment — both point to the same conclusion: the AI race has spread from model parameters to infrastructure and security paradigms.
