⚡ July 27 Tech Mega: SK Group Seals $750B AI Semiconductor Deal with Nvidia, Microsoft & Anthropic — HBM4 Co-Development, 2GW AI Factory, Largest Infrastructure Commitment in History

Largest-Ever AI Infrastructure Collaboration Born

In a landmark announcement from the K-AI Summit in San Francisco, SK Group has partnered with NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Anthropic on a sweeping series of AI infrastructure agreements totaling approximately $750 billion. The collaboration spans AI memory supply, AI factory construction, and gigawatt-scale data centers — the largest combined commitment in the history of AI infrastructure.

According to The Herald Business, SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won and NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang signed the agreements at the Fairmont Hotel in San Francisco, with South Korean President Lee Jae-myung in attendance. The deal represents a centerpiece of South Korea's national AI strategy.

NVIDIA & SK: $500B+ AI Factory and Advanced Memory Dual Engine

SK Group and NVIDIA signed a Letter of Intent (LOI) for comprehensive cooperation in AI infrastructure construction worth more than $500 billion. The partnership spans two core domains:

1. Gigawatt-Scale AI Factory: SK Telecom will receive next-generation GPUs from NVIDIA to build what would be South Korea's largest AI factory with a capacity of up to 2 GW. SK Telecom plans to deploy NVIDIA's DSX platform — the company's full-stack AI factory architecture — and introduce the Vera Rubin next-generation AI computing system equipped with SK Hynix's HBM4, with the first phase coming online in 2027.

2. Long-Term AI Memory Partnership: SK Hynix will supply AI memory to NVIDIA on a long-term basis, with joint development and optimization of next-generation HBM products to meet growing infrastructure demand across LLM training, agentic AI, and physical AI applications. SK Hynix previously announced that its entire 2026 HBM production capacity is already sold out, and this long-term partnership further cements its leadership in the HBM market.

"South Korea has everything it needs to emerge as a global AI powerhouse — world-class networks, data center capabilities, semiconductor technology leadership, and a vast industrial base," said NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang at the summit.

SK Hynix & Microsoft: Long-Term Server Memory Agreement

SK Hynix also reached a long-term memory supply agreement with Microsoft, under which the two companies will supply server memory optimized for AI workloads to Microsoft's data centers. As Microsoft continues expanding Azure cloud services and Copilot product AI capabilities, stable supply of AI-optimized memory has become a critical bottleneck. SK Hynix, as a global leader in AI memory, provides Microsoft with medium-to-long-term supply certainty for its data center buildout.

SK Telecom & Anthropic: GW-Scale AI Data Center MOU

SK Telecom and Anthropic signed a comprehensive Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) to cooperate on building a gigawatt-scale AI data center in South Korea. Anthropic — one of the world's most competitive AI model developers whose Claude series competes at the frontier alongside OpenAI's GPT series — plans to participate in SK Telecom's domestic AI data center construction project. The two parties will continue discussions on specific collaboration areas.

Notably, Anthropic's AI compute strategy has become genuinely multi-vendor: in addition to the SK Telecom partnership, it has separate arrangements with Google/Broadcom, Amazon (Trainium chips, 5GW), CoreWeave (NVIDIA GPUs), and SpaceX (Colossus data centers). The SK partnership further diversifies its global compute footprint.

SK Meets with AWS and OpenAI to Expand AI Infrastructure Cooperation

SK Group also held a separate meeting with Amazon Web Services (AWS) at the summit to discuss expanding domestic AI infrastructure cooperation. Building on their working relationship from the joint development of the Ulsan AI data center, the two companies envision combining SK Telecom's domestic AI data center construction and operating capabilities with AWS's global cloud and AI technology to secure gigawatt-scale AI computing capacity over the long term.

Additionally, Chairman Chey Tae-won met with OpenAI CEO Sam Altman to exchange views on long-term cooperation in AI infrastructure. SK and OpenAI have been deepening their strategic partnership since signing an agreement last October covering memory supply and AI data center establishment in southwestern South Korea.

AI Infrastructure Enters the 'Mega-Investment' Era

SK Group's massive investment commitments mark a definitive shift of global AI infrastructure into a "mega-investment" phase. Analysts point out that every link in the AI industry chain — from chip manufacturing and memory supply to data center construction — requires unprecedented capital investment and increasingly depends on deep cross-border supply chain collaboration.

UBS earlier published research estimating that SK Group's Terafab project alone will spend approximately $135 billion on wafer fabrication equipment over the next five years. Now, with the $500 billion+ SK-NVIDIA LOI plus agreements with Microsoft and Anthropic, AI infrastructure is becoming one of the largest capital allocation destinations globally.

"SK will work with NVIDIA to build a world-class AI factory, leveraging SK Telecom's AI infrastructure capabilities and SK Hynix's AI memory," Chey said. "We will contribute to making South Korea a global hub for AI innovation." He emphasized that the investment scale is "a realistic plan grounded in actual demand from the global AI market."

Industry Impact: HBM4 Supply Chain Accelerates Integration

The partnership has far-reaching implications for the semiconductor supply chain. SK Hynix, as the absolute leader in the HBM market, will further cement its position through the long-term NVIDIA partnership while accelerating HBM4 mass production. HBM4, the next-generation high-bandwidth memory standard, is expected to deliver over 6.4 Gbps per pin data transfer rates — roughly 50% improvement over HBM3E.

Market observers note that with SK Hynix's 2026 HBM capacity already fully sold out, this long-term agreement secures NVIDIA's HBM supply chain for years to come, while competitors Samsung and Micron face growing pressure to catch up.

As the global AI arms race intensifies, SK Group's $750 billion collaboration represents not just a business alliance but a landmark event signaling that AI has entered the era of super-scale investment. With emerging applications from agentic AI to physical AI driving relentless demand growth for compute infrastructure, control over core chip and memory supply chains will determine strategic advantage in the AI age.
