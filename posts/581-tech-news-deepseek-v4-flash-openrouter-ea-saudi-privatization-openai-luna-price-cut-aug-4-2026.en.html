⚡ Aug 4 Tech Update: DeepSeek-V4-Flash Tops Global Call Volume, Saudi Arabia Takes EA Private in a $55B Deal, OpenAI Cuts GPT-5.6 Prices and A-Share AI App Stocks Rally

On the first Monday of August, three big stories converged across tech and capital markets: Chinese open-source model DeepSeek-V4-Flash topped the global LLM leaderboard in call volume with roughly 7.1 trillion tokens for the week (and Chinese models swept the entire top five); Electronic Arts (EA) was taken private on Aug 4 in an approximately $55 billion all-cash buyout led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund — the largest all-cash privatization transaction on record; and, on the same day, OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Luna prices by 80% and Terra by 20%, sending a wave of AI-application stocks higher in China's A-share market. Here is the roundup.

1. DeepSeek-V4-Flash Tops Global Call Volume, Chinese Models Sweep the Top Five

According to TechWeb (Aug 4) and China Business News / National Business Daily (early Aug 4), fresh data from OpenRouter, the world's largest model aggregation platform, shows that for the week of July 28 – August 3, DeepSeek-V4-Flash topped the global LLM call-volume leaderboard with roughly 7.1 to 7.22 trillion tokens, up about 13% week over week. Positions two through five were also Chinese products: Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.5 came second at about 5.4 trillion tokens, Tencent's Hy3 third at about 4.89 trillion, DeepSeek-V4-Pro fourth at about 3.1 trillion, and Zhipu's GLM-5.2 fifth at about 2.87 trillion.

The just-released official build of DeepSeek-V4-Flash (the 0731 version, which opened its API on July 31) ramped extremely fast — on Friday Aug 3 alone it logged 1.02 trillion tokens in a single day, overtaking the previously leading 0423 build. Meanwhile, per OpenCode platform data, DeepSeek-V4-Flash processed as much as 8 trillion tokens on the platform on Aug 1 alone, with roughly 5 trillion from free trial quotas and 3 trillion from paid calls.

All told, global LLM call volume last week was about 56.8 trillion tokens, of which China's models accounted for 28.13 trillion — a 14th straight week above the US (which logged about 4.38 trillion tokens). Nine of the world's ten most-used models were Chinese. This "topping the leaderboard right after launch" performance is widely read as the "price-performance kill line" now being set by Chinese AI: DeepSeek-V4-Flash charges $0.14 per million input tokens and $0.28 per million output tokens, working out to an average of roughly 3 cents per benchmark task — far below Kimi K3 ($0.86), GPT-5.6 Sol ($1.86), or even Claude Fable 5 ($3.15).

2. EA Goes Private in a ~$55B Saudi-Led Buyout, the Largest All-Cash Privatization Ever

According to Beijing Business Today (Aug 4), Clareview Business / Caixin, and ZOL, the European Commission approved, under its foreign-subsidies regulation, the roughly $55 billion all-cash privatization of gaming giant Electronic Arts (EA) by a consortium led by Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) together with Silver Lake and Affinity Partners. EA confirmed completion of the deal on Aug 4 and has delisted from public exchanges.

The buyers are led by the Saudi PIF, US-based Silver Lake, and Affinity Partners (founded in 2021 by Jared Kushner, the president's son-in-law, with large Saudi backing). EA has called it the "largest all-cash privatization acquisition in history." Funding includes cash from each party, a roughly $36 billion rollover of PIF's existing EA shares, and a $20 billion debt facility fully committed by JPMorgan (about $18 billion funded at close). Because of the heavy leverage, S&P Global Ratings said it expects to downgrade EA's credit rating to junk status after the deal closes.

Founded in 1982, EA owns such franchises as The Sims, Battlefield, Need for Speed, and FIFA/EA Sports FC. Once the transaction closes, EA will end its life as a public company and no longer disclose financial and operating data. It is the latest in a wave of legacy gaming acquisitions — Microsoft's $75.4 billion purchase of Activision Blizzard still stands as the industry record — and Gulf capital has been steadily expanding its global gaming footprint, holding stakes in Nintendo, Activision, and Take-Two, and having fully acquired Scopely and Niantic, with the aim of turning Saudi Arabia into a global gaming and esports hub under Vision 2030's diversification away from oil.

3. OpenAI Cuts GPT-5.6 Prices, and A-Share AI App Stocks Rally

According to Sina Finance / JRJ (published Aug 4, ~10:54) and IT Home (July 31), OpenAI cut the API prices of its GPT-5.6 family effective July 30: the value-oriented GPT-5.6 Luna dropped 80% (input fell from $1 to $0.20 per million tokens and output from $6 to $1.20), the balanced GPT-5.6 Terra dropped 20% (input to $2 and output to $12), while the flagship GPT-5.6 Sol held its price but gained a "Fast mode" delivering up to 2.5x the standard inference speed.

The engine room for this round of cuts is OpenAI's engineering optimization — its Sol inside Codex rewrote production GPU kernels, cutting end-to-end serving costs by about 20%, and a self-developed speculative-decoding draft model raised token-generation efficiency by more than 15% — letting the company slash prices just three weeks after launch. OpenAI's guidance: for complex tasks, use Sol for planning and design, then hand off to Luna for execution and coding — a "most expensive model thinks, cheapest model works" division of labor.

Catalyzed by this "80% price war," Chinese AI-application stocks rallied sharply on Aug 4 — Hanyi Shares hit its 20cm daily limit, while others (Jinshan Yunying, Xiechuang Data, YiTianXia, Hanxin Tech) rose more than 11%, Dana Intelligence over 10%, Huashengtiancheng and Hengyin Tech hit their limits, and BlueFocus and Guangyun Tech climbed over 5%. Analysts read the collapse in inference costs as a signal of "industrialized cost reduction in AI computing," relieving the profit squeeze on AI-software and marketing-export firms, sharply lowering the barrier to monetization for the downstream AI ecosystem and Agent workflows, and steering A-share capital from computing upstream to AI application and "AI + landed" themes.

From Chinese open-source models sweeping global call volume, to a gaming giant delisting into Gulf capital, to US frontier models joining the cost-price war and igniting application stocks — the cost economics, capital landscape, and application ecosystem of AI are resonating all in one day.
