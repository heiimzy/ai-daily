⚡ July 26 Tech重磅: Nvidia and SK Group Forge $500B+ AI Partnership, South Korea Summit Lands $950B in Chip Deals

1. Nvidia and SK Group Sign $500B+ Comprehensive Partnership

On July 24-25, at the K-AI Summit in San Francisco, Nvidia and South Korea's SK Group formally signed Letters of Intent (LOI) to establish a comprehensive partnership valued at over $500 billion to build AI infrastructure serving the surging demand for global compute. This collaboration represents one of the largest single strategic deals in global semiconductor industry history.

SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won and Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang jointly attended the signing ceremony. South Korean President Lee Jae Myung, Samsung Electronics Chairman Lee Jae-yong, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, and Broadcom CEO Hock Tan were among the political and business leaders witnessing the event.

South Korean Presidential Chief of Staff for Policy Kim Yong-beom disclosed at a press briefing that Korean conglomerates and global tech giants agreed on a series of cooperation projects with a combined value of $950 billion. Under these agreements, SK Group will supply $750 billion worth of high-performance semiconductors to global tech firms including Nvidia.

"In the AI era, competitiveness depends not just on how effectively AI is utilized, but on how much intelligence we can produce. By leveraging SK hynix's AI memory and SK Telecom's AI infrastructure capabilities, SK will collaborate with Nvidia to build a world-class AI factory." — SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won

2. SK Telecom to Build 2GW AI Data Center — World's Largest Single AI Compute Facility

As the centerpiece of the partnership, SK Telecom will use Nvidia's DSX full-stack AI factory architecture to build a 2-gigawatt AI data center in South Korea — the largest AI compute facility in the country, with the first factory scheduled to come online in 2027.

The facility will deploy Nvidia Vera Rubin accelerated computing systems powered by SK hynix HBM4 high-bandwidth memory. Vera Rubin is Nvidia's next-generation AI computing platform featuring approximately 336 billion transistors, delivering up to 10x improvement in Agentic AI inference throughput over the Blackwell architecture, and introducing HBM4 memory with 22 TB/s peak bandwidth.

The companies aim to support sovereign AI, physical AI, agentic AI, and enterprise AI services across South Korea and the broader Asia-Pacific region. Global AI compute demand is surging at an extraordinary pace — Nvidia Blackwell GPU rental prices rose 48% to $4.08/hour in about 60 days, and GPT-5.5 launched at double its predecessor's per-token pricing.

3. SK hynix-Nvidia Long-Term HBM Memory Partnership

SK hynix is entering into a long-term AI memory partnership with Nvidia as a follow-up measure to solidify their previous technical collaboration. The agreement secures a stable supply of next-generation AI memory for Nvidia and enables joint development and optimization of HBM and other next-generation AI memory solutions.

SK hynix is the global leader in high-bandwidth memory (HBM) production. Together with Samsung Electronics, the two companies control approximately 80% of the global HBM chip market. HBM is the critical hardware component powering AI data centers and next-generation GPUs, with global capacity fully booked through the year and supply tightness continuing.

CNBC reported that Nvidia is aggressively securing HBM supply to address the global memory shortage. SK hynix listed on Nasdaq earlier this month — the largest semiconductor IPO in Korean history at $29 billion — to finance infrastructure expansion.

4. Korea AI Ecosystem: Samsung, Naver, Anthropic Join the Buildout

Multiple major deals were announced simultaneously during the K-AI Summit:

Samsung-Broadcom: Samsung Electronics signed a memorandum of understanding with Broadcom covering up to $200 billion in memory chips, foundry services, and advanced packaging through 2030. Samsung will develop Broadcom's next-generation AI accelerators based on its HBM technology and provide sub-2nm foundry processes.

Nvidia Invests $1B in Naver: Nvidia announced a $1 billion investment in South Korean internet giant Naver to expand its AI data center. U.S. private equity firm Brookfield has signed a nonbinding term sheet to provide up to $9 billion for the project, scaling capacity from 55 MW to over 200 MW. Naver's roadmap targets gigawatt-scale capacity by 2029.

Anthropic Partners with Samsung and SK hynix: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei confirmed the company has signed supply agreements with Samsung Electronics and SK hynix for its custom AI chip initiative. Anthropic joins Google, Amazon, and Meta in the "chip independence" movement to reduce reliance on external suppliers.

Nvidia-KAIST Joint Lab: Nvidia and the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) announced a joint AI research lab focused on agentic AI and development of a Korean large language model ("Korea AI").

5. Global AI Compute Arms Race Accelerates: Supply Tight Through 2028

These agreements come as the global AI compute arms race enters an intense new phase. Nomura confirmed in its latest report that the AI hardware cycle peak has been pushed to 2028, with global server revenue projected to grow 65% in 2027. Morgan Stanley estimates the five largest tech companies' AI spending will reach $1.1 trillion by 2027, representing 3.2% of U.S. GDP.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said at the summit: "South Korea has all the ingredients to become a global AI powerhouse — world-class networks and data centers, leadership in chip technology and vast industrial scale. Together with SK Telecom and SK hynix, we are building a new generation of AI factories that will power Korea's next wave of growth."

President Lee Jae Myung unveiled the "San Francisco AI Declaration," pledging to make South Korea an irreplaceable core nation in the AI supply chain. He emphasized that Korea aims to become not just a leading AI adopter but a global hub driving AI innovation.

From a broader perspective, the Nvidia-SK Group $500B+ partnership represents a landmark event in the global race for AI compute "supply chain sovereignty." In an era where compute power is national power, countries and corporations are investing in AI infrastructure at unprecedented scale, and South Korea, with its semiconductor manufacturing advantages, is positioning itself at the center of this transformation.
