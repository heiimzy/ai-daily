⚡ Aug 9 International Politics: IRGC lays out conditions to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, Hungary nominates former Supreme Court chief Baka for president, and West Bank settler attacks escalate

Over the past hour or two, several geopolitical and international-politics storylines have intensified from the Middle East to Central Europe to the eastern Mediterranean: Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) has publicly set conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz — including an end to the US naval blockade and compensation for war damages; meanwhile the US VP has claimed the US has 'destroyed' Iran's nuclear program, and the war continues to reshape Pentagon procurement toward lower-cost weapons; Hungarian Prime Minister Peter Magyar has nominated former Supreme Court President and ECHR judge Andras Baka for the presidency, with the Tisza party reportedly lining up votes to replace the Orban-era president; and settler attacks against Palestinian residents in the West Bank have kept rising over recent weeks. Below is a detailed readout with every source's publication time individually verified.

1. Iran: IRGC sets conditions to reopen the Strait of Hormuz

According to a rolling live blog from Al Jazeera (published around the early hours of Aug 9, 00:00 GMT), Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has set clear conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, including ending the US naval blockade and providing compensation for war damages. In parallel, the US Vice President's latest remarks claimed the US has 'destroyed' Iran's nuclear program, adding fresh uncertainty to the prospects for negotiations between Tehran and Washington.

Analysts note that the Strait of Hormuz has long been viewed as the 'choke point' of global energy shipping, and Iran's latest posture signals to Washington that any talks on reopening the waterway must proceed on the premise of lifting the blockade and recognizing compensation for war losses. The news has drawn acute attention across global energy markets and military movements in the region. (Source: Al Jazeera Iran War liveblog)

2. The Iran war changes the Pentagon: US defense shifts toward lower-cost weapons

Alongside the battlefield, the 'war on Iran' is accelerating structural change at the Pentagon. Multiple media outlets cite internal discussions noting that as the high-intensity war of attrition continues, defense and arms establishments increasingly recognize that relying primarily on a small number of expensive precision munitions is not optimal, and instead favor scalable, low-cost, rapidly resupplied weapon systems. This dovetails with signals that have emerged over recent months about releasing older inventory and rebuilding munitions production lines.

Analysts expect that in the short term, this shift will benefit mid- and low-end defense stocks with large-scale production capacity, while also redistributing the US fiscal and weapons-supply chains. It is another visible indicator of a 'war economy' worth tracking. (Source: Al Jazeera)

3. Hungary: Baska nominated for president as the Orban era hits an inflection point

In Central Europe, Hungarian Prime Minister Peter Magyar has formally nominated former Supreme Court President and European Court of Human Rights judge Andras Baska for the presidency. According to Hungarian media and reports from Al Jazeera, the Tisza party has selected Baska through an internal secret ballot and expects, combined with its constitutional majority, to win further political ground in the procedures to remove and replace Orban-era institutions.

In recent weeks Tisza has already removed Orban-era President Tamás Sulyok and set up impeachment procedures through constitutional amendments, seen as a key 'Orban-exit' step in ending the political framework Orban has dominated since 2010. Baska is known as a jurist who served as a president of the Supreme Court and held a firm stance on judicial and European legal matters. If confirmed, it would mark a significant symbol of Hungary's political-center recalibration. (Source: Al Jazeera English report)

4. Western Mediterranean: West Bank settler attacks rise

In the Middle East, settler violence in the West Bank continues to rise, with local Palestinian residents reporting that land is being steadily eaten away. According to reports from Al Jazeera correspondents reporting from the West Bank, as right-wing election dynamics heat up, new settler attacks and displacement incidents keep piling up. One resident told the camera: "We are not just facing a few radicals — we are facing the whole system closing in, step by step."

"We are not just losing a plot of land — we are losing the roots of the entire village." — a West Bank Palestinian resident to Al Jazeera

Analysis suggests that settler expansion and settler violence, against a backdrop of stalled Middle East diplomacy, are injecting new tension into the Israeli-Palestinian conflict dynamics, and future weeks should be monitored to see whether election dynamics push tensions higher in the region.

5. Outlook

Over the next 24–48 hours, several lines need scrutiny: first, the Hormuz negotiation outlook — the tension between Iran's conditions and the American reaction will directly affect global energy and shipping; second, the follow-through of Hungary's presidential nomination — whether Baska can pass the parliamentary vote as scheduled, and how the Orban camp responds; third, whether West Bank settler violence escalates as election approaches. On the whole, geopolitical frictions in the Middle East, the political recalibration in Central Europe, and eastern-Mediterranean conflict sketch the new landscape of international politics in mid-August.
