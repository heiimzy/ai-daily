⚡ July 26 International Roundup: Inside Trump's Iran Strike Pause — Patriot Interceptor Shortage Exposed, Iran-Oman Hormuz Talks Progress, Houthi Red Sea Offensive Opens Second Front

1. Inside Trump's Iran Strike Pause: Patriot Interceptor Shortage Takes Center Stage

After 13 consecutive nights of airstrikes on Iran, President Donald Trump abruptly ordered a halt on Friday (July 24), breaking the rhythm of daily attacks that had defined the past two weeks. The New York Times revealed the inside story behind this policy reversal — with dwindling Patriot interceptor stockpiles emerging as the primary concern.

According to two people briefed on the discussions, Trump's shift came after a Friday meeting with top advisers and Cabinet members. The private deliberations focused on the Pentagon's shrinking inventory of Patriot interceptors and other air defenses. A senior US official said three US soldiers were killed in Jordan on July 17 when a ballistic missile slipped through US air defenses during a barrage of Iranian missiles and drones — an event that exposed the enormous pressure on US air defense systems.

General Dan Caine, Chair of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, privately warned the president that resuming major combat operations was doable but would "dangerously deplete" interceptors available to Central Command. The Pentagon has used more than 1,200 Patriot interceptor missiles in the conflict, at over $4 million each, leaving inventories at worryingly low levels — and the situation has worsened since, military officials said.

A senior US official expressed doubts that resuming major combat would drive Iran back to the bargaining table, noting the strikes have actually had the opposite effect — keeping Iran cohesive and allowing leaders to focus attention on an external threat. Few if any in Trump's inner circle believed escalation was wise.

White House Communications Director Steven Cheung said the president has "always been consistent in saying he prefers a diplomatic solution" but retains all options. Trump himself told reporters: "Look, we're locked and loaded. We're ready to go, but we're talking to them, so maybe there will be, maybe there won't be a tipping point."

2. Iran-Oman Talks on Strait of Hormuz Management Make Progress

Just hours before Trump ordered the strike pause, an Omani diplomatic delegation arrived in Tehran to negotiate new arrangements for reopening the Strait of Hormuz. According to regional sources cited by Axios, the talks have made progress, and Oman and Iran could reach an agreement this weekend.

Iran's Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Esmaeil Baghaei confirmed Sunday (July 26) that Iranian and Omani deputy foreign ministers held multiple rounds of technical talks in Tehran on management of the Strait of Hormuz. Baghaei said both sides exchanged views on "common principles and operational mechanisms for managing safe shipping traffic while respecting the sovereign rights of the two coastal states," describing the talks as "useful" with "progress made." Technical and political consultations continue, though no change in the strait's traffic situation has occurred.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran should not be blamed for the escalation and offered to mediate talks between the Houthis and Riyadh, stating "there is no military solution."

If Iran and Oman reach consensus on reopening the strait, Trump will face the difficult decision of whether to accept the terms. Regional officials said a potential compromise centers on allowing Iran to continue managing vessel transit with fewer restrictions on commercial shipping.

3. Houthi-Saudi Clashes Erupt Along Red Sea: Jizan Refinery Hit

Despite the temporary US-Iran military pause, the Red Sea front is intensifying. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced Saturday (July 25) they struck Saudi Aramco facilities in Jizan and Yanbu with ballistic missiles and drones, in response to Saudi-led coalition airstrikes on Houthi-held Hodeidah.

Brigadier General Yahya Saree, the Houthis' military spokesperson, said in a prerecorded statement that attacks targeted Aramco facilities in the Red Sea cities of Yanbu and Jizan. Video verified by Reuters showed a large column of smoke rising from the direction of the Aramco refinery in Jizan, which can process up to 400,000 barrels of crude oil per day. Two Asia-based trading sources said they were informed of potential damage to fuel and oil storage sites at Jizan.

The Saudi-led coalition responded by striking Houthi military targets in Hodeidah, Marib, and al-Jawf provinces — the first major direct military action against Houthi targets since the 2022 ceasefire, signaling that the Yemen truce has effectively collapsed.

A surface-to-air battery operated by Greek personnel in Saudi Arabia intercepted two ballistic missiles and a drone over Yanbu, the Greek air force media office confirmed. The Houthis have declared a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia and claimed to have closed the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, which has become even more vital for Saudi oil exports since the disruption of Hormuz.

Analysts warn the conflict is widening as more actors join: the Houthis and Saudi Arabia are now directly engaged, while Israel has signaled readiness to enter the fray.

4. US Studies Plan to Seize Iran's Enriched Uranium With Thousands of Troops

The New York Post reported Sunday, citing informed sources, that the US government is studying a plan to seize enriched uranium stockpiles from Iranian nuclear facilities, potentially deploying thousands of troops for the operation.

According to the report, this "extremely complex" mission would require thousands of Army soldiers to assault nuclear facilities, clear minefields, and deploy engineering teams to secure defensive perimeters. A small special forces team would then execute the core uranium extraction — a process described as "extremely dangerous." US forces would need to clear facility entrances, repel Iranian resistance, and maintain full control of the air corridor for safe extraction.

Iranian military sources told Sputnik that Tehran's forces have rehearsed multiple US attack scenarios and are fully prepared for a ground invasion.

5. Iran Warns War Will Expand If US Restrikes

Iran's army warned Sunday (July 26) that the war in West Asia would widen if the United States chooses to press ahead with attacks on the Islamic republic, ahead of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's planned visit to Washington. Army spokesman Brigadier General Mohammad Akraminia told state television: "I believe that if the Americans once again fall for the Zionists' deception, or move in line with them, and insist on continuing the war, particularly through air strikes, geographically this will expand further."

IRGC spokesman Muhbi claimed Iran destroyed 11 fighter jets and helicopters parked at US bases, 17 military drones, an F-15 fighter, a P-8 maritime patrol aircraft, and other aircraft within 15 days — claims yet to be confirmed by the US military. Satellite images released by Iran showed what it said was the "complete destruction" of an ammunition depot at Jordan's King Faisal Air Base.

Muhbi also warned Britain that if it continues supporting US operations — including allowing B-1 Lancer strategic bombers to operate from RAF Fairford — it would become a "clear legitimate target" for Iran.

6. Zelenskyy Accuses Russia of Sharing Satellite Intel With Iran

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy launched a scathing indictment against Russia in his Saturday evening address, accusing Moscow of providing satellite intelligence to Iran to facilitate strikes on US military outposts and Gulf state facilities.

Zelenskyy revealed that Ukrainian intelligence has tracked active Russian satellite surveillance over US military bases and Gulf state infrastructure since early July, with the imagery subsequently appearing in Iranian hands. On July 19 and 20 alone, Russian satellites focused on four regional air bases — two in Bahrain, one in Jordan, and one in Kuwait — showing a "clear correlation" between Russian satellite imagery and Iranian strikes.

Simultaneously, Zelenskyy warned that Russia is preparing to receive an additional 30,000 North Korean troops, with preparations underway in Russia's Voronezh region since June. North Korea is also preparing to transfer more ballistic missile launchers to Russia.

In the Caspian Sea, Iran summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires on Sunday to protest a Ukrainian drone attack on an Iranian commercial vessel early July 25 that killed one sailor and wounded another. Iran's Foreign Ministry condemned the "aggressive act," warning it could further expand the geographic scope of the Russia-Ukraine war.

7. US Naval Blockade Remains in Full Effect

Despite the airstrike pause, US Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed Sunday that the naval blockade against Iran remains "in full effect." As of July 25, CENTCOM has redirected 12 commercial vessels trying to run the blockade, disabled 2 that didn't comply, and boarded 2 to ensure compliance.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired warning shots at six ships over the past 24 hours attempting to pass through the Strait of Hormuz, stopping them on grounds they were traveling "outside the designated route." Four ships had been halted on Saturday for "unauthorized passage."

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is scheduled to meet Trump in Washington on July 28, where he is expected to present updated intelligence on Iran's military and nuclear program. Israeli sources described the odds of a US-Iran deal as "minuscule" and escalation as "inevitable."
