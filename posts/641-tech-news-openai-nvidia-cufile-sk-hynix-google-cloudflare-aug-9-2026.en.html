⚡ Aug 9 Tech Roundup: OpenAI Opens Upside Text Chat to Free Users, Nvidia Open-Sources cuFile, SK hynix Eyes China Plant Stake Sale, Google Charts Its AI Momentum, Cloudflare Lifts Guidance

Midday update, Aug 9, 2026

Tech leaders moved in quick succession around midday on Aug 9: OpenAI said free ChatGPT users can now enjoy unlimited text chat; Nvidia open-sourced its cuFile GPU storage acceleration library; SK hynix is reportedly weighing a sale of a stake in its Chinese packaging subsidiary; Google published a new piece making the case for the next chapter of its AI momentum; and Cloudflare raised its 2026 full-year guidance while expanding its AI agent platform. Here is a time-verified roundup of the latest headlines.

OpenAI: Unlimited Text Chat for Free Users

According to AzerNews and other outlets reporting around noon Beijing time on Aug 9, OpenAI has moved to unlock unlimited text-based chat for free ChatGPT users. Free-tier accounts previously faced capped message counts or rate limits; with this change, most everyday text conversations no longer hit hard quota walls, letting users brainstorm, draft, and draft and search more freely.

Industry observers say the move is designed both to widen the free funnel and to better separate premium capabilities such as longer context windows and heavier multimodal reasoning. It continues OpenAI's playbook of pushing lightweight powers downstream while reserving heavier compute in subscriptions, and it helps counter free pressure from open-source and rival models.

Nvidia Open-Sources the cuFile Storage Library

On the GPU-compute side, Nvidia has open-sourced cuFile, its storage acceleration library for AI and GPU workloads. cuFile is engineered to let GPUs access file-system data in a zero-copy, near-direct manner, slashing the I/O overhead of moving datasets from host memory into VRAM — a big win for large-scale training and inference.

By open-sourcing it, Nvidia wants to standardize its GPU-AI data ecosystem: developers can integrate cuFile more freely into their own storage stacks, and RDMA, object storage and data lakes can be wired together more flexibly. For data centers and cloud providers, the move promises a more efficient, more customizable "storage-to-GPU" data path — another step in deepening its software ecosystem beyond the hardware itself.

SK hynix Weighs a Stake Sale in a Chinese Packaging Plant

The Korea JoongAng Daily reported that SK hynix is evaluating the sale of a stake in its packaging plant in Wuxi, China. That facility is part of SK hynix's in-country vertical chain and handles part of the packaging and testing for memory chips. The report says the stake size, potential buyers and final timing remain undecided, and the company has not issued an official response.

Analysts tie the possible move to US export controls, supply-chain hedging, and geopolitical risk. Memory players have generally been expanding advanced packaging capacity elsewhere while keeping mature production running in China. If the deal goes through, it will visibly reshape SK hynix's asset footprint in mainland China and sharpen the industry's focus on an "advanced capacity shifting out / mature steps staying" restructuring logic.

Google Charts "The Next Chapter of Our AI Momentum"

Google's official blog framed its recent work on the Gemini AI stack, cloud agents, and AI-infused Search and Workspace, plus a fresh capital-expenditure commitment, under the headline "The Next Chapter of Our AI Momentum." Commentary reads it as a "stage-setting roadmap" for investors and developers: it reaffirms the heavy bet on AI R&D while emphasizing how Google Cloud can turn Gemini into deployable, revenue-bearing agent experiences.

Read alongside Cloudflare's AI-driven outlook hike above, Google's message confirms the same takeaway: the 2026 AI race is shifting from "how big are the parameters" to "who can turn models into large-scale, profitable products and services." Smarters of data centers and cloud platforms are becoming a shared investment theme for both companies.

Cloudflare Raises 2026 Guidance, Expands Its AI Agent Platform

In the US session, Cloudflare (NET) carved out ~7.6% as it raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance and expanded its AI agent platform. Management credits the uplift to sustained AI-driven traffic and agent workloads, as boundaries around security, development, and infrastructure are being redefined.

Developer-side, AI agents need unified management of cross-site cookies, identity, security, and observability, and Cloudflare is positioning itself as the "edge control plane for AI agents." The extra ramp is a bet on "AI agent + edge network" as a new line of paid services, moving roughly in step with OpenAI, Google, and other players pushing agents into production.

Bottom line: the midday tech headlines — OpenAI free-tier widening, Nvidia's open-source storage boost, SK hynix rethinking its China footprint, and Google and Cloudflare each betting on "AI agent + cloud" — all point the same way: AI competition is moving beyond an arms race over parameters, toward productize and infrastructure instead.
