⚡ July 27 Afternoon Tech Roundup: Google Confirms Gemini 4 in Training — 'Most Ambitious' Pretraining Run Yet; Putin Signs Russian AI Law Introducing 'Sovereign AI Model'; Jim Fan & Fei-Fei Li's RoboTTT Breaks Robot Context Scaling

1. Google Confirms Gemini 4 in Training: Pichai's 'Most Ambitious' Pretraining Run

On July 27, Alphabet's Q2 2026 earnings call delivered a major signal — CEO Sundar Pichai officially confirmed that the next-generation flagship model Gemini 4 has entered pretraining, calling it the company's "most ambitious pre-training run yet."

When asked by analysts about Google's confidence in remaining at the frontier, Pichai stated: "For the next generation of frontier, you're going to need much larger base models. We are now training Gemini 4, and we're being very ambitious with it. I am very excited by the progress I'm seeing internally on Gemini 4, and I'm confident that people will be pleased when we are putting it outside." He acknowledged that Google needs to improve in coding and agentic coding, areas where the company has fallen behind competitors.

Pichai described Gemini 4 as a "larger base model" designed to bring Google back to the frontier. The announcement comes after Gemini 3.5 Pro was delayed due to coding performance issues, raising questions about Google's model iteration cadence. Pichai indicated Google would accelerate release frequency, targeting near-monthly model releases going forward.

Timeline: Multiple media outlets predict Gemini 4 could launch between November and December 2026. In the meantime, Google will continue fielding models like Gemini 3.6 Flash to maintain market competitiveness.

"We want to compete at the frontier level of where the frontier will be when Gemini 4 comes out, and so we are applying a lot of our compute and effort in that direction." — Sundar Pichai, Alphabet Q2 2026 Earnings Call

The earnings results showed Google Cloud revenue surging 82% year-over-year to $24.75 billion, driven by strong AI infrastructure demand. Despite model iteration concerns, Google's AI commercialization remains robust.

2. Putin Signs Russian AI Development Law: 'Sovereign AI Model' Becomes Legal Concept

Russian President Vladimir Putin on July 27 signed comprehensive artificial intelligence development legislation, establishing a complete legal framework for the development, deployment, and application of Russian foundational AI models. The law introduces two new concepts — "Sovereign AI Model" and "National AI Model" — and permits developers to access state data for large model training.

According to Sputnik News, Putin signed two complementary laws on the same day:

  • AI Content Labeling Law: Requires users to label audio and video content generated by foundational AI models, with large internet platforms required to provide technical support.
  • Copyright Exception Law: Permits use of copyrighted works for training Russian sovereign and national AI models without infringement, effective March 1, 2027.

This legislation marks Russia's formal entry into the global AI governance race. Through the "Sovereign AI" concept, Russia aims to build an independent, controllable AI technology ecosystem, reducing dependence on foreign AI technology. The move contrasts with US technology export controls on Chinese AI companies and the EU AI Act, as nations increasingly pursue legal frameworks to shape AI governance on their own terms.

3. Jim Fan & Fei-Fei Li's Team Unveils RoboTTT: Robot Context Scaling Breakthrough

NVIDIA Senior Research Scientist Jim Fan and Stanford Professor Fei-Fei Li's team, in collaboration with partners, released RoboTTT (Test-Time-Training Robot Policies) on July 27 — a groundbreaking approach extending robot visual-motor context to 8K time steps, equivalent to 5 minutes of "muscle memory," three orders of magnitude beyond current state-of-the-art.

Key breakthroughs:

  • Long context capability: RoboTTT can ingest entire videos as context and perform one-shot imitation learning from human demonstrations. Models trained with 8K time-step context achieved 63% performance improvement over 1K time-step versions.
  • Real-time self-improvement: The model can incorporate corrective feedback into its context and continuously adjust actions during execution, excelling in multi-stage long-horizon tasks.
  • No inference latency increase: By embedding test-time training (TTT) into the robot foundation model, historical information is compressed into a fixed-size hidden state, preserving long-range information without additional inference overhead.

In evaluations across three long-horizon dual-arm assembly tasks, RoboTTT achieved an average task completion score of 79%, significantly outperforming the single-step baseline GR00T N1.7. On the 5-minute, 10-stage "Gear Bot" task, RoboTTT was the only method to complete all stages successfully.

Jim Fan posted on X: "Soon, even 1 million context won't be a fantasy." The research team believes context length will become a new scaling direction for robot foundation models, providing a critical technical pathway for robots to move from laboratories to real-world complex environments.

Model scale continues marching upward from trillion parameters, with compute demand rising correspondingly.

Long-context capability will enable robots to handle increasingly complex continuous tasks, paving the way for large-scale industrial automation and service robot deployment.
