Zoox Secures First US Permit to Charge Fares for Wheel-Free Robotaxis, Beating Tesla

On July 30, the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) approved Amazon-owned Zoox for limited commercial deployment of its wheel-free robotaxi — the first time the US has allowed a vehicle with no human controls to charge passengers for rides. NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison confirmed Zoox may deploy up to 2,500 driverless taxis per year over the next two years. The company said it will soon begin fare-paying service in Las Vegas, then expand to other markets as state approvals come through; it is currently testing with passengers in parts of Las Vegas and San Francisco.

The core of the approval is an NHTSA exemption from federal rules requiring vehicles to be equipped with manual controls. Since safety regulations have historically assumed a human driver, Zoox becomes the first "purpose-built autonomous" company to receive a federal license for commercial fares. Its electric taxi uses a symmetric "carriage-style" design with two rows of facing seats and no steering wheel, pedals or mirrors, with a top speed of 75 mph (about 120 km/h). By comparison, Tesla has started producing its Cybercab without a steering wheel or pedals at its Texas Gigafactory this April, but has not yet announced regulatory approval; Alphabet's Waymo operates paid service by retrofitting autonomous systems onto existing vehicles.

NHTSA said it determined Zoox vehicles are comparable in safety to vehicles that meet federal motor vehicle safety standards; Morrison added that Zoox's system performance "exceeds the equivalent requirements" of compliant vehicles. As conditions of the exemption, Zoox must report collisions and improper road stops, keep all remote operators based in the US, and publish maps of its operating areas. "If we find significant safety problems, we have the authority to revoke the exemption."

China's First Fully Domestic High-End Photoresist Line Goes Into Production

According to disclosures by Dinglong (鼎龙股份) during recent institutional research, its Qianjiang Phase II KrF/ArF high-end wafer photoresist mass-production line has officially started operations with an annual capacity of 300 tonnes — bringing total capacity to 330 tonnes when combined with Phase I. The line produces core raw materials such as resins in-house with fully self-controlled formulations and processes, making it China's first fully domestic high-end photoresist production line and a major milestone for localization of critical chip materials.

Dinglong has developed more than 40 high-end wafer photoresists, with nearly 30 currently under joint validation at customers; eight ArF and KrF products have already won volume purchase orders from multiple major fabs, and several new products are expected to convert to orders within the year. The company is also building out BARC (bottom anti-reflective coating) and SOC (spin-on carbon) supporting materials for a complete self-owned supply chain. Its CMP polishing pads have reached volume production, and several specialized slurries for copper barrier layers, large wafers, silicon carbide substrates and TSV processes have passed full-flow validation at leading fabs and secured volume orders. The Xiantao slurry park is progressing steadily, and construction of the Qianjiang Phase III pad line has begun.

TCL CSOT: World's Only G8.6 Printed OLED Line Set to Start Production in Q4 2027

At 2026 ChinaJoy, TCL CSOT CEO Zhao Jun publicly confirmed for the first time the timeline for the company's Guangzhou t8 project: the world's only G8.6-generation printed OLED mass-production line — a total investment of RMB 29.5 billion — will officially start production in Q4 2027, with a monthly capacity of 22,500 large glass substrates (2290mm × 2620mm). The line topped out ahead of schedule in May this year, with yield and cost metrics already meeting customer mass-production standards.

Before the large line comes online, small-batch validation is accelerating. TCL CSOT has teamed up with several leading PC brands to validate printed OLED panels for laptops and monitors, and finished devices with the panels could ship as early as this year. Technically, printed OLED uses an inkjet direct-printing process that lifts material utilization above 90%, uses only one-tenth the vacuum equipment of evaporation-based routes, and can cut overall costs by 15%–35% at scale. It also enables standard RGB stripe pixel layouts, eliminating the text-blur issue of conventional OLEDs in office use.

Samsung and LG currently dominate IT OLED supply with Gen-6 evaporation lines, leaving Chinese panel makers mostly with niche orders. Once the G8.6 printed line ramps, TCL will build self-controlled, low-cost mid-size OLED capacity that directly challenges Korean dominance in laptops and high-end monitors.

Thunderbird iO AI Glasses Debut at ChinaJoy: Camera-Free, Lightweight, Focused on AI Subtitles and All-Day Recall

On July 31, the 2026 ChinaJoy expo opened in Shanghai, and Thunderbird Innovation's Thunderbird iO AI glasses made their offline debut. On-site photos show the iO uses a design close to ordinary eyeglasses and drops the camera module entirely, making it lighter and thinner for all-day wear in commuting and office settings.

The iO uses a monochrome green display that can surface basic information in the wearer's field of view — date, time, battery, temperature and all-day to-dos — with interaction focused on lightweight notifications. Its menu includes recording, AI subtitles, all-day to-dos, a teleprompter and an "All-Day Recall" (全天智记) feature covering meetings, speech prompts, reminders and schedule management. The recall feature continuously records the user's work and life activity, then uses AI to recognize, summarize and organize it into to-dos, memos or itineraries.

Unlike smart glasses that emphasize camera capture, the iO shifts focus to information display, voice interaction and AI-assisted note-taking — reducing privacy concerns around cameras while expanding the role of AI glasses in office and daily life.

Intel Discloses Five Panther Lake Industrial Processors: 25W TDP, -40°C to 100°C Range

Intel has disclosed five new Core Ultra 300-series processors in its official technical reference guide. The Panther Lake parts are not aimed at laptops but are special models designed for edge computing and industrial environments, with HR, RE and HRE suffixes — where H stands for high-performance mobile, E for embedded, and R for industrial-grade wide-temperature, meaning the line has passed industrial-grade reliability certification.

The models include Core Ultra X7 358HR, Core Ultra 7 366HRE, Core Ultra 7 365RE, Core Ultra 5 336HRE and Core Ultra 5 335RE. All are rated at a 25W base power and support Intel vPro management technology. The flagship Core Ultra X7 358HR packs 16 cores with 12 Xe-architecture graphics cores — matching the consumer Arc B390 iGPU — giving it solid on-device AI and rendering capability; the other four models range from 8 to 16 CPU cores with a uniform 4 Xe-core iGPU.

Intel confirms some models can operate continuously across a -40°C to 100°C temperature range and come with up to 10 years of supply guarantee, targeting robotics, automated factories, medical equipment and smart city deployments that demand maximum stability.
