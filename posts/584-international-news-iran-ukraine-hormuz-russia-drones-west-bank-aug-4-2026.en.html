⚡ August 4 International Update: Iran Reveals It Scrapped Strikes on Ukraine, Russia Launches 136 Drones Overnight, Palestinian FM Slams Settler Violence, Iraq Hopeful on Hormuz Understanding

International affairs in the early hours of Tuesday (August 4) revolved around three tracks: an unusual "strike-then-cancel" diplomatic episode between Iran and Ukraine; another mass overnight drone assault by Russia on Ukraine; and delicate diplomatic progress in the Middle East along both the Gaza and Hormuz tracks. Here is the latest international roundup.

1. Iran says it was ready to strike three sites in Ukraine, then cancelled after Kyiv's 'apology'; warns US warships off non-designated routes

According to Al Jazeera (updated 06:00 GMT, August 4), Mohsen Rezaei, a military adviser to Iran's Supreme Leader and former commander of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), told state television that Iran's armed forces had completed preparations to strike three sites in Ukraine before the operation was called off after Kyiv offered an "apology" over the July 25 attack on an Iranian ship.

Rezaei said Iran's forces had finished all preparations before the operation was scrapped. Iranian authorities say the July 25 strike in the Caspian Sea killed one crew member; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi confirmed last week that he had been assured by his Ukrainian counterpart, Andrii Sybiha, that the attack was unintentional and that Kyiv did not seek escalation. Rezaei also reiterated a hardline warning: Iran will not allow any vessel to use a route through the Strait of Hormuz other than the one Tehran has designated, and any hostile naval vessel attempting to use an "illegal route" would be hit.

2. Russia fires Iskander-M ballistic missile and 136 drones at Ukraine overnight; Kyiv intercepts 117

According to Ukrainska Pravda (published 08:12 Kyiv time, i.e. 05:12 GMT, August 4), citing Ukraine's Air Force, Russia launched one Iskander-M ballistic missile from Rostov Oblast and 136 attack drones — including Shahed (some jet-powered), Gerbera and Italmas models plus Parodiya decoy UAVs — from Oryol, Millerovo, and temporarily occupied parts of Donetsk Oblast and Crimea overnight on August 3-4.

The Air Force said that as of 08:00 local time, air defences had destroyed or jammed 117 drones; one ballistic missile and 14 UAVs recorded hits at 14 locations, with debris from downed aerial vehicles falling at three sites. It added that the attack was still ongoing, with Russian drones remaining in Ukrainian airspace, and urged residents to observe safety measures. The overnight barrage is the latest in a series of large-scale Russian drone attacks on Ukrainian cities, keeping pressure on power and civilian infrastructure across multiple regions.

3. Palestinian FM: surge in West Bank settler attacks has direct support from Israel's government and military

According to Al Jazeera (05:30 GMT, August 4), Palestinian Foreign Minister Varsen Aghabekian Shahin told Al Jazeera that the sharp rise in Israeli settler attacks across the occupied West Bank is being carried out with direct support and coordination from the Israeli government and military. "Settlers are more organized. They work in a more coordinated manner. And all of this is under the guidance of this Israeli government. And they are shielded and protected by the Israeli occupation forces," she said.

Shahin acknowledged criticism of the Palestinian Authority's (PA) inability to defend its citizens: "What we can do is what we can do." She said the PA is in contact with countries through statements, letters and appeals, and has received responses from some — "we would like to see more measures." Palestinian officials say at least 20 Palestinians have been killed so far this year as a result of settler violence. UN data previously showed settler attacks now account for 55 percent of Palestinian injuries in the West Bank in 2026, with the frequency rising from one incident every three days in 2020 to more than three per day this year.

4. Iraq hopeful of a 'significant understanding' with Iran on Hormuz as transits stay low

According to Al Jazeera (05:15 GMT, August 4) citing Iraq's state news agency, Iraqi Oil Minister Bassem Mohammed Khudair said talks are under way with Iran on "the smooth flow of oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz", expressing hope "of reaching significant understandings in the coming days that will contribute to the protection and ease of passage of shipments and tankers."

Meanwhile, Reuters, citing vessel-tracking firm Kpler, reported that only six vessels — three tankers and three bulk carriers — transited the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, down from seven the previous day; before the war the strait saw about 130 transits a day. At the Bab al-Mandeb, 20 ships passed on Monday, flat versus Sunday but still far below the roughly 41 per day averaged before the Houthis declared a maritime blockade of Saudi Arabia on July 20. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) has warned that nearly 6,000 seafarers remained stranded in the Gulf unable to safely leave as of July, as the standoff over Hormuz keeps global energy supply chains on edge.

From the Iran-Ukraine "strike episode" to the overnight drone war in Ukraine, and from West Bank settler violence to the Hormuz shipping standoff — the world on August 4 remains caught between talking and fighting. The next few hours of US-Iran diplomacy and the battlefield dynamics in Ukraine are well worth watching.
