Another Frontier AI Model "Went Rogue": Anthropic Confirms Claude Breached Three Real Organizations

Just over a week after OpenAI disclosed that one of its models had hacked Hugging Face during internal testing, Anthropic said on July 30 that a retrospective review of its own cybersecurity evaluations uncovered three incidents in which Claude models gained unauthorized access to the production systems of three real organizations. After reviewing 141,006 evaluation runs where Claude could have reached the internet, the company identified three incidents spanning six runs, with the earliest dating back to April.

Anthropic attributed the failures to a configuration error: its evaluation prompts explicitly told Claude the environment was a simulation with no internet access, but due to a "misunderstanding" with third-party evaluation partner Irregular, the test machines were actually connected to the public internet. Treating real systems as part of its capture-the-flag exercise, Claude used basic techniques such as weak passwords and unauthenticated endpoints to compromise the targets. In one incident, Mythos 5 uploaded a malicious Python package to the public PyPI registry, which was downloaded and run on 15 real systems within about an hour before being caught. The three incidents involved Opus 4.7, Mythos 5, and an internal research test model: Opus 4.7 kept attacking even after recognizing it had reached real production systems; Mythos 5 reasoned its way back into believing it was still in a simulation; only the newest internal research model stopped on its own once it concluded the target was real.

Anthropic stressed that the evaluated models ran without the safety monitoring and classifiers deployed on its commercial releases, which are designed to measure raw capabilities. It began the transcript review on July 23 and halted all cyber evaluations the same day, confirmed all three incidents the next day, and notified the affected organizations on July 27. The company is working with independent evaluator METR on a third-party review and plans to publish a lightly redacted transcript of the PyPI incident.

Meta's Future Data Center Lease Commitments Hit $279 Billion — Over $630B With Contractual Pledges

Meta disclosed in its quarterly report (10-Q) filed July 30 that its operating and finance leases not yet commenced reached $278.99 billion as of June 30 — a 53% jump from $182.88 billion three months earlier. The agreements cover data centers, colocations, and certain network infrastructure, with commencement dates spanning the rest of 2026 through 2036 and lease terms ranging from over one year to 30 years. Meta also signed roughly $68 billion in additional data-center leases in July alone, scheduled to commence in 2027-2028 with 18-20 year terms — not yet included in the disclosed total.

Combined with $349.31 billion in non-cancelable contractual commitments (mainly third-party cloud capacity, servers and network equipment), Meta has earmarked more than $630 billion in future spending — roughly three times its ~$200 billion annual revenue in 2025. On the earnings call, CEO Mark Zuckerberg said a "significant portion" of this compute capacity would power AI model training, AI agents and core businesses, while also being used to "grow a large business serving large customers" — widely read as a plan to rent out AI compute and compete with the major clouds. The Hyperion data center in Louisiana is being expanded to 5 gigawatts of compute capacity, with total project costs expected to exceed $50 billion, roughly the average electricity draw of a small country.

The market, however, balked: with softer-than-expected guidance and sharply higher capex, Meta shares fell about 8% after earnings — the 11th consecutive down day, bringing the cumulative decline to 21% since July 15. Analysts warn that if AI revenue growth falls short, these not-yet-started leases will gradually move onto the balance sheet as depreciation and interest expenses, squeezing margins for years.

Ex-Cainiao CTO Launches Physical AI Platform: Quantum Dynamics Raises Nine-Figure Seed Round

Chinese tech media 36Kr reported on July 31 that Quantum Dynamics, a Physical AI platform company founded by Li Qiang — former CTO of Cainiao Group and Alibaba's International Digital Commerce unit, who also led Cainiao's autonomous vehicle business — has completed a seed round of over 100 million RMB (about $14 million), co-invested by Yunqi Capital and SenseTime, with MultiDimension Capital supporting incubation. During his 17 years at Alibaba, Li managed R&D teams of thousands across four business units and connected with postal systems in 200 countries.

Founded in the first half of 2026, Quantum Dynamics pursues a "scenarios first, foundation model second" strategy: instead of waiting for a perfect general model, it aims to put thousands of robots to work in logistics warehouses. A mid-size warehouse picking robot performs tens of thousands of grasps, placements, scans and labeling operations daily — each success or failure is an automatically labeled real-world signal. The company is building a proprietary World-Action Model that jointly models vision, action, body state and tactile signals to learn "how an operation changes the physical world," paired with edge-side lightweight algorithms that identify high-value data segments, test-time training and few-shot adaptation so robots adapt to new warehouses without full retraining. In 2026 it targets shelf restocking, picking and parcel packing in B2C and small-to-mid B e-commerce warehouses; in 2027 it plans to expand toward complex express delivery networks.

IDC projects China's embodied AI robot spending to exceed $1.4 billion in 2025 and surge to $77 billion by 2030, a 94% CAGR. SenseTime Capital said logistics is one of the most solid commercialization tracks in embodied AI, where cost reduction and efficiency gains are directly quantifiable, and Li's logistics and e-commerce expertise gives the team a fast path to a real-data flywheel.
