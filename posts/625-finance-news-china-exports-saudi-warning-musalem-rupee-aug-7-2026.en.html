⚡ Aug 7 Finance Briefing: China July Exports Beat at +23%, Saudi Warns of Imminent Multi-Front Attacks, St. Louis Fed's Musalem Turns Hawkish, RBI Supports the Rupee, Asian Stocks Reverse

Five stories moved global financial markets over the past couple of hours: China's customs data showed July exports up about 23% year-on-year, beating estimates, with AI demand and tariff front-running underpinning shipments and the yuan strengthening in response; a senior Saudi official warned that multi-front coordinated attacks are imminent, with energy and oil facilities among potential targets, darkening the Gulf security picture; St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem struck a hawkish tone in Sao Paulo, saying inflation risks remain tilted higher; the Reserve Bank of India sold USD/INR to support the rupee; and Asian equities turned risk-off, with the Nikkei down about 1% and the KOSPI giving up an intraday push toward 6,400. Here is this round's finance and economy briefing.

1. China July Exports Rise About 23%, Beating Estimates: AI Demand Carries Trade, Yuan Firms

According to ForexLive and CNBC, Chinese customs data released Friday showed July exports up about 23% year-on-year in dollar terms, ahead of the roughly 22% expected. Imports rose about 27.5%, just shy of the near-28% forecast and a marked slowdown from June's roughly 36% jump — the fastest in five years. The monthly trade surplus came in at about $112 billion, above analyst estimates near $107 billion but narrowing from about $126 billion in June.

Two forces explain the strength. First, the global AI infrastructure build-out continues to absorb China's high-tech goods: combined goods trade reached 30.13 trillion yuan over the first seven months of 2026, up 17.3% year-on-year, and July exports of high-tech products — including industrial robots and 3D printers — grew more than 50% from a year earlier, accounting for close to 60% of the month's total export increment. Second, manufacturers raced goods onto US-bound vessels ahead of an anticipated tariff increase: Washington applied a new levy of around 12.5% on Chinese products in late July, replacing an expired temporary lower rate. Following the strong print, the yuan strengthened. Analysts note that with Q2 GDP growth slowing to about 4.3% — the weakest since Q4 2022 — exports remain the bedrock supporting China's manufacturing base.

2. Saudi Warns of "Multi-Front" Attacks: Gulf Security Darkens, Oil Risk Premium Builds

According to ForexLive's Asia-Pacific market live coverage, a senior Saudi official told both CNN and Reuters that the kingdom expects multiple coordinated attacks imminently, with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), Yemen's Houthis and Iraqi militias converging from the north, south and east. Potential targets are said to include civilian facilities, energy and oil installations, economic sites, critical infrastructure, airports and ports.

The warning lands as Gulf tensions remain elevated, with Strait of Hormuz diplomacy and US-Iran maneuvering still the core drivers of oil pricing. Risk-off sentiment spread quickly through Asian markets — the KOSPI, which had opened more than 1% higher and briefly touched 6,400, gave back its entire gain within the hour as the Saudi headlines hit, underscoring how quickly the geopolitical premium on crude can reprice.

3. St. Louis Fed's Musalem Turns Hawkish: Inflation Risks Tilt Higher, Policy Needs "Meaningful Restraint"

According to ForexLive, St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem used a speech and Q&A in Sao Paulo to reinforce the hawkish signal he has been sending since last week's FOMC meeting. He said inflation remains well above target with risks tilted higher, and argued monetary policy must impose "meaningful restraint" rather than stay easy in pursuit of productivity gains, warning that doing so would put the Fed's credibility at risk.

Musalem becomes the latest Fed official to publicly stress readiness to tighten, following Governor Lisa Cook's hawkish remarks in Anchorage last week. Markets read the tone as a sign that policymakers remain highly alert to the combination of sticky inflation, tariff shocks and energy-price volatility, keeping the path of rate cuts this year deeply uncertain and adding to swings in Treasury yields and the dollar.

4. RBI Steps In: Selling USD/INR to Support the Rupee

According to ForexLive, the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) sold USD/INR in the market to support the rupee, the latest move in this round's emerging-market currency defense. With the dollar firm and global risk appetite shaky, Asian currencies have broadly come under pressure, and India's central bank chose direct intervention over verbal guidance alone — a clear signal of its commitment to exchange-rate stability.

Analysts note that emerging-market central banks have been unusually active lately — from intervention talk around the yen to volatility in the Indonesian rupiah and the Korean won — reflecting persistent pressure from capital flows and rate differentials. Whether the rupee stabilizes will depend on the dollar and, crucially, on oil prices — the very variable at the center of the current geopolitical risk.

5. Asian Stocks: Nikkei Down About 1%, KOSPI Reverses on Risk-Off Mood

According to ForexLive's Asia-Pacific market data, Friday saw mixed trading across regional equities as multiple headlines collided: Japan's Nikkei fell around 1%, with AI- and chip-related losses outweighing broader gains — SoftBank Group dropped about 4% even after beating first-quarter profit expectations — while the Topix was roughly flat. In Korea, the KOSPI opened more than 1% higher and briefly touched 6,400 before reversing its entire gain within the hour on the Hormuz and Saudi attack-warning headlines.

Looking ahead, China's July inflation data is due Sunday, August 9 at 01:30 GMT, and will be closely watched for evidence of whether strong external demand is feeding into domestic price pressures — the next key data point for judging the resilience of China's economy and its policy room.
