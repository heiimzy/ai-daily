⚡ August 5 Finance Update: India's RBI Holds Repo Rate at 5.25%, Indonesia Q2 GDP Grows 5.29% Beating Estimates, Bessent Says BOJ Will 'Do What Is Best', Gold Eyes $4,200 on Hormuz Hopes

Four stories moved global macro and FX markets over the past few hours. In Asia, India's central bank delivered its bi-monthly policy decision, holding the repo rate at 5.25% for a sixth straight meeting with a neutral stance and trimming its FY27 inflation forecast to 5%; Indonesia's statistics bureau reported second-quarter GDP growth of 5.29% year over year — slower than the prior quarter but still beating estimates; U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent again weighed in on the yen, saying he is confident Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda will execute rate decisions appropriately and reaffirming U.S. support for stabilizing the currency; and gold held a firm bid above $4,150, with markets setting their sights on $4,200, while silver targets $64. Here is this round's finance roundup.

1. India's RBI holds rates for a sixth straight meeting: repo at 5.25%, inflation cut to 5%

According to FXStreet (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:40 GMT) and DD India (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:55 GMT), India's central bank kept its key repo rate at 5.25% at its August bi-monthly monetary policy meeting, in line with market expectations, while retaining a neutral stance. This marks the sixth consecutive hold — a sign of how carefully the Reserve Bank of India is balancing domestic inflation against external uncertainty.

More notably, the RBI lowered its inflation forecast for fiscal 2026-27 to 5% from 5.4%, though that still sits above the central bank's 4% mid-point target — a reminder that policymakers are not yet ready to start an easing cycle. The statement also flagged two key upside risks: monsoon rainfall and food prices, and persistent geopolitical uncertainty, especially with the situation in the Arabian Sea and around the Strait of Hormuz still tense. According to Finimize (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:58 GMT), the RBI's cash reserve ratio (CRR) timetable has now reached 3%, adding to expectations of looser headroom, yet the central bank chose prudence until the data confirms the path. The Indian rupee gave back some early gains after the decision, with markets broadly reading the move as a "patient wait-and-see" posture between inflation and growth.

2. Indonesia Q2 GDP grows 5.29% year over year: slower but above forecasts

According to Bloomberg (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:15 GMT) and Reuters (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:19 GMT), Indonesia's statistics bureau reported that second-quarter GDP rose 5.29% year over year, easing from 5.4% in the first quarter but still beating market expectations of 5.2%-5.3% and above the consensus forecast near 5.26%. Quarter over quarter, growth remained robust as well.

The key growth engines remain domestic demand and investment: household consumption stayed resilient, government investment and infrastructure spending continued to flow, and private-sector investment expanded on solid manufacturing and services activity. A stable economy gives Bank Indonesia policy room as the rupiah and capital flows stabilize. Analysts now watch second-half commodity prices — nickel and palm oil in particular — along with how global financing conditions treat emerging markets. Indonesia's growth staying among the fastest in global EM for several straight quarters underlines the resilience of Southeast Asia's largest economy.

3. Bessent backs the yen: "BOJ will do what is best," U.S.-Japan FX coordination deepens

According to Investing.com citing Reuters (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:25 GMT) and nippon.com (Aug. 5, 2026, 01:13 GMT), U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has been vocal about the yen and the Bank of Japan. Bessent said he is confident Governor Kazuo Ueda "will do what is best," and stressed U.S. support for Japan's efforts to stabilize the yen; he also reaffirmed that Washington stood on Japan's side during yen intervention, calling it a move to help stabilize Asia rather than mere currency politics.

The remarks are read by markets as a new phase in U.S.-Japan monetary diplomacy. Global FX has been highly sensitive to the yen this year — the currency at one point weakened to historic lows before rebounding on expectations of coordinated intervention. Bessent has repeatedly framed "exchange-rate stability" as a priority, saying the U.S. is ready to support Japan's currency through policy tools. Analysts point out that once the yen gains strong external backing, it can ripple through Japanese exporter earnings, carry trades and Asian capital flows; it also gives the Bank of Japan more breathing room on its rate path — supporting the currency today while laying groundwork for a potential policy normalization.

4. Gold firm above $4,150 on Hormuz hopes: markets eye $4,200, silver targets $64

According to FXStreet (Aug. 5, 2026, 03:36 GMT) and FXEmpire (Aug. 5, 2026, 04:33 GMT), gold stayed strong through the Asian session, with XAU/USD maintaining consistent buy support above $4,150. The key macro narrative is the one Treasury Secretary Bessent pushed — that Washington and Tehran could reach a deal within days to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. That optimism has lifted overall risk appetite while also reshaping how the market prices energy supply and geopolitical risk premiums. Yet this geopolitical cheer has not weakened gold's safe-haven bid, which stands in contrast to falling oil prices.

FXEmpire's technical view is that gold is trying to hold above $4,150, with the next key target the $4,200 round number; a clean break would open the door to a higher range. Silver is rising in tandem and eyeing $64. Separately, oil is retreating on easing geopolitical tensions and demand worries while the dollar index stays soft — both supportive for dollar-denominated gold. Notably, KITCO data (Aug. 4, 2026, 17:12 GMT) showed U.S. June JOLTS job openings fell to 7.36 million, near a recent low; a cooling labor market strengthens bets on monetary easing, a medium-term tailwind for gold. Analysts caution that if a Hormuz deal ultimately falls through, or U.S. jobs data surprises to the upside, gold's bullish thesis would face a test.

Broader context: this finance round centers on "central-bank resolve" — India choosing to hold amid inflation still above target and keep a neutral tone writes a memo on EM patience; Indonesia's GDP, slower yet above expectations, underscores the strength of Southeast Asia's growth engine; and Bessent's persistent backing of the yen turns "exchange-rate stability" into a clear thread running through U.S.-Japan monetary coordination, with spillovers into carry trades and global flows. At the same time, Hormuz negotiation hopes are pulling in two directions at once — easing energy and geopolitical premiums on one hand, and building a higher platform under gold on the other as risk re-pricing takes shape. For investors, India cutting its inflation forecast to 5% without starting to ease signals that "lowering rates waits for confirmation" remains the EM theme; meanwhile gold eyeing $4,200 and silver targeting $64 is the latest state of the precious-metals bid under the confluence of external and domestic factors.
