⚡ July 31 Tech Roundup: Google's AI Fixes More Chrome Bugs Than Past 2 Years Combined, LinkedIn Adds 'AI Slop' Button, Friend 2.0 Returns with Voice

🔐 Google's AI Tools Fixed 1,072 Chrome Bugs in June — More Than the Past Two Years Combined

Google announced Thursday that its internal AI tools helped patch a staggering 1,072 security vulnerabilities in Chrome during the month of June — more than the 1,036 bugs fixed across the previous 23 versions over the past two years combined.

A white paper published by Google shows the exponential increase. Doug Turner, Chrome's Director of Engineering, told TechCrunch that LLMs have "fundamentally shifted the economics of cybersecurity, transforming vulnerability discovery into an automated, industrial-scale operation."

"By applying models like Gemini, we are preemptively fixing vulnerabilities, outpacing our adversaries and making Chrome safer with every update," Turner said.

The 1,072 bugs were spread across Chrome 149 and 150. Of these, 22 were rated as critical severity. Google paid over $209,000 in bug bounties to external security researchers, with the largest single bounty of $97,000 going to an anonymous researcher for a critical out-of-bounds read/write in Chrome's ANGLE component.

Google's internal AI tools — including CodeMender (based on DeepMind/Gemini) and the Big Sleep agent — enable automated code analysis at machine speed. This trend extends beyond Google: Mozilla, Microsoft, and Palo Alto Networks are all using advanced AI models like Claude Mythos to discover hundreds of vulnerabilities in their own codebases.

"AI is compressing the timeline for vulnerability discovery, patching, and exploitation. Defenders and attackers now operate at machine speed, making traditional patch cycles obsolete."

Notably, the vast majority of the 429 vulnerabilities fixed in Chrome 149 were discovered by Google's own AI tools, signaling that AI-driven security automation has reached production grade.

📱 LinkedIn Launches 'Seems Like AI Slop' Reporting Button in War on Low-Quality Content

LinkedIn rolled out a new feature Thursday — a "Seems like AI slop" button in the three-dot menu on posts, allowing users to report content that appears to be AI-generated and low-quality.

LinkedIn Chief Product Officer Hari Srinivasan acknowledged the problem in a post: "AI slop is a top priority for all of us. We really care about this. People come to LinkedIn to connect with real people and share their real perspectives, ideas, and expertise."

The move comes amid alarming data: AI detection firm Pangram found that 41% of longform posts on LinkedIn were flagged as entirely AI-generated. The platform has been flooded with formulaic "B2B sales magic" AI slop.

Beyond the reporting button, LinkedIn introduced several complementary measures:

  • New classifiers: Automatically identify AI slop and low-quality content to reduce recommendations
  • Private flagging: Notify users when others feel their content comes off as inauthentic due to heavy AI use
  • AI writing tool removed: Replaced the "enhance with AI" feature with a proofread-only tool that doesn't change the user's voice
  • Automation defenses: Blocking "hundreds of thousands" of automated comments daily and millions of automation attempts over recent months

When a user clicks the report button, the post is immediately hidden from their feed. LinkedIn says these signals will be used to train AI models and improve feed quality. This marks the first major social platform to offer a dedicated "AI-generated content" reporting option, potentially setting a new industry standard.

🎤 Friend 2.0 Returns After Two Years: AI Emotional Companion Adds Voice, Price Doubles to $249

Two years ago, tech founder Avi Schiffmann launched Friend — an AI wearable pendant designed to combat loneliness. It connected via Bluetooth to the user's phone, constantly listened, and communicated through text messages. This week, the company announced a major overhaul: Friend 2.0 is here.

"Introducing friend 2.0," Schiffmann tweeted Thursday. The biggest change is a built-in speaker that gives the device a unique, consistent personality for voice conversations, rather than just text messages.

In promotional videos, a woman wearing Friend discusses her ex-girlfriend, and the device responds: "It's not bad to be gay." In another clip, a man shares his filmmaking plans and receives encouragement and praise from the device.

Schiffmann emphasizes that Friend is neither an assistant nor a replacement for a romantic partner, but a reliable conversational companion. The core philosophy: AI companionship is one of AI's killer use cases.

But the upgrade comes with a steep price increase. Friend 2.0 retails for $249, up from the original $99. The doubling of price may spark debate about the value proposition of AI emotional companion devices.

Friend 2.0 is now available for pre-order in the US and Canada. Analysts note that the AI wearable market faces headwinds — both Humane AI Pin and Rabbit R1 failed to deliver on their initial promises — raising questions about whether Friend 2.0 can find its niche.
