⚡ Aug 12 Tech News: Google Gemini Tops 1B Users, India RBI Backs AI Lending, Joby Buys Defense Firm for $500M, Universal Wins Four Patents

The hours from the early morning to mid-morning of Aug 12 brought dense general-tech headlines: Google's AI assistant Gemini passed 1 billion monthly users, which CEO Sundar Pichai called the company's fastest-growing product ever; India's central bank governor publicly urged lenders to use AI to approve loans that human officers would reject, while flagging "black box" and bias risks; eVTOL maker Joby agreed to buy defense technology firm Resonant Sciences for about $500 million, folding its defense and autonomous-flying programs in; and Universal was awarded four US patents in a single day, spanning a buoyant ride system, adaptive seating and an AI audio guide. Here is a closer look at each, with all source publish times individually verified.

1. Google's Gemini Passes 1 Billion Monthly Users, Called Its Fastest-Growing Product

According to AFP, carried by Gulf News (published Aug 12, 01:15 UTC), The Edge Malaysia (01:56 UTC) and The Economic Times (02:34 UTC), Google CEO Sundar Pichai announced Tuesday that the company's AI assistant Gemini has surpassed 1 billion monthly users. Pichai said on social media that Gemini "is our fastest growing product ever" and is the 14th Google product to reach a billion users. The milestone lands a week after a major reorganization of the company's AI division — Nobel chemistry laureate Demis Hassabis has recently stepped back from day-to-day management of Google DeepMind, and multiple leading researchers, including Jeff Dean, have departed. Meanwhile Google's next flagship model, Gemini 3.5 Pro (initially promised for June), has still not shipped, even as rivals have launched models seen as more capable, especially for generating computer code — currently the sector's most lucrative area. The report notes that amid this technological lag, Google's biggest asset remains its vast application ecosystem, from search and Gmail to Google Maps and YouTube, into which AI is being integrated. By comparison, OpenAI said in July it had crossed 1 billion users across all its interfaces, led by ChatGPT, without specifying monthly or weekly; research firm Sensor Tower said ChatGPT's share of the AI-assistant market fell below 50 percent in the first half of the year. For Google, a billion users is a win, but it also sharpens the pressure to convert that audience into a durable moat while catching up on model capability.

2. India's Central Bank Governor Wants AI to Approve Loans — but "The Model Decided" Is Not an Answer

According to British tech outlet The Register (published Aug 12, 01:18 UTC), Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Governor Sanjay Malhotra, in a speech at the FIBAC conference in Mumbai, urged India's financial institutions to use AI to approve loans that human lending officers would likely reject. He said the regulator "sees AI as a capability to be responsibly harnessed and not merely as a risk to be contained," offering several reasons: banks today struggle to justify credit to first-time borrowers, gig workers or small businesses without formal books, and "AI models, trained on alternative data – cash flows, GST filings, utility payments, digital footprints – can extend the frontier of 'bankable' India considerably further than manual underwriting ever could, at a fraction of the marginal cost per loan." Predictive models could also flag borrowers on the cusp of default "early enough to counsel rather than merely recover," and AI could power voice interfaces in local languages — India recognizes 14 major languages each spoken by 10 million-plus people. But Malhotra also flagged three risks: the "black box problem" — when an AI recommends denying credit to a small business, both borrower and regulator are entitled to know why; the risk AI could entrench "biases against certain geographies, certain occupations, certain communities"; and over-dependence on a cluster of tech vendors, which could spread flawed systems across the banking system. He stressed: "No matter how sophisticated the model, the responsibility for a bank's decisions rests with the bank, not with its algorithm. 'The model decided' can never be an acceptable answer to a customer, an auditor, or the Reserve Bank."

3. Joby Agrees to Buy Resonant Sciences for About $500M, Folding In Defense & Autonomous Flying

According to Reuters, carried by The Economic Times (published Aug 12, 03:01 UTC), eVTOL company Joby Aviation said Tuesday it would acquire defense technology firm Resonant Sciences for about $500 million, as it looks to scale within a fast-growing defense market. The eVTOL industry is increasingly pivoting to the military as commercial city-air-taxi development has taken longer than expected and defense spending has ramped up amid the wars in Ukraine and the Middle East. Deal details: Joby will pay roughly $450 million in cash plus about $50 million in its common stock; Dayton, Ohio-based Resonant designs and delivers advanced radio-frequency and mission systems for US national-security customers, systems that "help aircraft see, communicate, and operate in complex environments." After closing, Joby's defense programs — including hybrid and autonomous VTOL projects — move under Resonant, which becomes Joby's dedicated defense business, while its commercial aircraft business stays focused on the electric air taxi. Market reaction: Joby shares fell 6.5% in premarket trading. The deal is expected to close in the first half of 2027 and will give the combined company about 1 million square feet of manufacturing, integration and testing space across the Dayton region. Joby CEO JoeBen Bevirt told Reuters the move "allows our commercial team to be laser focused on delivering on the air taxi mission," while pairing Joby's products with Resonant's technologies could open new markets.

4. Universal Wins Four US Patents in a Single Day: Floating Rides, Adaptive Seats & an AI Guide

According to Attractions Magazine (published Aug 12, 01:29 UTC), Universal was awarded four US patents on Tuesday, covering ride systems, adaptive seating and AI technology. The report notes that because some of the underlying applications have been public for years, Tuesday's grants are not four brand-new inventions, but they do offer another look at what Universal is developing and protecting. Two stand out: [Floating ride system] US Patent 12,702,934 describes a buoyant passenger vehicle connected to a track-mounted support, or bogie, through extendable mechanisms that retract for firmer support and extend when the vehicle enters water, letting buoyancy lift it away from its support for more natural motion. The patent depicts both aquatic and aerial segments of a ride path, opening the possibility of vehicles transitioning between distinct experiences — a mechanic parallel to Mack Rides' Rocking Boat, but implemented with Universal's own approach. The underlying invention dates to a provisional application filed May 5, 2020.

[Active-fluid adaptive seat] US Patent 12,703,284 describes a ride-vehicle seat with a flexible membrane filled with what Universal calls an "active fluid": the material changes properties under an electrical or magnetic field, staying deformable as the passenger sits down so the seat pan conforms to the body, then stiffening to hold posture. Universal cites electrorheological and magnetorheological fluids as candidate materials. [AI audio guide & loading system] Another patent covers an audio-guide system that uses an AI model to generate landmark information, and the set also includes a loading system that could provide extra boarding time to an individual ride vehicle without stopping the rest of the attraction. Altogether, the batch shows Universal pushing toward immersive, personalized experiences — and confirms that AI plus "responsive physical systems" are moving fast into the traditionally heavy-asset theme-park industry.

5. Big Picture: From "AI Going Mainstream" to a Quiet Patent War

These four stories sketch four threads running through tech right now: first, a white-hot user-race — Gemini crossing 1 billion against OpenAI's comparable claims shows the near-term contest is about turning technology into a mass-market moat fast; second, AI moving from front-end assistants to back-end decisions — India's RBI pushing AI lending shows AI penetrating credit and risk systems that directly affect financial fairness, while regulators simultaneously answer who is accountable for black-box, biased algorithms; third, hard-tech migrating to defense and advanced manufacturing — the Joby acquisition reveals that as commercial eVTOL lags, drones, autonomy and defense budgets become the new growth engine; fourth, patents as an invisible battlefield — Universal's four patents remind us that theme entertainment, like consumer hardware, is building future experience moats out of responsive physical systems plus AI. All four sources were published between 01:15 and 03:01 UTC on Aug 12 — fresh, verified and safe to share.
