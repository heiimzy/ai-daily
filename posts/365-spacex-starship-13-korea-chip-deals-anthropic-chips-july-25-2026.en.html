SpaceX Starship Flight 13 Scores Near-Perfect Success: V3 Starship Deploys Operational Starlink V3 Satellites, Achieves Softest Splashdown

SpaceX successfully launched its 13th integrated Starship test flight (IFT-13) on Friday, July 24, from Starbase, Texas, marking the second flight of the V3-generation vehicle and the company's first Starship mission since its record-breaking June IPO. The Ship 40 upper stage delivered what the company described as a "near-perfect" performance, deploying 20 production-grade Starlink V3 satellites, completing a critical in-space engine restart, and achieving the softest splashdown to date.

Key flight milestones as reported by SpaceX and multiple outlets:

  • Launch & Separation: All 33 Raptor 3 engines on Booster 20 ignited successfully at 5:51 p.m. CT. Hot-staging separation occurred at T+2 minutes 18 seconds, with the V3's integrated separation ring performing nominally
  • Booster Return: B20 executed a controlled splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico, though only 8 of the planned 13 engines reignited for the landing burn, causing a harder-than-expected impact — the sole blemish on an otherwise flawless flight
  • Starlink V3 Debut: Between T+17 and T+27 minutes, Starship deployed all 20 Starlink V3 satellites via its Pez-like dispenser — the first time Starship has carried production-grade satellites rather than mass simulators. SpaceX confirmed successful radio and laser link communication with all 20 units
  • In-Space Engine Restart: At T+39 minutes, Ship 40 successfully reignited a Raptor engine in space for approximately 14 seconds, passing a critical test that Flight 12 in May had failed to complete
  • Softest Splashdown: After a 65-minute flight, Starship performed a flawless belly-flop to vertical transition and splashed down upright in the Indian Ocean northwest of Australia, remaining fully intact and transmitting telemetry while floating

SpaceX communications manager Dan Huot called it "the softest splashdown we've ever seen — this is a dream scenario for the heat shield data team. Lucky number 13!" Elon Musk posted on X: "Starship is intact, floating in the ocean and transmitting telemetry!"

The Starlink V3 satellites represent a major leap in capability, with each satellite delivering approximately 1 Tbps downlink capacity — roughly 10 times that of the current V2 Mini generation. SpaceX filed an application on July 7 to operate up to 100,000 third-generation Starlink satellites, nearly 10 times the current on-orbit constellation. Only Starship has the payload capacity to deploy such a massive constellation.

For NASA's Artemis program, the successful flight provides critical momentum. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman shared a photo of Starship passing the Moon on X, commenting "Pretty clear from this shot that SpaceX knows where we are going!" However, a Government Accountability Office (GAO) report released Thursday warned that SpaceX still faces "major challenges" in orbital refueling technology and Raptor engine reliability, and is more than a year behind on several key milestones.

South Korea's Samsung and SK Hynix Seal $950 Billion in AI Chip Deals with U.S. Tech Giants

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung announced Saturday that Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have secured a combined $950 billion (1,375 trillion won) in long-term chip supply agreements with U.S. technology giants, announced at the San Francisco AI Summit. The historic deals represent a fundamental restructuring of the global AI semiconductor supply chain.

The agreements break down as follows:

  • SK Group — Nvidia: $500 billion: SK Hynix signed a long-term HBM supply partnership with Nvidia, covering HBM4 and next-generation AI memory co-development. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang personally announced the expanded relationship during his meeting with President Lee
  • SK Group — Other partners: $250 billion: Additional long-term memory procurement contracts with Anthropic, OpenAI, and other AI companies
  • Samsung Electronics — Broadcom: $200 billion: A strategic MOU spanning HBM4/HBM4E memory supply, sub-2nm foundry services, and advanced packaging for AI chips

In addition, Nvidia announced a $1 billion investment in Korean cloud company Naver for AI data center expansion. SK Telecom will build a 2-gigawatt AI data center in South Korea powered by Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform and SK Hynix HBM4 memory, targeting 2027 operations.

Presidential policy chief Kim Yong-beom emphasized these are "not overseas investment plans but long-term procurement commitments" — effectively pre-orders guaranteeing demand for Korean-made chips over the next five years. He added that Nvidia CEO Huang tells SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won "more chips" every time they meet, suggesting demand may soon exceed even the announced $500 billion figure.

Anthropic Joins AI Chip Self-Development Race, Approaches SK Hynix

Anthropic has joined the growing list of AI companies designing their own chips, following Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium), Meta (MTIA), and OpenAI (Jalapeño). SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won revealed at the San Francisco AI Summit that Anthropic has approached SK Hynix for chip supply to manufacture its own semiconductors.

"It's remarkable to see an AI developer with chip ambitions," Chey said on stage alongside Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei. Anthropic's vertical integration strategy began in November 2025 with a $50 billion data center investment plan. In April 2026, its self-developed chip plans emerged, covering both ASIC and GPU designs aimed at reducing dependence on external chip suppliers amid the global compute shortage.

This trend reflects a fundamental shift in Silicon Valley's AI hardware strategy. Custom-designed chips can deliver significantly better cost efficiency — OpenAI's Jalapeño chip, built with Broadcom, claims approximately 50% better inference cost efficiency than general-purpose GPUs. As model parameters balloon into the trillions, inference cost has become the primary bottleneck for AI commercialization, making in-house silicon a strategic necessity.

Black Forest Labs Launches Flux 3: Native Audio Video Generation Reaches New Heights

German AI company Black Forest Labs released Flux 3 on July 24 — a multimodal foundation model trained jointly on images, video, and audio that generates clips up to 20 seconds with native sound. According to The Decoder, this marks the Stability AI spinout's first major foray into video generation, building on its well-known Flux image models.

In early evaluations using 10-second 720p clips, Flux 3 demonstrated strong performance: preferred over Luma Ray 3.2 in 93% of comparisons, Runway Gen-4.5 in 77%, and Grok Imagine Video in 69%. Margins narrowed against top competitors — 60% against Kling v3 Pro and 52% against Seedance 2.0 and Gemini Omni Flash — placing Flux 3 among the leading pack.

The model also includes a dedicated action prediction module powering Flux-mimic, a video action model already being tested at Audi for robotics applications. Black Forest Labs has committed to open-weight access under the name "Flux 3 Dev," preserving the open-source ethos as competitors increasingly lock down frontier creative AI tools.

Jensen Huang's First X Post Backs Open-Weight AI: 25 Tech Giants Sign Joint Letter

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang posted his first-ever message on X on July 24, not about chips or products, but sharing a joint open letter titled "Open-Weight AI and American AI Leadership" signed by 25 technology companies. "AI will transform every industry, empower every company, and be built by every nation," Huang wrote. "The world needs both frontier closed-source and frontier open-source models."

Signatories included Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft, IBM, Palantir, Hugging Face, Mistral AI, Perplexity, a16z, and Y Combinator. Notably absent were OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google — the three largest closed-source AI labs — highlighting Silicon Valley's fundamental divide over AI technology strategy.

The letter's timing is significant as Washington debates whether to ban Chinese open-source AI models following the rapid emergence of Kimi K3 from Moonshot AI and DeepSeek, which have been closing the gap with U.S. frontier models. Huang's message: open-weight models are essential to maintaining American AI competitiveness, and restrictive regulation would harm the broader ecosystem.
