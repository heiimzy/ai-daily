⚡ Aug 3 AI Chip Update: Biren Completes MiniMax H3 Inference, MediaTek Embraces Intel Packaging

Biren tunes MiniMax H3 for 'Day-0' inference on its Bili GPUs

Chinese GPU maker Biren announced on August 3 that it has completed 'Day-0' adaptation and tuning of MiniMax's new frontier model H3 across its flagship Bili GPU lineup, built on the open-source SGLang inference framework. 'Day-0' means the model runs on domestic silicon from its first day of release, with critical software stacks, operator libraries and framework layers matched up front.

As reported by Yicai Finance, the adaptation covers operator mapping, memory allocation and distributed inference configuration for the Bili series. Biren says the deep synergy between SGLang and its hardware delivers high-throughput, low-latency inference for H3 — another step in 'software-hardware co-optimization' between domestic models and domestic silicon. The company has long argued that winning the GPU ecosystem isn't just about single-card peak performance, but about the speed and breadth of model adaptation.

With domestic compute racing to catch up, 'Day-0 adaptation' is fast becoming the new battleground metric among Chinese GPU makers as they court developers. Biren's move — aligned with the hot open-source model lineup from Tencent's Hy3 to DeepSeek — demonstrates its ecosystem momentum in concrete terms.

MediaTek for the first time confirms CoWoS + Intel EMIB-T dual-track for AI ASIC

At its latest earnings call, MediaTek (2454) confirmed for the first time that its AI ASIC (custom chip) projects use Intel's EMIB-T advanced packaging alongside TSMC's CoWoS — the first time the company has disclosed its second advanced-packaging partner, marking a shift from a single supply chain to a flexible, multi-track strategy for giant AI datacenter chips.

Commercial Times reported that CEO Rick Tsai said the company continues to deepen design-collaboration optimization with TSMC while staying close to key advanced packaging partners, combining 2nm process design expertise with large-chip architecture capabilities to help customers develop reticle-scale, high-performance ASICs that support rapidly growing AI infrastructure.

EMIB-T is the latest version of Intel's EMIB (Embedded Multi-die Interconnect Bridge) 2.5D packaging platform. It uses small silicon bridges to connect multiple dies rather than a large silicon interposer, lowering cost while preserving high-bandwidth, high-density interconnect and HBM integration, and can integrate chips roughly 8-10x the reticle size. For years advanced packaging has been dominated by TSMC CoWoS; MediaTek's move signals that the era of reticle-and-beyond AI chips demands more than a single packaging technology.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics fast-tracks Philippines AI MLCC plant for next-gen Nvidia accelerators

South Korea's Seoul Economic Daily reported on August 3 that Samsung Electro-Mechanics (009150.KS) will bring its third MLCC (multilayer ceramic capacitor) plant, under construction in Calamba, Laguna, Philippines, into operation from the end of the first quarter next year — earlier than planned — to meet surging demand for high-capacity components used in AI accelerators. The plant will serve as a dedicated facility for high-capacity 'AI MLCCs' to be mounted on Nvidia's next-generation accelerators 'Vera Rubin' and 'Feynman'.

To lock in supply, the company has signed long-term agreements (LTAs) with around ten clients, including tier-1 hyperscalers. The industry expects Samsung Electro-Mechanics' CAPEX to jump from about 3 trillion won this year to 6 trillion won next year - nearly doubling. As AI servers increasingly run on 'intelligence,' passive components once dismissed as 'ordinary parts' are becoming a hidden bottleneck fiercely competed for across the AI supply chain.

Google reportedly to deploy 12-15 million TPU v9 chips by 2028, potentially surpassing Nvidia

Quoting Taiwanese media and, an analyst report from Fubon, ITHome reported that Google plans to deploy 12 to 15 million of its self-designed ninth-generation TPU v9 AI accelerator chips by 2028. Analysts estimate Nvidia's AI GPU shipments could reach 12.4 million units in 2028, meaning Google's AI accelerator volume could match or even exceed Nvidia's by 2028.

The report notes TPU v9 will feature a four-compute-die architecture imported by the huge advanced process and packaging capacity needs, making it difficult for Google to rely on TSMC alone, and pushing it to seek capacity from Intel Foundry and Samsung Foundry. Earlier, Intel was reported to have won an order to make roughly 3 million TPUs under packaging. If the figures are correct, Google would not only become the world's biggest consumer of AI accelerators but also reshape the compute landscape between 'would-be self-designed hyperscaler silicon' and 'commercial Nvidia GPUs.'
