ICANN Delegates the .web TLD: A Decade-Long Battle Ends as Verisign Prepares to Open Registrations

The Internet Corporation for Assigned Names and Numbers (ICANN) has officially completed the root delegation of the .web top-level domain, with Verisign — the world's largest domain registry — as the designated operator, planning to open registrations in the second half of this year. The move brings a nearly decade-long fight over the domain to a close.

It began in July 2016, when ICANN held the final auction for the .web registry. NuDotCo, a Verisign partner, won with a $135 million bid. But Altanovo Domains (formerly Afilias) and others filed objections, and after multiple Independent Review Process (IRP) proceedings the domain never went live. The dispute centered on the fact that Verisign had not bid directly: it quietly signed a proxy agreement with NuDotCo, which bid on its behalf and would transfer the registry rights to Verisign after winning — without disclosing the proxy deal to ICANN or updating its application, which rival registries argued violated multiple compliance rules.

In June 2023, the ICANN Board ruled that NuDotCo had not violated the rules, allowing the .web rollout to proceed; by July 2026, all remaining disputes had been resolved (details confidential) and Altanovo's IRP request was withdrawn, clearing the way for ICANN's formal delegation. One caveat: Verisign's registry prices tend to run high, so .web pricing could become a focus for users once it launches.

83% of PS5 Games Run Without Downloads — Yet Sony Still Pushes All-Digital

According to ongoing research by gaming testing firm Does It Play, 83% of PS5 games tested run without downloading anything beyond the disc. While 17% of those titles have notable bugs or missing features, 66% offer a complete, fully functional experience — the firm has tested 792 PS5 games in total.

The data, however, is not stopping Sony's digital push: by 2028, that share for new releases will drop to zero, meaning all PlayStation players will need a persistent online connection and full reliance on server uptime — a risk highlighted by recent PSN outages. For comparison, Xbox Series X fared worse in the same tests: of only 78 Xbox titles tested, 38% required additional downloads and just 50% were fully playable, well behind the PS5's 66%.

Analysts note that however the numbers are read, they are hardly encouraging: even if Sony reversed its decision to drop physical releases — which currently looks unlikely — the industry must act to preserve gaming culture and heritage.

Ex-OpenAI Researcher Urges Colleagues: Cash Out Now, Don't Wait for the IPO

Andrew Ho, who joined OpenAI's technical team in late 2025 and left after about eight months to found a startup, wasted no time telling former colleagues to take the money via tender offers instead of waiting for an IPO. He himself holds roughly $700,000 in equity he cannot yet cash out — last October, OpenAI completed a $6.6 billion employee tender offer that more than 600 employees joined, making many overnight millionaires.

Ho's real target is the valuation logic of the entire frontier-lab race. His math: if an AI lab sustains an 80% gross margin and the market grants a 20x P/E, supporting a $1 trillion valuation would require $100-200 billion in annual revenue — but in reality labs must keep pouring money into training the next model. That is the AI industry's inescapable red-queen race: sprinting not to pull ahead, but merely to hold market share. Revenue is uncertain while spending is all but certain — which is exactly why many labs carry ever-higher valuations yet keep raising and burning cash.

In his view, frontier-lab valuations rest on extremely optimistic assumptions; if the market reprices, a doubling is unlikely, but a halving is quite possible. Tellingly, the day before his tweet, US tech stocks suffered a broad pullback: the Nasdaq 100 fell into technical correction, Meta dropped over 10% in a day, and Google fell more than 8% over the week. Ho stresses he is not bearish on AI itself — he left to build a company supplying reinforcement-learning datasets to frontier labs, arguing that as scaling gains slow, high-quality training data will become scarcer than compute, with labs potentially spending over $100 billion on data in the coming years.

BYD July Sales Hit 419,200: Nearly 180,000 Overseas, Extending Global EV Leadership

BYD released its July production and sales report on the evening of August 1: 419,200 vehicles sold, with overseas sales approaching 180,000, keeping the company firmly at No. 1 in global new-energy vehicle sales. The Chinese EV giant has now held monthly volumes comfortably above the 400,000 mark for several consecutive months, with the overseas share steadily climbing.

The results cap a strong July for Chinese automakers: Chery sold 276,800 vehicles with monthly exports breaking 200,000 for the first time, and Leapmotor delivered 101,267 units to become the first new EV brand to top 100,000 monthly deliveries — new energy and overseas expansion remain the two clearest growth tracks.
