⚡ July 25 International Politics Roundup: US 13th Night of Iran Strikes, Iran Retaliates Against 3 Countries, China-Philippines Clash at Scarborough Shoal, Israel Expands West Bank Settlements

1. US Completes 13th Consecutive Night of Strikes Across Iran

US Central Command (CENTCOM) announced early Friday that it had completed its 13th consecutive night of strikes against Iranian military targets. The operation, concluded at 9 PM ET Thursday, spanned the entire country — from Bandar Abbas and Qeshm Island on the Strait of Hormuz, through Ahvaz in Khuzestan province, to the Caspian Sea coast in Gilan province.

According to Iranian state broadcaster IRIB, US missiles struck an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy headquarters in the Ziba Kenar area of Gilan province, while also hitting Piranshahr in West Azerbaijan Province near the Iraq border. Four people were killed and five injured in the city of Ahvaz. CENTCOM said the strikes targeted "Iranian military command centers, drone storage facilities, communication networks, coastal surveillance sites, and maritime capabilities" to further degrade Iran's threat to civilian shipping.

Despite continued IRGC attacks on vessels, CENTCOM insisted the Strait of Hormuz "remains open for transit." More than 50,000 US service members are currently deployed across the Middle East.

2. Iran Retaliates with Arash Suicide Drones Against US Bases in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait

Iran's military announced Friday it had carried out large-scale drone and missile strikes against American military facilities across the Middle East in retaliation for the 13th night of US attacks.

Bahrain: The IRGC struck Isa Air Base with Arash kamikaze drones, targeting fuel depots, large equipment warehouses, barracks, and significantly damaging a surveillance tower used by the US 5th Fleet. Air raid sirens sounded across the island nation.

Jordan: Iran claimed drone attacks against Al-Azraq base, targeting "aircraft hangars, aviation maintenance hangars, and a barracks." Jordan's military said it intercepted seven missiles and six drones with no casualties or material damage.

Kuwait: The IRGC struck Ali al-Salem Air Base, claiming to have destroyed an ammunition depot and inflicted US casualties. Additional attacks targeted US equipment warehouses at Camp Buehring (Al-Adiri) and troop positions at Camp Arifjan and Camp Doha near Kuwait City.

In Iraq's northern Kurdish region, five explosive-laden drones were shot down near Erbil International Airport, which temporarily closed before resuming normal operations.

3. Trump Threatens 'Massive Attack Bigger Than Ever Before,' Freezes Iranian Assets to Pay for Ship Damages

President Donald Trump told Axios on Friday that he is "very close to making a decision" on launching "a massive attack — bigger than ever before" against Iran. "We are fully prepared for it," Trump said, adding that while Israel "would join within two minutes if asked," the US could accomplish all military objectives alone.

Trump also announced on Truth Social a controversial policy: frozen Iranian assets held by the United States would be used to pay for all damages to ships and cargo. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the move an "incendiary precedent," warning that "no one's assets are safe once governments normalize such confiscation."

Iran simultaneously rejected a temporary ceasefire proposal delivered through Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi. Tehran stated it was not interested in a "provisional agreement that leaves the Strait of Hormuz's final status unresolved." Iranian officials declared the confrontation had entered an "either all or none" phase.

4. Houthi Red Sea Blockade: Brent Crude Briefly Tops $100/Barrel

Yemen's Houthi rebels, who control northern and western Yemen, announced a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia this week. On Thursday, they struck two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea's Bab el-Mandeb strait, sending Brent crude prices surging 7% to briefly breach $100/barrel for the first time since May. By Friday morning, prices had retreated to around $97/barrel.

Trump threatened "major military punishment" against both Iran and the Houthis if attacks continue. US gasoline prices climbed to a national average of $4.11/gallon, according to AAA. With both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb under threat, Saudi Arabia has diverted millions of barrels of oil daily through its East-West pipeline to the Red Sea port of Yanbu.

5. South China Sea: China Coast Guard Water Cannon Blasts Philippine Vessels at Scarborough Shoal

The Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) reported on Friday that Chinese Coast Guard ships again used water cannons against Philippine government vessels at Scarborough Shoal (Panatag Shoal/Huangyan Island) — the third confrontation this week. PCG Spokesperson Rear Admiral Jay Tarriela stated that CCG vessel 3107 fired water cannons at BRP Cape San Agustin at 6:26 AM, followed by another vessel attacking BRP Datu Paduhinog at 7:35 AM, approaching within 7 meters and risking collision.

The PCG reported that BRP Datu Paduhinog was directly hit by CCG 21581's water cannon for 18 minutes, followed by two more direct attacks. Up to 10 Philippine vessels faced 15 Chinese Coast Guard and maritime militia ships. The PCG "strongly condemned the dangerous and unprofessional actions" of China.

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed at the ASEAN Foreign Ministers' Meeting that a US Coast Guard vessel had intervened during Monday's Ayungin Shoal clash to help rescue injured Philippine personnel, reaffirming US commitments under the Mutual Defense Treaty. ASEAN and China reported "significant progress" toward concluding a Code of Conduct in the South China Sea by year's end.

6. Israel West Bank: Netanyahu Orders New Settlement Outposts After Deadly Clash

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu ordered the establishment of new settler outposts in the occupied West Bank on Friday, following a violent confrontation that killed two Israelis and four Palestinians. The violence erupted near the settlement of Havat Gilad and the neighboring Palestinian village of Tell.

Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz announced a "series of steps" including raids on Palestinian villages suspected of hosting fighters and "accelerating the legalization of farm outposts and establishment of new ones." The Israeli military said it was preparing for a "widespread counterterrorism operation" in the West Bank, postponing all soldier leave and deploying forces in the area. The Israeli security cabinet was scheduled to convene Friday for an emergency session.

7. Gaza Ceasefire Talks Collapse: Netanyahu and Trump Say Hamas Doesn't Want a Deal

Reuters reported Friday that both Prime Minister Netanyahu and President Trump have effectively abandoned Gaza ceasefire negotiations with Hamas, stating it had become clear the Palestinian militants "don't want a deal." Netanyahu said Israel was considering "alternative options" to achieve its goals of bringing hostages home and ending Hamas rule. A senior Hamas official countered that talks had been "constructive" and could have led to a deal "if the enemy had the will to reach one."
