1. Zelenskyy: Proposals Handed to US, Warns of Russian Autumn Mobilisation

According to Al Jazeera (published 05:26 UTC, Aug 12), Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says Kyiv has handed the United States new proposals aimed at ending the 4.5-year war with Russia. "We have conveyed our proposals to the American side," Zelenskyy said in his nightly video address. He said the US could help strengthen Ukraine's defences, particularly air defences, while increasing pressure on Moscow so that Russia prepares "for an end to the war rather than prolonging it". He offered no details on the proposals.

Talks to end Russia's invasion have largely stalled since the US and Israel launched their war on Iran. US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner were last in Moscow in January and have not yet visited Kyiv. Zelenskyy also accused Russia of preparing to use next month's parliamentary elections as a pretext for another large-scale military mobilisation — part of a plan to "create the appearance that Russians supposedly support the war". He alleged Putin "plans an additional rapid mobilisation of several hundred thousand Russians by the end of the year, plus another similar number next year". Moscow has previously denied that another mobilisation is necessary. Separately, US President Donald Trump announced that Russia has released former US Marine Robert Gilman after four years in detention, saying Putin agreed on a "humanitarian basis" and that Washington released no prisoners in return.

2. Indian Farm Exports Hit by Multiple Red Flags: Japan Halts Mangoes, China Revokes Rice Licences

According to an Al Jazeera investigation (published 05:34 UTC, Aug 12), Indian agricultural exports are facing simultaneous scrutiny from Japan, China, Europe and elsewhere. In Japan, the plant protection authorities in Yokohama sent a formal letter on March 31 barring all Indian mango shipments bearing inspection certificates issued on or after March 25 until operational standards improve. It is the first major disruption in India-Japan mango trade in nearly two decades — affecting six approved varieties (Alphonso, Kesar, Langra, Banganapalli, Chausa and Mallika) across the peak April–June export window. Japan imported roughly $1.54m worth of fresh and processed mango products from India in 2025-26; the sum is modest, but Japan is a premium market whose approval signals quality worldwide.

In China, on April 17 the General Administration of Customs revoked the import licences of three Indian rice exporters, claiming traces of genetically modified organisms (GMO) — a finding the exporters dispute, noting their shipments received GMO-free certification before departure. Rice makes up more than 20 percent of India's agricultural exports, with a record $12.5bn shipped in the last financial year (2025-26). Hong Kong has also suspended several Indian spices, and European inspectors have flagged others. Combined with heatwaves that destroyed much of Maharashtra's Alphonso crop and higher freight costs from West Asia disruption, Indian exporters face one of the most complex seasons in years.

3. UN Warns West Bank at 'Breaking Point' as Israeli Forces Arrest 23 Palestinians

According to Al Jazeera (published 04:59 UTC, Aug 12), Israeli forces have continued their crackdown in the occupied West Bank, arresting at least 23 Palestinians and demolishing several homes, as the UN warns the territory is at a "breaking point". Per the Palestinian Wafa news agency, those detained included two children, one with a disability; at least 11 others were arrested in the Jenin governorate and five in the Bethlehem area. In the village of Tal, southwest of Nablus, forces demolished the family home of Farouq Ramadan — one of four Palestinians killed in a confrontation with Israeli settlers on July 24. They also demolished another home near Yatta in the Hebron governorate and filled in a water well, displacing nine Palestinians.

Separately, dozens of settlers attacked Palestinians ploughing their lands in Al-Mughayyir, wounding six people including three children and two women, and burned part of the valley; in Khirbet Ras al-Ahmar settlers destroyed parts of the main water pipeline, cutting off 30 herding families. Addressing the UN Security Council on Tuesday, Deputy Special Coordinator Ramiz Alakbarov said the West Bank was at "breaking point": Israeli forces and settlers have killed 76 Palestinians, including 18 children, this year, while about 3,800 Palestinians — nearly half of them children — have been displaced. The UN has documented more than 1,430 settler attacks in 2026 across roughly 260 Palestinian communities. Since January 2023, 127 communities have been fully or partially displaced, affecting more than 6,390 people, with attacks spreading from Area C into Areas A and B.

4. Netanyahu Rejects Trump's Gaza Peace Plan, Tensions Surface

According to Al Jazeera (video report, 05:01 UTC, Aug 12), Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has rejected Donald Trump's Gaza peace plan, publicly exposing tensions between the two leaders. Al Jazeera's analysis, titled "Can Trump keep his 'irritation' with Netanyahu in check?", notes Trump has voiced growing frustration over the plan's lack of progress, and Netanyahu's open rejection has widened the rift. With the US–Israel war on Iran ongoing, the Gaza dispute adds another layer to an already volatile Middle East picture alongside the Strait of Hormuz standoff and talks with Tehran.

5. Trump Says US in 'Total Control' of Strait of Hormuz After Missiles Disable Cargo Ship

According to Al Jazeera (live updates, Aug 12, continuously updated), US President Donald Trump says the situation with Iran is "going fine" and that US forces are in "total control" of the Strait of Hormuz, telling reporters at Joint Base Andrews that forces are in "total control" of the waterway. Meanwhile, US Central Command says a helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles at the steering gear of a Panama-flagged cargo ship that ignored warnings while attempting to sail towards an Iranian port, disabling the vessel. Al Jazeera's live blog also reports that Qatar says negotiations between Oman and Iran over the future of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz are making major progress. With the US continuing to act against vessels linked to the Iranian blockade and oil prices fluctuating on the repeated twists in the waterway's status, global energy markets remain on edge.
