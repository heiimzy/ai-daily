⚡ Aug 8 Finance Roundup: Bitcoin ETFs Draw ~$100M, Ethereum ETFs Add $50M, BIP-110 Replay Warning, Hormuz Talks Near Breakthrough

Over the past hour or two, several big market stories landed at once: U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs posted roughly $98.85M in daily net inflows while spot Ethereum ETFs added about $49.6M, with capital concentrated squarely in BTC and ETH as Solana and XRP ETFs drew zero flows; a prominent Bitcoin developer warned that a BIP-110-triggered chain split could expose users to replay attacks that cost them real BTC; XRP Ledger v3.3.0 proposed six amendments, including "Confidential Transfers," targeting $530M+ in tokenized assets; and a U.S. official said Iran-Oman talks are close to an agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, pressuring oil and inflation expectations. Below is the breakdown, with source publication times verified.

1. Crypto Inflows Return: Bitcoin ETFs Draw ~$100M, Ethereum ETFs Add $50M

Per flow data published Saturday morning by Bloomingbit and Crypto Briefing (reflecting Friday's U.S. session), U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net inflows of roughly $98.85M to $102M on the day, while spot Ethereum ETFs added about $49.6M to $50M. Meanwhile Solana and XRP spot ETFs sat idle with no inflows — a reminder that institutional money is still flowing overwhelmingly into the two largest assets while "altcoin ETFs" remain on the sidelines.

On price action, Bitcoin pushed back above $65,000 after Friday's surprisingly negative U.S. jobs report and is now trading around $64,900, up ~1.3% over 24 hours; Ethereum is near $1,915, up ~1.1%. One sentiment driver came from Washington: Trump again called crypto "a big deal" on Friday and said "people are paying with Bitcoin," adding fuel to the risk-on move. After a painful stretch of outflows in July, the renewed ETF inflows are widely read as institutional accumulation below $65K.

2. BIP-110 Fork Risk: Developer Warns Replay Attacks Could Cost Real BTC

Kevin Loaec, CEO of wallet firm Wizardsardine and a Bitcoin developer, told CoinDesk that if the BIP-110 proposal triggers a chain split, users who transact on-chain without knowing how to safely split assets between the two chains could lose real BTC to a "replay attack." The mechanics: immediately after a fork, both the original chain and the forked chain accept the same transaction signatures, so a transaction signed on the forked chain could be replayed onto the main network by a third party — e.g., selling forked-chain coins could cause the same signed transaction to be replayed on mainnet, draining the user's actual Bitcoin.

Loaec advised ordinary investors to avoid any on-chain transactions during a fork period unless they have the technical expertise to safely separate the two chains' assets. BIP-110, which aims to restrict non-payment data such as images and text from Bitcoin transactions, requires support from more than 55% of the most recent 2,016 blocks to activate; miner support currently stands at only ~2.6%, far below the threshold, so activation looks unlikely — but the technical trigger for a split still exists, keeping the community's debate over blockchain data storage alive.

3. XRP Ledger Proposals: Confidential Transfers Target $530M+ in Tokenized Assets

CoinDesk reports that XRP Ledger version 3.3.0, released this week, includes six proposed amendments, headlined by "Confidential Transfers" for institutional users: it lets institutions encrypt balances and payment amounts on Multi-Purpose Tokens (MPTs) while keeping accounts and token types visible. The ledger can still cryptographically prove transactions balance without reading the underlying numbers — a privacy layer purpose-built for tokenized finance.

The feature targets XRPL's fast-growing real-world asset (RWA) market: onchain data aggregator RWA.xyz tracks roughly $1.38 billion of distributed RWA on XRPL, including $845.7M of RLUSD, $212.6M from Ondo, $116.1M from VERT Capital, $55.4M from Archax, and $11.6M from Societe Generale — leaving more than $530M of tracked tokenized assets outside RLUSD. Aviva Investors also launched a tokenized share class of its U.S. Dollar Liquidity Fund on the ledger last month. The other five amendments cover batching up to eight transactions (with an all-or-nothing mode), fee sponsorship, permission delegation, dynamic MPT property changes, and operational improvements that cut node memory use by 10%-15%. None are live yet: XRPL amendments require at least 80% support from trusted validators, held continuously for two weeks, before activation.

4. Strait of Hormuz Talks Near Breakthrough: Oil and Inflation Expectations Ease

Quoting an unnamed U.S. official, ANI reported that talks between Iran and Oman on the Strait of Hormuz have made progress and Washington expects an agreement "soon," which would allow standard commercial oil shipments to resume and restore exports disrupted by the roughly five-month-old conflict. The official said that "once the deal is announced to restore commercial shipping without impediments, the United States will lift the blockade of Iranian ports," while stressing that U.S. actions will remain "performance-based and tied to Iran's implementation of its commitments." Trump has separately voiced optimism that progress could lower fuel prices and stabilize one of the world's most critical maritime oil routes, saying the conflict could end "pretty soon."

The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly a fifth of global seaborne crude, so its status directly shapes oil prices and inflation expectations. After Friday's negative payrolls print and the market's growing bet that the Fed will hold in September, a confirmed Hormuz reopening would lower both energy costs and inflation pressure, reinforcing the "dovish" trade behind the recent strength in gold, Treasuries and risk assets. To be sure, Iran's Acting Defence Minister Ebn-e-Reza reiterated Thursday that the country's armed forces are "fully prepared" to counter any external threat — execution details and Iranian compliance remain open questions.

5. What to Watch

For investors, the next 24-48 hours hinge on three variables: whether the Hormuz deal is formally announced, which would directly move oil and shipping; whether Bitcoin can hold above $65,000 and ETF flows keep the "BTC/ETH twin-inflow" pattern; and the approaching BIP-110 activation window, where fork and replay-risk chatter could keep short-term sentiment choppy. Taken together, the "weak jobs + easing geopolitics + institutional crypto inflows" mix is building a fresh narrative for risk assets in mid-August — but Iranian compliance, the September FOMC, and fork-related technical risk remain three swords hanging over the market.
