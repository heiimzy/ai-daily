⚡ August 4 Tech Update: China's First Mandatory L3/L4 Autonomous Driving Safety Standard, Synopsys-Nvidia Chip-Design Agentic AI, Honor Robot Phone Set for Aug 12, AutoNavi World Model Runs 24 Hours

Tuesday (August 4) in the tech world is speeding from "software intelligence" toward "physical intelligence": from China's MIIT finalizing the country's first mandatory safety standard for L3/L4 autonomous driving, to Synopsys joining Nvidia to bring autonomous agents into chip design, and from the Honor Robot Phone locking its launch date to AutoNavi's world model pushing the 24-hour continuous-inference limit, one thread ties the day together — AI landing in the physical world. Here is the latest general tech roundup.

1. China publishes its first mandatory L3/L4 autonomous driving safety standard, effective July 1, 2027

According to The Paper (16:32 Beijing time, August 4) and Fujian Southeast Net, the mandatory national standard "Intelligent and Connected Vehicles — Safety Requirements for Automated Driving Systems" (GB 44721-2026), drafted and coordinated by China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT), has been officially approved and published by the State Administration for Market Regulation and the Standardization Administration, and is scheduled to take effect on July 1, 2027. It is China's first mandatory national standard covering L3 (conditionally automated) and L4 (highly automated) driving systems, a systematic upgrade from the 2024 recommended standard GB/T 44721-2024 that moves autonomous-driving oversight from "recommended" to "mandatory."

The standard applies to M-class (passenger) and N-class (cargo) vehicles fitted with L3/L4 systems, and excludes automated parking systems. Its core principle is that an automated driving system (ADS) must meet at least the level of a "qualified and attentive human driver", must not pose unreasonable risk to users or other road users, and must keep identifying whether it operates within its operational design conditions (ODC) while activated. In response to automakers that previously leaned on test mileage, city coverage and model size to market their capabilities, the standard introduces a Safety Case mechanism, requiring companies to systematically argue system safety in a "claim—argument—evidence" structure, and folding simulation, proving-ground and on-road testing into a unified verification framework — a shift from "proving it can drive" to "proving it is safe."

The standard also sets differentiated requirements for L3 and L4: L3 focuses on human-machine handover — if a fallback user fails to take over in time, the system must execute a Minimal Risk Maneuver (MRM) to bring the vehicle to a Minimal Risk Condition (MRC); L4 shifts the emphasis to the system's own risk-handling capability, which must not rely on remote assistance to complete all dynamic driving tasks. Under the rollout rules, newly type-approved models comply from the effective date, while already-approved models must comply from the 13th month after (roughly a one-year transition). As of May 2026, China had opened more than 57,000 kilometres of test roads for intelligent and connected vehicles and issued 237 national and industry standards. Momenta global solution chief architect Rao Qing said the move marks China's autonomous-driving industry formally entering a new, scalable phase "with safety as the bottom line and standards to rely on."

2. Synopsys × Nvidia: long-running autonomous agentic AI for chip design unveiled at DAC

According to Jiwet Network via Sina Weibo (13:38 Beijing time, August 4), at the 2026 DAC Chips to Systems Conference, Synopsys (Nasdaq: SNPS) announced a collaboration with Nvidia to accelerate agentic AI for engineering. Built on Nvidia's accelerated computing platform, powered by NVIDIA Nemotron and safeguarded through NVIDIA OpenShell Runtime, Synopsys developed fully autonomous, long-running agent capabilities for chip and electronic-system design, debuting at the DAC conference.

Synopsys said these capabilities are set to become a major productivity multiplier for R&D teams — surpassing traditional task-based agents by turning time-consuming chip verification and thermal-simulation workflows into automated insight generation, engineering-efficiency gains and system-performance optimization, moving "self-driving" chip design from concept into engineering practice.

3. Honor robot phone Robot Phone set for August 12 launch, nationwide reservations open

According to Phoenix Tech (August 4) and Sina Finance's Wealth Weekly (14:50 Beijing time, August 4), the world's first robot phone, the Honor Robot Phone, is officially set to launch on August 12, with two colors — "Galaxy Gray" and "Starlight Silver" — now open for nationwide reservations. The device first appeared at WAIC 2026 as the launch vehicle for Honor's industry-first companion-style multimodal agent operating system, Agentic OS, taking AI from the digital screen into the physical world.

On hardware, the Robot Phone integrates an industry-precision four-degree-of-freedom titanium mechanical gimbal (deploying in 0.8 seconds, supporting 360° tracking and 90°/180° intelligent movement at CIPA 5.5-level stabilization), a dual 200-megapixel camera setup with the in-house imaging chip "Yuguang H1," and a full ARRI color-science workflow; it runs on the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 with a 6.3-6.4-inch 1.5K four-key bezel-less flat display and an on-device large model (YOYO) capable of emotion sensing. Through the OpenClaw open platform, developers can drive physical actions with natural language, forming a complete "perception—reasoning—decision—execution" loop — widely seen as a flagship embodiment of China's "moment" in embodied-intelligence hardware.

4. AutoNavi's ABot-World-0: the world's first interactive world model with 24-hour continuous stable inference

According to Sina Tech (evening of August 3) and AI Base (10:10, August 4), Amap (AutoNavi), a subsidiary of Alibaba, announced that its in-house general-purpose interactive world model ABot-World-0 has completed a new round of upgrades — achieving 24-hour continuous stable inference on a single consumer-grade GPU, with no meaningful degradation in visual quality, physical consistency or interactive response. That makes it the world's first product to push interactive world-model inference from "minutes" to "days."

For a long time, mainstream world models could only sustain single continuous generations of roughly 30 seconds to 1 minute; as inference extended, they tended to suffer from visual collapse, object deformation and broken physics, rooted in error accumulation during autoregressive video generation. To solve this, AutoNavi proposed a new LongForcing long-horizon training paradigm, having the model continuously feed its own generated outputs back as subsequent inputs while constraining content to remain within a stable, reasonable world distribution — effectively mitigating error accumulation and distribution drift. ABot-World-0 has now been fully open-sourced and runs locally on a single consumer GPU, and its capabilities have been integrated into AutoNavi's general world-model platform ABot-World Studio, opening new technical paths for long-horizon interaction, simulation, robot training and digital twins.

5. AI drug discovery moves again: Phylo partners with Chugai to deploy agentic AI into discovery workflows

According to Phylo's official blog (August 4), AI biotech startup Phylo, Inc. announced a partnership with Japan's Chugai Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd. (a member of the Roche Group) to deploy Biomni Lab — Phylo's agentic AI platform for biomedical research — across Chugai's drug-discovery workflows. As an Integrated Biology Environment, Biomni Lab brings together hundreds of biomedical resources and best-in-class AI capabilities in one workspace, connecting to Chugai's proprietary research data inside its secure environment so that agents convert data into stronger hypotheses and better-supported decisions across single-cell analysis, human genetics, disease biology and target evaluation.

Phylo co-founder and CEO Kexin Huang noted that Chugai's scientists were among Biomni's earliest users and that the goal of the partnership is to bring the full platform to more researchers as biopharma goes "AI-native." Chugai head of Biological Technology Dept. Sohei Oyama said that as scientific data keeps growing in scale and complexity, scientists need tools purpose-built for drug-discovery challenges. Following Phylo's July 28 deal with Japan's Ono Pharmaceutical, this marks another agentic-AI deployment with a Japanese pharma, underscoring the accelerating commercialization of "agents + biopharma."
