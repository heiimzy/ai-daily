⚡ August 4 Finance Update: SpaceX First Earnings Since IPO Due Tonight After Near-Halving From Highs, European Stocks Hit Record High, Palantir Surges 16% Premarket, PBoC Announces 500B Yuan Reverse Repo

Global markets converged on a day packed with earnings and policy news on Tuesday (Aug 4). In U.S. premarket trading, two heavyweight reports from Palantir and Caterpillar fueled a surge of more than 600 points in Dow futures. European stocks touched an all-time high intraday, led by tech and upbeat results from Bayer and HSBC. All eyes are on SpaceX, which reports its first quarterly results since its IPO after Tuesday's close, just ahead of the largest lockup expiration in U.S. history on Aug 6. In China, the PBoC announced a 500 billion yuan outright reverse repo operation for Aug 5. Here is this round's finance roundup.

1. SpaceX Reports First Post-IPO Earnings Tonight: Shares Nearly Halved From Peak, Over $1T in Market Value Erased

According to Reuters and Cnyes (Aug 4), SpaceX (SPCX-US) will release its first quarterly report since its IPO after the U.S. close on Aug 4 (Q2 of fiscal 2026), with Elon Musk hosting his first earnings call as CEO of a public company. The stock closed Monday at $114.53, up 5.68% on the day, but still roughly 43% below its post-IPO record high of $201.80 set on June 16, and below its $135 IPO price. Measured from the peak, the company's market value has fallen by more than $1 trillion from roughly $3 trillion to about $1.4 trillion.

The market's focus is on three numbers: AI revenue, losses, and capital expenditure. Analysts expect SpaceX's Q2 revenue of roughly $6.67 billion to $6.93 billion, up about 64% year over year, with AI compute revenue roughly doubling quarter over quarter to about $1.88 billion (helped by a new cloud services agreement with Anthropic), Starlink broadband subscribers reaching about 12.5 million by quarter-end with revenue up over 50% to nearly $4 billion. The company remains in a heavy investment phase, with quarterly capex expected near $14 billion and free cash flow still under pressure. Meanwhile, the first tranche of about 911.5 million locked-up shares becomes tradable on Aug 6 (worth roughly $100 billion), followed by further tranches of about 300 million shares every 15-20 days — described by Caixin and Wall Street See as the largest lockup expiration in U.S. capital-markets history. Deutsche Bank defended SpaceX ahead of the print, maintaining a Buy rating and $255 price target, arguing that the space and Starlink businesses alone can support the current valuation, and that pricing the AI business at "zero" is overly punitive.

2. European Stocks Hit Record High: STOXX 600 Touches 656.85, Bayer and HSBC Beat Expectations

According to Reuters (as of Tuesday evening Beijing time), the pan-European STOXX 600 index touched an all-time high of 656.85 intraday, with tech stocks up about 1.7% leading gains as investors shifted focus from U.S.-Iran tensions to a busy earnings season; Germany's DAX, France's CAC 40 and Italy's FTSE MIB were up roughly 1.4%, 1.2% and 1.3% respectively intraday. Among single names, Germany's Bayer jumped about 4% after reporting a surprise 1.9% increase in quarterly operating profit; HSBC posted second-quarter pretax profit of $10.1 billion, beating estimates; while Lufthansa tumbled about 9% (Q2 adjusted operating profit roughly halved on higher fuel costs) and e-commerce group Zalando fell 13%-17% (after cutting its 2026 revenue and profit guidance). Oil rebounded about 1% on Tuesday, with Brent back near $86 a barrel, reflecting lingering doubts about how quickly U.S.-Iran diplomacy can deliver.

3. U.S. Premarket: Palantir Surges 16%, Caterpillar Tops $20B in Revenue, Dow Futures Gain Over 600 Points

According to Yahoo Finance and Investopedia (U.S. premarket, Aug 4), after Monday's broad rally that left the Dow at a record closing high, U.S. stock futures extended gains Tuesday morning: Dow futures rose as much as about 600 points (roughly 1.2%), Nasdaq-100 futures climbed about 1.1%, and S&P 500 futures added about 0.3%, keeping the benchmark within striking distance of its record.

The driver was two blowout reports. Palantir (PLTR) posted Q2 revenue of $1.94 billion, above the $1.80 billion consensus, with adjusted EPS of $0.41 also beating estimates, and raised its full-year revenue guidance — U.S. commercial revenue surged 149% year over year to $764 million, a quarter CEO Alex Karp called "otherworldly," powered by sovereign AI demand; after initially sliding in after-hours trade, the stock soared about 16% to $145.85 in premarket trading. Caterpillar (CAT) delivered Q2 revenue above $20 billion for the first time at $20.54 billion, beating on both profit and revenue, and raised its full-year revenue growth outlook while saying tariff costs would land at the lower end of its prior range — the stock rose about 9%-11% premarket, leading Dow components and lifting Dow futures. SpaceX and AMD are both due to report after tonight's close, and investors are waiting with bated breath.

4. PBoC Announces 500B Yuan Outright Reverse Repo for Aug 5; Polysilicon Futures Extend 'Anti-Involution' Rally

According to the PBoC announcement (via Sing Tao) (Aug 4, 5:06 p.m. Beijing time), the People's Bank of China announced that on Aug 5 it will conduct a 500 billion yuan outright reverse repo operation with a 3-month (92-day) tenor, using fixed-quantity, interest-rate bidding and multi-price auction, maturing on Nov 5, 2026. This continues the central bank's recent practice of announcing outright reverse repo operations early in the month, helping to offset liquidity drains from the August surge in certificate-of-deposit maturities (back above 3 trillion yuan) and heavy government bond issuance, signaling a relatively accommodative policy stance.

In commodities, polysilicon futures extended their "anti-involution" rally: after the main contract hit the daily limit across tenors on Aug 3, closing up 8.99% at 35,890 yuan/ton, it jumped again at Tuesday's open, rising over 4% intraday to a high of 36,550 yuan/ton. On the policy front, the State Administration for Market Regulation held a price-compliance guidance meeting for the entire PV supply chain in Yancheng, Jiangsu on July 31, requiring companies not to price below production costs, and the "Cost Accounting Model Guidelines for the PV Industry" took effect, upgrading anti-involution efforts from industry self-discipline to compliance supervision backed by enforcement; spot polysilicon prices rose 7.67% to 35,300 yuan/ton in response. Analysts caution that the market remains oversupplied with high inventories, and the move is largely policy-expectation driven, pending detailed enforcement rules.
