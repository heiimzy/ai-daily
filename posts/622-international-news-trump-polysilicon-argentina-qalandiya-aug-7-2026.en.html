⚡ Aug 7 International Politics: Trump Imposes 15% Polysilicon Tariff on China, Argentines Protest Property Law, Israel Raids Qalandiya

Over the past hour or two, three major developments have rippled across the world of international politics: U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order imposing a 15% tariff on imported polysilicon products and setting minimum import prices, targeting China's near-monopoly on the material; thousands in Argentina rallied in the capital over President Javier Milei's "Inviolability of Private Property" bill, prompting police to fire tear gas, rubber bullets and water cannons; and Israeli forces carried out a "large-scale" raid on the Qalandiya refugee camp in the occupied West Bank, wounding at least 51 Palestinians. Here is this round's summary.

1. Trump Signs Order: 15% Tariff and Minimum Import Prices on Polysilicon to Counter China

U.S. President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Thursday imposing a 15% tariff on imported products made from polysilicon, while also setting minimum import prices for polysilicon and related goods. Polysilicon is a critical raw material for semiconductors and solar panels, and China is by far the world's largest producer, holding a near-monopoly on production.

The move follows a national security investigation into overseas polysilicon production. Trump said the U.S. had for decades "allowed foreign firms to weaken United States producers in the polysilicon sector," noting that imports had slashed America's share of global polysilicon production from 50% in 2005 to under 2% in 2024. The measures are due to take effect in December, alongside incentives to boost domestic production.

China's embassy in Washington responded that the move "seriously disrupts" trade between the two countries and that Beijing will act to protect its companies, accusing Washington of "abusing state power to go after Chinese businesses." Analysts called the tariff the latest escalation in Washington's efforts to limit China's role in critical technology supply chains, following earlier U.S. restrictions on drone imports, while Beijing this week announced countermeasures including stricter export controls on drones. Hemlock Semiconductor and Wacker Chemie, the main U.S. producers, are widely expected to benefit.

2. Argentina: Thousands Protest Milei's "Private Property" Bill as Police Fire Tear Gas

On Thursday, thousands of Argentines rallied in Buenos Aires against President Javier Milei's "Inviolability of Private Property" bill. Organized by left-wing groups and trade unions, demonstrators chanted "the homeland is not for sale" outside the Senate before security forces dispersed them with tear gas, rubber bullets and water cannons.

According to EFE, the protest had been peaceful before police intervened, though AFP reported some demonstrators threw stones at officers. The bill aims to streamline eviction procedures, change rural land-use rules and limit government expropriation; an earlier draft also sought to raise the cap on foreign-owned land from 15% to 25%, but the government dropped that clause amid backlash. Milei's administration hopes to attract investment, while critics warn of "colonization, fragmentation, fracture and balkanization."

Research by the University of Buenos Aires and other institutions shows nearly 5% of Argentina's territory (an area roughly the size of England) is already foreign-owned, and in some provinces foreign ownership already exceeds the current 15% cap. "Defending our territory is crucial… it is our Argentina that we must defend; it is not for sale," one protester told EFE.

3. Israel Mounts "Large-Scale" Raid on Qalandiya Camp: at Least 51 Palestinians Wounded

Israeli forces launched a "large-scale" raid on the Qalandiya refugee camp in the occupied West Bank, with the Jerusalem Governorate confirming to Al Jazeera that at least 51 Palestinians were wounded. Troops demolished shops near the Qalandiya checkpoint on Thursday, imposed a curfew on the neighboring town of Kafr Aqab, and sealed off roads linking the area to Jerusalem.

The Palestine Red Crescent Society said 35 people were hospitalized after severe beatings and another six treated at the scene, with at least one person struck by a rubber-coated steel bullet and six treated for tear gas inhalation. PRCS said its ambulance crews had been blocked from reaching the wounded since late Wednesday night but still managed to evacuate several patients. Soldiers also fired stun grenades at journalists and ordered them to leave, while dozens of Palestinians, including women and children, were detained for field interrogations in homes used as makeshift holding sites. Separate raids were carried out on Jenin the same day.

Rights groups have recorded more than 11,000 Israeli military and settler attacks across the West Bank in the first half of this year, with over 2,300 Palestinians displaced due to settler violence and related restrictions. A UN-appointed commission concluded in June that Israel's authorities have repeatedly failed to prevent foreseeable settler attacks.
