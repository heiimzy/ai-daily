⚡ July 25 International Politics Roundup: First Night Without US Strikes in 2 Weeks, Iran Declares 'One-for-One' Retaliation, Houthi Missile Hits Saudi Jizan, Guterres Visits Syria

🕊️ First Night in Two Weeks Without US Strikes; Trump Says Iran Getting "Very Serious"

Saturday (July 25) marked the first day in 14 consecutive days without any official announcement of US military action against Iran. US Central Command issued no statement on new strikes, and there were no overnight reports of attacks across the region — a significant lull confirmed by the BBC, Reuters, and other major news organizations.

Speaking to reporters at the White House Oval Office on Friday afternoon, President Donald Trump said he had not yet made a final decision on launching major strikes against Iran. "No I haven't," Trump replied when asked whether he had decided.

"We are talking to them. I think they're being serious. I think ... they are being by far the most serious that we've seen them, but that doesn't mean we get there. We'll see what happens," Trump said. He added that the US remains "locked and loaded" and "ready to go."

Asked about his exit strategy, Trump outlined two options: "There's a military exit where we just keep going just the way we are, and we can even make it a heavier dose, and it's knocking out everything they have. Or there's a smarter strategy that you make a deal." He said the tipping point for a decision would be if Iran were close to developing a nuclear weapon.

The New York Times had earlier reported that Trump met with senior advisers to discuss intensifying military operations against Iran. The White House did not respond to requests for comment on whether any decisions were reached.

☠️ Iran Commander Declares "One-for-One" Retaliation Doctrine

Major General Ali Abdollahi, commander of Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters, issued a statement on Saturday declaring a drastic new "equation" for the battlefield.

"We now regard as a definitive and declared equation of the battlefield that for every proud Iranian citizen martyred, one American force will be sent to hell," Abdollahi said in a message published by state news agency IRNA. Addressing the United States directly, he added: "We have prepared free one-way tickets to hell for you."

The declaration marks the first time Tehran has formally articulated a systematic one-for-one retaliation mechanism. Iran's Health Ministry has reported 3,434 deaths since the US-Israel "Epic Fury" campaign began on February 28.

Meanwhile, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told the state-run Iran Daily that "there is no military solution" to the Yemen conflict and the Bab al-Mandab situation, calling for "dialogue." He confirmed ongoing exchanges of messages with Washington via mediators.

On the operational front, Iran's Revolutionary Guard continued strikes on Friday — targeting the Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait (claiming destruction of a "very large" US ammunition depot), a US Navy Fifth Fleet watchtower in Bahrain, and Al-Azraq Air Base in Jordan. Jordan reported intercepting 7 missiles and 6 drones.

🔥 Saudi Strikes Houthi Hodeida, Houthi Missile Hits Jizan — Red Sea Front Fully Activated

The Saudi-led coalition confirmed early Saturday that it had carried out "decisive and forceful" strikes against Houthi military targets in Yemen's Hodeida province — the first large-scale direct Saudi military action against the Houthis since the 2022 ceasefire.

AFP cited Yemeni security sources saying the strikes hit telecom facilities in Hodeida city and the nearby Kamaran Island. Multiple explosions illuminated the night sky near Hodeida port. The coalition clarified that the port itself was not targeted and remains operational.

The Houthis retaliated swiftly. Early Saturday, Ansarollah media announced on Telegram that a "Yemeni missile strike" had caused fires in the Saudi city of Jizan. Saudi civil defense authorities briefly issued "potential danger" alerts for Jazan and Yanbu governorates.

The escalation was triggered by Houthi attacks on two Saudi oil tankers — the Encelia (struck on the bow) and the Layla — in the Red Sea on Thursday. On Friday, another Saudi-flagged vessel, the NCC MASA, was hit while sailing in the Red Sea, sustaining minor hull damage. The Houthis had declared a "naval blockade" on Saudi Arabia on July 20.

UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said he was "deeply alarmed" by the renewed Houthi attacks on commercial vessels, warning they could escalate regional tensions and draw Yemen deeper into a wider conflict.

The Guardian commented that the Saudi-Houthi exchange of fire marks the spillover of the Middle East conflict onto a "new front line."

🚢 US Military Disables Second Tanker Attempting to Evade Iran Blockade

US Central Command announced Friday that American forces had fired on and disabled the tanker M/T Lavine in the Gulf of Oman for attempting to evade the US blockade of Iranian ports.

"We disabled the tanker after the crew attempted to run the blockade at least four times prior," Navy Captain Tim Hawkins, CENTCOM spokesman, told AFP. "The crew was repeatedly warned, didn't comply, and US forces on scene disabled the ship after firing into its engine room. The ship is no longer transiting to Iran."

This is the second commercial vessel disabled since the US reimposed its blockade on Iranian ports on July 14. The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) separately reported an "incident involving a tanker and military forces" in the Gulf of Oman.

Brent crude prices have been highly volatile — briefly surging past $100/barrel this week before retreating to ~$96 on Friday. Goldman Sachs has warned that Q4 oil prices could break $120 if both the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb remain constrained. The International Maritime Organization (IMO) reports approximately 500 vessels and 6,000 seafarers still stranded near the Strait of Hormuz.

🏛️ UN Chief Guterres Arrives in Syria for First Official Visit in 17 Years

United Nations Secretary-General Antonio Guterres arrived at Damascus International Airport on Saturday morning — marking the first official visit by a serving UN chief to Syria since 2009, before the country's devastating 14-year civil war.

Guterres was greeted by Syrian Foreign Minister Asaad al-Shaibani. During his visit, he will meet President Ahmed al-Sharaa — a former al-Qaeda commander who was under UN Security Council sanctions until last year. The visit is seen as a major diplomatic milestone for al-Sharaa, who has sought to rebuild international ties since assuming power after the 2024 ouster of Bashar al-Assad.

The UN chief will also inspect the UN Disengagement Observer Force (UNDOF) in the occupied Golan Heights, monitoring the ceasefire with Israel. UN spokesman Stephane Dujarric said Guterres plans to emphasize that Syria has an opportunity to build "a future that will be more stable, more inclusive, and more prosperous for all Syrians." He is expected to address Syria's new parliament on Monday.

🌊 South China Sea Heats Up: 5th Confrontation in 5 Days, PLA Live-Fire Drills

Tensions in the South China Sea continue to escalate. On Thursday (July 24), Chinese Coast Guard vessels again fired water cannons at Philippine government ships near Scarborough Shoal (Huangyan Island), with one Chinese vessel approaching within 7 meters of a Philippine ship. A CNN journalist was aboard the Philippine vessel and witnessed the water cannon attack firsthand.

This was the third confrontation in five days between Chinese and Philippine vessels in the South China Sea, and the second consecutive day of clashes at Scarborough Shoal. The Philippine Coast Guard reported three government vessels were hit by water cannons.

Meanwhile, Chinese maritime authorities conducted live-fire exercises from July 23-24 near Dongshan Island in Fujian province, in parts of the Taiwan Strait. Taiwan's Ministry of National Defense reported on July 25 that 29 PLA aircraft, 6 naval vessels, and 5 official ships were detected around Taiwan in the 24 hours from 6:00 AM July 24 to 6:00 AM July 25. Seventeen aircraft crossed the median line of the strait.

Taiwan's National Security Council Secretary General Wu Chao-hsieh condemned the exercises as an effort to "coerce Japan, Taiwan, and the Philippines simultaneously."

⚠️ Zelenskyy Warns of Massive Russian Attack Within 48 Hours

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy issued an urgent warning on X on Saturday, stating that intelligence indicates Russia has "readied missiles" for a massive attack "within the next 48 hours."

"Please take care of yourselves and pay attention to air raid alerts," Zelenskyy wrote. The warning follows a devastating Russian missile strike on the Kyiv region on Friday that killed at least 10 people and injured approximately 100 more. The strike hit an arms exhibition where Ukrainian defense industry representatives were present. Separate guided aerial bomb strikes in Slovyansk killed five more people. The Latvian consulate in Slovyansk was also damaged, prompting EU top diplomat Kaja Kallas to condemn "Moscow's utter disregard for international law."

Zelenskyy is scheduled to meet Trump in Washington next week to discuss peace proposals. Romania's air force shot down a second Russian drone entering its airspace in less than 24 hours on Saturday.

🤝 Diplomacy: US-UK to Form Hormuz Coalition; Netanyahu to Visit White House Tuesday

Axios and the BBC report that the United States and United Kingdom are planning a high-level conference in London next week to form an international coalition to protect commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and Chairman of the Joint Chiefs Dan Caine will attend. Washington is seeking allied contributions including minesweepers, naval vessels, and drones.

An Omani delegation has arrived in Tehran to discuss strait shipping management. Oman, which controls the strait's southern coastline, has long served as the principal intermediary between Tehran and Washington.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office confirmed he will travel to Washington on Tuesday (July 28) to meet Trump at the White House — Netanyahu's first visit since the Iran war began. Trump revealed that if he asked Israel to join a major military operation, they would agree "in two minutes."

Pakistani Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar met his Iranian counterpart Araghchi in Tehran on Friday to explore reviving US-Iran talks, with Reuters reporting that China is backing the mediation push. Araghchi appeared to downplay the need for mediators before the meeting, insisting the US must change its position.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi met Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov and Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi on the sidelines of the SCO foreign ministers meeting in Kyrgyzstan, calling the SCO a "force for stability" in the world.

The Wall Street Journal exclusively reported that Bahrain and Kuwait secretly sent warplanes to strike Iranian drone and missile storage facilities earlier this month — the first direct retaliation by these Gulf states. Kuwait's ambassador to the US subsequently denied the report; Bahrain's government spokesperson did not respond to requests for comment.

Air raid sirens sounded across Bahrain on Saturday. Australia raised its travel advisory for Bahrain to "Do Not Travel" due to the deteriorating security situation.
