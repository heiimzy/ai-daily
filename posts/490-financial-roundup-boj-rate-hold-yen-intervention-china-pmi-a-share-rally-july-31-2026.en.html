Bank of Japan Holds Policy Rate at 1.0%: Focus Shifts to a Possible Year-End Hike to 1.25%

The Bank of Japan kept its policy rate at 1.0% at the July 30-31 monetary policy meeting, in line with expectations. It was the first hold since June's 25-basis-point hike, which took the rate to a 31-year high and marked the first time at 1% since 1995. A Reuters survey shows most analysts expect the BOJ to raise rates again to 1.25% before year-end, and heading into the meeting some speculated that hawkish board member Hajime Takata could dissent by proposing a 25bp increase.

The decision was accompanied by the quarterly Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices. The key question for markets is Governor Kazuo Ueda's tone at the press conference: with the yen still under pressure and Tokyo inflation accelerating toward the 2% target, the question is whether Ueda can signal hawkishness firmly enough to discourage yen bears without rattling a fragile recovery. Heading into the meeting, markets priced roughly 24% odds of an October hike and 68% for December.

Nikkei Confirms Rare US-Japan Coordinated Yen Intervention: Yen Surges 3.3%, US Treasury Conducts 'Rate Check'

Japan's Nikkei reported on July 31 that the yen's sharp rally in the New York session on July 30 — from around 162.80 to as low as 157.98 per dollar, a gain of more than 3.3% intraday and the biggest one-day jump since December 2023 — was driven by the Japanese government and the Bank of Japan buying yen and selling dollars. Citi monitoring flagged roughly $8.1 billion in USD/JPY sell orders triggered within a 10-minute window.

What made the move unusual was the US involvement: the Nikkei reported that US monetary authorities conducted a "rate check" — a preliminary step of asking major banks for quotes before intervening — with the New York Fed reportedly requesting quotes from several banks on instructions from the US Treasury. Simultaneous engagement by US and Japanese authorities is extremely rare. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent had earlier said the yen appears "severely undervalued." It was Japan's first intervention since April-May 2024, when Tokyo spent a record 11.73 trillion yen (about $73 billion) defending the currency.

The yen's strength rippled through Asian FX: the Korean won briefly broke below 1,420 per dollar in overnight trading, touching 1,419.0 — its strongest level in nine months. In Asian trading Friday, USD/JPY rebounded to around 160.5-160.8. Meanwhile spot gold briefly broke above $4,100 an ounce, putting it on track for its first monthly gain since February.

China's July Manufacturing PMI Unexpectedly Contracts to 49.2: New Orders at 38-Month Low, Easing Bets Firm

China's official manufacturing purchasing managers' index fell to 49.2 in July, the National Bureau of Statistics said on July 31 — below June's 50.3, below the 50-point expansion threshold, and missing economists' median forecast of 50.0. It was the first contraction since February, ending a four-month run at or above 50. The headline was dragged down by the new orders sub-index, which fell to 48.5, the lowest in 38 months, as domestic orders slumped and typhoons disrupted production while the export front-loading momentum (June shipments to the US up 14%, overall exports surging 27%) began to unwind.

Weakness spread well beyond manufacturing: the construction PMI slumped to a record low of 47.0, the services gauge hit its weakest reading since the initial Covid-19 lockdowns, and the composite PMI dropped to 49.3, the lowest since the pandemic ended in 2022. A day earlier, the Politburo's mid-year meeting pledged more proactive fiscal policy and moderately loose monetary policy, promised to roll out "incremental policies," and stressed improving the "resilience and confidence" of capital markets. Analysts say the disappointing PMI will sharpen expectations for easing, though the readout stopped short of concrete policy steps.

A-Shares Stage Midday Rebound: ChiNext Jumps 5.04%, STAR 50 +5.91%, Over 4,500 Stocks Rise

Chinese stocks rallied hard on the morning of July 31. At the midday close, the Shanghai Composite gained 0.76% to 3,833.65, the Shenzhen Component rose 3.16% to 13,705.42, the ChiNext surged 5.04% to 3,408.15, and the STAR 50 jumped 5.91% to 1,682.27. Half-day turnover on the two main exchanges reached 1.79 trillion yuan, up about 332 billion yuan from the previous session, with more than 4,500 stocks advancing.

The AI chain led the charge: AI-application names including Chinese Online, Rongxin Culture, BlueFocus and Yidian Tianxia hit their 20cm daily limits; computing-power leasing names such as Meiliyun, Yunsai Zhilian, Hongjing Technology and Litong Electronics also sealed limit-up, along with semiconductor names Shengjing Micro and Shengjian Technology and robotics plays Zhongda Lide and Fenglong. Banks lagged, with Agricultural Bank of China down over 4%. The rebound was catalyzed by overnight Wall Street strength — the Nasdaq jumped 2.78%, Microsoft soared 15.5% for its best day in 18 years, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index surged more than 8% to end a five-day losing streak — combined with the "bad news priced in" reaction to the PMI and policy warmth from the Politburo.

Strategists say the oversold technical rebound in A-shares could extend if Beijing accelerates fiscal and monetary incremental measures; meanwhile, doubts over whether AI capex will translate into revenue, elevated US-Japan rate differentials, and oil-price pressure from geopolitical conflict remain key risks for risk assets globally.
