⚡ Aug 10 Finance Update: Bitcoin Tops $65K, BTC ETFs Pull in $854M, Asian Stocks Rally, Gold Up 7% Weekly, ChiNext Drops 2%

Global financial markets delivered several key moves at midday on August 10: Bitcoin broke above $65,000 with US inflation data due this week set to decide the next direction; spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $854M in net inflows last week, with BlackRock's IBIT taking $694M; Friday's soft US nonfarm payrolls cooled rate-hike expectations, sending Japan, Korea and Taiwan stocks higher across Asia — Korean chip stocks surged so sharply that the market briefly triggered a circuit breaker; A-shares diverged with the ChiNext index down 2% while healthcare and consumer names gained; and gold posted a weekly gain of more than 7% as safe-haven demand met rate-cut trades. Details below; all source publication times have been verified.

1. Bitcoin Tops $65,000: US Inflation Data This Week Is the Key Test

According to CoinDesk (published around 12:26 Beijing time, Aug 10), Bitcoin has broken above the $65,000 level, with key US inflation data due this week as markets closely watch its implications for the Federal Reserve's policy path. The report noted that almost every major cryptocurrency was green on the week except XRP, with BTC, ETH and BNB each up nearly 3% while global stocks traded near record highs — a broad risk-on backdrop.

According to Futu (12:39 Beijing time, Aug 10), the US inflation data due this week is seen as the decisive near-term variable: if inflation keeps cooling, it would reinforce rate-cut trades and support risk assets including crypto; if it rebounds, it could once again roil global markets (Sources: CoinDesk, Futu).

2. Spot Bitcoin ETFs Drew $854M Last Week; BlackRock's IBIT Took $694M

According to PANews (Aug 10, about 2 hours ago), SoSoValue data shows that during last week's trading days (US Eastern time Aug 3-7), spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded net inflows of $854 million. The top performer was BlackRock's IBIT with weekly net inflows of $694 million, bringing its all-time cumulative net inflows to $61.17 billion; Fidelity's FBTC followed with $116 million in weekly inflows and $10.04 billion cumulative.

Meanwhile, spot Ethereum ETFs saw $245 million in net inflows last week, marking five consecutive weeks of inflows; BlackRock's ETHA added $203 million (cumulative $11.65 billion) and Fidelity's FETH added $24.15 million (cumulative $2.12 billion). Sustained institutional inflows show major money managers continue to scale up digital asset exposure (Source: PANews).

3. Asian Stocks Rally: Soft Payrolls Cool Rate-Hike Bets, Japan/Korea/Taiwan Lead

According to Asia TV News (12:18 Beijing time, Aug 10), Friday's weak US nonfarm payrolls data cooled market expectations of further Fed rate hikes, lifting Asia-Pacific equities in early trade. Per KLSE Screener (12:55 Beijing time, Aug 10), Japanese and Taiwanese stocks led Asia higher while Malaysia slipped against the trend; Japan's Nikkei rose about 2% earlier, led by AI-related names.

Per Invezz (12:56 Beijing time, Aug 10), South Korea's KOSPI surged as Asian markets rallied after the US jobs shock rattled Fed bets; according to Sohu (12:07 Beijing time, Aug 10) citing market reports, Korean chip giants opened sharply higher and the Korean market briefly triggered a circuit breaker, underscoring a rapid surge in sentiment toward the semiconductor sector (Sources: Asia TV News, KLSE Screener, Invezz, Sohu).

4. A-Shares Diverge at Midday: ChiNext Down 2%, Healthcare and Consumer Gain

According to Eastmoney's midday market report (12:10 Beijing time, Aug 10), A-shares showed structural divergence at the lunch break: the ChiNext index fell 2% while healthcare services and consumer sectors strengthened. Against a broadly higher Asia-Pacific tape, A-share growth names saw profit-taking as funds rotated into defensive and consumer assets — a notable style rebalancing at the start of the week (Source: Eastmoney).

5. Gold Up More Than 7% Weekly: Safe-Haven Demand Meets Rate-Cut Trades, CPI Is the Final Test

According to Sina Finance (11:57 Beijing time, Aug 10), gold rose more than 7% last week, and multiple institutions have issued fresh forecasts with a strongly bullish tone. Per Fx678 (12:14 Beijing time, Aug 10), with US CPI due in just two days, markets are weighing whether silver can extend its rally; per FX168 (11:20 Beijing time, Aug 10), shifting Fed policy expectations and yen volatility have made gold the last line of defense.

All told, this precious-metals rally is driven by two forces: "rate-cut trades" and "geopolitical safe-haven demand." Weak payrolls strengthened bets on Fed easing, and lower real-rate expectations directly support zero-yield gold, while geopolitical uncertainty amplified safe-haven buying. If this week's US inflation data keeps cooling, precious metals could gain another catalyst (Sources: Sina Finance, Fx678, FX168).

From Bitcoin breaking $65,000 and ETFs pulling in $854M weekly, to Asian stocks rallying and gold gaining 7%+ weekly, and A-shares diverging — this hour's finance news revolves around one thread: "soft payrolls → cooling rate-hike bets → risk-asset repricing." Crypto, precious metals and equities are all pricing in easing expectations in tandem, and this week's US CPI report will be the key checkpoint to validate that logic.
