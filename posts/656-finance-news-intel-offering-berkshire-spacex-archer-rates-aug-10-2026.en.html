⚡ Aug 10 Finance Update: Intel to Raise $15B in Stock Offering, Berkshire Buybacks Hit $4.5B, SpaceX Back Near IPO Price, Boeing Swaps Units for Archer Stake, Fed 'Higher for Longer'

Monday's premarket signals are dense and clear: Intel announced a $15 billion common stock offering to fund surging AI compute demand; Berkshire Hathaway's Q2 operating earnings rose 16% with $4.5 billion in buybacks, as the new CEO finally started deploying the giant cash hoard; SpaceX shares rebounded to near their $135 IPO price; Boeing sold three subsidiaries to eVTOL upstart Archer in exchange for a 19.75% stake; and the Fed's 'higher for longer' narrative firmed up as September or October rate-hike odds climbed. All source publication times were verified within the two-hour window.

1. Intel Announces $15B Common Stock Offering to Fuel AI Demand

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 12:49 GMT; updated 13:15 GMT), Intel announced a $15 billion common stock offering on Monday to support skyrocketing customer demand for AI compute (Source: CNBC). The chipmaker highlighted physical AI, purpose-built silicon and advanced packaging among its major growth opportunities, saying proceeds will support corporate needs including capital expenditures and working capital; underwriters also get a 30-day option to buy up to an additional $2.25 billion in common stock.

Intel's confidence rests on strong fundamentals: last month it posted its fastest revenue growth in nearly 15 years and raised capex guidance to $20 billion, with CFO David Zinsner saying most of the spending would support factory tooling and flagging a "meaningful increase" in 2027. Goldman Sachs estimates tech giants' capex is on track to hit $765 billion this year and $1.2 trillion in 2027 — Intel's raise is the latest footnote in the AI infrastructure supercycle.

Market sentiment has clearly warmed: Intel shares are up 175% in 2026 and have roughly quintupled over the past year, lifted by the AI buildout and the U.S. government's 10% equity stake aimed at bolstering domestic chip manufacturing. The stock traded slightly lower premarket after the offering was announced.

2. Berkshire Q2 Operating Earnings +16%, $4.5B Buybacks: The New CEO Finally Deploys Cash

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 9:07 AM EDT; updated ~13:32 GMT), Berkshire Hathaway's Class B shares rose about 2% in morning trading after the conglomerate reported Q2 operating earnings up 16% year over year, fueled by strength in energy, railroad and other businesses that more than offset weaker insurance underwriting (Source: CNBC).

What cheered Wall Street even more was the turn in capital deployment: Berkshire repurchased about $4.5 billion of its own shares in the quarter, a sharp acceleration from just $235 million in Q1; it became a net buyer of stocks for the first time in 15 quarters, buying nearly $20 billion more equities than it sold; and its cash pile fell to $365.5 billion at the end of June from a record $397.4 billion three months earlier. Analysts note that new CEO Greg Abel is actively putting Buffett's hoard to work — TD Cowen highlighted that Berkshire "finally deployed cash," with its equity portfolio expanding led by purchases of Alphabet shares, which are now among its five largest holdings (the company disclosed a $10 billion investment in the Google parent earlier this year to help fund its AI development).

"My broader conclusion is that Berkshire's earnings beat was less about the quarter itself and more about the early evidence that Greg Abel is actively putting Buffett's cash hoard to work while maintaining Berkshire's long-standing discipline around capital allocation." — Bill Stone, CIO, The Glenview Trust Company

Not everyone is convinced: Michael Burry, the investor best known for his wager against the housing market before the financial crisis, worries Abel may feel pressure to deploy Berkshire's enormous cash pile and deliver returns over a shorter horizon rather than wait for the kind of unusually attractive opportunities Buffett favors — Burry says he does not find Berkshire an attractive investment going forward. UBS's Brian Meredith remains positive, calling Berkshire "an attractive defensive investment supported by a strong balance sheet."

3. SpaceX Stock Rebounds to Near Its $135 IPO Price; Deutsche Bank Bullish on $100B Target

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 13:34 GMT; updated 13:36 GMT), shares of Elon Musk's SpaceX rose Monday, bringing the stock back around its $135 IPO price, briefly hitting the benchmark in early trading after a rocky post-IPO ride that saw it close as low as $108.27 just days ago (Source: CNBC).

Last week the rocket maker delivered its first earnings report since its historic Nasdaq debut in June: Q2 revenue came in at $7.81 billion, above the $6.93 billion analysts expected. CFO Bret Johnsen said on the call that the company is on pace to reach $100 billion in annualized recurring revenue by the end of the year; Deutsche Bank analysts said Monday that target is "likely very achievable," driven mainly by the neocloud business and the acquisition of AI coding company Cursor. Citi revised its 2026 and 2027 forecasts higher over the weekend, reiterating a buy with a $200 price target and plans to ratably adjust toward the $900+ long-term valuation it outlined at initiation as Starship milestones are hit. Wolfe Research, while praising the "big beats," cautioned investors not to mistake management aspirations for the most likely outcomes.

4. Boeing Sells Three Subsidiaries to Archer for a 19.75% Stake; Archer Surges 16% Premarket

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 12:00 GMT; updated 13:13 GMT), Boeing is selling Wisk Aero, SkyGrid and Insitu to electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) company Archer Aviation in exchange for a 19.75% stake in Archer's Class A shares, plus options to buy more shares over the next four years (Source: CNBC). On the news, Archer shares surged 16% in premarket trading while Boeing slipped 0.2%.

The three units each bring distinct capabilities: Wisk Aero has been developing an autonomous eVTOL, SkyGrid builds air traffic management systems for air taxis, and Insitu manufactures high-altitude drones used worldwide, including by the U.S. Navy. For Boeing, the deal continues CEO Kelly Ortberg's streamlining push — since taking over in August 2024 he has repeatedly said Boeing must focus on its three primary businesses: commercial airplanes, defense and space. For Archer, the transaction strengthens its eVTOL portfolio and network, with Insitu extending its military business; Archer is targeting commercial eVTOL passenger flights by the end of 2026 or early 2027. CEO Adam Goldstein called it "the next big step forward in becoming a diversified platform, rapidly growing our revenue base and bringing scale to our business."

5. Fed 'Higher for Longer': July CPI Due Wednesday, September or October Hike Odds Climb

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 12:41 GMT), even as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh moves to curtail so-called forward guidance, investors largely expect interest rates to rise in the months ahead (Source: CNBC). The central bank has held rates steady all year — a 9-3 majority voted last month to keep the benchmark in a 3.5%-3.75% range — with inflation still running well above the 2% target.

Despite a weaker-than-expected July jobs report, July CPI due Wednesday is expected to show another modest increase, keeping a September hike "firmly in play," according to an Aug. 7 note from Bank of America Global Research. CME FedWatch pricing indicates the Fed could still move as soon as September, but the odds are higher for an October move. Longer-dated Treasury yields have climbed overall since Warsh succeeded Jerome Powell on May 22, pushing 30-year fixed mortgage rates higher.

For consumers, "higher for longer" means rising borrowing costs on mortgages, car loans and credit card debt. Economist Mark Hamrick notes that "some households have been leaning on borrowing to plug the gap between high prices and their own financial resources." He also points to the constructive side: higher rates can slow spending and borrowing, cool the economy and ease inflationary pressure — "calling higher-for-longer a mixed blessing might be a stretch, but there are constructive aspects."
