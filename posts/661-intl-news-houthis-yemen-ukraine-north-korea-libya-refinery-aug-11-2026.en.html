⚡ Aug 11 World Update: Houthis Missile Yemeni Cities, Zelensky Mocks Putin's North Korea Reliance, Libya's Largest Refinery Hit by Drone, Trump's Secret Flight from Turkey

Several major international stories broke on the afternoon of August 11: Yemen's Houthis launched ballistic missile attacks on the Red Sea port of al-Makha (Mocha) and residential areas of central Marib, a day after Houthi missiles killed at least seven people and wounded 30 in al-Makha; Zelensky mocked Putin's reliance on North Korea in his nightly address, saying "for the first time in its history, Russia cannot wage war without reinforcements from North Korea," while a Ukrainian drone strike 600 miles deep inside Russia killed 13 people; Libya's largest refinery at Zawiya caught fire after a drone attack, with the National Oil Corporation warning it may declare force majeure; and the Washington Post reported Trump was secretly moved to a secondary military plane in Turkey after the NATO summit over an Iranian assassination threat. Details below; all source publication times verified.

1. Houthis Launch Ballistic Missile Attacks on Two Yemeni Cities: Red Sea Port and Marib

According to Al Jazeera (06:05 GMT on Aug 11), the armed forces of Yemen's internationally recognized government accused the Houthis of launching another round of ballistic missile attacks on the Red Sea port city of al-Makha and on residential areas in the central city of Marib, without stating whether Monday's attacks caused casualties. The attacks came a day after Houthi missiles killed at least seven people and wounded 30 others in al-Makha, causing extensive damage at the city's port area.

Elsewhere in Yemen on Monday, a Houthi drone strike killed two Yemeni government soldiers and wounded seven others in the south-central province of Shabwa, according to local media. Yemeni forces, in a series of posts on X, said they targeted Houthi rebels and their sites in eastern al-Jawf province, northwestern Saada province and the al-Tibas area of central Marib province. Al Jazeera noted the tit-for-tat attacks are the deadliest in years in Yemen's civil war and mark a major escalation in a conflict reignited by the war launched by the United States and Israel against Iran on February 28. The Iran-backed Houthis captured Sanaa in September 2014; a Saudi-led coalition intervened in March 2015 on the side of the government. A truce in April 2022 ended major fighting, but violence reignited on July 13 when a Saudi air strike hit the Sanaa airport runway, preventing an Iranian flight carrying a high-ranking Houthi delegation from landing.

2. Zelensky Mocks Putin's Reliance on North Korea: Deadly Strike 600 Miles Inside Russia

According to The Independent (Ukraine-Russia war live blog, updated 13:29–14:01 Beijing time on Aug 11), Zelensky mocked Putin's reliance on ally North Korea in his nightly video address, saying Russia was preparing to deploy an additional North Korean contingent on its territory and had received additional ballistic missiles from North Korea. "For the first time in its history, Russia cannot wage war without reinforcements from North Korea," he said. He also warned countries in Asia that North Korea will present a "greater danger" to Japan, South Korea and the Philippines as it gains military experience and corrects shortcomings in the process.

Meanwhile, a Ukrainian drone attack on Monday hit targets over 600 miles (about 965 km) deep inside Russia, striking industrial and civilian infrastructure in Nizhnekamsk and the Taneco oil refinery in the Tatarstan region, killing at least 13 people, including seven Uzbek citizens and one child. Nizhnekamsk Mayor Radmir Belayev declared a period of mourning in a post on Telegram. The BBC (22:34 Beijing time on Aug 10) earlier reported this was one of the deadliest strikes Ukraine has carried out inside Russia since the invasion began.

3. Libya's Largest Refinery Catches Fire After Drone Attack: NOC Warns of Force Majeure

According to Al Jazeera (04:37 GMT on Aug 11), firefighters in Libya are battling a massive blaze at the Zawiya refinery following a drone attack, with the country's National Oil Corporation (NOC) warning it may declare a force majeure and shut down operations if attacks continue. Libya's Ambulance and Emergency Service said on Tuesday there were no "serious injuries," with most patients treated for smoke inhalation.

Footage verified by Al Jazeera showed huge flames and thick black smoke billowing over the facility — the largest operating refinery in Libya, with a refining capacity of 120,000 barrels per day, located about 40km (25 miles) west of Tripoli. The NOC said the fire broke out on Monday evening at tank number 402-T, belonging to the Brega Oil Company, which contained about 4.5 million litres (1.2 million gallons) of gasoline and was "directly targeted, resulting in a severe fire before the tank completely collapsed." The NOC said the attack followed drone strikes on a water desalination plant at the refinery on Sunday and on a naphtha reservoir on Saturday. It declared a "maximum emergency" in the region and urged authorities to investigate. There was no immediate claim of responsibility. Hours later, the NOC said the refinery was "still being subject to sabotage attacks," with a drone targeting an oil blending and filling plant operated by the Zawiya Oil Refining Company.

4. Report: Trump Secretly Switched Planes in Turkey to Evade an Iranian Assassination Threat

According to a Washington Post exclusive (05:53 GMT on Aug 11; followed up by The Independent and AP at 13:42 Beijing time), Trump was secretly transported away from last month's NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey, using a secondary military plane while an elaborate deception made it seem he was aboard Air Force One. The report says the covert movement was triggered by an assassination threat from Iran targeting Trump. Reporters and several White House personnel were left believing they were traveling on the same aircraft as the president as his return journey to Washington commenced.

The Post based its account on reviewed documentation, a US official aware of the operation, and another individual familiar with the president's travel arrangements. Trump had originally arrived in Turkey on a newly retrofitted red, white and navy blue jet presented as a gift from Qatar; before leaving, he announced he would complete the first leg home on an older, baby-blue Air Force One aircraft, and was then flown to the Mildenhall air base in the UK. The White House and Secret Service kept details under wraps, underscoring the security pressure on America's top leader amid intense US-Iran tensions. Meanwhile, according to AP (12:10 Beijing time on Aug 11), with other strategies to end the war with Iran fizzling, the Trump administration is pivoting back to sanctions pressure on Tehran.
