⚡ Aug 8 Finance News: U.S. Sheds 23,000 Jobs in Surprise July Report, S&P 500 Hits Record High, Gold Tops $4,400, White House Renews Push to Fire Fed's Cook

Over the past hour or two, global markets were ignited by a surprisingly "bad" data point: the U.S. economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 nonfarm payroll jobs in July, far below the roughly 80,000 gain analysts had expected — and investors celebrated, betting the Federal Reserve will now back away from a September rate hike. The S&P 500 closed at a record high, while gold futures blew past $4,400 per ounce. Meanwhile, two big stories landed in Washington: the White House renewed its effort to remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook after a Supreme Court ruling, and the Senate passed a sweeping Russia energy sanctions bill by 86-11. Here is the breakdown, based on sources whose publication times were verified within the past 1-2 hours.

1. July Jobs Report Turns Negative: -23,000 Payrolls, May-June Revised Down by 103,000

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday that nonfarm payrolls fell by 23,000 in July, versus the consensus forecast of roughly +80,000. It was a rare negative print — and worse underneath: May and June were revised down by a combined 103,000, with June cut to just +20,000. Average monthly job growth over the past three months is now near 20,000, essentially flat.

The weakness was concentrated in government: local government employment fell by roughly 50,000, largely in education, with retail also cutting jobs across wholesale clubs, hypermarkets, gas stations and general merchandise stores. The private sector added about 30,000 jobs. Despite the headline loss, the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%, the lowest in two years — not because more people found work, but because labor force participation dropped to 61.4%, the lowest since 2021. Average hourly earnings rose 3.2% year over year (below the 3.5% expected), with private nonfarm pay at $37.62.

2. Market Frenzy: S&P 500 Record Close, Gold Breaks $4,400

Weak jobs data was treated as a gift: investors bet the Fed will skip a September hike. The S&P 500 closed at a record high Friday with its best week since April; the Dow and Nasdaq also posted strong weekly gains. Rate futures showed September hike odds tumbling from 57% before the report to 43.9% after, while Polymarket traders put September hike odds at roughly 34%.

Safe havens shone even brighter: gold futures jumped about 3% on the day, breaking above $4,400/oz, after trading in a $4,067-$4,359 range through early August. The 10-year Treasury yield fell toward 4.62% and the dollar dropped about 0.4%. Polymarket traders now price a 57% chance gold touches $4,500 in August; J.P. Morgan and Goldman Sachs 2026 targets range from $4,500 to as high as $6,300. In commodities, Brent crude rose roughly $1 in late U.S. trading Friday on persistent uncertainty over whether the Iran war will end and the Strait of Hormuz will reopen.

3. White House Renews Push to Fire Fed Governor Lisa Cook

The other headline of the evening was Fed personnel: media reported that the Trump administration has revived its effort to remove Fed Governor Lisa Cook, the first Black woman to serve on the Fed's board, nominated by Biden in 2022. A letter dated August 5 and signed by White House Deputy Chief of Staff Dan Scavino accused Cook of "mortgage fraud" — claiming she listed two homes, one in Georgia and one in Michigan, as her primary residence to obtain favorable mortgage rates — and warned the alleged crime is punishable by up to 30 years in prison, demanding a response within three weeks.

In June, the Supreme Court blocked Trump's earlier attempt to fire Cook, though it cleared the way for removing heads of other independent agencies. Cook's lawyer Abbe D. Lowell said there is "no valid cause" for removal and that they will challenge the effort again. Notably, on August 5 Cook said in Alaska that inflation is "too high" and that she is "prepared to act" — signaling openness to rate hikes, directly clashing with Trump's demand for "the lowest interest rate in the world." No president has attempted to remove a Fed governor since the central bank's founding in 1913; Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, in office since May, has not delivered the rate cuts Trump wants, holding rates for a fifth straight meeting at 3.50%-3.75%.

4. Senate Passes Sweeping Russia Energy Sanctions Bill 86-11

On Capitol Hill, the Senate passed a comprehensive Russia energy sanctions bill Friday by 86 votes to 11. The bill, long championed by the late Sen. Lindsey Graham, authorizes the president to sanction the five largest buyers of Russian energy and the five countries doing the most to help Russia circumvent energy sanctions, with tariffs of up to 100% on Russian energy imports; buyer lists are reassessed every 180 days. It also includes exemptions for countries whose natural gas imports account for less than 15% of Russian gas exports and that take steps to reduce purchases, and it extends the Iran sanctions framework through 2031.

The House is in recess and is not expected to vote until September. China and India, major buyers of Russian crude, could face strain in trade relations. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky welcomed the bill, saying it helps "increase pressure on the aggressor."

5. Outlook: September Hike Odds Cool, Inflation Data Takes Center Stage

After the payrolls report, markets now turn to next week's July CPI print — the first test of whether the "easing trade" can hold. Before the Fed's September 15-16 meeting, one more jobs report and one more CPI reading are due. Nancy Vanden Houten, lead economist at Oxford Economics, said expectations for rate hikes have been "scaled back" since last month's decision, while new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is deliberately offering little forward guidance, making September a genuine coin flip. For investors, the "weak jobs + high inflation + political pressure on the Fed" combination points to wider swings in asset prices in the weeks ahead: gold bulls eye $4,500, equity bulls watch hike odds fall further, and energy prices and the fate of the Strait of Hormuz remain the biggest wildcard hanging over inflation.
