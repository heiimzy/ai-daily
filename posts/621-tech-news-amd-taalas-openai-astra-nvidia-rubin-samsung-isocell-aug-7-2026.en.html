⚡ Aug 7 Tech Briefing: AMD to Acquire Taalas for AI Inference, OpenAI Reportedly to Launch Astra Next Week, Nvidia Weighs Lower HBM Config for Rubin Ultra, OpenAI's First Hardware Priced at $300-400

In the past hour or two, five stories dominated the tech world: AMD announced plans to acquire AI inference chip startup Taalas, which can "etch" any AI model directly into custom silicon; OpenAI is reportedly set to unveil a brand-new pretrained model called Astra (codename mewfour) as early as next week, said to be the largest model trained since GPT-4.5; Nvidia is reportedly weighing reduced memory configurations for its next-gen Rubin Ultra GPU amid an HBM shortage; Samsung unveiled the 200MP ISOCELL HPC sensor with industry-first native 16-bit RAW output; and Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reports OpenAI's first AI hardware is a donut-shaped smart speaker priced at $300-400. Here is this round's tech briefing.

1. AMD announces plan to acquire Taalas: turning AI models into custom chips

AMD today (Aug 7) announced plans to acquire Taalas, an AI inference chip startup. Taalas designs accelerators for AI inference with a core idea of customizing chips for a single AI model — deliberately sacrificing generality in exchange for lower cost and higher output speed.

On the technical side, the AI inference chip Taalas has demonstrated is based on Meta's Llama 3.1 model, built on TSMC's mature (older) process node with high-speed SRAM on the chip itself. This approach delivers faster inference — for specific models, output speed can be thousands of times faster than traditional GPUs at lower cost; the trade-off is that the chip is bound to one AI model and cannot be upgraded later. Taalas CEO Ljubisa Bajic said on the company's website that it has built a "platform that turns any AI model into custom silicon", and that once it receives a previously unsupported model, it can bring it to hardware in just two months.

AMD did not disclose the deal size in the announcement. According to public records, since its founding in 2023, Taalas has raised a total of $219 million (about RMB 1.481 billion) in venture capital. The acquisition is widely seen as a significant move by AMD to strengthen its position against Nvidia in the AI inference race.

2. OpenAI reportedly to launch Astra next week: the largest model trained since GPT-4.5

Tipster @synthwavedd posted on X yesterday (Aug 6), claiming that OpenAI targets launching the Astra model next week, with the internal codename mewfour. According to the tipster, it is a brand-new pretrained model and the largest model OpenAI has trained since GPT-4.5.

IT Home reported on Aug 1 that an internal version of Astra had already solved 10 important open math problems; at Sol API rates, the total token cost of finding solutions to those problems was about $2,000 (about RMB 13,524). On capabilities, Bleeping Computer reports the model can organize and coordinate AI agents, suiting long-horizon, high-difficulty tasks, targeting stronger reasoning and collaborative workflows. If it ships as expected next week, Astra would become another flagship foundation model from OpenAI beyond the GPT-5.6 family.

3. Nvidia reportedly weighing lower Rubin Ultra GPU configs as HBM shortage bites

Tech publication The Information reported yesterday (Aug 6) that Nvidia is considering reducing the Rubin Ultra GPU configuration to cope with an HBM memory supply shortage. Citing three people familiar with the matter, the report says Nvidia has tested at least three lower-HBM Rubin Ultra GPU configurations over the past several weeks, each requiring less HBM capacity than the originally announced spec. Cutting HBM capacity inevitably hurts overall performance, though the report says Nvidia is trying to offset the performance impact through other means.

IT Home reported on Aug 4 that TrendForce analysis indicated Nvidia is evaluating alternatives beyond the originally planned 12Hi HBM4E, with possible downgrade paths from HBM4E to HBM4, and from 12Hi HBM to 8Hi HBM — the former because of uncertainty in HBM4E validation progress at the three major DRAM makers; the latter because fewer stacked DRAM dies per stack lets Nvidia build more HBM stacks from the same amount of DRAM dies, shipping more products when total DRAM supply is constrained. GF Securities analyst Jeff Pu said today on X that Rubin Ultra's HBM capacity could drop from 384GB to 192GB/256GB (HBM4/HBM4E).

4. Samsung unveils ISOCELL HPC: industry-first native 16-bit RAW 200MP sensor

Samsung Semiconductor announced yesterday (Aug 6) the release of its flagship mobile image sensor ISOCELL HPC, featuring 200 megapixels on a 1/1.3-inch format with native 16-bit RAW output. The flagship sensor's headline feature is native 16-bit RAW support — officially claimed as the industry's first smartphone CMOS sensor capable of outputting raw image data at this bit depth.

By capturing more color values and light gradations at the hardware level, ISOCELL HPC gives ISPs (image signal processors) and computational photography algorithms more headroom, improving dynamic range in complex lighting, richer and more accurate color reproduction, and stronger highlight and shadow recovery in high-contrast scenes.

Structurally, ISOCELL HPC introduces DeepPix technology on top of the ISOCELL architecture, expanding photodiode capacity and the shared floating diffusion node to boost single-pixel full well capacity by 60% over the previous generation. The technology achieves more efficient charge transfer via dual vertical transfer gates (D-VTG) and increases floating diffusion capacitance through a shared FD structure. On zoom HDR: most image sensors today only support HDR at specific zoom ratios, while ISOCELL HPC extends single-frame HDR to all in-sensor zoom levels — 1×, 1.5×, 2×, 3× and 4×.

5. Gurman: OpenAI's first AI hardware is a donut-shaped smart speaker at $300-400

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman revealed in today's (Aug 7) Power On newsletter that OpenAI's first AI hardware is roughly hockey-puck-sized (about 115.8 cubic cm), priced at an estimated $300 to $400 (about RMB 2,029 to 2,705), with a possible 2027 release. It would be OpenAI's first self-branded AI hardware, following its earlier co-branded AI programming keyboard.

Positioning-wise, the device is essentially a "smart speaker without a display" designed for home use — portable enough to carry around the house in one hand — and is being developed by OpenAI together with former Apple designer Jony Ive. It is said to have a "donut shape," roughly the size of a hockey puck (a standard puck is about 7.62 cm in diameter, about 115.8 cubic cm in volume).

On hardware, Gurman says the device includes movable parts, some of which move automatically to signal that the device is responding or interacting with the user; it will also carry lights, a camera system and other sensors. On interaction, the device runs similarly to ChatGPT's voice mode on phones, using more advanced models to support interactions closer to human conversation.
