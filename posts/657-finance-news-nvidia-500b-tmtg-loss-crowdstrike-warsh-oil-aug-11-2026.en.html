⚡ Aug 11 Finance Update: Nvidia Lines Up $500B in Financing With Six Asset Managers, Trump Media Posts $238M Q2 Loss, Cybersecurity Stocks Hit Records, Trump Confirms Fed Call, Oil Jumps 5% Back Above $82

Monday's financial signals were dense and diverse: Nvidia signed financing frameworks with six large asset managers to mobilize more than $500 billion, trying to turn AI compute into Wall Street's newest 'investable asset'; Trump Media posted a Q2 net loss above $238 million, dragged down by digital-asset write-downs; CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks hit record highs as AI-agent threats reignited cybersecurity demand; Trump confirmed speaking 'briefly' to Fed Chair Kevin Warsh a few days ago; and oil jumped about 5% back above $82 as doubts grew over a Hormuz deal. All source publication times were verified.

1. Nvidia Teams Up With Six Asset Managers in a $500B+ AI Compute Financing Push

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 22:09 GMT), Nvidia is trying to turn its AI chips into Wall Street's newest asset class, partnering with six large asset managers on a $500 billion financing push that treats compute infrastructure much like commercial real estate, toll roads or other assets to borrow against (Source: CNBC). The chipmaker said Monday it signed memorandums of understanding with Apollo Global Management, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield Asset Management, Goldman Sachs and KKR to establish financing platforms for its customers.

Executives from all seven companies joined CNBC's Becky Quick in a rare live joint interview. The effort aims to mobilize more than $500 billion in third-party capital for hyperscalers, frontier AI labs and enterprises to build out data centers and acquire Nvidia hardware — a potentially important shift in how AI infrastructure gets funded. By using institutional credit, insurance funds and private capital to underwrite GPUs and data centers, Nvidia is helping end users secure financing without tapping their own balance sheets.

"Technology chips are really for the first time... an investable asset." — Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, in the CNBC interview

Huang argued that because Nvidia's hardware is broadly adopted, flexible and transferable, lenders can underwrite compute as a revenue-generating asset. Whether this genuinely unlocks hundreds of billions in capital — and draws regulatory scrutiny over "compute assetization" — will be the story to watch.

2. Trump Media Posts Q2 Net Loss Above $238M, Hit by Digital Assets

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 22:33 GMT), Trump Media & Technology Group (TMTG) reported a fiscal-second-quarter net loss of more than $238 million on revenue of less than $2 million, dwarfing the roughly $20 million it lost in the same period last year (Source: CNBC). The company said the loss was driven mainly by declines in non-cash assets, including more than $190 million in losses from digital assets, pledged digital assets and equity securities.

The company's $1.7 million in quarterly revenue, mostly from ad services on Truth Social, was up 89% year over year, but operating expenses exceeded $165 million — roughly 275% higher than a year ago. CFO Phillip Juhan explained on the company's first-ever earnings call: "Our operating expenses are largely impacted by the price volatility of digital assets." Separately, The New York Times reported earlier Monday that Truth Social's traffic — already far smaller than rivals like X — fell sharply this summer.

3. Cybersecurity Stocks at Records: CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Jump 5%

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 21:43 GMT), cybersecurity stocks CrowdStrike and Palo Alto Networks both rose more than 5% to new highs on Monday after the industry's annual Black Hat conference in Las Vegas renewed demand for AI-powered security tools (Source: CNBC).

"The single most consistent theme across our conversations — partners, vendors and customers alike — was that AI agents have fundamentally changed the threat landscape," analysts at BTIG wrote in a note. While AI agents have become the predominant attack threat and the environment is "meaningfully worse," deployment of AI security tools is only in the "early innings." Businesses are turning to cybersecurity vendors for new agentic tools to fend off adversaries in a hyper-accelerated threat landscape fueled by new cyber models.

Executives and potential customers gathered in Las Vegas last week in search of ways to secure systems from rogue AI agents. BTIG lifted its price target on Palo Alto to $380 a share — roughly 4% upside from Friday's close. The company's identity platform stands to benefit from the proliferation of AI agents, while products such as XSIAM and Chronosphere build a "data moat."

4. Trump Says He Spoke 'Briefly' to Fed Chair Warsh a Few Days Ago

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 21:23 GMT), President Donald Trump said Monday that he spoke recently to Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh, while denying a Wall Street Journal report that the two had spoken repeatedly since Warsh became chair in May (Source: CNBC). Trump said he and Warsh had been in touch "one time, briefly, a few days ago."

The statement reignited concerns about Fed independence — Trump has weighed in on interest-rate policy far more than most predecessors. Warsh has said he is happy to hear from the president or anyone else on rates but will make up his own mind about where they should go. The Fed did not respond to a request for comment.

Direct contact between the president and the Fed has been rare in recent years: Trump has held only one regular scheduled meeting with former Chair Jerome Powell since returning to the presidency in 2025. Yet Trump has put intense pressure on the central bank — last week he revived a push to fire Governor Lisa Cook, who is serving a 14-year term. With "higher for longer" expectations colliding with White House political pressure, markets have grown more sensitive to Fed independence.

5. Oil Jumps About 5% Back Above $82 as Doubts Grow Over a Hormuz Deal

Per CNBC (published Aug 10, 19:26 GMT), oil prices jumped about 5% Monday as doubt grew that the U.S. and Iran will reach a deal to increase ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz (Source: CNBC). U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures climbed about 5% to close at $82.13 per barrel; Brent settled about 5% higher at $87.72.

Trump told Axios on Sunday that the U.S. is "only semi-negotiating" with Iran, after insisting last week that talks were actually underway. He indicated he would rely on the U.S. naval blockade to pressure Tehran rather than another wave of airstrikes — "We are just watching Iran with its huge inflation and the fact they have no money," he told Axios. Trump canceled a planned attack on the Islamic Republic on Aug. 1 to allow for negotiations.

Meanwhile, oil in the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) has fallen below 300 million barrels, the lowest level since January 1983. Prices sold off more than 7% last week after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC that a deal to open Hormuz with freedom of movement for ships could come soon — but any agreement still looks uncertain.
