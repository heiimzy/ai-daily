⚡ Aug 11 Tech Update: Anthropic IPO in Sept, Rubin Ultra Spec Cut, C919 First International Flight, Navitas Sues Renesas, Glass iPhone Not Canceled

Multiple major tech stories broke on the morning of August 11: Anthropic is reportedly preparing for an IPO as soon as September or early October at a valuation of up to $965 billion; Nvidia is reportedly testing 192GB/256GB versions of its Rubin Ultra accelerator to cope with the advanced HBM memory shortage; Air China's C919 will operate its first international commercial flight tomorrow (August 12) on the Beijing-Ulaanbaatar route; power-semiconductor maker Navitas has sued Renesas in the U.S. over alleged GaN patent infringement; and Bloomberg clarifies that Apple's "all-glass iPhone" has not been canceled, with next year's Pro models still set to feature a glass body. Details below; all source publication times verified.

1. Anthropic Could Go Public in September: $965B Valuation, Downplaying Chinese AI Competition

According to The Wall Street Journal (compiled by ITHome at 03:57 GMT on Aug 11), Anthropic is engaging with potential investors to build confidence ahead of what could be one of the largest IPOs in history. The AI giant — which has surged ahead thanks to its breakout coding tool Claude Code and is currently valued at up to $965 billion — plans to list in September or early October. Sources say Anthropic executives are emphasizing rapid growth in pre-IPO meetings and downplaying competition from Chinese AI firms, arguing that Anthropic focuses on frontier models and that Chinese systems generally still trail top models by at least several months in overall capability.

The company nonetheless faces fresh challenges: cheaper Chinese AI systems gaining popularity, tension between Anthropic and the Trump administration, and growing grassroots opposition to data-center construction across the U.S. Anthropic said in May that its annualized revenue had surpassed $47 billion earlier that month, and in recent weeks it has signed new compute-supply agreements with SpaceX, Google and others. The pricing and aftermarket performance of Anthropic's IPO will be a key reference for how investors value leading AI developers. Its biggest rival OpenAI is expected to follow with its own IPO, potentially as late as next year.

2. Nvidia Trims Rubin Ultra Specs: Testing 192GB/256GB HBM4 Versions to Ease HBM Shortage

According to The Information (compiled by ITHome at 03:45 GMT on Aug 11), Nvidia is planning lower-spec versions of its Rubin Ultra accelerator to cope with the shortage of advanced HBM (high-bandwidth memory). At GTC 2026, Jensen Huang showed a Rubin Ultra compute tray with four compute dies and 1TB of HBM4E — a single GPU using 16 memory stacks for up to 1024GB (1TB) of capacity.

The report says Nvidia is testing 192GB/256GB versions of Rubin Ultra with fewer than the originally announced 16 memory stacks, and plans to step down from HBM4E to HBM4. The reason: HBM4E adds customizable base logic dies on top of the usual next-gen improvements, and its production difficulty makes it hard for memory makers to keep up with Rubin Ultra's launch cadence. With global HBM supply tight and memory prices climbing, this "trade specs for supply" move highlights just how strained the memory segment of the AI supply chain has become.

3. C919's First International Commercial Flight Tomorrow: Beijing-Ulaanbaatar

According to ITHome (03:32 GMT on Aug 11), Air China announced today that its 12th C919 route (Beijing to Mongolia's capital Ulaanbaatar) will launch tomorrow, August 12 — marking the C919's first international commercial flight. Air China has so far taken delivery of 12 C919 aircraft and operates 11 domestic routes.

The first international C919 service is flight CA723, departing Beijing Capital at 15:00 on Aug 12 for Ulaanbaatar, with the return leg CA724 leaving Ulaanbaatar at 19:00. Moving from domestic routes to international commercial operation marks a key step for China's homegrown narrowbody jet in airworthiness recognition, international route operations and overseas support capability — a milestone in China's civil aviation equipment self-reliance drive.

4. Navitas Sues Renesas: GaN Patent War Heats Up, a Month After Being Countersued by Wolfspeed

According to ITHome (03:41 GMT on Aug 11), power-semiconductor company Navitas announced on August 10 that it has filed a patent-infringement lawsuit against Renesas in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas, alleging that Renesas's main gallium-nitride (GaN) product lines infringe multiple of its U.S. patents. Navitas President and CEO Chris Allexandre said Navitas is an early innovator and leader in GaN that "first brought GaN technology to scale," has invested heavily in R&D across GaN and high-voltage SiC, and protects its innovations through strong IP — adding that the company respects legitimate IP and expects competitors to do the same.

Notably, just last month Navitas itself was accused by Wolfspeed of infringing its GaN and silicon-carbide (SiC) patents. The back-and-forth shows the compound-semiconductor patent landscape heating up fast — GaN and SiC, the two leading next-generation power-semiconductor technologies, are becoming battlegrounds as demand surges from data-center power, fast charging and electric vehicles.

5. Bloomberg: Apple's "All-Glass iPhone" Is Not Canceled, Coming to Next Year's Pro Models

According to Bloomberg (compiled by ITHome at 02:24 GMT on Aug 11), after reports that Apple had canceled its long-rumored "all-glass iPhone" planned for the 20th anniversary of the iPhone, Bloomberg's latest reporting says otherwise: the redesign is still on track and will be applied to next year's Pro models. The two devices reportedly carry internal codenames V73 and V74, with glass on both the front and back extending toward the edges, wrapped by a metal frame in the middle.

Earlier the same day, Jefferies analyst Edison Lee cited supply-chain checks claiming the "all-glass iPhone" had been canceled due to poor manufacturing yields, prompting the firm to downgrade Apple and cut its price target from $285.56 to $263.66. Bloomberg explicitly pushed back, saying "Apple's product roadmap hasn't changed." Bloomberg notes the project has evolved since its original conception: Apple's design team initially pushed a more aggressive all-glass, less-metal approach, but that plan was abandoned early over challenges connecting multiple glass panels and scaling mass production — the final design settles on glass front/back plus a metal frame. The dispute over the "all-glass iPhone" underscores how much anticipation surrounds Apple's 20th-anniversary flagship.

6. Big Picture: From the AI Capital Feast to a Homegrown Jet Going Global

This hour's general-tech news runs along three threads: AI capital — Anthropic closing in on a trillion-dollar valuation for its IPO while Nvidia trades specs for HBM supply shows AI infrastructure investment still booming, yet memory shortages and costs are becoming hard constraints on the supply chain; hard-tech going global — the C919's first international commercial route takes China's homegrown jet overseas, a landmark for Chinese aviation; semiconductor patent battles — the GaN litigation triangle among Navitas, Renesas and Wolfspeed shows power-semiconductor competition intensifying; and Apple's "all-glass iPhone" roadmap saga keeps consumer-electronics anticipation high.
