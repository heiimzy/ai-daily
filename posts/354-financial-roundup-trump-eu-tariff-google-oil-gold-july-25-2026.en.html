⚡ July 25 Financial Roundup: Trump Launches Section 301 Probe Into EU Over Google Fine, US Stocks Mixed With Chip Rout, Brent Posts 10% Weekly Gain

1. Trump Opens New Trade War Front: Section 301 Investigation Into EU

President Donald Trump announced a formal Section 301 trade investigation into the European Union on Friday, retaliating against the bloc's €890 million ($1 billion) antitrust fine on Google under the Digital Markets Act (DMA). In a lengthy Truth Social post, Trump accused the EU of "robbing American companies" and threatened "a substantial TARIFF" at the earliest possible moment.

"The European Union is at it again and, as usual, taking direct aim at GREAT American Companies!" Trump wrote, citing $15 billion in fines against Apple, $3 billion against Meta, and $2.5 billion against Amazon. "The penalties will be entirely reversed and, we anticipate, a substantial TARIFF to be placed on them at the earliest possible moment."

The investigation marks a significant escalation in transatlantic trade tensions. It comes just one day after the White House imposed new 10%-12.5% tariffs on 60 trading partners under the same Section 301 authority, covering 99.4% of U.S. imports. Those tariffs took effect at 12:01 AM ET Friday, replacing the expiring 10% global levy that had served as a temporary measure after the Supreme Court struck down Trump's earlier "Liberation Day" tariffs in February.

The European Commission offered a "cautious welcome" to the new tariff framework, noting it respects the Turnberry deal caps of 15% on EU goods. However, Trump's latest threat could unravel that fragile agreement. U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer warned Thursday that the EU's enforcement action undermined "constructive dialogue" and posed a "real risk" to transatlantic trade stability.

2. Wall Street Mixed: Dow Rises 235 Points, Nasdaq Slides on Chip Rout

U.S. stocks ended mixed on Friday as investors weighed tariff shocks, geopolitical tensions, and a pullback in oil prices. The Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 235.60 points (+0.46%) to 51,947.25. The S&P 500 edged up 3.68 points (+0.05%) to 7,411.98, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 161.87 points (-0.64%) to 24,975.82.

All three major indices posted weekly losses: the Nasdaq dropped 2.1% (second consecutive weekly decline), the S&P 500 lost 0.6%, and the Dow fell 0.4%. The Magnificent Seven index declined approximately 4.8% for the week, now 11% below its May record — erasing over $2 trillion in market value.

Semiconductor bloodbath

Chip stocks led the selloff. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOXX) plunged 4.3%. Key movers:

  • Intel (INTC) -7.9% — despite a Q2 earnings beat, concerns over foundry customer acquisition weighed
  • Micron (MU) -7.0%
  • AMD (AMD) -3.3%
  • Broadcom (AVGO) -2.7%
  • SK Hynix ADR -8.8%

Analysts attributed broad-based semiconductor weakness to lingering doubts about profitability despite accelerating AI infrastructure investment.

Apple bucks trend, rises 3.5%

Apple was the standout tech gainer, rising 3.5% and providing the main lift to the Dow. The iPhone maker has largely sat out the AI spending spree — a strategy investors have been rewarding. Apple shares have gained 11% in July and 18% year-to-date in 2026.

3. Commodities: Brent Falls From $100 But Posts 10% Weekly Gain

Brent crude futures settled at $96.78/barrel on Friday, down 3.88% — the first decline in a week — but still up 9.9% for the week. WTI crude fell 3.12% to $89.31. On Thursday, Brent had briefly touched $102/barrel, a two-month high, after Houthi rebels attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea and Trump threatened "massive" military retaliation against Iran.

Friday's pullback was driven by reports that Pakistan, with Chinese backing, is pushing to restart U.S.-Iran peace negotiations. However, Trump's Axios interview — in which he said he is "close to making a decision" on a massive attack and that Iran "has not received enough pain" — largely offset the ceasefire hopes.

RBC Capital Markets' Helima Croft warned that a full-scale regional war could push Brent crude past the all-time record of $146/barrel set in 2008. "The conflict has entered a decidedly more dangerous phase," she said. "It could shift the sentiment of 'the market always finds a workaround' camp."

U.S. gasoline prices rose to a national average of $4.11/gallon, with diesel above $5.20/gallon. The U.S. strategic petroleum reserve has been substantially depleted, leaving the market with less cushion against supply disruptions than in the spring.

4. Gold Under Pressure at $4,030 as Oil Spike Backfires on Safe-Haven Demand

Gold slipped 0.4% to $4,030.09/oz on Friday, far below Wednesday's two-week high of $4,125. Despite escalating geopolitical risks in the Middle East, the $100+ oil price surge has boosted inflation expectations, sending bond yields and the dollar higher — a trifecta that has undermined gold's traditional safe-haven appeal.

The 10-year Treasury yield peaked at 4.712% during the week — the highest since January 2025 — before settling near 4.681%. The 30-year bond yield approached 5.201%. The U.S. dollar index climbed to a three-week high.

Silver bucked the trend, rising 1.4% to $58.40/oz, while platinum gained 0.6% and palladium was flat. Analysts interpreted the gold-silver divergence as a rotation from safe-haven demand toward industrial metals.

In China, major banks including ICBC, Postal Savings Bank of China, and Ping An Bank suspended retail paper gold trading on Friday, aiming to shift price discovery from paper contracts to physical gold and reduce market volatility.

5. Fed Rate Hike Odds Surge: July FOMC in Focus

CME FedWatch data shows the probability of a rate hike at next week's FOMC meeting has surged from 13% a week ago to 36%, with September hike odds reaching 80%. The dramatic repricing reflects resurgent inflation fears driven by the oil spike and Trump's new tariff regime.

The 10-year Treasury yield rose a net 13 basis points this week. Newly appointed Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is seen as favoring limited forward guidance, adding to policy uncertainty. The European Central Bank held its deposit rate at 2.25% on Thursday but left the door open for a September hike.

Morgan Stanley strategist Daniel Skelly noted: "Additional pressure from concerns about Mag-7 capex spending levels and surging oil prices suggests summer index trading may stay volatile."

The earnings calendar intensifies next week: Microsoft and Meta report on Wednesday, followed by Apple and Amazon on Thursday. If more hyperscalers replicate Alphabet's pattern of heavy AI capex with negative free cash flow, the AI monetization question could shift from company-specific to sector-wide repricing.
