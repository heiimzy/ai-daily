⚡ Aug 10 Tech Update: TSMC July Revenue Up 44.7%, Samsung HBM4 Yield Nears 80%, PDD Launches 'Fastest Next-Day' Entry, 18-Year-Old Linux Flaw SCTPhantom Exposed, SAXON Q Quantum Computers Open Pre-Orders

Multiple major tech stories landed on the afternoon of August 10: TSMC posted July consolidated revenue of NT$467.58 billion, up 44.7% year over year; Samsung's HBM4 yield is reportedly nearing 80%, accelerating its capacity ramp; PDD officially launched a "fastest next-day delivery" core entry point, pushing into the instant-retail stronghold of JD.com and Meituan; Tencent Zhuque Lab disclosed an 18-year-old Linux kernel flaw, SCTPhantom, that enables privilege escalation and even container escape; and German quantum computing firm SAXON Q opened pre-orders for 128/512-qubit computers that run at room temperature. Details below; all source publication times have been verified.

1. TSMC July Revenue Hits NT$467.58B, Up 44.7% YoY

According to IT Home (published 13:32 Beijing time on Aug 10), TSMC released its July 2026 consolidated revenue report: monthly revenue reached NT$467.58 billion (approximately RMB 98.005 billion at current exchange rates), up 5.6% month over month and 44.7% year over year. On a cumulative basis, consolidated revenue for January-July 2026 reached NT$2.87 trillion (about RMB 601.985 billion), up 37% from the same period last year.

Analysts note that both monthly and cumulative growth remain at elevated levels, pointing to sustained demand recovery for advanced-node manufacturing led by AI chips, with utilization staying healthy. This follows earlier reports that TSMC has further raised its 2026 capital expenditure forecast to US$60-64 billion, a clear expansion signal as AI compute supply-chain momentum keeps building (Source: IT Home).

2. Samsung HBM4 Yield Nears 80%, Q3 Sales Targeted at 3x Last Quarter

According to IT Home (published 13:54 Beijing time on Aug 10), citing South Korea's Seoul Economic Daily quoting sources on Sunday, Samsung Electronics' yield on HBM4 memory has neared 80%, approaching the target it originally set for the end of 2026, and the improved yield is accelerating its HBM4 capacity ramp. Sources said Samsung's yield was only around 60% when it began initial HBM4 mass production in February — far below the level required for large-scale DRAM commercialization, which had constrained wafer allocation.

With yields continuing to climb, Samsung is now willing to shift more DRAM wafer capacity to HBM4: the company reportedly plans to grow Q3 HBM4 sales to more than three times last quarter's level, expand HBM4's share of total HBM revenue to over 60% in the second half, and by year-end reach an HBM market share comparable to its overall DRAM share. If realized, this would significantly reshape competition in AI-accelerator memory (Source: IT Home, citing Seoul Economic Daily).

3. PDD Launches "Fastest Next-Day" Core Entry, Pushing into JD and Meituan Turf

According to Sina Tech (published 14:22 Beijing time on Aug 10), PDD has officially launched a "fastest next-day delivery" core entry point covering categories including fresh fruit and daily necessities, further penetrating the instant-retail heartland dominated by Meituan and JD.com. Previously, PDD had no first-level entry for this; the "next-day" badge was only dynamically shown on product or search result pages via algorithms. Elevating it to a core navigation slot on the homepage is seen by industry observers as a major upgrade to PDD's delivery-timeliness capability and a key step toward shorter fulfillment windows.

PDD customer service confirmed the entry was launched recently, saying it is "to optimize the consumer experience and provide assurance services." Asked whether food delivery would follow, the representative said "no notification yet." On timeliness guarantees, PDD noted: for items carrying a "next-day / day-after" service promise, if delivery misses the committed window after a successful group buy, buyers receive at least a RMB 3 no-threshold voucher (Source: Sina Tech).

4. Linux Kernel Flaw SCTPhantom: 18 Years Old, Enables Privilege Escalation and Container Escape

According to IT Home (published 13:45 Beijing time on Aug 10), Tencent's OS security team (Tencent Zhuque Lab) disclosed a Linux kernel vulnerability named SCTPhantom, tracked as CVE-2026-64564, residing in the SCTP network communication feature and dating back 18 years. A low-privileged local program can exploit it to gain the highest system privileges and, in certain scenarios, even break out of a container to take control of the host. The Linux kernel has already released a fix.

SCTP (Stream Control Transmission Protocol) supports multiple network addresses; combined with dynamic address reconfiguration, it can add, remove or adjust addresses during a connection. SCTPhantom stems from a logic flaw in how the kernel handles SCTP address-change requests — it uses different address information when checking a deletion request versus executing the subsequent operation, allowing attackers to bypass validation and escalate privileges. Teams running containers and virtualization environments are advised to evaluate and upgrade their kernels promptly (Source: IT Home).

5. Room-Temperature Ready: SAXON Q Opens Pre-Orders for 128/512-Qubit Quantum Computers

According to IT Home (published 15:01 Beijing time on Aug 10), German diamond-based nitrogen-vacancy (NV) quantum computing firm SAXON Q announced this month that its 128-qubit SXQ128 and 512-qubit SXQ512 systems are now open for pre-order. Both systems run in standard server racks at room temperature on standard power — no cryogenic cooling, vacuum equipment, dedicated facilities, or specialist maintenance, recalibration, and environmental control are required, sharply lowering the deployment barrier for quantum computing.

According to the company, the systems can run quantum workloads such as convolutional neural networks with a claimed 6-10x energy-efficiency advantage over GPUs. Using its proprietary sulfur co-implantation process, SAXON Q achieves over 85% qubit preparation success and qubit fidelity as high as 99.92%. Through its proprietary multi-core quantum operating system, each core of the SXQ128 provides 8 fully entangled qubits, while each core of the SXQ512 provides 16. On timing, SXQ128 is slated for general sale in late summer this year, and SXQ512 in Q2 2027 (Source: IT Home).
