⚡ Aug 6 Tech Roundup: Pixel 11 Pro Details Leak, DuckDuckGo's Clean Links, Nashville Halts a Data Center, Anthropic's LLM Cryptanalysis

In the past hour, fresh developments surfaced across consumer electronics, privacy tools, urban infrastructure, and AI security research: new details about Google's Pixel 11 Pro leaked, including a "HiLight" colorway; DuckDuckGo added a "Copy Clean Link" feature to its iPhone app that strips tracking parameters when you copy a URL; Nashville voted to use eminent domain to stop a data center project next to the zoo; and a thoughtful technical review assessed Anthropic's Mythos model, which just produced noteworthy results in cryptanalysis. Here is this round's tech roundup.

1. Google: Pixel 11 Pro colorway and product details leak

With the expected launch window for the Pixel 11 series approaching, rumors around the Pixel 11 Pro keep heating up. The latest update: strings inside Google's Contacts app now reference a "Pixel 11 HiLight" colorway, hinting that the next Pixel 11 Pro will introduce a new highlight color scheme as a visual differentiator from previous generations. Combined with recently leaked Pixel Watch 5 marketing images and Google clearing out official Pixel 10 cases, observers widely believe the Pixel 11 lineup has entered the final preparation stage before launch.

According to aggregated reports from multiple tech outlets, the Pixel 11 Pro is expected to run Google's next-generation Tensor chip, with a stronger focus on on-device AI, plus upgraded camera and display specifications. Google's inventory-clearing moves and the removal of official cases point to an imminent launch. 2. DuckDuckGo: iOS app adds "Copy Clean Link" to strip tracking from shared URLs

According to ZDNet, DuckDuckGo's iOS app has added a practical new feature — "Copy Clean Link": when you copy a link, it automatically removes the tracking identifiers after the question mark and keeps only the clean original URL. For example, if you copy a long link from Facebook such as example.com/article.html?utm_source=facebook&utm_campaign=US&fbclid=..., those tracking parameters would normally be copied and shared along with the content; with this feature enabled, only example.com/article.html is copied.

The feature appeared in the app's latest App Store update (late July). DuckDuckGo has not yet commented, but the reviewer describes it as "an important step forward" for privacy — it lets users share and forward links without leaking UTM tags, fbclid, and other tracking fingerprints along with the content. One caveat: Copy Clean Link only cleans the copied link itself. It does not block cookies or other trackers that load when a link is opened; its role is to make sharing clean links considerably easier.

3. Nashville: eminent domain used to halt data center project near the zoo

Nashville, Tennessee is putting the brakes on a data center project located right next to the Nashville Zoo. According to CoStar, the Nashville city council has voted to approve using eminent domain to halt the data center from moving forward near the zoo. Data centers — with their high energy use, noise, cooling-water demands, and knock-on effects on nearby property values — have increasingly triggered pushback from communities and local governments across the U.S.

In this case, the project's location next to the zoo made the land's ecological and public value a key point of contention. The council's decision means the city can legally acquire the parcel, preventing the data center from breaking ground altogether. The move reflects how the tension between the AI compute buildout and local planning and ecological protection is becoming visible at the governance level — more cities are using planning rules, taxes, and even eminent domain to re-price the wave of data center construction.

4. Expert view: Anthropic's Mythos does cryptanalysis — will LLMs break encryption?

Anthropic recently published a batch of cryptanalysis results obtained with its Mythos model, sparking widespread discussion. Renowned cryptographer Jean-Philippe Aumasson offered a detailed technical assessment with a headline conclusion: LLMs will not break the symmetric encryption algorithms we use today, but their potential deserves attention. Mythos' papers show two kinds of results — an attack on the post-quantum signature scheme HAWK that reduces HAWK-512's estimated security from 128 bits to at most 108 bits (practically breakable only in the toy version), and a key-recovery attack on 7-round AES-128 (the full version has 10 rounds), which poses no threat to AES but is a rare new cryptanalytic result.

The more significant takeaway, the author argues, is the negative result: Mythos found no stronger attack against full-round AES, which reinforces how robust symmetric ciphers (AES, ChaCha, SHA-3) remain after decades of empirical hardening. Anthropic also released CryptanalysisBench, an LLM benchmark covering AES, ChaCha, and NIST competition algorithms including BLAKE. The commentary concludes that LLMs are more likely to help with auxiliary work — finding errors in complexity estimates and security proofs — than to break established ciphers. It is a calm, professional calibration of the relationship between AI and encryption.
