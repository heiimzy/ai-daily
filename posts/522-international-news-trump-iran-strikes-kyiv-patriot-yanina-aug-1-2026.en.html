⚡ Aug 1 World News: Trump Orders New Strikes on Iran as Soon as This Weekend, Russian Ballistic Missiles Kill at Least 9 in Kyiv, Ukraine Sinks Russian Ship Yanina, Trump Backtracks on Patriot License

Trump Orders a Fresh Military Campaign Against Iran, Possibly Beginning This Weekend

The Wall Street Journal, citing US officials, reported Saturday (August 1) that President Trump has ordered the US military to launch a new campaign against Iran that could begin as soon as this weekend and last for several days, aimed at forcing Tehran to surrender — while officials cautioned the strikes could still be called off if there is diplomatic progress (CNN live updates, 10:00 AM EDT August 1 = 10:00 PM Beijing time).

Multiple sources told CBS News that the United States and Israel are planning one of the heaviest bombing campaigns of the war against Iranian energy infrastructure, with attacks possibly continuing throughout the weekend and oil refineries and power plants among likely targets; Axios cited a US official saying Trump is seriously considering striking Iranian energy targets in the coming days but has not yet given final orders. At a televised cabinet meeting at Camp David on Friday, Trump said: "We will be hitting them very hard. At some point, they're going to say, 'We just can't take it anymore.'"

"We will be hitting them very hard. At some point, they're going to say: We just can't take it anymore." — Trump at the Camp David cabinet meeting (via The Associated Press)

Iran has signaled defiance. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, warned via the WANA news agency that continued US naval blockade "will not only lock the Strait of Hormuz tighter, but will also shut down other straits and chokepoints," with the price paid by the global economy, the energy market and American voters. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps also launched drone attacks on targets in Kuwait in the early hours of Saturday: Kuwait's military said it detected and destroyed hostile drones in its airspace since dawn, describing what it called an Iranian attack that hit a government facility in the north and civilian property of a company on Bubiyan Island, causing material damage from falling shrapnel but no casualties.

  • US Central Command (CENTCOM) said Friday that since reimposing its naval blockade of Iran in mid-July it has rerouted 30 commercial vessels, disabled two and boarded two to verify compliance;
  • The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported two incidents off Oman on Saturday: one tanker was struck by an "unknown projectile" about 11 nautical miles northeast of Lima, damaging its engine room and leaving the vessel not under command, while a second tanker northeast of Khasab reported seeing a large splash and explosion nearby — no casualties were reported;
  • Brent crude settled at $90.12 a barrel and WTI at $84.67 on Friday, both down more than 5% on the week, as Middle East fighting and Red Sea risks continued to unsettle energy markets.

The US-Israel war on Iran, which began on February 28, has now run for about five months. The truce under a memorandum of understanding signed June 17 has effectively collapsed, with neither side yielding on Iran's nuclear program or control of the Strait of Hormuz.

Russian Ballistic Missiles Hit Kyiv, Killing at Least 9; Patriot Interceptors Depleted, Only One Missile Intercepted

Russia launched a massive overnight assault on Ukraine on Saturday, firing 35 missiles — 27 of them ballistic — and 185 attack drones of various types, with the capital Kyiv as the main target. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed at least nine people were killed and 30 wounded, including four children; a residential building in the Shevchenkivskyi district partially collapsed with people trapped inside, warehouses caught fire and some areas lost power (CNN, updated 8:02 AM ET August 1 = 8:02 PM Beijing time).

The most striking detail was the air-defense result: Zelensky said Ukrainian forces managed to intercept only one of the 27 ballistic missiles — "simply because there are no interceptors for the Patriot systems." He stressed that the shortage of ballistic-missile interceptors only encourages Russia to launch such deadly attacks: "Every package of ballistic missile interceptors saves the lives of our people. And every night without them results in more casualties."

Russia's defense ministry said Saturday it had carried out a "massive strike" in the Kyiv region targeting "military-industrial complex enterprises and logistics centers," and claimed its air defenses destroyed 274 Ukrainian drones overnight. This was the second major attack on the capital this week — a large Russian assault on Thursday had killed at least nine people and wounded more than 50 across Ukraine, and a suspected Russian cruise missile landed in Poland during that attack. Ukraine's Air Force has said the Patriot is the only system in its arsenal reliably capable of intercepting ballistic missiles, but stockpiles are critically low, forcing crews to fire interceptors at a one-to-one ratio to stretch supplies.

Ukrainian Naval Drones Sink Rosatom-Affiliated Container Ship Yanina in the Black Sea

In the early hours of Saturday, the FESCO container ship Yanina — a company under Russia's state nuclear corporation Rosatom — was struck by two Ukrainian uncrewed surface vessels in Black Sea waters about 130 nautical miles (209 km) from Novorossiysk. The ship sank after sustaining damage, and all 17 crew members were rescued and reported in good condition. President Zelensky confirmed the strike on social media: "The sanctioned Russian-flagged container ship Yanina, with a capacity of over 100,000 tonnes, went to the bottom thanks to the precision of our Defense Forces."

Rosatom CEO Alexey Likhachev condemned the attack, saying the vessel was sailing in international waters carrying civilian cargo such as frozen food and construction materials: "Such an attack cannot be interpreted as anything other than piracy and maritime robbery." The rescue was carried out by Russian Black Sea Fleet naval aviation together with the merchant vessel Delphinus, whose crew pulled 16 sailors from the water overnight despite the risk of further drone attacks.

  • The same night, Ukrainian long-range drones struck three oil refineries in Ufa, Bashkortostan — Bashneft-UNPZ, Bashneft-Novoil and Bashneft-Ufaneftekhim — with a combined annual processing capacity of more than 23 million tonnes, about 1,600 km from the Ukrainian border;
  • SBU drones also hit three Podlet-K1 and one Kasta-2E2 radar stations in the Krasnodar region, plus electronic warfare and air-defense positions in occupied Crimea;
  • Ukraine has targeted Russian-flagged ships in the Black Sea since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, previously striking several tankers of Russia's so-called "shadow fleet."

Trump Backtracks on Licensing Ukraine to Build Patriot Interceptors

Hours before the Kyiv attack, Trump walked back his earlier pledge on licensing Ukraine to produce Patriot interceptors, telling reporters at a Camp David cabinet meeting: "We have not agreed to that. We're talking about it, but it's a hard thing to give away that kind of technology — there are people you give that technology to, and someday they can turn on you." Trump had said on the sidelines of the NATO summit in Ankara on July 8 that he would authorize Ukraine to build Patriots, and Zelensky said after his July 28 visit to Washington that the two sides had reached a political understanding.

The reversal comes at Ukraine's most vulnerable moment for air defense. The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) estimated this week that the Iran war has consumed about two-thirds of America's Patriot interceptor stockpile, with the most advanced variant possibly down to around 759 missiles; Zelensky has asked Washington for 300 interceptors to get through the winter, a request Trump has not yet granted. Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha appealed Saturday: "It is the battle for the skies that will define the trajectory of this war. The stronger the air shield over Ukraine, the closer peace becomes."

Taken together, a looming new escalation in the US-Iran war, Russia's intensifying ballistic-missile campaign against Ukraine's depleted air defenses, and the continued conflict over Black Sea shipping have kept international politics on edge heading into the first weekend of August.
