⚡ August 4 Finance Update: Asian Markets Slip, Brent Steadies, 25 US States Sue Over Tariffs, Bezos Plans Amazon Share Sale

After US stocks opened August with a broad rally and the Dow at a record close, Asian markets opened higher but slipped on Tuesday (August 4), as investors digested the optimism from easing geopolitical tensions while staying cautious on Middle East oil supply, the Fed's path and the durability of the AI rally. Meanwhile, 25 US states jointly sued the Trump administration over its new tariffs, and Amazon founder Jeff Bezos filed a fresh share-sale plan. Here is the latest finance roundup.

1. Asian stocks open higher then slip; Brent crude steadies near $85

According to Al Jazeera (August 4, data as of 04:00 GMT) and Reuters, after Wall Street's three major indexes all rose on Monday with the S&P 500 closing just shy of its record 7,620.90, Asia-Pacific equities opened higher but gave back gains on Tuesday. As of 04:00 GMT (around the Tokyo lunch break), Japan's Nikkei 225 turned down about 0.7%, South Korea's KOSPI slipped about 1% — the latter still recovering from a week of violent swings driven by AI memory-chip demand — and Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index edged lower by about 0.5%. Profit-taking after the early-session surge was the main pressure, with investors divided on AI valuations and the path of US-Iran tensions.

On commodities, crude oil steadied after Monday's roughly 5% plunge. Monday's drop came after President Donald Trump said he had shelved a fresh strike on Iran and signaled renewed talks. Brent crude then bounced more than 1.3% to trade back near $85 a barrel during the Asian session, while US WTI crude held firm. The market is weighing whether Tehran will restore unrestricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz, a route that carried roughly a fifth of global oil supply before the conflict. The US 10-year Treasury yield eased to around 4.68%, lending support to risk assets.

2. 25 US states sue the Trump administration over new tariffs, calling them a 'pretext'

According to the Associated Press, Reuters and The Guardian (August 4), a coalition of 25 states led by New York sued the Trump administration, challenging newly imposed tariffs of 10% to 12.5% on goods from 60 trading partners. The states argue the new duties are a "pretext" for tariffs the Supreme Court had already struck down — the high court rejected the earlier tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act in February, and the Court of International Trade also rejected the administration's use of Section 122 of the Trade Act.

The complaint says the US Trade Representative announced the new tariffs, justified on forced-labor grounds, on July 23 — one day before the temporary Section 122 duties were due to expire — in an apparent effort to keep the tariff regime running without interruption. The 60 economies covered by the new duties together account for about 99.4% of US imports. The states are asking the Court of International Trade to halt the tariffs, declare them unlawful and order refunds of duties already paid. The state-level suit follows two earlier challenges by small businesses in July over the related Section 301 tariffs, marking an escalation of the trade-policy fight.

3. Bezos files Form 144 to sell up to 15 million Amazon shares (~$4.07 billion)

According to a US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) Form 144 (filed August 3) and financial media including Stocktwits and GuruFocus (early August 4), Amazon (AMZN) founder Jeff Bezos filed an intent-to-sell notice for up to 15 million common shares, to be sold through Morgan Stanley Smith Barney starting August 3. At the prevailing market price, the block was valued at roughly $4.07 billion. The filing shows the shares were originally acquired as "founder stock" in July 1994.

Importantly, the sale is being executed under a pre-arranged Rule 10b5-1 trading plan (adopted November 14, 2025 and running through February 26, 2027), rather than a decision made in response to Amazon crossing the $3 trillion market-cap threshold for the first time on Monday. Bezos previously sold about 25 million shares under the same plan, raising roughly $5.7 billion between late June and late July. If the latest 15-million-share block is completed in full, his stake would fall from about 884 million shares to around 869 million shares, roughly 8.1% of Amazon's 10.79 billion outstanding shares, with the remaining position still worth about $244 billion at Monday's close. Amazon shares slipped about 1% in Monday's after-hours trading on the news.

From "Wall Street's surge" to "Asia's pullback," and from a trade-policy lawsuit to a mega-cap founder's share sale, the August 4 financial landscape is clear: optimism from easing geopolitics is being offset by valuation, policy and uncertainty realities. This week also brings SpaceX's first quarterly report since going public, AMD earnings and Friday's US jobs report — all worth tracking closely.
