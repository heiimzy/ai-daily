⚡ Aug 4 World Update: Iran Denies US Talks in a 24-Hour Reversal, Ukraine's Magura Drones Strike Crimea Radars for the First Time, Russia-Ukraine Strikes Claim More Lives

Monday's international stage swung between talk and fight: President Trump had just announced he was calling off the "biggest attack since World War II" on Iran and said US-Iran talks would resume that afternoon — only for Tehran to publicly deny it hours later. Ukraine's military intelligence released exclusive footage of Magura naval drones striking Russian radar systems on land in Crimea for the first time, and Russia and Ukraine traded another deadly round of long-range strikes. Here is this roundup.

1. 24-Hour Reversal: Iran Denies Talks With the US, Trump Blasts Tehran as "Unbelievably Duplicitous"

On Saturday, President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that, at the request of Iran and Middle Eastern countries including Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar, he had called off a new round of strikes on Iran — what he called "the biggest attack since World War II" — and that US-Iran negotiations would begin Monday afternoon, covering the full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and the "denuclearization" of Iran, with a deal "imminent."

But on Monday, Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei gave a strikingly different version at his weekly press briefing: "We are not currently negotiating with the United States. Our negotiations are with Oman to secure passage through the Strait of Hormuz." He said Iran had no plans to host foreign delegations or send negotiators abroad in the coming days; Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi is on a religious pilgrimage in Iraq. A senior Iranian source told Reuters that no talks were planned and Araghchi would be unavailable at least until the end of the week.

Trump hit back on Truth Social: "Iranian Leadership is unbelievably duplicitous! They ask for a meeting, some would say 'beg,' talks begin, with more scheduled in the immediate future, and they say, openly and proudly, that they're not having any discussions, that nothing is being talked about, and they're only dealing with 'Oman.'" He also insisted Monday that "whether Iran wants to admit it or not, we are, in fact, talking," and repeated that the strait is controlled by the US — with no ships allowed to reach Iran "unless a Deal, or Total Surrender, is accomplished."

Diplomacy was not entirely stalled: Baghaei confirmed that talks with Oman on a new route through the strait were in their "final stages" — a corridor that would be "neither the northern route nor the southern route," with separate entry and exit lanes, respecting both sides' sovereign rights and security interests. He stressed, however, that this does not mean reopening the strait: as long as the US naval blockade of Iranian ports continues, "the situation in the Strait of Hormuz will remain unchanged." President Masoud Pezeshkian wrote on X: "We must strive to compel the enemy to remain committed to what it has signed." CNN reported Monday that US Central Command is crowdsourcing "new creative and unconventional ways to pressure and punish Iran" from its troops — an intelligence officer sent such an email to analysts — while Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth insisted the Pentagon remains "locked and loaded" to strike Iran at "levels not seen since World War II." In markets, Brent crude settled about 4.6% lower at $83.85 a barrel and WTI fell about 4.7% to $80.66; the UKMTO reported Monday that a tanker off the coast of Oman heard an explosion near the vessel on Sunday night, 20 nautical miles northeast of Khasab, with the vessel and crew safe.

2. A First: Ukraine's Magura Naval Platforms Strike Russian Radars on Land in Crimea

Ukraine's Defense Intelligence (HUR) released exclusive combat footage on August 3 showing its elite Group 13 special unit using new Magura naval platforms fitted as FPV drone carriers in late July to strike two Russian radar-related targets in occupied Crimea: a command vehicle of a Podlet radar system and a Parol-4 identification friend-or-foe (IFF) radar interrogator. HUR said it was the first time the Magura platforms enabled Ukrainian forces to launch FPV drones against Russian ground targets in Crimea.

"From now on, our Maguras threaten the Russian occupiers on land as well — this fundamentally shifts the fight into a new dimension. Overall, our operations during the war against Russia confirm that the invaders will have no place in Crimea — neither in the waters, nor in the skies over the Black Sea, nor on Crimean soil. Crimea is Ukraine," said Group 13's commander, call sign "Thirteenth." HUR notes the Magura family includes the V5 and V7 combat variants and the non-combat V6P; Group 13 had previously destroyed nine Russian vessels and damaged five more with Magura drones, along with three helicopters and two fighter jets destroyed and another helicopter damaged. The Magura series was first publicly presented on May 14, 2025 and has been a signature of Ukraine's Black Sea campaign.

Meanwhile, explosions and fires were reported across Crimea in the early hours of August 3: an electrical substation in Kerch was struck and the Crimean Bridge was closed around 1 a.m.; an oil depot in Feodosia and Kerch's central market caught fire; satellite imagery showed a fire near a worker camp about 300 meters from the TES oil and gas terminal. Ukraine's General Staff confirmed strikes on Russian logistics targets — a repair base and warehouse used by an artillery brigade near Nove Zhyttia, warehouses near Okunivka and Naumivka, and a communications hub near Artemivka. Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces intercepted 131 drones overnight over Crimea, the Black Sea and several Russian regions.

3. Escalating Mutual Strikes: Six Dead in a Krasnodar Resort Village, Three at a Kryvyi Rih Petrol Station, Russia Captures Two Kharkiv Settlements

Russia and Ukraine reported another round of deadly strikes on Monday (August 3), killing at least 17 people across both sides. Veniamin Kondratyev, governor of Russia's southern Krasnodar region, said drone debris fell on the resort village of Arkhipo-Osipovka near Gelendzhik, killing six people including three children and wounding nearly 40. Sergei Aksyonov, the Moscow-installed head of occupied Crimea, said the peninsula's death toll had risen to four with two injured, while acting Belgorod Governor Alexander Shuvaev reported four civilian deaths and 17 injuries in border settlements.

On the Ukrainian side, a Russian strike on a petrol station in the Kryvyi Rih district of the Dnipropetrovsk region killed three people, including an emergency response worker and his eight-year-old son; Governor Oleksandr Hanzha said the attack sparked a fire that damaged the station and nearby vehicles. The same day, Russia's Defense Ministry said its forces had captured the settlements of Bilyi Kolodiaz and Uspenivka in the Kharkiv region, and claimed it had struck four bulk carriers carrying cargo for Ukraine's armed forces — three in Black Sea waters and one in the port of Mykolaiv. Russia's transport ministry said it was taking all necessary measures to protect cargo logistics in the Azov-Black Sea basin after drone attacks on vessels.

Notably, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told Ukrainian diplomats on Monday that Ukraine's long-range drone technology, developed during the war, has helped Kyiv sign military cooperation agreements with nine countries — from the Black Sea "drone fleet" to today's ship-launched drone strikes on land, Ukraine is converting unmanned innovation into both battlefield and diplomatic leverage. Analysts note that the mutual strikes have settled into a cycle of retaliation: Russia pounds cities with glide bombs and missiles while Ukraine sends drones deep into Russian territory against energy and logistics nodes, with each side blaming the other for civilian casualties.

Whether the US-Iran "talks-denial-talks" loop can be broken, whether Magura will reshape the Black Sea-Crimea battlefield, and when the Russia-Ukraine strikes cool down — these three threads will shape the Middle East and European security landscape in the weeks ahead.
