⚡ Aug 2 Finance Update: OPEC+ Raises September Output 188K bpd to Complete Cut Rollback, Bitcoin Rebounds Above $63.5K, Russia Bans Moscow Mining Through 2032, AI Hedge Fund Loses ~$35B

Sunday, August 2 - global markets are repricing around two themes: Middle East de-escalation and the unwinding of AI leverage. OPEC+ agreed at a Sunday online meeting to raise output by 188,000 barrels per day from September, completing the full rollback of the 2023 voluntary cuts; Bitcoin rebounded about $1,500 back above $63,500 after President Trump called off new strikes on Iran, with nearly $100 million in short liquidations over 24 hours; Russia signed a decree banning crypto mining in Moscow and surrounding regions from August 15 through 2032; and Leopold Aschenbrenner's "Situational Awareness" AI hedge fund blew up on 400% leverage, with assets collapsing from $45 billion to roughly $10 billion in a matter of days.

OPEC+ Confirms 188K bpd September Increase, Completing the Cut Rollback

According to Reuters (published 8:59 pm HKT on August 2), the seven core OPEC+ members - Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman - said after a virtual meeting on Sunday that they will raise production quotas by 188,000 barrels per day from September. It is the sixth consecutive monthly increase (Xinhua) and marks the full unwinding of the 1.65 million bpd voluntary supply cut agreed in April 2023.

In practice, however, much of the increase remains on paper: with the Strait of Hormuz nearly paralyzed by the Iran war and Russian and Kazakh exports disrupted by the Ukraine conflict, successive monthly hikes this year have had little market impact. Jorge Leon of Rystad Energy said: "OPEC+ has finished unwinding its voluntary cuts. The next challenge is managing the surplus that could emerge as export flows normalise." His base case is a fourth-quarter pause while the group prepares for 2027 quota negotiations.

Details: the roughly 2 million bpd of cuts dating back to 2022 remain in place until the end of this year; OPEC+ is reviewing members' production capacity to set 2027 baselines, and Iraq is among members pushing for higher individual quotas. UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo noted that many members cannot actually produce up to their official targets due to a "decline in production capacity," so the hike is more symbolic than physical. Market reaction was muted: WTI at $86.52 (+2.97%), Brent spot at $89.86 (+3.11%). The next meeting is set for September 6 to decide October output.

Trump Calls Off Iran Strikes; Bitcoin Rebounds Above $63,500

Trump announced on Truth Social late Saturday that he would call off the new round of strikes on Iran at the request of Tehran and other Middle Eastern nations, saying parties are working on a deal that includes the "immediate, complete and total opening of the Hormuz Strait" and an end to Iran's nuclear threat, with Israel joining the commitment.

Bitcoin responded immediately. According to CryptoPotato, BTC had fallen to an 18-day low of $62,200 the evening before, then rebounded roughly $1,500 to about $63,500; CoinCodex data showed Bitcoin at $63,455 at 06:00 UTC on August 2, up 0.67% in 24 hours. CoinGlass data showed nearly $100 million in short liquidations over 24 hours, with traders watching a daily close above $64,700 as the key level. Altcoins moved harder: Cardano jumped about 9% in 24 hours to become the coin of the day.

"The weekend reclaim of $63K reads as stabilization inside a longer drawdown - Bitcoin is still down about 20.6% over 90 days and -41.9% year-over-year." - WalletInvestor market wrap

For investors, the bounce once again shows how closely crypto prices now track Middle East headlines and risk sentiment - the real direction test comes Monday when Asian markets open and flows return.

Russia Bans Mining in Moscow and Surroundings From August 15 Through 2032

According to Odaily, Forbes.ru and others, Russian Government Decree No. 936 (signed by Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin on July 25 and published on the official legal information portal on August 1) prohibits cryptocurrency mining - including participation in mining pools - in Moscow city, the Moscow region, and eight municipal districts plus the city of Lgov in Kursk Oblast, from August 15, 2026 through December 31, 2032.

The move aims to relieve pressure on the power grid. According to estimates cited by Russia's Energy Ministry, roughly 1 GW of electricity in Moscow and the Moscow region is consumed by mining; the ban targets 65 data centers with a combined 734 MW of capacity. Similar restrictions were previously imposed in energy-short regions such as Siberia - this is a continuation of Russia's "zone-based" mining policy: banned where grids are strained, allowed elsewhere.

For the industry, a ban lasting more than eight years removes one of the country's biggest population and compute centers from the legal mining map, potentially pushing hash power toward friendlier jurisdictions. Price impact was negligible - mining bans rarely move the tape the way flows do.

"Situational Awareness" AI Hedge Fund Blows Up: $45B to ~$10B

According to a Sina Finance report on August 2, the "Situational Awareness" hedge fund run by 24-year-old Leopold Aschenbrenner, a former OpenAI researcher, has blown up: assets under management collapsed from a peak of $45 billion to about $10 billion within days, a decline of more than 70%.

The chain of events is clear: heavy positions in semiconductor and AI stocks crashed, triggering margin calls on leverage as high as 400%; sources say Aschenbrenner was forced to sell his entire public leveraged book - including battered positions in SK Hynix and CoreWeave - at a discount to Citadel. Roughly two-thirds of the fund's assets sat in public long/short positions, with the rest in private stakes in AI startups including Anthropic.

The backstory matters too: Aschenbrenner shot to fame in 2024 with his macro manifesto "Situational Awareness," then raised a $225 million seed round in July 2024 to launch the fund - despite having no prior public-markets asset management experience (he earlier worked at the now-bankrupt FTX's foundation and was fired from OpenAI amid allegations of improperly sharing internal information).

Wall Street analysts call the collapse a textbook example of AI-investing volatility: the long-term industry thesis may still hold, but a 400%-levered speculation model does not survive liquidity stress. For ordinary investors, it is a case study in the dangers of crowded trades and extreme leverage.
