⚡ July 28 Evening Tech Roundup: Anthropic CEO Breaks Silence — "Never Advocated for Ban on Open-Weight Models," China Accuses US of "AI Hegemonism," Enigma Robotics Raises $71M Public Robotics Platform, Apple Retakes Global Market Cap Lead

1. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei Breaks Silence on Open-Weight Models

In a formal blog post published Monday, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei finally addressed the escalating controversy over open-weight AI models, stating unequivocally: "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models." The statement comes after the company faced widespread criticism for being the only major frontier AI lab that refused to sign an open letter supporting open-weight models — a letter initiated by Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang that has since gathered over 50 signatories including OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Meta.

Amodei argued that "open-weights models that don't have dangerous capabilities are a public good" — they cost nothing beyond the compute needed to run them, and provide genuine value to businesses, developers, and researchers. However, he pushed back against the letter's assertion that broader access necessarily helps defenders more than attackers.

"I worry that biology will have a strong attacker-defender asymmetry, where sufficiently capable models may be able to quickly weaponize pandemic-level viruses with widely available materials, whereas defense against these agents is a multi-year operational task in the best case," Amodei wrote. "Questions like this should be empirically answered by rigorous pre-release testing, not assumed in advance."

Instead of a ban, Amodei proposed three alternative measures:

  • Enforcing chip export controls to crack down on smuggling of advanced processors into China;
  • Cracking down on industrial-scale distillation — Anthropic previously accused Alibaba's Qwen lab of running the largest distillation campaign ever, using 25,000 fake accounts for 29 million interactions with Claude;
  • Mandatory pre-release safety testing for all sufficiently capable models regardless of origin or openness, while exempting less capable models from startups and academia.

Notably, Amodei expressed support for a global model safety testing organization, suggesting that "even the CCP would need to be on board" for it to be effective, but adding that limited cooperation on preventing AI biological weapons "may be possible because it is in China's interest too."

2. China Accuses US of "AI Hegemonism," Warns of Countermeasures

China's Ministry of Commerce fired back at Washington on Monday, accusing the United States of "AI hegemonism" and threatening countermeasures after senior US officials warned Chinese AI companies could face investigations, sanctions, and trade restrictions over alleged technology theft via model distillation.

The ministry stated that Washington was threatening Chinese companies with punishment based on "allegations alone" and characterized the entire affair as an effort to hold back China's technology sector rather than a genuine intellectual property grievance. It noted that model distillation is a widely used AI training technique and that the US had provided neither factual nor legal grounds for the sanctions it was threatening.

The industry remains sharply divided. According to OpenRouter data, Chinese open-source models now account for 46.4% of routed token usage on the multi-model API gateway, compared to 35.7% for US models — raising the question of whether a ban is even technically feasible at this stage.

3. Enigma Robotics: 17-Year-Old Microsoft Prodigy Raises $71M for Public Robot Platform

Enigma Robotics, founded by Jonathan Jacobi — who became Microsoft's youngest-ever employee at age 17 — has emerged from stealth with a $71 million seed round led by Index Ventures and Ribbit Capital. The round included participation from executives at OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, xAI, Cognition, and Wiz.

Jacobi began studying computer science at 13, completed his degree while still in high school, and by 17 became the youngest-ever employee at both Microsoft and cybersecurity giant Check Point. After serving as an officer in Israel's elite Unit 8200, he co-founded Enigma with fellow Unit 8200 veteran Gal Niv.

Alongside the raise, Enigma launched Robots.online — described as the world's first live public AI robotics experience. Anyone can remotely pilot 100 real AI-powered robots and have them handle physical objects in real time. The platform functions as both a public experience and a data pipeline: every session generates footage of human-robot interaction that feeds directly back into Enigma's foundation models.

The robotics foundation model space is increasingly competitive. Skild AI has raised $1.4B for a "universal brain," Physical Intelligence is reportedly raising $1B at an $11B valuation, and Genesis AI emerged with $105M. Enigma's $71M positions it to compete on interface design and public data collection rather than sheer compute scale.

4. Apple Reclaims Global Market Cap Crown at $4.95 Trillion

Apple has overtaken Nvidia to become the world's most valuable listed company, with its market cap reaching approximately $4.95 trillion after a 1.17% share price rise on Monday. Nvidia fell 4.99% to $196.51, dropping below the psychological $200 level, bringing its market cap to $4.78 trillion.

The catalyst for Nvidia's decline was a Wall Street Journal report that the chipmaker is in discussions to provide approximately $250 billion in financial guarantees for OpenAI's planned 10-gigawatt data center project in Ohio — a facility that could cost over $500 billion in total. The arrangement would see Nvidia simultaneously serving as supplier, investor, and financial guarantor, reviving "circular financing" concerns that have dogged the company.

Apple, by contrast, has been reducing its capital expenditure on data centers and AI infrastructure for three consecutive quarters, running most AI tasks on-device and routing complex requests through its Private Cloud Compute system. The company's restrained spending strategy has unexpectedly made it a safe haven amid growing market anxiety over AI infrastructure costs.

Apple is set to report fiscal Q3 earnings on July 30 — the first earnings report since the launch of its AI-powered Siri and Apple Intelligence features.
