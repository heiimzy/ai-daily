⚡ July 25 Tech Roundup: AMD Launches Full-Stack AI Assault on NVIDIA, $5B Anthropic Deal, OpenAI's $30B Data Center, Samsung Enters Humanoid Robotics

I. AMD Advancing AI 2026: The "Helios" Counterstrike

On July 23, AMD played its most comprehensive hand in years at Advancing AI 2026 in San Francisco, launching a complete product line spanning CPUs, GPUs, and rack-scale AI systems — a direct challenge to NVIDIA's dominance from data center to edge computing.

Helios Rack-Scale System: Taking on NVIDIA Vera Rubin

CEO Dr. Lisa Su unveiled Helios, a fully integrated rack-scale AI system combining 6th-gen EPYC Venice processors, Instinct MI455X GPUs, Pensando networking, and ROCm software. AMD claims Helios delivers 15% more AI compute, 50% more memory, and 50% more scale-out bandwidth than NVIDIA's Vera Rubin NVL72, while producing up to 30% more tokens per dollar. Helios is now in full production with shipments expected in Q3 2026. Early customers include Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta, Oracle, and Anthropic.

6th-Gen EPYC "Venice" Processors

AMD introduced four tailored EPYC 9006 series SKUs: the flagship SP7 with up to 256 cores for AI agent sandbox workloads (Q4 2026), SP8 covering 8-128 cores for general enterprise (H1 2027), and LP low-power model (codenamed Verano) with up to 72 cores for AI host nodes (H2 2027). AMD claims a 256-core Venice delivers 2.2x the throughput of NVIDIA's 88-core Vera CPU. EPYC now holds 46% server CPU revenue share with over 60% of Fortune 100 companies as customers.

Instinct MI455X GPU and MI450 Series

The MI455X delivers 34x higher token throughput and up to 18x lower token cost than its predecessor MI355X. AMD announced an aggressive annual GPU roadmap: Instinct MI500 in 2027 and MI600 in 2028. The MI450 series will power the Helios systems.

Ryzen AI Embedded X100: Chips for Robots

AMD launched the Ryzen AI Embedded X100 series for physical AI and robotics, based on Strix Halo silicon with up to 16 Zen 5 CPU cores, 40 RDNA 3.5 compute units, and an XDNA 2 NPU, supporting up to 128GB unified memory. The chips operate from -40°C to 105°C with 24/7 reliability for up to 10 years. Sampling began in June 2026 with mass production expected in Q4. AMD also introduced the Kria AI Robotics Developer Platform combining CPU, GPU, NPU, and FPGA compute on a single system-on-module.

Su raised AMD's AI infrastructure market forecast from $500B by 2028 to $1.4 trillion by 2030, approaching the size of the entire semiconductor market. "Agentic AI is creating a new growth vector — the CPU market alone will grow from $25B today to $200B by 2030," she said.

II. AMD and Anthropic Announce $5 Billion Strategic Partnership

AMD and Anthropic announced a landmark partnership at Advancing AI: AMD will invest up to $5 billion in Anthropic and deploy up to 2GW of AMD Instinct MI450 Series GPUs in Helios rackscale solutions to run Claude. The first gigawatt deployment is expected in H1 2027.

The engineering collaboration is equally significant: AMD will accelerate ROCm software stack development — its answer to NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem, long considered the primary reason developers default to NVIDIA hardware. Anthropic's compute strategy is now genuinely multi-vendor, complementing existing deals with Google/Broadcom (its largest), Amazon (Trainium chips, 5GW), CoreWeave (NVIDIA GPUs), and SpaceX (data centers). "We can map the right workloads to the right hardware," said Chief Compute Officer Tom Brown.

Microsoft also announced at the event that it will deploy Helios to power Azure AI services. Additional partnerships included Cerebras for hybrid inference, AT&T for enterprise AI, and Cisco for deskside inference solutions.

III. OpenAI Unveils $30B+ Georgia Data Center

On July 24, OpenAI officially announced Project Camellia — a $30B+ data center campus in Effingham County, Georgia. The 1,400-acre site within the Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub will house four buildings drawing 3.2GW of power under a 25-year Georgia Power agreement, delivered in phases from 2028 through 2032.

CFO Sarah Friar offered a candid assessment: "Compute is a very scarce resource at the moment. We're going up that vertical wall of demand and there's just not enough tokens available." A gigawatt-scale data center costs roughly $50 billion fully loaded and takes about three years to build, meaning decisions made today won't relieve shortages before 2028. Project Camellia is part of OpenAI's broader $750 billion infrastructure plan through 2030, alongside deals with Oracle (~6GW) and Amazon ($138B over eight years for Trainium access).

OpenAI committed to: not passing infrastructure costs to local ratepayers, using closed-loop water cooling, $80 million for local schools and healthcare, and $71 million in Codex credits for Georgia college students.

IV. Samsung Launches Humanoid Robot Division

On July 21, Samsung Electronics officially launched its Robotics eXperience (RX) Business Promotion Office, consolidating years of scattered robotics work into a CEO-controlled unit and declaring humanoid robots its next major business.

Shallow-π: A Factory-Proven Robot Brain

Samsung Research had already published Shallow-π, a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that compresses AI control steps to one-third of previous requirements and makes 17 decisions per second. In field testing, it achieved 95% success on ultra-precision water hose insertion tasks requiring sub-millimeter accuracy, coordinating 22 degrees of freedom across dual arms in just 40 milliseconds.

Industrial-First Strategy

Samsung's deployment plan follows a precise sequence: deploy advanced humanoids in its own global manufacturing facilities first, offer B2B solutions to other manufacturers second, and enter the consumer market last. The company aims to transform all manufacturing sites into "AI autonomous factories" by 2030. A "Robot Data Factory" at its Gumi facility will generate training data as a byproduct of real production lines.

Samsung hired Lee Dongkun from Hyundai Motor Group (parent of Boston Dynamics) as EVP to lead the division, and holds ~35% of Rainbow Robotics (developer of Hubo, Korea's first bipedal robot). The global humanoid robot market, currently $2-5 billion, is forecast to reach $15-40 billion by 2033. Competitors include China's Unitree ($16K/unit), Tesla Optimus (AI5 chips fabricated at Samsung's own Texas foundry), and Figure AI (one robot per hour production rate).
