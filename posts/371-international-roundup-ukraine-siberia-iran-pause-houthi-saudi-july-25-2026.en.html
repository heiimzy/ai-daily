⚡ July 25 International Roundup: Ukraine Drone Strike Hits Siberia Refinery 2,000 km Away, Kazakh Leader Tells Putin War Must End, First Night Without US Strikes on Iran, Houthis Retaliate Against Saudi Aramco

1. Ukraine Drones Travel 2,000 km to Ignite Siberian Oil Refinery

On July 25, Ukraine's Special Operations Forces (SOF) conducted a long-range drone strike against the Tyumen Oil Refinery in western Siberia, approximately 2,000 kilometers from the Ukrainian border. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed the operation, describing it as part of a broader campaign of "systemic sanctions" on deep Russian territory.

The SOF's statement said their drones "covered more than 2,000 kilometers to reach the target," noting that while the refinery's key processing units were protected by anti-drone netting, "this failed to save them." Footage circulating online showed flames engulfing part of the refinery. The underground Ukrainian resistance movement "Chernaya Iskra" (Black Spark) facilitated the strike.

The Tyumen Oil Refinery (formerly the Antipinsky Refinery) is one of Russia's largest independent refineries with a processing capacity of up to 9 million tonnes of oil per year, specializing in high-quality gasoline and diesel fuel. This marks the second successful Ukrainian strike on this facility in one month — the previous attack occurred on June 20.

Zelenskyy also confirmed strikes on a military-industrial plant in Kirov (hit for a second consecutive day), a Wildberries logistics warehouse in Yekaterinburg, a fuel depot in Rostov-on-Don, and multiple targets in the Caspian Sea — including a Russian missile boat, two internationally sanctioned cargo vessels (Port Olya 2 and Begey) used to transport military cargo between Iran and Russia, and a Lukoil offshore oil platform at the Filanovsky field.

"We achieved very strong results with long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea — including vessels used in military cargo shipments involving Iran, as well as a warship," Zelenskyy said. The strikes reportedly used upgraded long-range drones developed by Ukrainian company Fire Point, with an operational range of up to 3,000 kilometers.

2. Kazakh President Tells Putin to His Face: 'This War Must Stop'

At the Russia-Kazakhstan summit in Omsk, Siberia, Kazakh President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev delivered a remarkable public rebuke to Vladimir Putin, telling the Russian leader directly that the Ukraine war "must end."

"It is a shame, young people are dying... All this has to be stopped," Tokayev said in televised remarks while sitting next to Putin. He proposed "freezing" the conflict and returning to the Istanbul negotiating track before moving "toward a long-awaited peace." The statement — made just hours after Ukrainian drones struck deep inside Siberia — carries enormous symbolic weight.

Russia's Defense Ministry claimed its air defenses destroyed 328 Ukrainian drones overnight across more than 20 regions, from Belgorod and Bryansk to the Moscow region and the Sea of Azov. Ukraine reported that Russian forces conducted a "double-tap" strike on a Nova Poshta courier facility in Sumy, killing three drivers, while one person was killed and nine wounded in Zaporizhzhia.

Romania's defense ministry confirmed Saturday that its F-16 fighter jets shot down a drone that had breached Romanian airspace near the Ukrainian border. President Nicusor Dan confirmed the interception on social media.

3. First Night in Two Weeks Without US Strikes on Iran

After 13 consecutive nights of US airstrikes, Saturday July 25 morning brought a dramatic pause — the United States did not announce any new strikes on Iranian territory. Iran's Health Ministry spokesman Hossein Kermanpour wrote on X that "Iran experienced a peaceful night, with no reports of attacks." It was the first quiet night since July 12.

President Donald Trump told reporters at the White House that he had not yet decided on a new wave of major strikes. "We are talking to them right now. I think they're being serious — by far the most serious that we've seen them, but that doesn't mean we get there," he said. However he also stressed: "We're locked and loaded. We're ready to go."

When asked about an exit strategy, Trump outlined two options: "There's a military exit where we just keep going just the way we are, and we can even make it a heavier dose, and it's knocking out everything they have. Or there's a smarter strategy that you make a deal." The New York Times earlier reported that Trump met senior advisors to discuss expanding military operations against Iran, though no final decisions were confirmed.

Despite the pause in strikes on Iranian soil, the battlefield remained active. Iran's IRGC claimed it had destroyed multiple US MQ-9 Reaper drones at bases in Jordan — including 8 still in their packaging awaiting delivery — along with one F-15 fighter jet, one P-8 aircraft, one C-17 transport, and 8 refueling aircraft. The US military responded by deploying a B-1B Lancer long-range strategic bomber to strike IRGC targets — the first B-1 mission in nearly two weeks.

Iran's IRGC Navy also announced it had intercepted four vessels attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz via "illegal and unsafe routes" over the past 24 hours, forcing them to change course after warning shots.

4. Houthis Strike Saudi Aramco Facilities, Saudi Air Defense Intercepts Ballistic Missiles

The Red Sea front escalated sharply on Saturday. In retaliation for Saudi airstrikes on Yemen's Hodeida province on Friday, Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels launched dozens of ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones targeting Saudi Aramco oil facilities in the Red Sea cities of Jizan and Yanbu.

Houthi military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree announced the operation targeted "sensitive Aramco facilities" in both cities, describing it as a response to "the Saudi enemy's targeting of civilian facilities in Hodeida and Kamaran island." Saudi civil defense confirmed multiple sirens in both Jizan and Yanbu provinces due to "potential danger," later declaring the all-clear.

Greek security sources cited by Reuters reported that Saudi air defense systems intercepted two ballistic missiles launched from Yemen targeting oil refineries in Yanbu. Saudi-led coalition spokesman Maj. Gen. Turki Al-Malki stated the coalition had delivered a "firm, resolute, and proportionate military response," emphasizing all strikes targeted only "legitimate military targets that the terrorist Houthi militia uses to threaten commercial vessels in the Red Sea."

The Houthis had declared a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia on July 20, attacked two Saudi oil tankers on July 22, and have now directly struck Saudi refining infrastructure. Saudi Arabia has been forced to divert 100% of its crude oil exports through the Red Sea since April to bypass Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz. Analysts warn that with both global energy chokepoints now under threat, Saudi Arabia faces an unprecedented logistical crisis.

The White House National Security Council condemned the Houthi attacks. Trump had previously warned of "major military punishment" against the Houthis and their Iranian backers. The US and UK plan to hold high-level talks in London next week on forming an international coalition to protect commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz.

5. Trump's Dual-Track Strategy: Talks Continue While Military Option Stays on the Table

The Trump administration is pursuing a dual-track approach on Iran — pursuing diplomacy through Pakistani, Chinese, and Omani channels while maintaining military pressure. Pakistan's Interior Minister Eskandar Momeni visited Islamabad this week for exploratory discussions on restarting US-Iran talks, three Pakistani sources told Reuters.

On the nuclear front, US intelligence assesses that Iran's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei — who succeeded his father Ali Khamenei after the latter was killed on February 28 — is more open to developing nuclear weapons than his predecessor. Iran has reportedly moved some centrifuge equipment to the heavily fortified underground "Pickaxe Mountain" nuclear facility, which Trump has publicly threatened to bomb.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is scheduled to meet Trump in Washington on Tuesday, July 28. Meanwhile, Bahrain and Kuwait have reportedly sent warplanes to directly strike Iranian targets for the first time — a major escalation by Gulf Arab states. The Middle East stands at a critical crossroads: whether the diplomatic window has genuinely opened, or whether the region is on the verge of a far wider conflagration, will likely become clear within the next 48 hours.
