⚡ Aug 9 Tech Roundup: Xinshang Micro 350nm Lithography Passes Verification, Unitree IPO Subscription Tomorrow, South Australia Signs Historic OpenAI MoU, MateBook Pro S Teardown, SK hynix Ex-Employee Jailed

In the past hour or two, the broader tech world has delivered a packed agenda. Shanghai's Xinshang Micro said its self-developed 350nm stepper projection lithography machine passed process verification and won repeat orders from leading customers, another step for China's domestic semiconductor equipment; Unitree, the A-share "first humanoid robot stock," will open its IPO subscription on Aug 10 at RMB 150.80 per share, with DeepSeek and Tencent on the strategic investor list; on the other side of the planet, South Australia's premier signed a historic memorandum of understanding with OpenAI at its headquarters; Yu Chengdong released a teardown video of Huawei's MateBook Pro S HarmonyOS laptop, billed as the "world's lightest 14-inch laptop"; and a former SK hynix employee was sentenced to 18 months for leaking semiconductor secrets to a Huawei affiliate. Details below, with each source's publication time individually verified.

1. Xinshang Micro: 350nm stepper projection lithography passes process verification, wins repeat orders

According to Sina Tech, Shanghai Xinshang Micro's self-developed 350nm stepper projection lithography machine has passed process verification. The tool, model AST6200, has also secured repeat orders from leading industry customers, marking a shift from validation toward volume delivery. Jiwei (集微网) and Sohu Tech earlier reported that Xinshang Micro's lithography machines are "ramping up fast" as orders from top customers land one after another.

The 350nm node is not cutting-edge, but it serves a stable and important base of demand in power devices, analog chips, MEMS and specialty processes. Continued progress on mature-node lithography helps domestic fabs reduce reliance on imported equipment and deepen the self-sufficient supply chain. Analysts note that repeat orders for the AST6200 signal that customer qualification and fab adoption have entered a positive cycle, so the pace of subsequent volume ramp is worth watching (Source: Sina Tech, Jiwei, Sohu Tech).

2. Unitree: A-share "first humanoid robot stock" opens IPO subscription tomorrow at RMB 150.80

According to MyDrivers, Sohu and Sina Finance, Unitree's STAR Market IPO price has been set at RMB 150.80 per share, with the online subscription window opening Aug 10. Based on the offering size, the company is expected to raise roughly RMB 6.099 billion; one lot (500 shares) requires about RMB 75,400. The stock code is 688836 and the online subscription code is 787836, implying a listing-market value of around RMB 60.9 billion.

Notably, Unitree also disclosed its strategic placement list, which includes DeepSeek and Tencent, underscoring the market's enthusiasm for the humanoid-robot track. As a leading maker of quadruped robots and general-purpose humanoids, Unitree is widely seen as the A-share "first humanoid robot stock," and its listing could have a demonstration effect across the humanoid supply chain — robotics-related stocks were already hot topics in weekend discussions (Source: MyDrivers, Sohu, Sina Finance).

3. South Australia × OpenAI: state government signs historic MoU with the maker of ChatGPT

According to techAU and The Australian, South Australia Premier Peter Malinauskas, on his US trip, signed a memorandum of understanding at OpenAI's headquarters alongside OpenAI co-founder and president Greg Brockman. This is the first time an Australian state or territory government has entered a formal agreement directly with the creators of ChatGPT.

The MoU's core pillars include accelerating AI adoption in public services, local AI upskilling programs for universities and businesses, joint research on complex state-level problems, and attracting global venture capital and tech talent to Adelaide. The Premier stressed that any rollout will remain strictly human-led, bound by legal, privacy, security and procurement safeguards. South Australia has already attracted IREN's A$10 billion AI data center campus (up to 800MW of capacity), so the software-layer tie-up with OpenAI is seen as a key step in completing the state's "from power generation to large language model execution" stack (Source: techAU, The Australian).

4. Huawei: Yu Chengdong tears down MateBook Pro S HarmonyOS laptop, "world's lightest 14-inch laptop"

According to China.com and Sohu Tech, Huawei's consumer business chairman Yu Chengdong released a teardown video of the MateBook Pro S HarmonyOS laptop, personally revealing the engineering inside the device billed as the "world's lightest 14-inch laptop." The video highlights its lightweight structure, thermal design and system integration.

The MateBook Pro S runs HarmonyOS and emphasizes ultra-thin portability plus multi-device synergy. The teardown follows Yu's earlier viral moment at the launch event, when he briefly misquoted the MateBook Fold price as RMB 2,499 instead of RMB 24,999, and it extends Huawei's aggressive push for its "HarmonyOS PC" lineup. Against a soft overall PC market, Huawei is differentiating through the HarmonyOS ecosystem and extreme thinness to break into the premium ultraportable segment (Source: China.com, Sohu Tech).

5. SK hynix: ex-employee jailed 18 months for leaking semiconductor secrets to a Huawei affiliate

According to Lianhe Zaobao, a former SK hynix employee has been sentenced to 18 months in prison for leaking semiconductor trade secrets to a Chinese company. South Korean media report that the employee had received a job-hopping offer from a Huawei-affiliated company and printed about 6,000 pages of confidential semiconductor documents before leaving. The court found the conduct constituted infringement of business secrets and handed down an 18-month prison term.

The case is another sign of tightening oversight of semiconductor talent and technology outflows in South Korea. SK hynix and Samsung Electronics have both strengthened protection of core process and memory trade secrets, and prosecutors and courts have imposed heavier sentences in technology-leak cases linked to China. Analysts say that as global memory and advanced-process competition intensifies, battles over semiconductor intellectual property will only become more common (Source: Lianhe Zaobao).

From domestic lithography equipment scaling up, to the leading humanoid-robot maker debuting on the STAR Market, to a state government signing directly with an AI giant — this hour's tech stories map to three threads: semiconductor self-reliance, hard-tech capital markets and global AI infrastructure. Combined with Huawei's product cadence and tighter semiconductor IP enforcement, the competitive landscape of the global tech industry is being reshaped on multiple fronts at once.
