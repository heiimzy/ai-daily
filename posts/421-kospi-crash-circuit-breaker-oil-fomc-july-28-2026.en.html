⚡ July 28 Financial Wrap: KOSPI Crashes 6.8% at Open Triggering Circuit Breaker; Fed FOMC Meeting in Focus as Rate Hike Odds Hit 38%; Oil Plunges 7% on US-Iran Pause

South Korea's KOSPI Opens in Free Fall: Index Plunges 6.84%, Triggering 42nd Market Stabilization Mechanism of 2026

South Korea's benchmark KOSPI index suffered a brutal sell-off at the open on Tuesday, gapping down 5.26% to 6,400.27 before rapidly extending losses to 6.84% at 6,293.71, triggering a sell-side circuit breaker (sidecar) mechanism. This marks the 42nd activation of market-stabilization measures on the Korea Exchange this year and the eighth such event in July alone.

According to the Korea Exchange, at 9:06 a.m. KST, the most-traded KOSPI 200 futures contract fell more than 5% and sustained that level for over one minute, automatically activating the sell-side sidecar — suspending program-trading sell orders for five minutes. As of 9:14 a.m., losses were broad-based across heavyweight stocks: Samsung Electronics plunged 8.27%, SK Hynix tumbled 9.91%, SK Square dropped 9.76%, Samsung Electro-Mechanics cratered 11.32%, Hyundai Motor fell 5.46%, and Samsung Life declined 8.03%. The only major stock in positive territory was Samsung Biologics, up 0.65%.

Analysts attributed the crash to a confluence of three factors: overnight weakness in U.S. semiconductor stocks with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index falling 2.2% and Nvidia dropping 5%; the stunning IPO of Chinese memory chipmaker CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies) on the Shanghai STAR Market, which soared 466% on Monday to a market cap of $487 billion, stoking fears of global memory oversupply; and persistent geopolitical uncertainty surrounding U.S.-Iran tensions.

"The Korean market's extreme concentration in just two memory chip stocks — Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix together account for over 50% of the KOSPI weighting — has amplified single-sector volatility to unprecedented levels." — iM Securities Analyst

Foreign investors and institutions were net sellers of 486.8 billion won and 277.3 billion won respectively, while retail investors bought 752.9 billion won on the dip. Since the start of 2026, the KOSPI has triggered 13 circuit breakers (trading halts of 20 minutes when the index falls more than 8%), exceeding the total since the mechanism was introduced in 2000.

Federal Reserve's July FOMC Meeting Begins: Rate Hike Odds Surge to 38%

The Federal Reserve's Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) commenced its two-day meeting on Tuesday (July 28-29), only the second under Chair Kevin Warsh. Markets are laser-focused on Wednesday's 2:00 p.m. ET rate decision and Warsh's press conference at 2:30 p.m.

According to the CME FedWatch Tool, markets are pricing a 62% probability that the Fed holds rates steady at the current 3.50%-3.75% target range, and a 38% chance of a 25-basis-point hike — up sharply from roughly 10% just two weeks ago. This represents the highest pre-meeting hike probability since the Fed ended its tightening cycle.

The dramatic repricing reflects three compounding inflation pressures: first, the U.S.-Iran conflict pushed Brent crude above $100/barrel last week, reviving energy-inflation fears; second, the Trump administration's new 12.5% tariff on Chinese goods adds imported inflation risk; and third, AI infrastructure investment continues to drive capital goods demand, keeping core inflation stubbornly elevated.

Warsh's No-Forward-Guidance Strategy Creates Unprecedented Uncertainty

Since taking office, Warsh has abandoned the forward-guidance approach of his predecessor Jerome Powell, stating that "the FOMC should not judge for the market." At his June press conference, he declared, "I've got nothing more to say than the statement itself." This strategy has dramatically increased uncertainty around each meeting outcome. Since July is a non-projection meeting — with no Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) or dot plot released — every nuance in the statement language and Warsh's commentary will be heavily scrutinized.

Internal FOMC divisions are stark. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack have publicly called for "modestly higher interest rates," arguing that June's inflation decline may prove fleeting. Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and New York Fed President John Williams take a more cautious view, suggesting the current rate level is sufficiently restrictive. Markets expect potential dissenting votes if rates are held steady.

A Reuters survey of 104 economists showed 100% expect rates to remain unchanged at this meeting, though roughly three-quarters also see a hike as possible later this year. Goldman Sachs Research still forecasts two rate cuts in 2026, while Citigroup warns Warsh may be forced to pivot hawkish under inflation pressure.

Oil Prices Plunge Over 7% as US-Iran Pause Extends, Geopolitical Premium Evaporates

Crude oil prices suffered their biggest single-day decline in a month on Monday. WTI crude for September delivery settled 7.5% lower at $82.61/barrel, while Brent crude fell 8.7% to $88.36/barrel — a sharp reversal from last week when Brent briefly surpassed the $100 threshold.

The trigger: U.S. President Donald Trump confirmed on Monday that Washington is engaged in "deep talks" with Iran and that the pause in military strikes has entered its third day. U.S. Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz stated Sunday that the U.S. is giving diplomacy "some space." However, Trump warned that "time is short" and that military action would resume if negotiations fail to deliver breakthroughs.

The oil rout pushed the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield down about 4 basis points to 4.64%, and energy stocks sold off sharply: Occidental Petroleum fell 4.1%, ExxonMobil dropped 1.4%. Conversely, airlines and cruise operators rallied — American Airlines gained over 3%, Carnival rose over 3% — on expectations of lower fuel costs.

Market strategists see a binary path ahead for oil: a durable US-Iran ceasefire and gradual reopening of the Strait of Hormuz could send Brent back toward $80, while a breakdown in talks could propel prices toward $120/barrel.

Apple Approaches $5 Trillion Market Cap, Reclaims Global No. 1; Chip Rout Continues

U.S. stocks closed mixed on Monday. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.51% to 52,210.08, the S&P 500 edged up 0.02% to 7,413.18, while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.18% to 24,932.08. The session was marked by a pronounced sector rotation — money flowed out of semiconductors and AI infrastructure into software, consumer staples, and healthcare.

Apple shares rose over 1% to yet another all-time high, pushing its market capitalization toward $5 trillion and reclaiming the title of the world's most valuable company from Nvidia. Microsoft added 1.9% and Walmart gained 2.1%, anchoring the Dow at a one-week high. Meanwhile, Nvidia dropped about 5% — its worst single-day loss since June 5 — while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 2.2% to its lowest since May 20. Memory chip stocks were hit hardest: SanDisk plunged more than 11%, SK Hynix ADRs fell over 7% below their IPO price, and Kioxia ADRs have now dropped more than 57% from their late-June peak.

This week is also a super-cycle for Big Tech earnings. Microsoft and Meta report after the bell Wednesday, followed by Apple and Amazon on Thursday. Markets will closely scrutinize AI capital expenditure guidance and whether massive infrastructure investments are translating into revenue growth. S&P 500 Q2 earnings are expected to surge 39% year-over-year, with AI-related companies contributing the majority of growth. Of the 135 S&P 500 companies that have reported so far, 86% have beaten estimates.

Macro data is equally dense this week: the U.S. Q2 GDP advance estimate and June core PCE inflation data will both be released on Thursday (July 30). The composite signal — whether strong growth with sticky inflation, or cooling growth with moderating prices — will determine whether the market's hawkish Fed expectations hold or reverse.
