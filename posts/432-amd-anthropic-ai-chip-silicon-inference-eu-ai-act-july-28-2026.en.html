1. AMD and Anthropic Sign Multi-Billion Dollar AI Chip Deal — Nvidia's Hegemony Faces Its Biggest Challenge

The most important AI deal of summer 2026 has been sealed. AMD and Anthropic have signed an AI server cooperation agreement worth tens of billions of dollars. Anthropic will purchase up to 2 gigawatts (GW) of AMD's next-generation Instinct MI450 chips, with delivery starting in the first half of 2027. AMD plans to invest up to $5 billion in Anthropic upon meeting specific deployment milestones — marking AMD's first direct investment in an AI company.

The strategic significance of this deal far exceeds the dollar amount. It signals that one of the largest buyers in the AI model market is beginning to systematically build a compute supply chain independent of Nvidia.

The MI450: AMD's Flagship AI Chip

The MI450 is built on TSMC's 2nm process, delivering 40 PFLOPS at FP4 precision and 20 PFLOPS at FP8 — double the performance of current flagship MI355X. Memory has been upgraded to HBM4 with 432GB per chip and 19.6 TB/s bandwidth. AMD also unveiled the Helios rack-scale solution: 72 GPUs integrated per rack with 31TB of HBM4 memory. AMD claims Helios delivers 30% more tokens per dollar invested than competing solutions.

Claude Breaks the CUDA Moat in a Single Weekend

At AMD's Advancing AI 2026 event on July 23, Anthropic co-founder and Chief Compute Officer Tom Brown revealed a stunning detail: Anthropic's engineering team used Claude — entirely autonomously — to complete a full adaptation of the AMD Instinct MI355 chip and ROCm platform over a single weekend. An engineer simply set Claude to the adaptation task and let it run over the weekend. By Monday, they had the actual performance curve of Anthropic's leading model running on the new hardware, continuously improving throughout the weekend.

This event is widely seen as signaling the beginning of the end of CUDA's ecosystem lock-in. Anthropic's Compute Diversification Strategy

Anthropic has now secured over 12 GW of total compute capacity across four supply chains: up to 5 GW from Amazon AWS (based on Trainium chips), up to 5 GW from Google TPUs, 220,000 Nvidia GPUs at SpaceX's Colossus 1 facility (~300 MW), and now 2 GW of AMD MI450. Anthropic's ARR has soared from $9 billion in December 2025 to $47 billion by May 2026, with Q2 GAAP revenue expected to exceed $10.9 billion. This exponential growth makes single-vendor dependence untenable.

Combined with OpenAI's 6 GW and Meta's 6 GW orders, AMD's committed AI accelerator deployment now totals 14 GW. By 2028, a significant share of frontier model training workloads will run on non-Nvidia silicon.

2. Silicon Core Technology Unveils BALLBET AI Inference Chip Reshaping Market Landscape

On the morning of July 28, Silicon Core Technology (硅芯科技) officially demonstrated its next-generation AI inference chip — BALLBET — at a tech open day in Shenzhen. Built on a 6nm process with over 94 billion transistors, the chip supports INT8/FP16 mixed-precision inference, delivering 3.2x higher AI inference throughput while consuming 45% less power than its predecessor.

In benchmark tests running 70B-parameter LLM inference tasks, a single BALLBET card generates approximately 8,000 tokens per second, with 2.6x better performance-per-watt than competing solutions. Total cost of ownership (TCO) over three years is reduced by approximately 40%. The chip supports PCIe 5.0 interfaces with microsecond-level multi-card interconnect latency, enabling drop-in replacement without major infrastructure overhauls.

Early adopters are already seeing results: one major Chinese cloud provider reported a 62% reduction in inference costs and 28% lower response latency for search and recommendation workloads after deploying BALLBET chips. The chip directly competes with Nvidia's L40S and AMD's MI300X, employing a novel "sparse-aware architecture" that dynamically skips redundant computation.

3. EU AI Act Omnibus Enters Force — Article 50 Transparency Rules Apply From August 2

The EU Digital Omnibus on AI (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) entered into force on July 27, 2026, introducing key amendments to the EU AI Act. Most critically, Article 50 transparency obligations remain on schedule for August 2, 2026 — unaffected by the Omnibus's delay of high-risk provisions.

Four Transparency Obligations Effective August 2

AI Interaction Disclosure (Article 50(1)): AI systems that interact directly with people must inform users they are interacting with AI, unless obvious from context. Applies to all providers operating in the EU market.

Machine-Readable Content Marking (Article 50(2)): Generative AI systems producing audio, image, video, or text must mark outputs as artificially generated in machine-readable format. Systems already on the market before August 2 have until December 2, 2026 to comply.

Emotion Recognition & Biometric Notice (Article 50(3)): Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorization systems must inform exposed individuals.

Deepfake Labeling (Article 50(4)): Deployers generating or manipulating deepfakes must disclose artificial origin; AI-generated text for public interest must be labeled unless under human editorial review.

Violations of Article 50 carry fines up to €15 million or 3% of global annual turnover (whichever is higher). This creates substantial compliance pressure for all global AI companies operating in the EU market.

High-Risk AI Provisions Delayed to 2027

The Omnibus postpones full compliance for high-risk AI systems (Annex III) to December 2, 2027, and high-risk AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) to August 2, 2028. A new prohibition on AI systems generating non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material was added to Article 5, effective December 2, 2026, with penalties up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover.

The AI Office has gained expanded supervisory powers over vertically integrated AI providers. Industry analysts view this phased approach — transparency first, risk management later — as the EU's attempt to balance innovation with safety.
