China's First "Xianglong" Tunnel Boring-Blasting Machine Rolls Off Line in Wuhan: Merging TBM and Drill-and-Blast Methods to Crack a "Bottleneck" in Tunnel Construction

On the evening of Aug 1, according to Xinhua News Agency, China's first tunnel boring-blasting machine, "Xianglong," officially rolled off the production line at the CRSIG (China Railway Science & Industry Group) Jiangxia manufacturing base in Wuhan, Hubei Province. The equipment combines the two mainstream tunnel excavation methods - mechanized boring and drill-and-blast - filling a gap in integrated construction equipment for tunnels in complex geological conditions.

"Xianglong" was jointly developed by Tsinghua University and CRSIG. The machine is 70 meters long, with a boring diameter of 4.55 meters, a central cutterhead diameter of 1.2 meters, and a total weight of about 400 tons. It features an innovative hollow cutterhead structure, with the hollow cutterhead and central cutterhead working in coordination. It can flexibly switch among "blast-first-then-bore," "bore-first-then-blast," and "full-face boring" operating modes, combining the advantages of pre-blasting treatment with mechanized continuous excavation.

Qin Pengxiang, a special researcher at Tsinghua University's National Key Laboratory of Water Science and Water Conservancy Engineering, said the machine will solve a long-standing "bottleneck" in China's major engineering construction. Traditional TBMs frequently jam when encountering rock bursts, fractured zones, and large soft-rock deformation; the traditional drill-and-blast method is flexible but carries heavy safety management pressure and limited efficiency. Tests show that in ultra-hard rock conditions, after pre-splitting treatment, the boring-blasting machine can improve excavation efficiency by 30%.

Wang Chenghai, head of the CRSIG boring-blasting machine project, said the machine and its core components are fully independently controllable and will soon be deployed in a key engineering project. In the future, it will serve national major water conservancy and hydropower projects, as well as transport corridors and deep underground resource development.

CCC Certification for EV Chargers Becomes Mandatory Today: Uncertified Devices Can No Longer Be Made, Sold or Imported

Aug 1 news, according to Xinhua: the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR / National Certification and Accreditation Administration) today made compulsory product certification (CCC) management for EV power supply equipment (commonly known as charging piles) effective as of Aug 1, enforcing key safety indicator tests including electric shock protection, short-circuit protection, and fire and heat resistance, along with on-site quality assurance capability and product consistency checks, to firmly shore up the bottom line of product quality and safety.

As China's new energy vehicles continue to proliferate, EV charging equipment has become essential infrastructure for everyday travel. Starting Aug 1, EV power supply equipment without CCC certification may not be manufactured, sold, imported, or used in other business activities. For equipment already in operation before Aug 1, operators must strictly comply with the Product Quality Law and the Consumer Rights Protection Law to ensure their goods and services meet the requirements for protecting personal and property safety.

Going forward, SAMR will continue to strengthen supervision over CCC certification of EV charging equipment, cracking down on manufacturing and sales without certificates and on counterfeiting certification marks, to ensure strict entry control, strict process management, and strict risk control, creating a consumption environment where the public can buy and use with confidence.

DeepSeek-V4-Flash Official API Launches Public Beta: Inference Price-Performance Again in the Spotlight

Aug 1 news: The official API of DeepSeek-V4-Flash has entered public beta. As a lightweight member of the DeepSeek V4 family focused on high-value inference, V4-Flash has drawn attention for its performance in math, coding, and long-context scenarios, benchmarked against Zhipu GLM-5.2, and its highly competitive pricing has again fueled discussion about an "inference price war" in the industry.

DeepSeek models have long been known for open-source availability and high cost-effectiveness, repeatedly generating buzz in overseas developer communities. The official API launch of V4-Flash means competition among domestic LLM vendors in the lightweight inference track is intensifying further - with compute costs still high, whoever can push per-inference costs lower will gain an edge in scale scenarios such as agents and edge applications.

Industry analysts note that V4-Flash targets high-frequency, low-latency, large-scale inference demand, and its public beta performance will also pave the way for the broader V4 ecosystem. For developers, an additional high-value model option means application-layer cost structures can be further optimized.
