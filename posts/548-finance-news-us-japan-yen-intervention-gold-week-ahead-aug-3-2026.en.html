⚡ Aug 3 Finance Update: US and Japan Stage First Joint Yen Intervention Since 2011, Bessent's Notepad Leaks 'Buy JPY $5-10B', Gold Holds $4,000 Into NFP Week

Early Monday, August 3, global FX markets are digesting the biggest currency story in nearly 15 years: Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama is expected to announce that Tokyo and Washington took joint action in the currency market to arrest the yen's slide to 40-year lows - the first coordinated US-Japan intervention since 2011. Meanwhile, gold defended the $4,000 level all week, MUFG sees a broad range around $4,000 for 2026, and a data-and-earnings super-week lies ahead with US July nonfarm payrolls, the ISM manufacturing PMI and SpaceX's first post-IPO earnings.

US and Japan Stage First Joint Yen Intervention Since 2011; Yen Jumps 4.3% in Two Days

According to Reuters, two Japanese government officials said Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama is expected to announce on Monday (August 3) that Tokyo and Washington took joint action in the currency market to arrest the yen's slide to 40-year lows. One source confirmed the two countries took "joint action," adding that "the operation is still ongoing."

The market has already seen the impact: the dollar-yen pair swung violently over two sessions - on July 30 the yen surged 3.3% intraday from near 164, its weakest since 1986, the biggest one-day jump since December 2023; on July 31 it added another 1.32%, for a cumulative gain of about 4.3% to as strong as 157.28. Nikkei reported that Bank of Japan data suggests Tokyo sold as much as $58.97 billion buying yen in New York trading hours on Thursday; analysts estimate the total intervention at between ¥6 trillion and ¥8.45 trillion.

"This is the first joint FX intervention by Japan and the US in nearly 30 years. The New York Fed sold euros to buy yen on behalf of the US Treasury, with the transactions executed through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley." - Financial Times

The US shift in stance is striking: Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was photographed at a Friday cabinet meeting with a notepad reading "To Do: Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil" - and he said last week the yen "seems very undervalued to me." A source said the US Treasury also informed several banks on Friday that it might intervene and told them to "stand ready for future action." If confirmed, it would be the first time the US Treasury directly bought yen since 1998.

Why Washington Joined In: Hawkish Fed, Surging Treasury Yields

Behind the rare cooperation lies the shared pressure of a hawkish Fed and rising Treasury yields. On July 29 the Fed held rates steady by a 9-3 vote, with three regional presidents voting for an immediate 25bp hike - the most dissents since 2016; new Chair Kevin Warsh refused to provide forward guidance, and market pricing for a September hike has climbed to about 63%. J.P. Morgan chief US economist Michael Feroli moved his Fed hike call forward to December (25bp) and said the risk of a September hike is "real."

The rate differential has propped up the dollar and crushed the yen: the 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.22%, its highest since 2007, while USD/JPY pressed toward 163.24, the weakest yen since 1986. Analysts say Washington's willingness to help Tokyo reflects a deeper concern: if Japan had to sell its huge Treasury holdings to fund intervention, it could trigger a selloff in US debt and send yields even higher. In a rare English-language post on X, Japan's MOF said it had "a broad range of tools to address market liquidity needs," including access to the Fed's FIMA repo facility for temporary dollar liquidity without outright Treasury sales.

In cumulative terms, Japan has now spent roughly ¥18 trillion (about $114.3 billion) on intervention since late April, surpassing the 2024 record of ¥15.3 trillion for a full year. The BOJ held rates steady on Friday but signaled a strong chance of an early hike, and market odds of a September BOJ move have risen from 15% to about 30%. Osamu Takashima of Citi in Tokyo says the yen is unlikely to revisit 164 in the near term because Washington appears willing to help Japan defend its currency.

Gold Holds $4,000; NFP Week May Decide the Next Move

Spot gold closed the week near $4,040-4,045 an ounce (down 1.47% on July 31), defending the psychological $4,000 level four times in a week. A hawkish Fed capped the metal's upside, but Middle East tensions and soft US data underpinned it - Q2 GDP grew at an annualized 1.5% (vs. 2.1% expected) and core PCE eased to 3.3% year-on-year.

Per 21st Century Business Herald on August 2, a growing number of analysts view $4,000 as an attractive "value zone": Jeff Sarti, CEO of Morton Wealth, said gold holding $4,000 despite the prospect of Fed hikes suggests investors are starting to discount the central bank's hawkish rhetoric; some strategists argue that if the 2-year Treasury yield breaks below 4%, gold could target $4,500-4,750 by year-end. MUFG, in a report published August 2, expects gold to trade in a broad range around $4,000 for 2026 - softer yields and geopolitical demand should defend the $4,000 area, while renewed Fed tightening expectations would cap sustained upside. Fund flows are also turning: Chinese gold ETFs absorbed more than RMB 8.5 billion in July through July 30.

"Rates - especially real rates - are nowhere near high enough to overturn the case for holding gold. Long-end yields are rising, yet gold remains quite resilient; the market narrative may be shifting." - Jeff Sarti, CEO, Morton Wealth

The breakout direction likely comes from the data: if Friday's (August 7) payrolls are weak and short-end yields fall, gold could retest $4,100; if employment stays resilient, the $4,000 floor faces a bigger test.
