⚡ July 27 Tech Mega: Kimi K3 2.8T Open Weights Go Live — 594GB Download; Nvidia Invests $1B in Naver for 4.5% Stake; CXMT Lists on STAR Market, Surges 471% to Become Most Valuable A-Share Company

I. Kimi K3 Open Weights Officially Released: World's Largest Open-Source Model

On July 27, Moonshot AI delivered on its promise to release the full model weights of Kimi K3, making it the largest open-weight AI model ever created. The 2.8-trillion-parameter model is now available for download on Hugging Face in MXFP4 precision format, with the weight files totaling approximately 594GB.

Kimi K3 employs a sparse Mixture-of-Experts architecture with 896 experts, activating only 16 per token (approximately 1.8%), with an average of 48 billion active parameters. It introduces Kimi Delta Attention — a hybrid linear attention mechanism — and Attention Residuals for improved inter-layer information flow. The company claims a 2.5x improvement in scaling efficiency over the previous Kimi K2 generation. The model natively supports a 1-million-token context window and possesses multimodal vision capabilities.

On benchmarks, Kimi K3 scored 1,679 on the Frontend Code Arena, outperforming Anthropic's Claude Fable 5, and ranked first in 6 out of 7 domains including Brand & Marketing, Reference-Based Design, and Data & Analytics. It ranks fourth on the 189-model Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and first for Code WebDev on arena.ai. However, independent testing revealed a 51% hallucination rate on factual tasks — a metric not disclosed in Moonshot's official benchmark charts.

License and Deployment Requirements

Kimi K3 is released under a modified MIT license: commercial users with approximately 100 million+ monthly active users must display "Kimi" branding in their product UI. Minimum hardware requirements are 8× H100 80GB GPUs, though Moonshot recommends 64+ accelerators for production deployment. The model uses MXFP4 quantization-aware training and supports vLLM inference via KDA prefill cache.

Kimi K3 represents the first publicly available 3T-class open model, marking China's entry into frontier model competition at the highest level. Its performance approaches or exceeds top US closed-source models in specific domains, reshaping the global open-source AI landscape.

II. Nvidia Invests $1 Billion in Naver, Becomes Third-Largest Shareholder

On July 27, Nvidia and South Korean internet giant Naver announced a strategic investment agreement: Nvidia will acquire approximately 7.24 million newly issued Naver shares at 204,500 won ($140) per share, totaling $1 billion for a 4.5% stake. Upon completion, Nvidia will become Naver's third-largest shareholder after the National Pension Service and BlackRock.

The investment is structured as a third-party allotment — Naver's first such issuance in 22 years since transferring to KOSPI in 2008. Payment is scheduled for October 30. To offset shareholder dilution, Naver will cancel approximately 4.9 million treasury shares worth about 1 trillion won on August 3.

The strategic core of this deal is deepening the Nvidia-Naver AI infrastructure partnership. In June, Naver Cloud and Nvidia signed an agreement to jointly develop gigawatt-scale AI factories. This equity investment upgrades the relationship to a capital alliance: Nvidia locks in Naver as a long-term hardware customer while vertically integrating GPU supply, DSX platform software, and operational management through its equity position.

Korea's AI Infrastructure Strategy

Naver plans to use this capital to build the GAK Sejong AI Data Center, with a total budget of approximately $10 billion. Initial deployment targets 55MW, scaling to 200MW by 2028 with a long-term ambition of 1GW. Brookfield serves as the primary capital partner, providing up to $9 billion in project financing.

This Naver investment complements last week's SK Group's $750 billion AI semiconductor partnership, jointly forming Nvidia's comprehensive Korea strategy. Naver's stock fell 5.68% on announcement day, reflecting market concerns over near-term dilution versus long-term infrastructure optionality.

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, who signed the agreement with Naver founder Lee Hae-jin at Nvidia's California headquarters, stated that South Korea "has all the ingredients to become a global AI powerhouse." This is not merely a commercial investment but a critical piece of Nvidia's global AI infrastructure supply chain strategy.

III. CXMT Lists on STAR Market, Surges 471% to Become Most Valuable A-Share Company

On July 27, China's leading DRAM manufacturer CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies, ticker 688825.SH) officially began trading on Shanghai's STAR Market in a blockbuster debut that reshaped China's equity market landscape. Shares opened at 49.5 yuan, surging 471.59% above the IPO price of 8.66 yuan, pushing its market capitalization to 3.31 trillion yuan ($460 billion) — surpassing ICBC as the most valuable company on China's A-share market.

The IPO saw an initial issuance of 6.688 billion shares raising approximately 57.9 billion yuan. With full exercise of the over-allotment option, total fundraising could reach 66.6 billion yuan, making it not only the largest IPO in STAR Market history but also the third-largest in A-share history. A record 9.4288 million investors participated in the online subscription tranche, with a lottery ratio of 0.4714% — a new STAR Market record.

ByteDance $7 Billion Deal Revealed

According to media reports, CXMT signed a five-year DRAM supply agreement with ByteDance this month valued at over $7 billion. The massive order reflects ByteDance's enormous demand for AI computing infrastructure and validates that domestic DRAM chips have reached international mass-deployment standards in both performance and capacity.

CXMT is currently building two new fabrication plants in Shanghai and Hefei, which will double its production capacity to over 600,000 wafers per month. An insider source indicated that CXMT could surpass Micron in capacity by 2030 if current momentum continues. According to SemiAnalysis, CXMT's 2026 annual revenue may exceed $50 billion, with its global DRAM market share projected to rise from 9% in 2025 to 12% in 2027.

Industry Chain Ripple Effects

CXMT's listing triggered a broad rally across the semiconductor supply chain. Chengdu Huawei and CSSC Gas both surged over 10%, while Zhongke Feice rose 8% and Huafeng Test & Control gained 5%. Equipment makers such as Piotech and AMEC participated as strategic investors. Analysts at West China Securities believe CXMT's listing fundamentally rewrites A-share semiconductor valuation logic by filling the gap for a pure-play DRAM manufacturing champion.

CXMT's 2025 revenue reached 61.8 billion yuan with a return to profitability. H1 2026 revenue is projected at 110-120 billion yuan, representing year-over-year growth of 612-677%.

Market Implications

The three major tech stories of July 27 together signal an unprecedented capital and compute-intensive phase for the global AI and semiconductor industries. From Kimi K3's open weights challenging the frontier-model oligopoly, to Nvidia's strategic locking of Korean AI infrastructure, to CXMT deploying domestic DRAM capacity at scale for China's AI giants — Chinese AI supply chain influence continues to rise, semiconductor import substitution is entering mass-production phase, and capital intensity has reached historic highs. These signals collectively point toward a rapidly reshaping AI industry landscape in 2026.
