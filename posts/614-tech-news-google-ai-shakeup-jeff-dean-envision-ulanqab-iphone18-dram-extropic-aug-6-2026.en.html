⚡ Aug 6 Tech Roundup: Google AI Leadership Shakeup, Envision's World's Largest AI Compute Monolith, Apple Rushes DRAM for iPhone 18, Extropic's $75M Thermodynamic Chip

In the past hour or so, major stories broke across the global tech world: Google's AI leadership was shaken overnight — DeepMind chief Demis Hassabis is stepping back from daily management while chief scientist Jeff Dean is leaving to start a new company, sending Alphabet shares down; Envision Energy announced its "Xinghe Base" in Ulanqab — the world's largest AI compute super-monolith — has officially entered production; tech reporter Tim Culpan says Apple is urgently buying DRAM for the iPhone 18 Pro and its first foldable; and US startup Extropic signed a non-binding agreement worth up to $75 million to develop "thermodynamic smart chips." Here is this round's tech roundup.

1. Google AI leadership shakeup: Hassabis steps back, Jeff Dean leaves to found a startup

Google's AI organization is going through a rare leadership earthquake. Two bombshells landed almost simultaneously: Google DeepMind chief Demis Hassabis announced he is relinquishing day-to-day management duties to become Chairman of Google DeepMind and Chief Scientist of Alphabet; at the same time, Google's chief scientist Jeff Dean announced he is leaving to co-found a new company, Discovery Loop, with Sanjay Ghemawat, Oriol Vinyals, and Quoc Le, aiming to use AI to automate scientific research and engineering experimentation. Alphabet shares fell nearly 4% on the news — some Chinese outlets reported the drop at over 5%.

According to Google CEO Sundar Pichai's official blog post, Hassabis is not leaving the company and will still participate in major technical decisions — his role simply shifts from organizational management to long-term strategy, freeing him to focus on AGI, fundamental science breakthroughs, and global AI governance, while continuing his work with Isomorphic Labs (AI-driven drug discovery). His day-to-day management role goes to Koray Kavukcuoglu, currently CTO and chief AI architect, who is promoted to Senior Vice President of Google DeepMind, reporting directly to Pichai. Gemini model research, frontier AI research, the Gemini app, and developer teams will all fall under him. Kavukcuoglu joined DeepMind in its early days and has worked with Hassabis for more than 13 years, contributing to landmark projects like WaveNet and DQN. In an internal letter, Hassabis said the next-generation Gemini 4 model is progressing well, and that his office will move to DeepMind's new London building, Platform 37.

Compared with Hassabis's orderly internal transition, Jeff Dean's departure is a far bigger shock. Dean joined Google in 1999 and over nearly 27 years helped build Google Search infrastructure, the Google File System, MapReduce, BigTable, TensorFlow, TPU, and Pathways — a résumé that reads like half of Google's technical history. Discovery Loop's mission is to automate the scientific method loop — forming hypotheses, designing and running experiments, analyzing results, and iterating — using frontier AI and massive compute so that experiments can run in parallel at scale. All four co-founders are among the most-cited researchers in AI: Ghemawat is a foundational figure in distributed systems, Vinyals is a former Gemini tech lead and a key AlphaFold contributor, and Quoc Le is a Google Brain pioneer known for AutoML. Per foreign media reports, Google participated in the new company's funding as a founding investor and cloud services partner, with the seed round co-led by Khosla Ventures and Radical Ventures; the amount and valuation have not been disclosed.

The shakeup comes as Google's AI business faces mounting pressure: the flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro, originally planned for a June release, has been delayed multiple times and still has no launch date; two weeks ago Google raised AI capex without giving a timeline for new models, and its stock fell about 7%; and talent has been bleeding — Transformer paper co-author Noam Shazeer previously left for OpenAI, while Nobel laureate and AlphaFold lead John Jumper joined Anthropic.

2. Envision's Xinghe Base goes live: world's largest AI compute super-monolith in Ulanqab

On August 6, Envision Energy announced that it has built the world's largest AI compute "super-monolith" in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia, with the "Envision Xinghe Base" officially entering production. According to Shanghai Securities News, the super-monolith uses an ultra-high share of direct green-power connection, with a 120,000-square-meter footprint (about 20 standard soccer fields), million-card parallel capability, and million-P compute scale — making it the single AI data center with the strongest token output capacity in the world and a new record for AI infrastructure density.

The Xinghe Base is Envision's super-compute hub in the Gobi region, with a planned total capacity of 2GW — China's largest AI compute park and "token factory." It is the first project delivered under Envision's Mission Gobi plan, which targets 5GW of green AI compute centers in the Gobi by 2030. On the power side, the base leverages the region's strong wind resources, with self-built wind farms and dedicated transmission lines providing direct green power — giving it the highest green-electricity share among AI super-monoliths.

Zheng Zihao, GM of Envision's AIDC business, said the Xinghe Base is built for the urgent need of leading tech and AI companies to use domestic compute at scale, and represents a made-in-China "new energy + AI compute infrastructure" solution. He added that Envision plans to bring replicable Chinese AI data center solutions to regions worldwide facing urgent compute demand, weak energy infrastructure, and scarce power resources. As China's AI compute rapidly moves toward 100,000-card and even million-card interconnects, the Xinghe Base consolidates a million cards within a single campus — a new paradigm for integrating renewable energy with compute.

3. Apple is rushing to buy DRAM for the iPhone 18 Pro and its first foldable

Independent tech journalist Tim Culpan reports that Apple is urgently buying DRAM memory chips for the iPhone 18 Pro series and its first foldable phone (expected to be named iPhone Ultra). With less than six weeks until the fall 2026 launch event, Apple needs to secure supply for launch. The report notes that TSMC is fabbing Apple's A20 Pro chip with a mobile CoWoS packaging process, but the current bottleneck is a shortage of DRAM chips, which is constraining A20 Pro production capacity.

On the supply side, with Micron unable to meet demand, Apple is turning to SK Hynix, Samsung, and potentially CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies) — which, just a day earlier, was reported to have seen DRAM revenue surge 716% year over year in Q2 with the fastest growth in the world, and was also reported to have rejected Apple's price-cut demands. This DRAM scramble also echoes Yu Chengdong's recent warning that memory prices are soaring and phones may see broad price increases. The combination of memory price inflation and AI demand crowding out capacity is shaping up to be one of the biggest supply-chain variables for consumer electronics in late 2026.

4. Extropic lands $75M: thermodynamic chips aim to beat GPUs with far lower power

According to a report from Sohu Tech, Massachusetts-based AI hardware startup Extropic has signed a non-binding letter of intent with the US Commerce Department, making it eligible for up to $75 million through the CHIPS R&D Office (CRDO) to accelerate development of next-generation "thermodynamic smart chips." The chips are designed to perform probabilistic computation by exploiting the inherent thermal fluctuations of CMOS transistors, rather than relying on conventional digital logic — on key generative compute workloads, their energy efficiency could far surpass traditional GPUs while consuming only a fraction of the power.

Extropic founder and CEO Guillaume Verdon said: "The current AI era is accelerating exponentially, and energy is its fundamental constraint." Unlike GPUs that depend on cutting-edge process nodes, Extropic's thermodynamic sampling unit (TSU) chips are designed to run on mature semiconductor processes, so they can be manufactured using existing US fab capacity and reduce reliance on advanced overseas nodes. The company has successfully tested two X0 prototype chips at two different fab sites. The next milestone is Z1, its first production-scale thermodynamic chip, with a future Z1.5 to be built at US fabs — reportedly the first time the US would have domestic manufacturing capability for thermodynamic computing hardware. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick said the investment in domestic computing technology is aimed at strengthening the US semiconductor industry, expanding manufacturing capacity, and creating high-paying jobs. Note that the agreement is currently non-binding: actual disbursement of federal funds depends on final agreement negotiations and the achievement of project milestones.
