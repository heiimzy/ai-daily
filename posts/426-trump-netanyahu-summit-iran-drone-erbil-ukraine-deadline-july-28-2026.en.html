⚡ July 28 World Affairs: Trump Meets Netanyahu on Iran Nuclear Program, Iranian Drones Strike Erbil Near US Consulate, Ukraine Peace Deadline Slashed to 12 Days

1. Trump Meets Netanyahu at White House: Iran Nuclear Threat and Strait of Hormuz Top Agenda

President Donald Trump hosted Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House on Tuesday, July 28 — their first face-to-face meeting since the joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February. Speaking aboard Air Force One ahead of the summit, Trump asserted that "without the United States, Israel wouldn't exist" and credited his decision to destroy Iran's "nearly operational nuclear weapons" with saving the country.

White House officials outlined three core agenda items: restoring safe navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, restarting negotiations on Iran's nuclear program, and reconciling US-Israeli differences on Iran strategy. Netanyahu told reporters upon departing Jerusalem that Iran remained the "top priority" and that he would emphasize "both great determination and great wisdom" during the talks.

Analysts noted subtle but significant policy differences between the two leaders. Trump has paused military strikes on Iran to pursue diplomacy, telling reporters he is engaged in "very deep talks" while warning there is "not much time — either it goes fast or not at all." Netanyahu, by contrast, is expected by experts such as Alex Vatanka of the Middle East Institute to again push for "regime change in Tehran." Vatanka commented, "I don't think it will go down well with President Trump because, politically in the US, this war is hugely unpopular."

"At such a complex moment, you must show both great determination and great wisdom. We will discuss all items on the agenda, the most important of which is Iran." — Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu

Adding another dimension of complexity, Trump confirmed ahead of the meeting that he intends to proceed with F-35 fighter jet sales to Turkey despite Israeli opposition. "Nobody tells me what we should or shouldn't sell. Turkey has been a good ally," Trump said aboard Air Force One.

2. Iranian Drones Strike Erbil Near US Consulate: Multiple Explosions Rock Northern Iraq

In a major test of the fragile US-Iran ceasefire, Iranian drones struck multiple locations near the US Consulate in Erbil, the capital of Iraq's Kurdistan region, early Tuesday morning. According to Al Jazeera citing local police, at least 4 Iranian drones targeted the Khalifan and Soran areas northeast of Erbil, causing at least 7 explosions and large fires.

Police reported that two drones crashed near the targeted sites. In response, more than 10 US warplanes and drones were reportedly flying over Erbil. Iranian state broadcaster IRIB reported the destruction of an American drone in Al Anbar province.

The attacks come during a precarious three-day pause in US-Iran hostilities that began last Friday (July 25). While both sides have refrained from direct military exchanges, Iran's Revolutionary Guards turned away six "offending ships" attempting to cross the Strait of Hormuz on Monday. Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya Central Headquarters accused the US of conducting an "illegal maritime blockade" and warned that such actions "will be considered an expansion of the regional war."

Saudi Arabia also came under drone attack on Monday, with its defense ministry intercepting multiple drones launched by "Iran-backed armed groups" targeting oil facilities. Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi rebels separately claimed they attacked Saudi Arabia's East-West Pipeline in retaliation for Saudi drone incursions.

3. Trump Slashes Ukraine Peace Deadline: From 50 Days to 10-12 Days

President Trump announced Tuesday that he is dramatically shortening the 50-day deadline he had set for Russian President Vladimir Putin to reach a peace deal on Ukraine, reducing it to just 10 to 12 days starting July 28. "I think I already know the answer — what is going to happen," Trump said. "Fifty days, I wanted to be generous, but we just don't see any progress being made."

On July 14, Trump had warned that unless Moscow agreed to a peace deal within 50 days, the US would impose up to 100% in secondary tariffs on Russian imports and buyers of Russian oil. Dmitry Medvedev, deputy chairman of Russia's Security Council, responded that the ultimatum could bring Moscow and Washington "closer to direct conflict," calling the move a "threat and a step towards war."

The announcement coincides with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's journey to Washington for talks with Trump. Zelenskyy met UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham on Monday aboard the HMS Queen Elizabeth in Portsmouth, where they discussed joint drone production and Ukraine's urgent need for ballistic missile interceptors. Burnham announced the UK would provide Ukraine with the intellectual property for its "Stone Cloak" electronic warfare system capable of jamming Russian air defenses. Zelenskyy will also attend the funeral of the late Senator Lindsey Graham before meeting Trump.

Fighting on the ground continues to intensify. Ukrainian drones struck a steel fabrication plant in Chekhov, Moscow Oblast, overnight, while a blackout was reported in occupied Crimea's city of Feodosia. Russia's Defense Ministry reported intercepting 276 Ukrainian drones overnight. Rostov Oblast Governor Yuri Slyusar confirmed that a Ukrainian drone hit an apartment building in Rostov-on-Don, killing 5 people including one child.

"We are going to have to look, and I am going to reduce that 50 days that I gave him to a lesser number because I think I already know the answer." — President Donald Trump, July 28, 2026

4. Oil Prices Continue Freefall: Brent Crude Falls Below $87 to 8-Day Low

International crude oil prices continued their dramatic decline on Tuesday. Brent crude futures for September delivery fell 1.66% to $86.89 per barrel, the lowest in 8 days, while US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude dropped 1.76% to $81.16. Monday's session was even more brutal, with Brent crashing as much as 8.2% and briefly breaching below $87.

The selloff is driven by the rapid unwinding of geopolitical risk premium as the US and Iran maintain a three-day pause in hostilities. Trump has explicitly stated that "very friendly negotiations" with Iran are underway, while Iran and Oman discuss resuming commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Marine Traffic data shows 29 verified transits through the strait between July 24 and 26, including 21 vessels exiting and 8 entering.

However, analysts warn that risks remain. Iran's Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei denied direct talks with the US, insisting that "Iran will never allow the United States to determine the timing of war and peace." A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Iran would halt its attacks only as long as Trump's "self-imposed ceasefire" continues. The fragile truce could collapse at any moment if either side resumes strikes.

5. Syria Pursues Security Deal with Israel: Potential Historic Middle East Realignment

In what analysts describe as a potentially seismic shift in Middle Eastern geopolitics, Syrian President Ahmed al-Sharaa confirmed in an interview with Al Jazeera that Syria is actively pursuing a security agreement with Israel through international mediation, aiming to pave the way for "comprehensive peace."

Al-Sharaa made clear that any agreement would not affect Syria's claim to sovereignty over the Golan Heights — occupied by Israel since 1973 — signaling a carefully calibrated strategic move rather than a surrender. Secretary of State Marco Rubio initiated the process of removing Syria from the State Sponsors of Terrorism (SST) list on July 8, a critical step toward reintegrating the country into the international community.

If Syria joins the Abraham Accords — which have already normalized relations between Israel and the UAE, Bahrain, and Morocco — it would mark the most significant Middle Eastern diplomatic realignment since the Arab Spring. The US is pursuing a three-pronged strategy: lifting sanctions, removing the terrorism designation, and brokering Israeli-Syrian normalization talks. Regional experts describe this as the most consequential potential redrawing of the Middle East's geopolitical map in decades.
