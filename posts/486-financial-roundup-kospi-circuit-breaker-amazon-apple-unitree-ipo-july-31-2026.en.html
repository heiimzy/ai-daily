⚡ July 31 Financial Roundup: KOSPI Surges 15% Triggering Circuit Breakers, Amazon Q2 Crushes Estimates, Unitree Robotics Launches $620M IPO

KOSPI Surges 15% in Historic Rebound, Circuit Breakers Triggered

South Korea's KOSPI index surged 15.04% in early trading on Friday, triggering buy-side circuit breakers in what became the largest single-day gain in the index's history. As of 9:35 a.m. KST, the KOSPI stood at 6,434.96, up 841.40 points from the previous close. The KOSDAQ also triggered circuit breakers, rising 8.11%.

The explosive rebound came after the Korean stock market suffered its worst monthly crash since the 1997 IMF crisis, with the KOSPI plunging over 34% from its June peak — a decline steeper than even Russia's war-battered RTS index. Seoul authorities scrambled to stabilize markets with new leveraged ETF regulations, raising cash deposit requirements to 30 million won for single-stock leveraged products.

Semiconductor giants led the rally: SK Hynix soared 24.81%, Samsung Electronics jumped 20.77%, SK Square surged 29.92%, and Hyundai Motor gained 8.55%. Foreign investors net purchased a massive 55.994 trillion won, while institutions added 857 billion won. Individual investors net sold 57.353 trillion won. "Strong earnings from Microsoft and Amazon have eased concerns about the AI investment cycle," said Han Ji-young, an analyst at Kiwoom Securities. "The recent selling driven by deleveraging and forced liquidation appears to be stabilizing."

Amazon Q2 Crushes Estimates: AWS Growth Hits 37%, Stock Jumps 9%

Amazon reported blockbuster second-quarter results after Thursday's close, blowing past every Wall Street estimate. Revenue surged 20% year-over-year to $200.6 billion, beating consensus by over $4.1 billion. Net income skyrocketed to $62.6 billion ($5.75 per diluted share), far exceeding the $1.82 estimate, though this included $53.4 billion in non-operating income primarily from Anthropic investments.

AWS was the standout performer, with segment revenue reaching $42.2 billion — up 37% year-over-year, the fastest growth in 18 quarters. AWS's annualized revenue run rate has reached $169 billion. CEO Andy Jassy said, "AWS is booming, growing 36.7% year-over-year in Q2. Our AI and Chips businesses each eclipsed run rates of more than $25 billion." Advertising revenue also grew strongly at 26% year-over-year.

Amazon raised its 2026 capital spending guidance to $220 billion, up 10% from the February plan of $200 billion, driven largely by AI infrastructure, robotics, and satellite investments. Jassy cited rising memory chip costs as the key factor. Amazon's 2026 capex will far exceed 2025's $128 billion spend. The stock jumped over 9% in after-hours trading.

The performance stood in stark contrast to Meta, which fell 8% after reporting a 91% free cash flow collapse and weaker Q2 profit. "This is a tale of two AI investment strategies," said Stephen Evans, CIO at Pave Finance. "One company is growing profits while spending heavily; the other is letting costs erode its bottom line."

Apple Reports Record June Quarter Revenue, Stock Dips 4% on Services Miss

Apple reported fiscal Q3 revenue of $109.4 billion, up 16% year-over-year, setting a new June quarter record. EPS came in at $2.02, up 29% year-over-year — also a June quarter record. Gross margin reached 50.1%, boosted by approximately 2 percentage points from tariff refunds. However, Services revenue of $30.74 billion (up 12%) missed the $31.36 billion consensus, sending the stock down about 4% to $319.09 in after-hours trading.

iPhone, Mac, and Services all set June quarter records, with double-digit revenue growth across every geographic segment. This was Tim Cook's final earnings call as Apple CEO before handing the reins to hardware engineering chief John Ternus on September 1. Under Cook's leadership, Apple's market cap grew from roughly $600 billion to approximately $5 trillion. Apple stock remains up about 23% year-to-date, the best performer among the Magnificent Seven.

Unitree Robotics Launches $620M STAR Market IPO in Record 104 Days

Chinese robotics star Unitree Robotics formally launched its IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market on Thursday, aiming to raise 4.2 billion yuan ($620 million) at an implied valuation of 42 billion yuan. The company is offering 40,446,434 shares, representing 10% of post-IPO share capital. The subscription date for retail investors is August 10.

The 104-day approval process set a STAR Market record, underscoring Beijing's push to fast-track capital market access for strategic AI and robotics champions, even as broader domestic listings face heightened regulatory scrutiny. Unitree is the world's leading manufacturer of humanoid and quadruped robots, and showcased the GD01 — the world's first mass-produced manned transforming mecha — at WAIC 2026.

Asian Markets Rally Ahead of BOJ Decision

Asian markets broadly rose Friday, buoyed by Wall Street's tech rally (Nasdaq +2.78% to 25,122, S&P 500 +1.66% to 7,437) and suspected Japanese yen intervention. Japan's Nikkei 225 climbed 0.71% to 61,867, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng edged up 0.20% to 25,858. China's Shanghai Composite traded mixed, closing at 3,804 after the Politburo pledged to "enhance capital market resilience and confidence" through deeper investment and financing reforms.

The Bank of Japan is set to announce its policy decision later Friday, with consensus expecting rates to remain at 1% — the highest level since 1995. Tokyo's core CPI accelerated to 1.9% in July (above the 1.8% consensus), supporting further tightening. All eyes are on Governor Ueda's press conference for signals on whether the next hike comes in October or December. A Reuters poll shows 86% of economists expect rates to reach 1.25% by year-end.

The yen surged over 3% against the dollar on Thursday, with USD/JPY plunging from above 163 to below 158, amid suspected intervention by Japanese authorities — the first since Japan spent ¥11.7349 trillion ($71.7 billion) in April-May. The dollar index dropped below 100 to 99.97. In commodities, spot gold rebounded to around $4,112/oz. U.S. Treasury yields remained elevated, with the 10-year at 4.66% and the 30-year at 5.21%, near 2007 highs. WTI crude traded around $84/barrel.
