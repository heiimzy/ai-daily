⚡ July 25 Financial Roundup: Bitcoin ETFs Record $225M Outflow to End Seven-Day Streak, Russia Passes Landmark Crypto Legalization, Kazakhstan Oil Output Crashes 21% on CPC Shutdown, Hormuz Crossings Plunge 60%

1. Bitcoin ETF Inflow Streak Ends Abruptly: BlackRock IBIT Leads $225M Reversal, BTC Falls to $64K

The US spot Bitcoin ETF market experienced a sharp reversal on July 24. According to Farside Investors data, the 11 listed spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a combined net outflow of $225 million, ending a seven-session inflow streak that had attracted nearly $1 billion.

The selling was heavily concentrated: BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) alone accounted for $202.5 million — nearly 90% of the total outflow. Bitwise BITB shed $7 million, Fidelity FBTC lost $5.6 million, and ARK 21Shares ARKB saw $4.3 million in redemptions. Morgan Stanley's MSBT was the only fund to record positive flows, adding $5 million.

IBIT's massive single-day redemption suggests large institutional investors are reducing risk exposure. Since its launch, IBIT has accumulated over $60.6 billion in net inflows.

Bitcoin's price dropped accordingly, falling from its weekly high near $67,000 to around $64,109, a two-day decline of over 3%. On-chain data shows IBIT transferred approximately 3,126 BTC (valued at ~$203 million) to Coinbase Prime, confirming that sell pressure is materializing.

The macro backdrop adds multiple headwinds: the 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.71% (an 18-month high), escalating US-Iran conflict pushed Brent crude briefly above $100, and stalled progress on the CLARITY Act crypto regulatory bill before Congress's August recess all weighed on crypto investor sentiment.

2. Russia's State Duma Passes Comprehensive Crypto Legalization Bill

On July 24, Russia's State Duma passed the "On Digital Currency and Digital Rights" bill in its third and final reading, establishing the country's first full legal framework for cryptocurrency trading. The law takes effect on September 1, 2026.

Key provisions include:

  • Licensed operations: Only companies registered on the central bank's special registry may operate crypto exchanges and custody services, with a transition period until July 2027 for existing platforms
  • Retail limits: Individual investors face an annual purchase cap of approximately 300,000 rubles (~$3,500) through licensed intermediaries; qualified and institutional investors are not capped
  • Not legal tender: Crypto remains non-legal tender; the ruble stays the sole official means of payment
  • Cross-border exceptions: The existing ban on crypto for domestic payments is retained, but exceptions are made for cross-border trade settlements, mining settlements, and security tokens

This legislation is a milestone for Russian financial history. After years in a legal gray area, digital assets will now operate within a formal regulatory framework. Analysts believe the move could attract significant crypto capital that was previously outside the system, while providing Russia with new channels for cross-border settlements amid international sanctions.

3. Triple Oil Supply Shock: Kazakhstan CPC Pipeline Halts, Hormuz Crossings Crash 60%

Kazakhstan: CPC Black Sea Terminal Suspended, Tengiz Output Halved

Kazakhstan's Energy Ministry confirmed Thursday that the Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC) has stopped receiving Kazakh crude and suspended loadings at its Black Sea terminal following a series of Ukrainian drone attacks on tankers. The consequences are severe:

  • Kazakhstan's total oil and condensate output plunged from a July average of 2.07 million bpd to 1.63 million bpd — a 21% drop
  • Chevron-operated Tengiz, Kazakhstan's largest oilfield, saw output more than halve from 925,000 bpd to 406,000 bpd — a 56% decline
  • Kazakhstan is losing an estimated $32 million per day in oil revenue
  • The CPC pipeline carries ~2% of global daily crude supply and handles over 80% of Kazakhstan's oil exports

The CPC consortium includes Chevron, ExxonMobil, and other international oil majors. At least five tankers have been attacked near the CPC terminal since July 7, including the Chevron-chartered Yasa Polaris (July 7), Nordic Zenith (July 17), and ASIA and NISSOS IOS (July 19).

Strait of Hormuz: Kpler Reports 60% Plunge in Crossings

Maritime traffic monitor Kpler reported that the Strait of Hormuz saw only six vessel crossings on Thursday, a 60% decline from the previous day. This data confirms that the world's most critical energy transit chokepoint remains effectively blockaded despite sporadic diplomatic efforts.

Brent crude briefly broke above $100/barrel on Thursday before retreating to the $96-97 range on Friday, still posting a weekly gain of approximately 13.5%. Goldman Sachs maintains an $80 Q4 base case but warns Brent could exceed $120 if Hormuz disruptions persist and risks spread to the Bab el-Mandeb Strait and Suez Canal routes.

Global oil markets are facing an unprecedented triple supply route disruption: the Strait of Hormuz (20% of global seaborne oil), the Bab el-Mandeb Red Sea passage, and the Black Sea CPC pipeline. IEA data shows global oil inventories at multi-decade lows, with strategic reserves already drawn down by 163 million barrels between March and May.

4. Emerging Markets Under Pressure: Philippine Peso Hits Record Low 61.847, Korean Retail Investors Flee to US Stocks

Philippine Peso Breaches 62

The Philippine peso weakened further on Friday, closing at 61.847 to the dollar — a new all-time low. The currency's decline is driven by multiple factors: new US tariffs of 12.5% on Philippine exports, Brent crude breaching $100 which raises import inflation, and ongoing capital outflows from emerging markets.

The Philippine Stock Exchange index (PSEi) edged down 0.03% to 6,281.01, ending the week in negative territory. RCBC chief economist Michael Ricafort noted that the full impact of US tariffs on 60 trading partners (10-12.5%) will take time to materialize.

Korean Retail Investors Quadruple US Stock Purchases

According to the Korea Financial Investment Association, Korean retail investors' net purchases of US stocks from July 1-24 reached $2.53 billion, roughly four times the $632.96 million recorded for all of June. This exodus contrasts sharply with domestic market deposits, which fell from an all-time high of 139 trillion won on June 4 to below 104 trillion won — the lowest level in approximately five months.

South Korea's KOSPI index closed Friday at 6,690.62, down 5.7% for the week. By individual security, the top net purchase by settlement amount was the Direxion Daily Semiconductors Bull 3X Shares ETF ($1.5 billion), followed by SK Hynix ADRs ($613.67 million), and the Direxion Daily MSCI South Korea Bull 3X ETF ($213.37 million).
