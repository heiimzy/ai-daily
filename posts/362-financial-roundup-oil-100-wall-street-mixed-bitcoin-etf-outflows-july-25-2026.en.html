⚡ July 25 Financial Roundup: Brent Crosses $100 Then Retreats, Nasdaq Falls for Second Week, Bitcoin Slips Below $64K on $225M ETF Outflows

🛢️ Oil: Brent Breaks $100 Barrier Then Retreats 3.9% on Pakistan-Brokered Peace Hopes

Global energy markets experienced a week of extreme volatility. Brent crude surged past the $100 per barrel psychological threshold on Thursday (July 23), touching an intraday high of $102 — the highest level in eight weeks — before retreating 3.88% on Friday to settle at $96.78. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude closed Friday at $89.31, down 3.12%. Despite Friday's pullback, Brent still posted a weekly gain of approximately 9.85%, with WTI up about 9.21% for the week.

The core driver of oil price volatility was geopolitical escalation. On Thursday, Iran-backed Houthi rebels struck two Saudi oil tankers — the Encelia and Layla — using drones and missiles in the Red Sea. The Encelia was set ablaze, marking the first time since the Iran war began that ship attacks spread beyond the vicinity of the Strait of Hormuz, opening a dangerous new front in the conflict.

President Trump responded by threatening "major military punishment," sending Brent rapidly past $100. However, Friday's retreat was driven by diplomatic developments — Reuters reported that Pakistan, with China's backing, was working to revive negotiations between the U.S. and Iran. A Pakistani government official told Reuters: "China is dissatisfied, believing that Iran's attacks on Gulf countries and the blockade of the Strait of Hormuz are harming its own interests."

In an analysis published today (July 25), Fortune magazine cited multiple analysts warning that Trump has "essentially no remaining levers" to pull to lower oil prices. The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) has dwindled to 311 million barrels — near 40-year lows and down nearly 60% from its 2009 peak of 726 million barrels. Pickering Energy Partners founder Dan Pickering warned that if both the Bab el-Mandeb strait and the Strait of Hormuz remain nearly shuttered, oil prices could easily rise back toward the late-April high of $124 per barrel in August.

📊 Wall Street: Mixed Close, Nasdaq Down for Second Week, Semiconductor Bloodbath

U.S. stocks closed mixed on Friday (July 24). The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 235.60 points (+0.46%) to 51,947.25. The S&P 500 edged up 3.68 points (+0.05%) to 7,411.98. The tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell 161.87 points (-0.64%) to 24,975.82.

All three major indices finished the week in the red: the Dow fell 0.38% for its third consecutive weekly decline, while the Nasdaq dropped 2.91% and the S&P 500 fell 0.61%, both for their second straight weekly loss.

The semiconductor sector was hit hardest. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) plunged 4.25%, with all 30 component stocks closing lower. Memory chip stocks suffered a bloodbath:

  • SanDisk -10.79%
  • SK Hynix -8.81%
  • ARM -8.14%
  • Intel -7.89% (despite beating Q2 earnings estimates, concerns over foundry customer acquisition)
  • Micron -6.99%
  • Western Digital -6.9%
  • Seagate -6.75%

Among mega-cap tech, Apple bucked the trend with a 3.53% gain to provide the Dow's biggest lift. Nvidia fell 0.92%, Tesla dropped 2.08%, Meta slipped 1.80%, and Amazon declined 0.66%. Google rose 0.24% while Microsoft edged up 0.03%.

Analysts attributed the broad tech weakness to Alphabet's decision to raise its 2026 capex guidance by 8% to $195-205 billion, fueling concerns about AI investment returns. Credit default swap spreads on hyperscaler companies hit record highs, even as overall credit markets remained near their tightest levels in years.

₿ Bitcoin Slips Below $64K: $225M ETF Outflow Ends 7-Day Inflow Streak

The cryptocurrency market hit headwinds this week. Bitcoin fell to approximately $64,038 on Friday, down 2.06% in 24 hours and retreating from the $67,000 level touched earlier in the week. According to SoSoValue data, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $225.18 million in net outflows on July 23, ending a seven-day inflow streak that had attracted nearly $1 billion.

BlackRock's IBIT accounted for the bulk of the damage, with a single fund shedding $202.5 million. Bitwise's BITB and Fidelity's FBTC also bled smaller amounts. Morgan Stanley's MSBT was the lone fund with positive flows, adding $5 million.

The derivatives market felt the pressure too. Bitcoin long positions saw $87 million in liquidations on July 23, contributing to $312 million in total crypto leveraged position liquidations. Approximately 19,000 Bitcoin options contracts worth $1.2 billion expired, with the max pain level at $64,500.

Technically, analysts identified $63,700-$64,000 as the critical support zone. A break below this level could expose $62,000 and potentially the $60,000 psychological level.

🏛️ Fed Rate Hike Odds Surge: September Probability Hits 80%, 10Y Yield Hits 4.71%

The oil price surge is dramatically reshaping expectations for Fed monetary policy. The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield hit 4.71% on Friday, its highest since January 2025, while the 30-year yield approached 5.18% — levels not seen in nearly 19 years. Markets are now pricing in over 80% probability of at least one rate hike by year-end, with September hike odds jumping from ~68% a week ago to ~80%.

The CME FedWatch tool now shows a ~35.8% probability of a 25-basis-point hike at next week's FOMC meeting (July 28-29), up sharply from 12.8% just one week ago. J.P. Morgan chief U.S. economist Michael Feroli expects the Fed to hold rates steady next week but forecasts at least two hawkish dissents.

In his first semi-annual Monetary Policy Report, new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh listed AI infrastructure construction alongside tariffs and geopolitical conflict as three major inflation drivers for the first time. The report uses the phrase "price stability" 14 times — up from just 5 in the prior year's report — and removed references to "average inflation targeting" from the Powell era, signaling a decisive shift toward a more hawkish framework.

Global bond markets are under synchronized pressure. German 10-year Bund yields rose above 3%, while Japan's 10-year yield climbed to 2.82%. UK 30-year gilt yields held above 5% all week for the first time since 2008.

💴 Yen Sinks to 40-Year Low: 163.84 Approaching 164, Finance Minister Reaffirms Intervention Readiness

In forex markets, the Japanese yen suffered its worst week in over two months. USD/JPY touched 163.98, the highest level since November 1986. Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama reiterated on Friday the government's readiness to take "decisive action" in the foreign exchange market.

The dollar index rose approximately 0.7% for the week, on track for its biggest weekly gain in five weeks, buoyed by rising oil prices and expectations of Fed tightening. EUR/USD slipped to 1.1369, down about 0.6% for the week, after the ECB held rates steady on Thursday while keeping the door open for a September hike — now priced at 70.8% probability.

The U.S. Treasury Department on Thursday issued an unusual statement calling "excess volatility in the yen undesirable," effectively supporting Japan's intervention posture.

🌐 US-EU Trade War Escalates: Trump Threatens Section 301 Investigation

Beyond the ongoing U.S.-China trade tensions, transatlantic friction is intensifying. The European Commission on Thursday fined Google €890 million ($1 billion) for violating the Digital Markets Act (DMA), ruling that the company gave preferential treatment to its own services in search results and restricted app developers from directing users to external platforms.

Trump responded with a lengthy Truth Social post, announcing an immediate "Section 301 Investigation" into what he called the "ROBBING of American Companies." He wrote: "The United States of America is not a 'PIGGYBANK' for Europe, nor will we allow it to be!" and warned that "a substantial TARIFF" would be placed on the EU "at the earliest possible moment."

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer had previously warned that Brussels' enforcement actions could unravel the trade deal struck in Turnberry, Scotland, last year that capped U.S. tariffs on European goods. The warning came just one day after the Trump administration imposed double-digit tariffs on imports from around 60 countries under forced labor provisions, replacing the previous 10% global tariffs struck down by the Supreme Court.

📈 Week Ahead: The Super Week — FOMC Meeting + Big Tech Earnings

Next week shapes up as one of the most consequential market weeks of the year:

  • July 28-29: Fed FOMC rate decision — the first major policy test of the Warsh era
  • Microsoft, Meta (Wednesday), Apple, Amazon (Thursday): Four of the "Magnificent Seven" report earnings, representing 34% of S&P 500 market cap
  • Bank of Japan and Bank of England: Rate decisions on the same week
  • OPEC+: Early August meeting, expected to approve another ~188,000 bpd production increase

Goldman Sachs' trading desk reported that overall flow was 12.6% sell-side, with long-only money showing a 21% selling skew — "there is barely any bid." The S&P 500 has fallen below its 50-day moving average, with market makers in negative gamma territory. Implied weekly volatility for the S&P 500 has reached 1.85%.

In this "summer of turmoil," global equities face a quadruple stress test of oil prices, inflation, interest rates, and geopolitical risk — while the sustainability of AI capital spending hangs over the tech sector like the sword of Damocles.
