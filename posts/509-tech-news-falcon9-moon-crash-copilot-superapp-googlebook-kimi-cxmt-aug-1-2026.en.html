SpaceX's Falcon 9 Upper Stage Is About to Crash Into the Moon — and the Flash Could Be Visible From Earth

Wired reports (7:38 PM ET / 23:38 GMT) that a spent SpaceX Falcon 9 upper stage is expected to hit the Moon's sunlit western limb near Einstein crater at around 6:34 am UTC on August 5, traveling at more than 5,400 mph (about 8,700 km/h). If the forecast holds, it would be the first impact flash ever recorded on the sunlit face of the Moon — with a debris plume tall enough to potentially spot from Earth with a small telescope. The prediction rests on two preprint studies, including one posted to arXiv on July 27 led by William Jo, a doctoral candidate at UT Austin's Cockrell School of Engineering. Jo ran a high-resolution physics simulation of what happens when 3,900 kilograms of hollow metal strikes the lunar surface. UT Austin professor David Goldstein, who supervised the work, says the stage is "like an empty eggshell": instead of burrowing in like a cannonball, it collapses from its edges inward, throwing up a broad curtain of soil up to 183 kilometers wide plus a thin, faster spike nearly straight up — displacing about 12,700 kilograms of debris in all. Astronomer Laurence Trafton estimates that a plume reaching 50 kilometers past the limb would span about 28 arcseconds, "easily resolved by a small telescope." But the plume will be roughly 30 to 300 times fainter than the blazing Moon beside it, demanding a very steady mount; it should peak about 90 seconds after impact, and the US Southeast offers the best viewing odds. Bill Gray of Project Pluto first flagged the impact. The stage carried two landers to the Moon in January 2025. Scientists hope to read the Moon by the mess it throws up — NASA slammed probes into the Moon in 1999 (Lunar Prospector) and 2009 (LCROSS) for similar purposes, while a 2022 upper-stage impact widely billed as a Falcon 9 turned out to be most likely a discarded Chinese booster. With both the US and China racing to put humans back on the Moon, the dust kicked up by stray rockets is becoming a real hazard for future lunar bases.

Microsoft to Launch a Copilot AI 'Super App' Later This Year

cnBeta reports (7:54 AM Beijing / 23:54 GMT) that Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella disclosed on the company's earnings call that Microsoft plans to launch a new Copilot AI "super app" for consumers and enterprises later this year, combining conversational chat, code writing, the collaboration tool Cowork, and a new class of autonomous agents called Autopilots in a one-stop experience. The move formally puts Microsoft in the AI "super app" race alongside OpenAI's ChatGPT Work and Anthropic's Cowork inside Claude — the shared goal being to keep users inside a comprehensive app covering many scenarios, rather than a single chat or coding tool. Microsoft has roughly 450 million commercial seats, but only about 30 million paid Copilot users, still trailing ChatGPT. The super app is seen as a key step to boost retention and commercial conversion, and a major card in Microsoft's bid to regain ground on the AI consumer front.

US Lawmakers Investigate DoorDash's Use of Moonshot AI's Kimi K2.6 Model

The South China Morning Post reports (6:53 am, Aug 1, Hong Kong time / 22:53 GMT) that House Select Committee on China chairman John Moolenaar and House Committee on Homeland Security chairman Andrew Garbarino sent a letter on Friday to DoorDash co-founder and CEO Tony Xu, requesting by August 14 a list of every Chinese AI model DoorDash uses and details of security tests conducted, and asking DoorDash staff responsible for AI infrastructure, software security, model evaluation, procurement, and legal or compliance review to appear for an in-person briefing by August 21. The trigger: Xu had previously disclosed that DoorDash experimented with a model from Chinese startup Moonshot AI, developer of the Kimi models (Kimi K2.6). The lawmakers acknowledged that US companies may adopt PRC-developed open-weight models for "competitive capabilities, lower costs, greater customisation, and alternatives to reliance on a small number of proprietary model providers" — but stressed that "those practical considerations do not eliminate the need for risk-based safeguards or diminish the national security concerns associated with growing dependence on models developed by entities subject to PRC jurisdiction." It is Washington's latest move against the accelerating adoption of Chinese AI models by US companies, and another flashpoint in US-China AI friction.

Lenovo's First Googlebooks Have Leaked — Two Laptops and a 2-in-1 Tablet

Engadget reports (10:09 PM GMT; IT Home followed at 6:43 AM Beijing / 22:43 GMT), citing leaked images shared by Android Headlines, that Lenovo appears to be working on the first "Googlebook" devices. Google announced Lenovo as a launch partner when it introduced its new Android-based take on ChromeOS in May, but what hardware the platform would run on had largely been a mystery — until now. The images show three devices with Googlebook branding on their keyboards: two laptop sizes in the style of Lenovo's Chromebook Plus 14, and a 2-in-1 tablet with a kickstand and keyboard resembling the IdeaPad Pro Gen 2. All three are stark white with a light strip that looks like a "Glowbar," with varying USB-C and USB-A port configurations. It is the first sign the platform is not laptop-only — tablets can be Googlebooks too, opening more form factors for Google's AI-centric desktop OS. Google hasn't announced an official launch date beyond "later this year"; besides Lenovo, ASUS, Dell and HP were also named hardware launch partners.

China's DRAM Champion CXMT Tops 4 Trillion Yuan in Market Value

IT Home's morning briefing (7:22 AM Beijing / 23:22 GMT) reports that CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies), China's leading domestic DRAM maker, has seen its market value break 4 trillion yuan for the first time. CXMT listed on Shanghai's STAR Market on July 27, closing its debut day up 465.82% with a market value above 3.28 trillion yuan — the largest in A-shares — and continued climbing to surpass the 4 trillion yuan mark on Friday, July 31. The backdrop is a new global memory upcycle: Apple CEO Tim Cook described surging memory prices as a "once-in-a-century flood" forcing price increases on Mac and iPad products; Samsung has warned the memory shortage could last into 2028; Qualcomm announced chip price hikes starting in September; and Nvidia and AMD GPUs are reportedly rising at least 10%. As the flagship of domestic memory, CXMT has become one of the most-watched A-share names of the cycle, and its valuation swings are widely seen as a barometer for China's semiconductor self-reliance drive.
