⚡ Aug 4 Tech Update: Cloudflare Launches Programmable Wallets for the Agentic Internet, Microsoft Project Perception Enters Public Preview, Cisco Open-Sources Antares AI Security Models, Bending Spoons to Acquire Airtable for $1.28B

Tuesday (August 4) saw the tech world racing forward on two converging tracks — the agentic economy and agentic security. Cloudflare unveiled a programmable wallet built for the agentic internet, giving AI agents a machine-native path to "no-account, no-key, micropayment" commerce. Microsoft pushed its agentic security system Project Perception into public preview, fielding red, blue and green agent teams for closed-loop offense-and-defense inside Defender. Cisco open-sourced its Antares family of small-language AI security models. And Bending Spoons, just a month past its IPO, announced its first acquisition. Here is our roundup.

1. Cloudflare Launches Cloudflare Wallets: Programmable Wallets and Stablecoin Micropayments for the Agentic Internet

According to the Cloudflare blog (published 9:00 AM ET Aug 4 / 21:00 Beijing time), Cloudflare officially announced Cloudflare Wallets, a programmable wallet built for the "agentic internet." The company notes that AI agents today often get stuck on login pages "designed for humans": they lack a stable identity to sign up, and lack a native way to pay for APIs. Because of that, agents struggle to onboard to software, which limits the growth of agentic commerce.

Cloudflare Wallets lets you claim a wallet handle for your account as a stable username to connect with merchants, and supports storing stablecoins, purchasing services and receiving funds across the web. Each account can create Virtual Wallets for its agents, enabling them to buy APIs, MCP Tools, and content — while guardrails such as allowances, allow lists and maximum transaction sizes keep spending safe. Agents can then explore dozens or hundreds of services autonomously within safe limits, without constant human approval.

The launch completes a two-sided market alongside the previously announced Monetization Gateway: the Gateway lets sellers get paid headlessly via the x402 protocol (which attaches micropayments to HTTP requests, covering AI inference, data and content), while Wallets give buyers a machine-native way to pay. Account Wallets serve humans and Virtual Wallets serve agents, together forming a "headless marketplace" where agents can register, pay and call APIs with low friction and managed risk — a foundational step for agentic commerce infrastructure.

2. Microsoft Project Perception Enters Public Preview: Tri-Color Agent Teams Run Closed-Loop Defense in Defender

According to Microsoft's official blog and Cloud Wars (August 4, 2026), Microsoft has moved its agentic security system Project Perception into public preview, building a new "cyber stack" designed for the age of AI — marking the shift from "AI that assists" to "AI that acts."

Project Perception brings a workforce of specialized agents into Microsoft Defender: red agents probe like an attacker, mapping compromise paths before an adversary can exploit them; blue agents investigate, reason over context and determine what represents meaningful risk; green agents take corrective action and harden defenses. The three share intelligence and hand off work in a closed loop, forming a continuously learning system of defense that discovers, evaluates and improves an organization's security posture — with humans in control of every critical decision.

Under the hood, it adopts a multi-model architecture, billed by security compute units (SCUs), that dynamically selects the best model by quality, reliability, latency and cost. Crucially, Microsoft released its first dedicated cybersecurity model, MAI-Cyber-1-Flash: working with the MDASH multi-agent security harness, it scored 95.95% on CyberGym (1,507 real-world tasks from 188 OSS-Fuzz projects) — a sharp jump from 88.45% in May, and roughly 12 points above Anthropic's Mythos. It handles about 90% of security tasks, routing the hardest 10% to larger models like GPT-5.4. Project Perception will extend from Defender to more Microsoft security products in the coming months.

3. Cisco Open-Sources Antares AI Security Models: First Open-Weight Releases Hit Hugging Face

According to Cisco Newsroom (August 4, 2026), Cisco introduced Antares, a family of specialized small-language models (SLMs) purpose-built for cybersecurity, with the first two now available as "open weight" releases on Hugging Face. Cisco's VP and chief AI scientist Amin Karbasi explains the name: Antares is a colossal red star that, being far away, is invisible because our sun dominates the sky — much like vulnerabilities, which may be just a few hidden lines amid millions of lines of code, yet can have an enormous impact.

Cisco's strategy targets a real obstacle: general-purpose LLM defense is costly for many organizations and exposes codebase data to cloud risks. Antares answers with specialized, affordable, locally deployable small models, making AI security accessible to more organizations, including non-enterprise users, while avoiding data-residency compliance concerns. It marks the first step toward an "open, evaluable, integrable" security-intelligence ecosystem — the broader community can evaluate, integrate and improve these models through open benchmarks.

4. Bending Spoons to Acquire Airtable for $1.28B in Cash: First Deal Since IPO

According to TechCrunch (4:30 AM PT Aug 4, i.e. 19:30 Beijing time), Bending Spoons — the European app-consolidation powerhouse that went public last month at an $18 billion valuation — announced it has agreed to acquire spreadsheet-and-database startup Airtable for $1.28 billion in cash, its first acquisition since listing.

Founded in 2013, Airtable has raised more than $1.4 billion across funding rounds; valued at over $11 billion at its 2021 peak, its shares were trading at about a $4 billion valuation on secondary markets earlier this year. Accounting for its net cash position, Airtable is now valued at roughly $2.25 billion, Bending Spoons said. On the revenue side, Airtable's annual recurring revenue reached approximately $480 million as of June 2026, growing over 20% year over year. In January, Airtable unveiled a new product line under the "Superagent" moniker — an orchestration platform for spinning up a team of AI agents — and CEO Howie Liu said it serves over 500,000 organizations, including 80% of the Fortune 100.

Bending Spoons' proven playbook is to buy brands at a discount, trim staff, streamline products and push them to profitability — having previously acquired Evernote, WeTransfer, EventBrite and Vimeo. Buying Airtable extends that play toward "data orchestration plus agentic workflows," and continues this week's active M&A streak in tech.
