⚡ Aug 6 Finance Briefing: Hang Seng Falls 1.49% as AI Rally Cools, Gold Hits 7-Week High Above $4,250, Aramco Cuts Sept Asia Prices to 6-Year Low, Fed's Cook Turns Hawkish, DRC Bars Copper & Cobalt Exports

In the past hour or two, five stories dominated global markets: Asian stocks slipped as the AI rally cooled, with Hong Kong's Hang Seng closing down 1.49%; gold climbed for a fourth straight day above $4,250/oz to a seven-week high, and a Wall Street fund manager says the bull case is intact with a $5,000 target; Saudi Aramco cut its September Arab Light official selling price for Asia to a six-year low; Fed Governor Lisa Cook turned hawkish, saying she is prepared to raise rates if necessary; and the DRC reportedly banned exports of copper and cobalt concentrates. Here is this round's finance briefing.

1. Asian markets: AI rally cools, Hang Seng falls 1.49%, China shares mixed with gold stocks up

According to Bloomberg's markets wrap, Asian stocks were set for declines as the AI rally cooled, with the pullback in US AI names rippling through Asia-Pacific tech. RTHK reported Hong Kong shares slipping as the regional retreat from tech deepened. At the close, the Hang Seng Index fell 1.49% to 25,530.28, the Hang Seng Tech Index dropped 2.28%, and the Hang Seng China Enterprises Index lost 1.22%. Tech names were broadly lower: Baidu fell more than 4%, while Alibaba, Xiaomi, Kuaishou, Tencent and JD.com each lost over 2%; battery makers led the declines with CATL down more than 5%; auto stocks also fell, with Leapmotor off over 4%.

In contrast, gold stocks led gains in a mixed China close, while insurers weighed on Hong Kong, per MarketScreener's wrap of the session. Coal names were also active — China Coal Energy rose over 2% after the thermal coal price index was raised across the board on Aug 5 (5,500 kcal at RMB 839/tonne, up RMB 5), and as Beijing's anti-"involution" policy keeps curbing overcapacity, lifting expectations for coal miners' earnings. Brokerages note that long-term contract-heavy names like China Shenhua and China Coal Energy should post steadier results.

2. Gold rises for a fourth day: above $4,250/oz at a seven-week high, experts eye $5,000 and prefer miners

According to FXStreet and MarketScreener, gold rose for a fourth consecutive day, breaking above $4,250 per ounce on Wednesday to a seven-week high, supported by geopolitics and macro data. Reporting on the move, CLS (Cailianshe) quoted Chris Mancini, co-portfolio manager of the Gabelli Gold Fund: monetary policy uncertainty is a short-term drag, but the forces underpinning the long-term bull market remain firmly intact — persistent geopolitical uncertainty, rising government debt, central banks reducing reliance on the dollar, and de-dollarization.

Mancini was explicit: "I think all the factors that drove gold to $5,000 an ounce are still there… I think we eventually get back to $5,000." He prefers gold miners over physical gold: with major producers' all-in sustaining costs near $2,000/oz while gold holds above $4,000, margins are fat; large producers are generating record free cash flow yet trade at only about 10–11x earnings, leaving valuations deeply discounted. His caveat: an oil-driven inflation rebound could push the Fed to hike again and slow gold's ascent — but it would not break the long-term trend.

3. Saudi Aramco cuts September Asia OSP: Arab Light at a $2.00 discount, a six-year low

According to Reuters, Saudi Aramco further cut the September official selling price (OSP) of its flagship Arab Light crude for Asian buyers to its lowest level in six years: a $2.00 per barrel discount versus the Oman/Dubai average, the weakest since June 2020. Last month's August OSP already delivered the steepest cut in more than two decades at a $1.50 discount — and September widens it further.

Other grades were adjusted as well: Super Light moved to a $0.35 discount (from a $0.15 premium, down $0.50); Extra Light to a $1.50 discount (down $0.50); Medium to a $2.00 discount (up $1.25); and Heavy to a $3.35 discount (up $1.25). With demand soft and OPEC+ adding supply, two consecutive deep cuts to Asian OSPs are read by the market as a direct pricing signal on Asian refinery buying appetite and the global supply-demand balance.

4. Fed Governor Lisa Cook turns hawkish: inflation "too high," prepared to raise rates

According to ANI and Sina Finance, Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook said in Anchorage, Alaska that inflation remains too high and she is "prepared to take action, if necessary, by raising rates." "Inflation is too high — I think the risks to the inflation side of our dual mandate are currently higher than the risks to the employment side," she said. She acknowledged that June data showed a cooling in inflation driven by a sharp drop in energy prices, but cautioned against over-reading a single data point, noting price growth is still well above the Fed's 2% target.

At last week's FOMC meeting, Cook was part of the 9–3 majority that voted to hold the benchmark rate at 3.50%–3.75%. She explained the vote reflected a desire to see how tariff effects, the energy supply shock from the Iran war, and AI-driven spending pressures feed into prices. Her warning: "With inflation above target for five consecutive years, higher inflation could become entrenched in price- and wage-setting behavior… in the current environment, we don't have that luxury."

5. DRC reportedly bars copper and cobalt concentrate exports; new tax regime in three months

According to Sina Finance citing foreign media, an official decree shows the Democratic Republic of Congo is banning exports of copper concentrate and cobalt concentrate. Under the new tax regime, by-products of significant economic value will be taxed using a 55% valuation coefficient. The new framework takes effect in three months, but miners must begin declaring by-products in their exports immediately.

The DRC is the world's largest cobalt producer and a major copper producer, so a ban on copper and cobalt concentrate exports would directly affect the global battery cobalt supply chain and copper concentrate processing flows, with potential price implications for both metals. Markets are waiting for more official detail and enforcement guidance; copper and cobalt futures are the focus of this round's commodity trading.
