⚡ Aug 9 Finance Roundup: Bitcoin ETFs See Best Week Since April with $865M Inflows, Korea Volatility Eases to Two-Month Low, Nasdaq to Launch 23-Hour Trading in December, Oil Retreats

Sunday afternoon, August 9, may find US and European equity markets closed, but crypto, Asia-Pacific markets and commodities are still sending important signals: US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded roughly $865 million in weekly net inflows — their best week since April — with BlackRock clients contributing nearly 80%; South Korea's stock volatility index fell to a two-month low after a historic sell-off flushed out leveraged positions; Nasdaq confirmed it will launch "23/5" trading on December 6, leaving US stocks closed for just about one hour a day; and oil prices retreated from geopolitical highs as hopes for a Strait of Hormuz reopening combined with supply-glut worries. Here is the verified rundown.

Bitcoin Spot ETFs Post Best Week Since April with ~$865M Inflows

Around 12:35-12:38 Beijing time on August 9, Moomoo, pluang and other platforms published matching data: US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded about $865.3 million in net inflows last week, the best weekly performance since April, with BlackRock clients buying roughly $693.5 million — about 80% of the weekly total. Earlier tallies from Crypto News and others put the five-day inflow streak at about $853.5 million, nearly five times the whole of July.

Including Ethereum spot ETFs, The Block estimates last week's combined crypto ETF inflows at about $1.1 billion — also the best week since April. Notably, the week coincided with the Coldcard cold-wallet hack that resulted in losses exceeding $100 million; the Crypto Fear & Greed Index has recovered to 31 but remains in "Fear" territory — and institutional money kept adding on the dip, which many read as a vote of confidence in Bitcoin's medium-term positioning.

  • Weekly net inflow: ~$865M (BTC spot ETFs)
  • BlackRock client purchases: ~$693.5M, about 80% of the total
  • BTC + ETH combined: ~$1.1B, best week since April

Korea Volatility Falls to Two-Month Low as ~$1.4B of Leverage Is Flushed Out

According to reports published around 12:05 Beijing time (aggregated by BigGo Finance and picked up by NDTV Profit), the most violent phase of South Korea's stock-market turmoil may be over: the Kospi volatility index (VKOSPI) has fallen to a two-month low from its record high in June. Forced liquidations totaling roughly $1.4 billion in June and July cleared out leveraged retail positions and drove margin debt to its lowest level of 2026. Meanwhile, regulators tightened limits on leveraged ETFs tied to chip giants Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, cutting both trading volumes and assets in those products.

Morgan Stanley estimates the deleveraging process is more than half complete. To recap the episode: the Kospi drew down nearly 40% from its June peak, global funds have sold more than $100 billion of Korean shares in 2026, and emerging-market funds are underweight the country. On valuation, the Kospi trades at a record-low 5.1 times forward earnings; Goldman Sachs keeps a bullish 12-month target implying roughly 90% upside. Still, many money managers are waiting for concrete evidence that normal price discovery has returned before re-entering.

In short: South Korea is transitioning from leverage-driven whiplash toward post-deleveraging normalization — lower volatility is welcome, but whether funds return depends on US inflation data and the next twist in the Middle East.

Nasdaq Confirms 23/5 Trading from December 6: US Stocks Closed Just One Hour a Day

Reports published at 12:36 Beijing time confirm Nasdaq's "Always-On" timeline: starting December 6, 2026, Nasdaq will trade US equities 23 hours a day, five days a week, leaving a daily maintenance window of roughly one hour. Today, Nasdaq's equity session runs from 4:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m. ET; the extension will effectively cover prime hours across most global time zones.

The rule change was approved by the US Securities and Exchange Commission early in the second quarter (April 10). Nasdaq CEO Adena Friedman said on the Q1 earnings call: "We're excited to be leading the transition to an Always-On market... we are excited to set a new standard for how regulated markets operate in an increasingly global and digital economy." Nasdaq-100 futures already trade 24 hours a day, five days a week.

For Asia-Pacific investors, 23-hour trading means they can trade US cash equities directly during Asian daytime hours, without relying on after-hours matching or futures. For market makers and data infrastructure providers, it marks the start of a new round of system upgrades and compliance spending.

Oil Retreats from Geopolitical Highs: Hormuz Reopening Hopes Meet Supply-Glut Fears

Around 11:40 and 12:30 Beijing time, Futu and other platforms reported that oil prices were falling as the Strait of Hormuz looked set to reopen; a veteran commodities analyst said the market is "scared," with geopolitical premiums deflating fast and supply-glut concerns back in the driver's seat. Earlier, when Iran published a draft management plan for the Strait of Hormuz and US forces struck Iranian energy infrastructure, crude spiked sharply with single-day swings above 7%.

Friday's US session told the same story from the equity side: with Trump calling off an Iran attack and oil easing, the Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all rose, the S&P 500 closed at a record high for the first time since June, and the index posted its best week since April with a gain of about 3.6% (per AOL and Yicai). One caveat: oil remains violently two-way — when Trump said attacks on Iran would continue, crude jumped more than 7% intraday (EnergyNow, 10:31 Beijing time) — bulls and bears are still fighting over the geopolitical variable.

  • Bullish for oil: US strikes on Iranian energy facilities, Hormuz toll rumors, Iran's draft strait-management plan
  • Bearish for oil: hopes Hormuz will reopen, post-destocking supply-glut worries, peace-talk expectations
In short: August 9 is a "risk-appetite repair" day — crypto ETF inflows at their best since April, Korea volatility easing, Nasdaq moving toward 23-hour trading, and oil's geopolitical premium deflating all point the same way: markets drifting back from panic toward normalcy.
