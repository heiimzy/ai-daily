⚡ August 5 Finance Update: AMD Data Center Revenue Doubles But Stock Slips, Michael Burry Warns of Possible 1987-Type Fall, Wayfair Jumps 30%, McDonald's US Business Misses, Paramount Skydance Raises Guidance

Tuesday (Aug 4 ET) after the bell brought another packed night of earnings and market sentiment: AMD reported second-quarter revenue up 50% year over year with data center sales doubling, yet investors reacted coolly and the stock slipped in extended trading; "The Big Short" investor Michael Burry is sticking with his bearish wagers, warning even as the S&P 500 hits records that the market could see a "1987-type fall"; US online furniture retailer Wayfair jumped about 30% after posting its strongest US growth since 2020 and $301 million in free cash flow; McDonald's reported mixed Q2 results with net income of $2.36 billion as its US business fell short, naming veteran Skye Anderson president of its US operations; and Paramount Skydance raised its full-year profit guidance, with streaming staying strong while linear TV weighed. Here is this round's finance roundup.

1. AMD Q2 Revenue Up 50%, Data Center Sales Double — But Stock Slips After Hours

According to CNBC (Aug 4, after the close), chipmaker AMD reported second-quarter results for the period ended June 27 on Tuesday that beat expectations, but the stock slumped in extended trading after its guidance trailed the most aggressive hopes on the Street.

On the numbers: AMD's overall revenue climbed 50% from $7.69 billion a year earlier, a sign of the company's central position in the market for AI chips; its Data Center unit — the main growth driver — posted sales of $6.7 billion, up 107% year over year, which AMD attributed to central processing unit and graphics processing unit sales. Over the past year, AMD's stock has nearly tripled, both on optimism that its Instinct-branded AI chips will take a meaningful slice of a growing market from Nvidia, and on the resurgence of its Epyc-branded CPUs, which AI experts now call an essential component for running agents.

Looking ahead, AMD guided to about $13 billion in revenue for the current quarter, plus or minus $300 million, versus LSEG expectations of $12.52 billion — although some analysts had been looking for guidance as high as $14 billion. In July, AMD raised its view of the semiconductor industry's size, saying it could be worth $2 trillion per year by 2028, with about $1.4 trillion of that coming from AI accelerators (GPUs), up from a prior estimate of $500 billion by 2028. The company will also start shipping Helios, its first rack-scale AI system, this year to companies like Meta, OpenAI and Oracle, marking AMD's first rack-level system and a further step into AI infrastructure.

2. Michael Burry Warns the Market May Be Near a "Major Top" With a Possible 1987-Type Fall

According to CNBC (Aug 4), Michael Burry — the investor of "The Big Short" fame — is sticking with his bearish wagers even as the S&P 500 surges to record highs, warning that the rally could still end in a sharp sell-off reminiscent of the 1987 stock-market crash.

In a Tuesday Substack post, Burry wrote: "I continue to believe it is possible we are near a major top, and possible a 1987-type fall, but the S&P 500 making new highs likely will bring new money into the market." The S&P 500 jumped 1.9% Tuesday to its first record close since June, buoyed by stronger-than-expected corporate earnings and another drop in oil prices as hopes grew that the Strait of Hormuz could reopen to maritime traffic; the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite soared 2.7%, extending its gain in the first two days of the week to nearly 5%.

Burry has been among Wall Street's most outspoken skeptics of the AI boom, arguing that demand for AI infrastructure is being fueled by financing arrangements that may prove unsustainable. He said the advance is creating a self-reinforcing cycle: "Remember, the market going up on falling volatility forces vol-targeting funds to leverage up, and brings leverage from other momentum strategies into play." In the face of the rally, Burry said he continues to hold short positions in the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX), Micron, Nvidia, Caterpillar, Palantir, Tesla and Applied Materials, remaining confident in his long-term outlook for those positions.

3. Wayfair Jumps About 30% on Strongest US Growth Since 2020 and $301M Free Cash Flow

According to CNBC (Aug 4), online furniture retailer Wayfair said Tuesday it saw its strongest growth in the US and best free cash flow since the pandemic during its second quarter, as the company continues to take market share from legacy brick-and-mortar retailers. Wayfair's stock closed about 30% higher on the day.

In the three months ended June 30, sales in Wayfair's largest market, the US, grew 8.7% to $3.1 billion — the region's fastest growth since 2020, the year the overall home goods industry surged and Wayfair's business grew 55%. The sales bump helped profitability, with free cash flow reaching $301 million during the quarter, also the strongest since 2020. On its analyst call, Wayfair said it expects momentum to continue in the current quarter with "high single-digit" percentage revenue growth — above the 5% analysts had been looking for, per LSEG — and gross margin of 29.5% to 30.5%.

CFO Kate Gulliver told CNBC the company is growing by taking market share, primarily from traditional brick-and-mortar competitors, as the housing market remains "stalled." CEO Niraj Shah highlighted that high-end brand Perigold grew more than 35%, and specialty retail brands grew nearly 20% in the quarter: "We are excited to see ramping growth in the Wayfair business and complementing that with outsize performance" from those brands.

4. McDonald's Posts $2.36B Q2 Net Income, US Business Falls Short, Names New US President

According to CNBC (Aug 4), McDonald's reported mixed quarterly results on Tuesday as the chain's US performance fell short of executives' expectations. CEO Chris Kempczinski said plainly on the earnings call: "We don't have a strategy problem. We simply didn't execute at the level we needed to in the second quarter."

For the quarter ended June 30, McDonald's reported net income of $2.36 billion, or $3.32 per share, up from $2.25 billion, or $3.14 per share, a year earlier, with adjusted results roughly in line with expectations. "While our playbook is working around the world, we see an opportunity to raise the bar in the U.S. and accelerate performance in our largest market," Kempczinski said in a statement.

To boost its home market, the company announced that Skye Anderson is assuming the role of president of its US business, effective Tuesday, succeeding Joe Erlinger, who led the division for more than six years. Anderson, a 26-year McDonald's veteran, previously served as chief operating officer of McDonald's USA and led its Global Business Services unit before that. Shares closed about 1% higher on Tuesday.

5. Paramount Skydance Raises Full-Year Profit Guidance: Q2 Revenue $6.91B, Streaming Grows, Linear Weighs

According to CNBC (Aug 4), Paramount Skydance raised its full-year guidance on Tuesday and reported second-quarter results that showcased the continued strengths of streaming and the weaknesses of linear TV. The company beat Wall Street expectations for revenue, but its portfolio of cable TV networks continued to weigh on the overall business; Paramount noted that cost cutting and "creative execution" for the traditional TV business helped improve margins and profit in the quarter.

For the period ended June 30, Paramount reported net earnings attributable to the company of $41 million, or 4 cents per share, versus $57 million, or 8 cents per share, a year earlier; total revenue was $6.91 billion, up slightly year over year. Revenue for the direct-to-consumer streaming segment — Paramount+, BET+ and free, ad-supported Pluto TV — was up 9% to $2.47 billion, while film studio revenue increased 16%. The company also raised its full-year profit guidance and said it remains "confident" about its merger with Warner Bros. Discovery. The report underscores that, amid a continuing industry-wide decline in linear TV, streaming has become the media giant's main growth engine.
