⚡ Aug 7 World Briefing: Erdogan in Saudi for Trilateral Talks, Trump's Endorsement Fails as Ogles Loses Primary, Mexico Deploys 1,500 Troops to Protect Avocado Regions

Over the past couple of hours, three major developments have moved on the world stage: Turkish President Erdogan arrived in Saudi Arabia on Friday for a trilateral meeting with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, with multiple outlets reporting the three sides could sign a joint defense agreement; in the United States, Rep. Andy Ogles of Tennessee lost his Republican primary despite Trump's "complete and total endorsement" — the second Trump-backed candidate to fall this week; and Mexico has deployed more than 1,500 soldiers to its main avocado-producing regions after the US suspended export inspections over security concerns, freezing shipments to its largest market. Here is this round's international briefing.

1. Erdogan in Saudi Arabia: Trilateral Meeting with the Crown Prince and Pakistan's PM, Defense Agreement in Sight

President Erdogan visited Saudi Arabia on Friday — his second trip this year and his first since the US-Israel war with Iran broke out. His office described the visit as a "routine one-day working trip," with presidency communications chief Fahrettin Altun saying the talks would focus on "bilateral relations and regional developments."

Erdogan was scheduled to hold a trilateral meeting with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, who arrived in the kingdom on Thursday for a three-day visit accompanied by Army Chief General Asim Munir. Al Jazeera, citing Saudi military sources, reported the three leaders could sign a joint defense agreement in Jeddah; Turkish intelligence sources said the basic terms had long been agreed. If signed, the pact would extend the Saudi-Pakistan Strategic Mutual Defense Agreement of September 2025 — under which an attack on one is treated as an attack on both — into a trilateral framework. None of the three governments has confirmed the reports, and the outcome could still be a more modest joint statement.

The timing is telling: it comes about a week after Saudi Arabia unveiled a multinational maritime security initiative to protect shipping in the Red Sea, Bab el-Mandeb and the Gulf of Aden, backed by 14 countries including Turkey and Pakistan. It also follows weeks of Strait of Hormuz disruption triggered by the US-Iran war, during which Pakistan has cast itself as a mediator in the Middle East crisis. Iran has struck US bases, hotels and energy facilities in Gulf states with missiles and drones, while Saudi Arabia continues to trade fire with Yemen's Houthis and Red Sea vessels keep coming under attack — all of which amplifies the geopolitical weight of this visit.

2. Trump's Endorsement Fails: Anti-Muslim Rep. Ogles Loses the GOP Primary

Tennessee Rep. Andy Ogles has lost his Republican primary. Despite Trump's "complete and total endorsement" earlier in the day, the firebrand congressman fell about 6.6 percentage points behind former state agriculture commissioner Charlie Hatch with roughly 90% of votes counted. Hatch, backed by Governor Bill Lee, will now face Democrat Mould, the mayor of Columbia south of Nashville, in November — a Republican-leaning district Democrats hope to flip amid economic discontent and anger over the "war on Iran."

Ogles was first elected in 2022 and moved to a new district after Republicans redrew the map to shore up their House majority. He drew bipartisan condemnation in March for posting on X that "Muslims don't belong in American society" and pluralism is a lie, and faced widespread backlash for remarks to activists in 2024. Hatch, by contrast, has explicitly rejected Ogles' anti-Muslim rhetoric, saying American Muslims are "law-abiding, good people" and that "the US military has good warriors" among them.

Ogles is the second Trump-endorsed candidate to lose in as many weeks — on Tuesday, Michigan Republican Amir Hassan, a Navy veteran, also fell short in his primary despite Trump's backing. With the US midterms heating up and all 435 House seats plus 35 Senate seats in play this November, the faltering Trump endorsement is becoming a bellwether for his political influence.

3. Mexico Deploys 1,500 Troops to Avocado Regions: US Pauses Inspections, Freezing a Billion-Dollar Trade

The Mexican government has deployed more than 1,500 soldiers to its main avocado-producing states after the United States suspended export inspections of Mexican avocados over security concerns, effectively freezing shipments. Roughly 90% of Mexico's avocados go to the US, its largest export market, so the stoppage threatens thousands of jobs and a supply chain worth billions of dollars.

The US pause stems from concerns about security along the inspection and logistics chain — Washington has repeatedly and briefly halted avocado imports from various Mexican regions over safety issues in the past. By moving troops into the growing regions, Mexico is signaling to Washington that security has been reinforced and exports can resume. For producers already under pressure from seasonality and weather, the standoff's resolution remains uncertain.
