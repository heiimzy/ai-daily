⚡ July 31 International Politics: Russian Missile Crashes Inside Poland Leaving 10-Meter Crater, US and Iran Trade Heavy Missile Barrages, Trump and Medvedev in Nuclear Exchange

Russian Kh-101 Cruise Missile Lands in Poland — NATO on High Alert

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk confirmed Thursday that an object that crashed into a field in eastern Poland was "in all probability" a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, leaving a 10-meter-wide (33-foot) crater a short distance from the village of Tarnawa Kolonia, 92 km (57 miles) from the Ukrainian border. Speaking at an emergency meeting in Lublin, Tusk stated that military experts had analyzed recovered fragments and "the majority point to it being a Russian Kh-101 missile."

Poland's military detected the object at approximately 3:40 a.m. local time (01:40 GMT) and scrambled an F-16 fighter jet to intercept it before it disappeared from radar minutes later. Residents in Tarnawa Kolonia reported hearing a loud bang at around 4:00 a.m., and police discovered the crash site about an hour later. No casualties were reported as the missile landed in an uninhabited area.

"We were prepared to shoot it down if it had continued its flight," Tusk said, adding that about 20 objects had been detected near Polish airspace overnight and that pyrotechnical analysis was being conducted. He emphasized there was "no reason to think Poland was the target."

NATO responded swiftly. NATO spokesperson Martin O'Donnell confirmed that allied air and ground defenses had been activated. Supreme Allied Commander in Europe, General Alexus G. Grynkewich, spoke with Poland's Chief of Defense, stressing that the alliance would "continue to take all necessary measures to defend NATO territory." European leaders including French President Emmanuel Macron, German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, and EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen issued statements condemning Russia's actions.

The missile incursion occurred during a massive Russian overnight aerial assault on Ukraine, in which Moscow launched 74 missiles and 284 drones, killing at least 8 people and wounding dozens. In the Kryvyi Rih area, a ballistic missile struck a home, killing six members of one family. Dozens more were wounded in Lviv near the Polish border.

US and Iran Trade Heavy Missile Barrages — Chinese Company Building in Kuwait Hit, 1 Killed

US-Iran military hostilities escalated sharply on Thursday. The US Central Command (CENTCOM) announced it had completed a "heavy wave" of strikes against Iran, hitting "dozens" of IRGC targets including military command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance sites, and maritime capabilities. The strikes were retaliation for an earlier Iranian ballistic missile attack on US forces in Jordan.

Hours later, Iran launched another barrage of missiles across the region. Jordan reported its air defenses intercepted five Iranian missiles for the second consecutive day. However, Kuwait's military confirmed that an Iranian strike hit a Chinese company's building in northern Kuwait, severely damaging the structure and killing one worker — the first reported Chinese national casualty in the expanding Middle East conflict.

Earlier, joint US-Saudi airstrikes hit Iran-backed militia positions in Iraq, killing at least 20 fighters and six Iranian advisers on Wednesday. Iran's official Islamic Republic News Agency reported three people killed and two wounded in a US strike on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, including a two-year-old child.

The conflict continues to spread geographically. Drone strikes ignited fires aboard two natural gas vessels at Egypt's Damietta Port — the first direct attack on Egyptian territory. Saudi Arabia reported coming under fire from Iran-backed militias in Iraq. Lloyd's List Intelligence data shows vessel movements through the Strait of Hormuz have fallen to nearly half their normal levels, driving up global energy prices.

Trump and Medvedev Trade Nuclear Threats — 'Dead Hand' Doomsday System Invoked

A dangerous war of words erupted between US President Donald Trump and former Russian President Dmitry Medvedev, now deputy chairman of Russia's Security Council, escalating to veiled nuclear threats. In a Truth Social post early Thursday, Trump told Medvedev to "watch his words," calling him "the failed former President of Russia" and accusing him of "entering very dangerous territory."

The confrontation began after Trump shortened his previously announced 50-day deadline for Russia to reach a ceasefire in Ukraine to just 10–12 days, threatening "secondary tariffs" on Russian energy buyers. Medvedev responded on X, accusing Trump of playing "the ultimatum game with Russia" and warning that "each new ultimatum is a threat and a step towards war — not between Russia and Ukraine, but with your own country."

After Trump's rebuke, Medvedev escalated further on Telegram, invoking the Soviet-era "Dead Hand" automated nuclear retaliation system — designed to guarantee a massive retaliatory nuclear strike even if Moscow's leadership is destroyed or incapacitated. Mocking the US president, Medvedev suggested Trump "re-watch his favorite zombie movies and remember how dangerous that non-existent 'Dead Hand' can be."

Analysts described the exchange as one of the most dangerous rhetorical confrontations between nuclear powers since the start of the Ukraine war. Although Medvedev holds a largely symbolic position, analysts believe his extreme remarks are almost certainly coordinated with the Kremlin. Trump's targeting of Medvedev rather than directly confronting President Vladimir Putin is widely seen as intentional — allowing him to project toughness while preserving potential space for direct negotiations.

Meanwhile, the US Senate advanced a bipartisan Russia-Iran sanctions bill introduced by the late Senator Lindsey Graham, which would authorize Trump to impose tariffs on major Russian energy buyers and expand sanctions on Tehran. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, fresh from his White House meeting with Trump, also met with over 60 US senators, calling for increased sanctions and support. Trump described the meeting as "very productive."

Ukraine Strikes Two Major Russian Oil Refineries in Overnight Raids

While President Zelenskyy was in Washington, Ukrainian forces launched overnight strikes on two of Russia's largest oil refineries, significantly escalating their campaign to cripple Russia's energy revenue. Ukraine's Security Service and General Staff confirmed that drones struck the Lukoil refinery in Perm (over 1,500 km from the Ukrainian border) and the Ryazan refinery (approximately 400 km from the border), both catching fire. The two refineries have a combined annual capacity of approximately 220 million barrels of oil.

Ukraine has been systematically targeting Russian oil infrastructure for months using domestically produced long-range drones and missiles, creating a fuel crisis that has embarrassed the Kremlin. Ryazan Governor Pavel Malkov confirmed that six people were hospitalized after the attack, with drone debris igniting fires at an unspecified "industrial site."

"Russia must realize that every day this war continues, the cost rises ever higher," Zelenskyy posted on social media. "We need to weaken the aggressor and keep up the pressure to end this war as soon as possible." A senior Ukrainian military official also revealed that over the past three weeks, Ukrainian forces had struck 201 Russian vessels in the Black Sea and Sea of Azov, along with a Russian naval base in the Black Sea.

Russia's Defense Ministry claimed its air defenses shot down 295 Ukrainian drones overnight across multiple regions and occupied Crimea. Ukrainian forces also reported strikes on a Russian radar station in Bryansk, a drone launch site in occupied Donetsk, and a road bridge.
