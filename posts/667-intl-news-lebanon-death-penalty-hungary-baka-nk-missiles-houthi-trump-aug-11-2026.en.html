Tuesday afternoon brought a wave of major international stories: Lebanon's parliament voted to abolish the death penalty, making the country the first in the Middle East to formally end the practice; Hungary's parliament elected former Supreme Court president András Baka as the new president, another clear break with the Orbán era; Zelensky accused Moscow of firing North Korean missiles in an overnight assault that killed at least nine people; a suspected Houthi strike on an Egyptian cargo ship in the Red Sea killed three crew members, while a US helicopter was reported to have hit an Iran-linked vessel off Pakistan; and the Washington Post reported that Trump secretly swapped planes via a catering truck to evade an Iranian assassination threat. 1. Lebanon Abolishes the Death Penalty, a First for the Middle East

According to France 24 / AFP (published Aug 11, 12:00 UTC), Lebanon's parliament has abolished the death penalty, making the country the first in the Middle East to formally end the practice. "The draft law aimed at abolishing the death penalty in Lebanon was approved after amendments were made to it," the speaker of parliament's office announced. Lebanon had not carried out an execution since 2004. Justice Minister Adel Nassar called the abolition "historic," noting that the specter of capital punishment had previously prevented Beirut from obtaining the extradition of suspects from countries where it has been outlawed. Ramzi Kaiss, Lebanon researcher at Human Rights Watch, told AFP the move was "a monumental step for human rights in the country, representing a clear break from a cruel and arbitrary sentence that should never be imposed." The abolition came as part of a series of bills in the current legislative session, including a general amnesty law debated for years.

2. Hungary Elects András Baka as President: Another Break with the Orbán Era

According to The Guardian's Europe live blog (Aug 11, 13:56 UTC), Hungary's parliament has elected András Baka as the new president by a vote of 160 in favor and six against. Baka, 72, the nominee of the governing Tisza Party, is a former president of the Hungarian Supreme Court and a former European Court of Human Rights judge: he was removed from the Supreme Court post in 2011 by then-prime minister Orbán, three years before his term was set to expire, and later won a case at the ECtHR against Hungary. In his acceptance speech, Baka said he believed Hungary "reached a period in its history when a serious reflection is needed about the functioning of the state," after the last two decades of "a serious crisis" under the previous government and deep societal divisions. He replaces Orbán loyalist Tamás Sulyok, who stood down—or was effectively removed—last month. "We cannot build the new Hungary on revenge," the new president told parliament.

3. Zelensky: Russia Fired North Korean Missiles in an Overnight Attack

According to France 24 (published Aug 11, 13:57 UTC), Russian attacks across Ukraine killed at least nine people overnight and wounded dozens, with Kyiv accusing Moscow of launching North Korean missiles in the raid. Per BBC reporting, strikes on Zaporizhzhia—which killed at least six and injured at least 19—involved ballistic missiles from Russia's ally. In a video address on Monday evening, President Zelensky said that "for the first time" Russia "cannot wage war without reinforcements from North Korea": "The more North Korean strikes there are here in Ukraine, in Europe, the more their missiles and soldiers are used, the more they correct their shortcomings and blind spots," he said, warning this poses a danger to the rest of the world. Warehouses of the online retailer Wildberries in Voronezh, southern Russia, were also hit by Ukraine, and Zelensky hailed an air strike on a Russian petrochemical plant in western Siberia as a "very significant achievement," promising "more Ukrainian deep strikes." Over the weekend he warned that as many as 50,000 North Korean soldiers were being sent to fight with Russian forces.

4. Red Sea Cargo Ship Hit, 3 Crew Killed; Another Interception Reported Off Pakistan

According to France 24 (published Aug 11, 13:34 UTC), three crew members were killed in a suspected Houthi attack on an Egyptian-owned cargo vessel in the Bab el-Mandeb strait—two Pakistanis and one Indonesian. If confirmed, the fatalities would be the first deaths in a Houthi strike on shipping since the Iran war began on February 28; the Houthis have not claimed the attack. The ship, sailing from Salalah, Oman via Djibouti, lost control after being hit and was approached by Yemeni coastguard. The Houthis declared a maritime embargo against Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea on July 20. Separately, the Panama-flagged container ship Vela Nova was struck by a missile about 71 nautical miles off Pakistan's coast, with the Wall Street Journal reporting a US helicopter fired a Hellfire missile at its rudder after it tried to evade the US blockade of Iran-linked shipping; if confirmed it would be the 12th vessel attacked by US forces since the blockade was announced in April and the third since it was reimposed on July 14. Shipping through the Bab el-Mandeb strait has fallen by more than 50%, with an average of 32 ships per day last week, down from 50 before the blockade.

5. Washington Post: Trump Secretly Swapped Planes via a Catering Truck Over Iran Threat

According to France 24 / The Washington Post (Aug 11, 12:28 UTC), President Donald Trump secretly swapped planes as he left last month's NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey: he boarded Air Force One in view of television cameras before being transported to a smaller C-32A military aircraft—a modified Boeing 757 often used by the vice president—with the help of a catering truck elevated to plane level on the opposite side. Journalists and some White House staff on Air Force One did not know he had been smuggled out and were told to close their window shades. Officials told CBS News that the US had detected a credible Iranian threat to fire a missile at the plane. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth boarded the C-32A separately using external stairs to make the flight appear normal. Trump flew to the UK en route home, reboarding Air Force One at RAF Mildenhall, where he was seen descending from the aircraft. The White House has not confirmed the secret switch, which came a day after the US renewed military strikes on Iran following a breakdown of negotiations.
