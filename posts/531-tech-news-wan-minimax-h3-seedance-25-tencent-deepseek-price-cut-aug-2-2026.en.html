⚡ Aug 2 Tech News: Alibaba Wan 2.1 Tops Hugging Face, ByteDance Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3 Ignite the AI Video Race, Tencent Cloud Cuts DeepSeek-V4 Prices Up to 97.5%

Over the past 24 hours, several races in the AI industry heated up at once. On the open-source side, Alibaba's Wan 2.1 overtook DeepSeek-R1 to top both Hugging Face leaderboards just six days after its release, powered by over one million downloads. In video generation, ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax's H3 landed back-to-back, turning "long-narrative consistency" and "native stereo 2K" into the new arms-race metrics. And on pricing, Tencent Cloud began cutting DeepSeek-V4 prices today, with cache-hit prices down as much as 97.5%.

Alibaba Wan 2.1 Tops Two Hugging Face Charts: Overtakes DeepSeek-R1 Six Days After Open-Sourcing

According to the latest leaderboards on the open-source community Hugging Face, Alibaba's Wan model has overtaken DeepSeek-R1 just six days after its open-source release, topping both the Trending Models and Trending Spaces charts, making it the most popular large model in the global open-source community right now. The latest data shows Wan 2.1 has surpassed one million combined downloads on Hugging Face and the ModelScope community, with more than 6,000 stars on GitHub. Microsoft's The Ultra-Scale Playbook ranks second, with DeepSeek-R1 currently third.

Wan 2.1 is released under the Apache 2.0 license, with all inference code and weights for the 14B and 1.3B parameter variants open-sourced, supporting both text-to-video and image-to-video generation. The 14B version stands out in instruction following, complex motion generation, physical modeling and text-in-video rendering, topping the authoritative VBench benchmark with an overall score of 86.22% and comfortably beating Sora, Luma and Pika. The 1.3B version beats larger open-source models, approaches some closed-source results, and needs only 8.2GB of VRAM to generate 480P video on consumer graphics cards, making it ideal for derivative development and academic research.

What the leaderboard shift means: the "popularity race" of open-source models is spreading from pure language models to video generation, and download counts plus GitHub stars have become the most visible barometer for Chinese models going global and building ecosystems.

The AI Video Race Heats Up: ByteDance Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3 Launch Back-to-Back

ByteDance Seedance 2.5: Built for 30-Second Long Narratives and Multi-Modal Reference

ByteDance has released Seedance 2.5, a major upgrade to its video generation model: it supports generating clips of up to 30 seconds in a single pass; multi-modal reference support has been strengthened, allowing up to 30 images and 10 audio clips to guide the output; and it adds timestamp-controlled editing and multi-round extension, letting creators produce coherent long-form narratives that keep character identity and rhythm consistent. The company says the new architecture also addresses common video-generation artifacts, delivering higher visual and audio quality for professional workflows.

MiniMax H3: Unified Omni-Modal Context with Native Stereo 2K

MiniMax launched H3, its new omni-modal generation model, on July 31 (API model ID: MiniMax-H3, with the consumer version live in the Hailuo AI app). Rather than a text-to-video model with add-ons, H3 reads text, images, video and audio as one unified context and returns video with native stereo sound, at 2K resolution and 4-15 seconds in length. One of the official example prompts: reference the camera movement from Video 1, have the character in Image 2 sing, and match the vocals to Audio 3.

  • H3-VAE: a full tokenizer overhaul delivering a stated 4x gain in effective sequence length - the enabling technology that makes native 2K economically viable;
  • In-context regeneration: the base model re-reads its own low-resolution output in context instead of relying on a bolt-on super-resolution module, better preserving small text and brand marks;
  • Pricing: MiniMax claims the per-second price at 2K is less than a third of mainstream models; third-party trackers put the pay-as-you-go rate at about $0.13 per second (roughly $1.95 for a 15-second clip);
  • Open weights: promised "in the coming days"; for now the API is the only path.
MiniMax (X): "Omni-Reference, Commercial-Grade Generation, Unbeatable Cost Efficiency, Open Weights. Your creative destiny, on your terms. Now Live at HailuoAI.video & MiniMax API."

According to third-party benchmark tracker Artificial Analysis, H3 ranks first in video editing, while trailing Google's Gemini Omni Flash in text-to-video and sitting behind both Seedance 2.0 and Gemini Omni Flash in image-to-video - a head-to-head contest between Chinese video models and international players like Google.

Tencent Cloud Cuts DeepSeek-V4 Prices: Cache-Hit Prices Down Up to 97.5%

Tencent Cloud announced that its agent development platform will cut prices for the DeepSeek-V4 series effective August 2, with the largest reduction reaching 97.5%. Specifically, DeepSeek-V4-Pro's inference input price drops from 0.012 yuan to 0.003 yuan per thousand tokens, and inference output from 0.024 yuan to 0.006 yuan - both down 75%; the cache-hit price sees the biggest cut, from 0.001 yuan to 0.000025 yuan per thousand tokens, a 97.5% reduction. For DeepSeek-V4-Flash, only the cache-hit price is adjusted this time, from 0.0002 yuan to 0.00002 yuan, down 90%.

This is another landmark move in China's LLM inference price war: as model capabilities converge and competition shifts to scale, cloud vendors are using "price for volume" to win developers and enterprises. The cliff-like drop in cache-hit pricing targets exactly the high-frequency, repeated-call scenarios of agents and long-context workloads.
