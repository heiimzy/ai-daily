⚡ Aug 11 World News: Syrian Court Sentences Assad to Death in Absentia, Vietnam's VinSpace Teams with SpaceX, Iraqi Kurdistan Counts the War's Toll

August 11 brings a wave of major international stories: a Syrian court has sentenced ousted president Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia, convicting him of war crimes and crimes against humanity during the country's civil war, while his cousin Atef Najib was also condemned in the same case and appeared in a courtroom cage; Vietnam's aerospace firm VinSpace announced a partnership with SpaceX to launch its first satellites as early as the second quarter of 2027 via the Transporter rideshare program; and Iraqi Kurdistan's prime minister says the region has "paid a heavy price" in the US-Iran war—struck more than 1,000 times by missiles and drones, with trade down 70% and losses estimated above $1.1 billion. Details on each item below; all source timestamps have been verified.

1. Syrian Court Sentences Assad to Death in Absentia for War Crimes

According to the BBC (Aug 11, 09:42 GMT, published about 26 minutes ago), a Syrian court has sentenced ousted dictator Bashar al-Assad to death in absentia after convicting him of war crimes and crimes against humanity committed during the civil war. Assad fled to Moscow with his family after his regime was overthrown in December 2024, ending his family's five-decade grip on power. In the same case, the court also sentenced to death Atef Najib, Assad's cousin and former head of political security in southern Syria's Deraa province—he appeared inside a cage in the courtroom as the judge read out his sentence.

Deraa is widely seen as the birthplace of the uprising against Assad: in March 2011, the detention and alleged torture of teenagers accused of writing anti-government graffiti sparked protests, which were met with a violent crackdown that helped ignite nationwide demonstrations and a civil war that killed hundreds of thousands. Syria's interim government began trying officials linked to the Assad regime earlier this year, and Najib is one of the highest-ranking officials to be put on trial, convicted of murder, "the intentional killing of children under 15" and "torture leading to death." It remains unclear whether Syria's new authorities will be able to secure Assad's extradition from Moscow.

2. Vietnam's VinSpace Teams with SpaceX for First Satellite Launches

According to Al Jazeera (Aug 11, 09:43 GMT, by John Power), Vietnam's aerospace company VinSpace has announced a partnership with SpaceX, using the US firm's "Transporter" rideshare program to deploy satellites into orbit in the second quarter of 2027. Under the agreement, the Hanoi-based company will be responsible for the research, development, manufacturing and operation of the satellites after launch, which the firm says will let it test its technology in real-world conditions, expand international cooperation and gradually commercialize space technology.

"Testing domestically developed satellite modules in orbit is an important condition for transforming the research and development capabilities of VinSpace's engineering team into real missions," CEO Vu Trong Thu said. "The contract with SpaceX is an important step forward in VinSpace's long-term strategy, aimed at contributing to the development of a commercial aerospace ecosystem in Vietnam, while connecting Vietnam with the global space value chain." VinSpace is owned by Vietnam's wealthiest man, Pham Nhat Vuong, and announced in April that it would aim to manufacture and launch its first satellites in 2027. Vietnam has launched multiple satellites since VINASAT-1, its first, in 2008; in 2024 the ruling Communist Party defined space technology as a national strategic technology for the first time, placing outer space on par with the maritime and subterranean domains.

3. Iraqi Kurdistan: Over 1,000 Strikes, Trade Down 70%, Losses Above $1.1 Billion

According to Al Jazeera (Aug 11, 09:47 GMT, by Sherko Raouf), hundreds of missiles and drones have hit Iraq's semi-autonomous Kurdish region since the US-Iran war broke out in February. Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG) Prime Minister Masrour Barzani told Al Jazeera the region has "paid a heavy price": "We have been targeted more than 1,000 times by missiles and drones directly from Iran or from inside Iraq by some of the militias. We think these are all unjustifiable attacks on Kurdistan. Unfortunately, we had casualties and we lost people. Many of our infrastructure have been hit badly." He added that the conflict and its security fallout had led to a 70 percent decrease in trade across the Kurdish region.

According to KRG data, the attacks hit military and security sites, energy facilities, bases of opposition Iranian Kurdish parties and civilian areas. A direct casualty is the economy: the oil and gas sectors, and the movement of companies and investment—all at a time when the region faces a chronic financial crisis amid disputes with the federal government in Baghdad over salary payments for state employees. Peshawa Hawramani, a KRG spokesman, said the scale of losses inflicted on the region by the war up to April is estimated at 1.5 trillion Iraqi dinars ($1.14 billion), figures that were handed over to the Iraqi government.
