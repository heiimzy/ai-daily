⚡ Aug 9 Tech Roundup: Samsung Targets -30°C Heat Pumps with ORNL and Opens Taylor Fab Internships, Lotte Mart Deploys 1,000 Ocado Robots, Apple Pulls Qwen Guide in China, LG Uplus Validates Edge AI

Over the past hour or two, multiple threads of general tech news moved fast from Seoul to Austin to Beijing: Samsung Electronics announced a partnership with Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) under the U.S. Department of Energy to develop heat pump technology that can operate in subminus-30°C conditions, while its Taylor, Texas advanced foundry kicked off its first internship recruitment ahead of year-end operations; Lotte Mart switched on a Busan fulfillment center run by 1,000 Ocado robots, taking on Coupang and Kurly in fresh grocery; Apple China deleted its manual for integrating Alibaba's Qwen into Apple Intelligence, with customer service saying regulatory approval is pending; and LG Uplus validated an edge-AI system that detects and restores home network faults on a Broadcom Wi-Fi 8 platform. Below is a detailed breakdown with publication times verified source by source.

1. Samsung × ORNL: Heat Pump Tech for -30°C Cold Regions

According to ChosunBiz (English edition, published around 9:41 a.m. Beijing time on Aug 9), Samsung Electronics will work with Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) under the U.S. Department of Energy to develop next-generation heating technology. The project, titled "Development of vapor-compression heating and multi-solution for cold regions," targets reliable high-efficiency heating down to minus 30 degrees Celsius, a long-standing pain point where conventional heat pumps lose performance in extreme cold.

On the technical side, Samsung plans to apply a rotary scroll method to the compressor to reduce energy loss and improve stability, combined with flash-injection cycle enhancement. The company says it will use the research data to bring heat pump products to the global market that minimize carbon emissions while maximizing performance and energy efficiency. The tie-up extends a growing trend of appliance giants co-developing efficiency technology with national laboratories. (Source: ChosunBiz)

2. Samsung Opens First Taylor Fab Internships Ahead of Year-End Ramp

The same day, Samsung Electronics launched the first internship recruitment for its advanced foundry plant in Taylor, Texas (ChosunBiz, published around 9:58 a.m. Beijing time). According to industry sources, the facility is set to begin operations at the end of this year, making the hiring push an early talent-stockpiling move. Samsung's Taylor site competes head-to-head with TSMC's Arizona fabs, and the scramble for U.S. semiconductor talent is heating up.

Analysts note that advanced fabs depend heavily on engineers and operators, and hiring before production starts can meaningfully shorten the output ramp. With CHIPS Act projects entering volume production, the "talent war" between Taylor and Phoenix is expected to intensify. (Source: ChosunBiz)

3. Lotte Mart's 'Zetta Smart Center Busan': 1,000 Ocado Robots Run Fresh Grocery Fulfillment

On the retail side, Lotte Mart announced that its Busan "Zetta Smart Center" online grocery fulfillment center is fully operational, deploying up to 1,000 Ocado robots (ChosunBiz, published around 10:00 a.m. Beijing time). Inside the refrigerated storage, grid-shaped rail equipment resembling a giant Go board fills the space, with each cell holding different products — from fresh food to chilled goods — picked and sorted by robots; except for a few steps such as inbounding and final packing, robots handle most tasks, with only about 300 people stationed at the 12,000-pyeong center.

Built on Ocado's Smart Platform (OSP), the center establishes a complete cold chain from inbound to delivery. Targeting the classic complaints of online grocery — wilted vegetables, missing items, and late delivery — Lotte says the new hub will serve about 4 million households in Busan and the Yeongnam region, competing head-on with Coupang and Kurly. Lotte argues its three decades of direct sourcing plus Ocado's AI robot logistics give it a real edge in fresh e-commerce. (Source: ChosunBiz)

4. Apple China Pulls Its Qwen Integration Manual

According to cnBeta (published around 9:32 a.m. Beijing time), Apple's official Simplified-Chinese Mac manual had confirmed that Apple Intelligence would work with Alibaba's Qwen large language model — but that manual has now been deleted. Multiple outlets report that Apple customer service says the feature is "awaiting regulatory approval", widely read as a compliance checkpoint in Apple and Alibaba's plan to bring LLM-powered features to mainland China.

Based on the earlier page, the Qwen extension applies to macOS 26.6 or higher, with conditions including a China-spec device, an Apple account region set to mainland China, and being physically located in mainland China. The extension deeply integrates with Writing Tools and Siri, letting users invoke Qwen for text creation and rewriting in apps like Notes and Mail, configured via System Settings → Apple Intelligence & Siri → Extensions. On Aug 8, several outlets had already reported Apple letting China Mac users plug Qwen into Siri; the manual removal is the latest twist. (Source: cnBeta)

5. LG Uplus Validates Edge AI That Self-Heals Home Networks

In telecom, LG Uplus announced it validated a technology in which AI detects signs of abnormal internet quality and restores the network on its own (ChosunBiz, published around 9:24 a.m. Beijing time). Working with Broadcom on a Wi-Fi 8 platform, the carrier demonstrated automatic recovery in which AI analyzes and optimizes home network status. Crucially, the AI computation runs inside the home router rather than on cloud servers, reducing latency and privacy exposure.

LG Uplus calls this "the first step toward realizing an intelligent home network in which AI autonomously manages customer-perceived quality," and says it will keep expanding cooperation with global technology partners. Edge AI plus self-healing networks is emerging as a way for carriers to cut truck rolls and improve customer experience. (Source: ChosunBiz)

Energy tech, chip talent, automated grocery retail, and China's AI ecosystem together sketch the general-tech landscape of early August.
