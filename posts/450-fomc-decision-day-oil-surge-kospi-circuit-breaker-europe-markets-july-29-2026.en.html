⚡ July 29 Financial Super Day: FOMC Decision with Historic Uncertainty, Oil Surges 4% After Iran Strikes, KOSPI Breaks Below 6,000, European Stocks Defy Turmoil

July 29, 2026 — Global financial markets are facing the most consequential single trading session of the year as three epochal events converge: the Federal Reserve's FOMC rate decision with the highest uncertainty in years, a sudden escalation in Middle East hostilities with Iran launching ballistic missiles at US forces, and South Korea's stock market collapsing into a second straight circuit breaker. Asset prices are swinging violently as investors brace for "Super Wednesday."

1. FOMC Decision Day: Warsh Era Brings Unprecedented Uncertainty

The Federal Open Market Committee concludes its two-day meeting on Wednesday, July 29, with the rate decision due at 2:00 PM ET (18:00 UTC) followed by Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET. The suspense surrounding this meeting is at a level rarely seen since September 2024.

According to CME FedWatch data as of Wednesday European trading, markets are pricing approximately a 30% probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike — more than triple the odds from two weeks ago when it stood at just 10.7%. While all 104 economists surveyed by Reuters expect the Fed to hold rates at 3.50%-3.75%, futures market positioning tells a different story: open interest in the August federal funds futures contract has surged to an all-time high of over 967,000 contracts, surpassing the previous record set in October 2024.

This rare divergence between economist consensus and market pricing stems from Chair Kevin Warsh's radical departure from predecessor Jerome Powell's forward guidance approach. Warsh has told Congress he has "zero tolerance" for persistently elevated inflation, stating flatly that "prices are too high." Two FOMC voters — Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack — have both signaled hawkish leanings, raising the possibility of dissenting votes. If both vote for a hike while the majority holds, it would mark the first time since December 1993 that two Fed governors dissented at the same meeting.

UBS Chief US Economist Jonathan Pingle said he has "not been this uncertain about a Fed rate decision in 20 years," noting that Warsh's lack of a track record and recent FOMC division compound the unpredictability. Markets are pricing an 80% probability of a September hike.

2. Middle East Flare-Up: Iran Fires Ballistic Missiles, Oil Surges 4%

After three days of relative calm that saw oil prices plunge 13.5%, the Middle East conflict erupted anew on Wednesday. The US Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed that Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) launched "multiple ballistic missiles in an attempted surprise attack on US forces based in the Middle East." All missiles were successfully intercepted.

In response, US and Saudi fighter aircraft struck "multiple terrorist logistics and weapons sites across eastern Iraq" in retaliation for "over 30 IRGC-directed aerial drone attacks in the last 72 hours" targeting US forces and Saudi energy infrastructure.

Oil prices reversed sharply. As of 09:00 GMT, Brent crude for September delivery surged 3.6% to $87.11 per barrel, while WTI rose 3.6% to $82.11. This follows Brent's 13.5% crash on Tuesday to $85.35 — the sharpest single-day drop of the current conflict cycle. July has seen Brent swing from around $72 at the start of the month to above $100 last week, then back down to $82, and now back to $87 — a volatility range exceeding 38%.

ING analysts noted that Saudi Arabia's 400,000 b/d Jazan refinery remains shut following Houthi attacks over the weekend, exacerbating tightness in middle distillate markets. The ICE gasoil crack spread has broken above $70/bbl to record levels, with the prompt timespread surging to backwardation above $80/bbl.

Iran also announced Wednesday that it has halted three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately 20% of global petroleum flows. Tehran rejected an Omani proposal for joint 50-50 control of the waterway, insisting on full control of inbound lanes and partial control of outbound routes.

3. KOSPI Crashes Below 6,000: Second Straight Circuit Breaker

South Korea's KOSPI index suffered another brutal session Wednesday, plunging as much as 11% in intraday trading and crashing through the psychologically critical 6,000 level to a three-month low. The Korea Exchange triggered a sell-side sidecar (circuit breaker) in both the KOSPI and KOSDAQ markets — the second consecutive day of such halts.

The KOSPI closed 6.0% lower at 5,663.24, while the KOSDAQ fell 5.98% to 663.66. SK Hynix shares collapsed as much as 20% before closing roughly 10% lower. The company's Q2 operating profit of 60.5 trillion won ($42 billion) soared 557% year-over-year but missed analyst expectations of 64.2 trillion won. SK Hynix has now lost over 50% of its market value since hitting an all-time high just one month ago. Samsung Electronics fell 4.5%.

The selling extended across Asia's semiconductor complex: Japan's Kioxia plunged 13.9%, Tokyo Electron fell 11%, and the Nikkei 225 dropped 1.5% to 61,434. Taiwan's TAIEX lost over 3%.

However, not all Asian markets succumbed to panic. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index rallied 2.0% to 25,808, led by a rebound in its tech sector. The Shanghai Composite edged up 0.4% to 3,828. Australia, Singapore, and India also posted gains, underscoring that the selloff remains concentrated in semiconductor names.

4. European Markets Open Higher: Energy Stocks Lead

European equity markets opened broadly higher on Wednesday, demonstrating resilience despite the geopolitical turmoil. At 08:37 BST, London's FTSE 100 rose 0.6% to 10,932.62, supported by energy sector gains on higher oil prices and strong earnings from Weir Group and Reckitt Benckiser. Germany's DAX gained 0.3%, while France's CAC 40 added 0.5%.

"The FTSE 100 has largely shrugged off events, with energy giants gaining ground on higher oil prices offering support," said Susannah Streeter, chief investment strategist at Wealth Club. She noted the conflict has "opened up a hornets' nest of hostilities, and each retaliatory sting threatens to draw yet more regional players into an increasingly difficult conflict to control."

In currency markets, the US Dollar Index held near a one-month high around 101.40. EUR/USD traded in a narrow range near 1.1400, GBP/USD around 1.3300, and USD/JPY near 163.60 as the yen continued to weaken on the US-Japan interest rate differential.

5. Gold Holds Above $4,000 Awaiting FOMC Direction

Spot gold traded in a tight $4,020-$4,050 range Wednesday, consolidating after two days of losses. While renewed Middle East tensions provide safe-haven support, the oil-driven rebound in inflation expectations has strengthened the case for a hawkish Fed, limiting upside for the non-yielding metal.

As of 09:00 GMT, gold was at $4,037/oz, up 0.22% on the day. Commerzbank lowered its year-end forecast from $4,800 to $4,500 but projects a recovery to $5,000 by end-2027, arguing that current market expectations of Fed rate hikes are "excessive" and that the central bank may begin cutting from mid-2027.

6. Crude Inventory Draw and OPEC+ Dynamics

On the supply side, the American Petroleum Institute (API) reported US crude inventories fell by approximately 3.3 million barrels for the week ended July 24. Gasoline stocks rose by 918,000 barrels while distillate inventories increased by 355,000 barrels. The more closely watched EIA official data is due later Wednesday.

Separately, Reuters reported that OPEC+ is likely to pause planned production increases for three months beginning in October, once the scheduled return of barrels following voluntary cuts is completed. If Middle East tensions persist, this decision would further tighten global oil supply.

JPMorgan estimates that each additional month of supply disruption could add roughly $7-8/bbl to Brent prices. A three-month disruption could push monthly average Brent to approximately $114/bbl. Goldman Sachs maintains a base case of eventual de-escalation with Q4 Brent averaging $80, but warns risks remain "tilted to the upside."

Looking ahead, markets face a triple wave of events tonight: the Fed rate decision (2:00 PM ET) → Warsh press conference (2:30 PM ET) → Meta and Microsoft earnings (after market close). The convergence of these three events will set the directional tone for global asset pricing in the final days of July and into August.
