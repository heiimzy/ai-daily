⚡ July 30 Financial Fallout: 30-Year Yield Hits 5.22% (2007 High), Microsoft Surges 8% After Earnings Beat, Meta Free Cash Flow Craters 91%, Asian Markets Rebound in Turmoil

1. Fed Aftermath: Bond Market Referendum on Warsh's Credibility

The Federal Reserve voted 9-3 to hold the federal funds rate at 3.5%-3.75%, marking the most dissenting votes since 2016. Cleveland Fed's Beth Hammack, Minneapolis Fed's Neel Kashkari, and Dallas Fed's Lorie Logan voted against, favoring an immediate 25-basis-point hike.

But the bond market's reaction spoke louder than the decision itself. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh described the internal divide as a "good family fight" and insisted there is no "magic wand" for inflation — yet markets delivered a blunt verdict:

The 30-year Treasury yield surged 11.5 basis points to 5.211%, its highest level since June 2007 — a 19-year high. The 10-year yield jumped 7 basis points to 4.67%, while the 2-year yield fell 4 basis points, producing the steepest yield curve steepening since the mid-1990s.

DoubleLine CEO Jeffrey Gundlach told CNBC that the long-bond selloff was a direct challenge to Warsh's anti-inflation credibility. The market's interpretation was clear: Warsh's hawkish rhetoric lacks concrete action, and inflation expectations are starting to de-anchor.

Evercore ISI's Krishna Guha noted that September — not July — is Warsh's true "credibility test moment." If summer inflation and energy data remain hot, he will have to hike to preserve the central bank's credibility.

The CME FedWatch tool shows September hike pricing falling from 79% pre-decision to 54%, but year-end pricing still fully incorporates two 25-basis-point hikes. Markets have merely delayed, not abandoned, rate hike expectations.

2. US Stocks Rout: Dow Plunges 1,153 Points, Nasdaq Enters Correction

US equity markets suffered their worst session since April 2025 during Warsh's press conference, with a sharp selloff accelerating into the close:

  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: Crashed 1,153.18 points (2.19%) to 51,594.14 — worst single-day decline since April 2025. Approximately $340 billion in market value was erased from the 30 Dow components.
  • S&P 500: Fell 112.63 points (1.52%) to 7,316.15. All 11 sectors ended in negative territory, with Industrials (-3.24%) leading losses.
  • Nasdaq Composite: Dropped 433.97 points (1.74%) to 24,442.94 — sixth consecutive session of decline. Now more than 10% below its record high, officially entering correction territory.
  • Philadelphia Semiconductor Index: Plunged over 5%, extending its losing streak to five sessions — the longest since December. KLA Corporation (-10.80%), Micron (-9.83%), Applied Materials (-8.40%), and Arm Holdings (-8.21%) led the carnage.

The VIX fear index surged 13.29% to 20.63, reflecting sharply elevated market anxiety.

3. Tech Earnings: Tale of Two Giants — Microsoft Soars 8%, Meta Crumbles 11%

Microsoft Q4: Across-the-Board Beat

Microsoft reported fiscal Q4 2026 results (ending June) after the bell, crushing expectations across every key metric:

  • Revenue: $90 billion, up 18% YoY, well above consensus
  • Net Income: $35.8 billion, up 31% YoY
  • EPS: Up 23% YoY, beating estimates by over 10%
  • Azure Cloud: Grew 43%, with full-year cloud revenue surpassing $100 billion for the first time
  • Anthropic investment contribution: $3.2 billion in revenue this quarter
  • Capital Expenditure: Up 70% YoY but 3.5% below expectations. Full-year CapEx guidance lowered to $175 billion, ~8% below prior estimates

Markets rewarded Microsoft's ability to balance AI infrastructure investment with profitability — the stock surged up to 9% after hours, settling at an 8% gain. Piper Sandler raised its price target from $540 to $550.

Meta Q2: Revenue Beat Obscured by Cash Flow Collapse

Meta's Q2 2026 results painted a mixed picture:

  • Revenue: $60.8 billion, up 28% YoY, slightly above expectations
  • Q3 Guidance: Median revenue guidance of $62.5 billion, below the $63.2 billion consensus
  • Free Cash Flow: Plummeted to $784 million, down 91% YoY — a four-year low
  • Full-Year CapEx Guidance: Lower bound raised to $130 billion

Investors punished Meta for its "unrestrained AI spending" with unclear near-term returns. The stock crashed roughly 11% after hours — its worst post-earnings reaction since 2022.

Qualcomm Q3: Net Income Down 25%, Chip Price Hike from Sept 1

Qualcomm reported fiscal Q3 2026 revenue of $9.95 billion (down 4% YoY) and net income of $2.0 billion (down 25% YoY). Handset chip revenue slumped 20% to $5.1 billion, dragged by a maturing Chinese smartphone market and memory chip prices surging ~300% YoY.

CEO Cristiano Amon announced a comprehensive chip price increase effective September 1, with "double-digit" percentage hikes aimed at restoring historical margin levels. Qualcomm also confirmed the acquisition of AI software company Modular and named Meta as its first hyperscale customer for AI data center processors.

Qualcomm shares fell about 4% after hours.

4. Asian Markets: Turbulent Rebound After Historic Selloff

Asian markets staged a volatile rebound on Thursday (July 30) after two days of catastrophic selling:

  • South Korea's KOSPI: Traded in a wide range, surging as much as 4% before closing up ~1.5%. The index is still staring at a 12% weekly decline — a record. Finance Minister Koo Yun-cheol publicly apologized for the single-stock leveraged ETF debacle that exacerbated the crash.
  • Japan's Nikkei 225: Closed up 1.74%, but still down 3% for the week.
  • Taiwan TAIEX: Attempted stabilization after a two-day crash of nearly 3,600 points. The FSC issued a late-night statement saying margin maintenance ratios remain well above forced-liquidation thresholds.
  • Australia's ASX 200: Slipped 0.50%.
  • MSCI Asia Pacific ex-Japan: Rose over 1% in early trading.

Samsung Electronics reported a 19-fold surge in Q2 operating profit to a record high, helping lift battered investor sentiment. SK Hynix, however, continued to struggle after its record earnings failed to meet already-elevated market expectations.

5. Hong Kong: ZJXC's Record IPO Breaks Issue Price on Debut

Zhongji Innolight (ZJXC, 03308.HK) — the world's leading optical module manufacturer — made its Hong Kong Stock Exchange debut today as the year's largest IPO. The H-share offering of 54.5 million shares at HK$980 each raised approximately HK$53.41 billion, the largest Hong Kong IPO since Alibaba's 2019 listing.

Yet the stock broke issue price on opening day, trading at HK$971 (down 0.92%). Losses widened to over 7% by midday. The company's A-shares (300308.SZ) had already crashed 15.69% two days earlier, with cumulative losses exceeding 35% from the June 22 all-time high — more than RMB 300 billion in market cap evaporated.

Three factors drove the debacle: (1) market rumors that 1.6T optical module prices could crash below $600 next year; (2) the ~21% discount of H-shares versus A-shares creating arbitrage pressure; (3) simultaneous listing of options and short-selling tools providing ammunition for bears.

Management scrambled to contain the damage, with Chairman Liu Sheng proposing an A-share buyback of RMB 4-8 billion and calling an emergency investor conference to clarify that current orders cover the entirety of 2026 production capacity.

6. Commodities & FX: Brent Back at $90, Gold Bounces on Safe-Haven Demand

Escalating Middle East tensions drove sharp commodity moves:

  • Brent Crude: Surged 7.9% to $90.74/barrel, reclaiming the $90 threshold. US Central Command resumed airstrikes on Iran overnight, targeting Qeshm Island and the southwestern city of Abadan in retaliation for Iran's ballistic missile attack on US forces in Jordan. Trump vowed to "hit them hard" and is weighing a 10-14 day intensive bombing campaign against Iranian missile capabilities.
  • WTI Crude: Rose 6.6% to $84.46/barrel.
  • Gold: Spot gold rebounded 0.87% to ~$4,070/oz, briefly breaching $4,100 during the session. A weaker dollar and renewed geopolitical risk jointly fueled safe-haven demand.
  • US Dollar Index: Fell 0.62% to 100.90. The unusual divergence — falling dollar alongside surging Treasury yields — signals markets are questioning both the US economic outlook and Fed credibility simultaneously.

7. China: ETF Onslaught, Tech Finance Data Push

In China, broad-based ETFs saw massive volume for a second consecutive day on July 29, with total turnover reaching RMB 109.7 billion across 23 products exceeding RMB 1 billion each. Month-to-date ETF net inflows reached RMB 435.99 billion as of July 28, with broad-based ETFs accounting for RMB 258.15 billion.

Nine Chinese government departments jointly issued a notice on strengthening data development and utilization in the technology finance sector, promoting open sharing and integrated application of sci-tech financial data.

Separately, the National Energy Administration announced that summer peak electricity demand has hit multiple record highs, reaching 1.553 billion kW, but power supply remains stable overall. China's new energy storage installations reached 153 GW by end of June, up 61% YoY.
