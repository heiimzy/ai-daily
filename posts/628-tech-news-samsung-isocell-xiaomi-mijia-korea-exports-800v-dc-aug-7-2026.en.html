⚡ Aug 7 Tech Briefing: Samsung's 200MP ISOCELL HPC Debuts DeepPix, Xiaomi Brings Mijia to India, South Korea Exports Top Japan, 800V DC Reshapes AI Data Centers

Four new developments across the tech world landed in the past hour or two: Samsung unveiled its new 200MP ISOCELL HPC smartphone image sensor with the debut of the DeepPix pixel architecture; Xiaomi launched the Mijia sub-brand in India, moving into AIoT smart home and large appliances against Samsung and LG; South Korea's first-half exports overtook Japan for the first time on the back of AI chips; and the industry is speeding up adoption of an 800V DC power architecture to reshape AI data centers. Here is this round's tech briefing.

1. Samsung Unveils 200MP ISOCELL HPC: DeepPix Pixels, Single-Frame 16-bit HDR, 4K@180fps

Samsung has officially launched its new 200MP smartphone image sensor, the ISOCELL HPC, headlined by the all-new DeepPix pixel architecture. According to Samsung, DeepPix uses Front Deep Trench Isolation (FDTI) to expand both the photodiode capacity and the shared floating diffusion, boosting the sensor's full-well capacity (FWC) by 60% over the previous generation — collecting more light while reducing noise for brighter highlights, deeper shadows and a wider dynamic range.

On paper, the ISOCELL HPC features a 1/1.3-inch optical format with 0.6μm pixels and an effective resolution of 16,384 x 12,288. It supports single-frame 16-bit HDR, capturing HDR information in one exposure to preserve natural colors and fine detail, plus 8K@30fps, 4K@180fps and 1080p@360fps (without autofocus) video recording. It also packs Tetra²pixel color filter, Super QPD autofocus and Smart-ISO Pro HDR, extends single-frame HDR across 1x, 1.5x, 2x, 3x and 4x zoom levels, and outputs RAW10/12/14/16. Samsung has not yet confirmed which device will debut the sensor — most expect a next-generation Galaxy S flagship.

2. Xiaomi Launches Mijia in India: From Phones to AIoT Smart Home, Taking on Samsung and LG

Xiaomi has officially introduced its smart ecosystem sub-brand "Mijia" in India, officially styled "Mijia by Xiaomi," signaling the company's intent to transplant its China-proven AIoT smart home playbook into the Indian market. According to Indian media including The Economic Times, Mijia will carry Xiaomi's smart home and whole-home connectivity strategy in India, expanding its portfolio from smartphone accessories into large appliances and lifestyle electronics — going head-to-head with Samsung and LG, which dominate the Indian home appliance market.

The launch comes at a pivotal moment — Xiaomi has now spent 12 years in India, where its smartphones have long ranked among the top sellers. With phone growth slowing, Xiaomi is shifting momentum toward higher-margin large appliances, smart home gear and ecosystem revenue. Industry watchers say the Mijia sub-brand marks Xiaomi's attempt to replicate its Chinese home-appliance playbook in India through a "phones x AIoT" ecosystem, with local manufacturing and supply chain depth likely to decide how far it gets.

3. South Korea's H1 Exports Overtake Japan for the First Time, Powered by AI Chips

South Korea's exports overtook Japan's in the first half of this year for the first time on record — a symbolic shift in Asia's export landscape. South Korean financial media reported that booming demand for AI chips and semiconductors powered the surge: global data center buildouts, exploding AI server demand and tight HBM high-bandwidth memory supply have kept Korean chip shipments growing at a double-digit pace.

Earlier official data already showed South Korea's July exports beating forecasts on robust AI investment demand, with chip exports up strongly for months in a row. Japan's exports, by contrast, have been relatively subdued amid softness in autos and machinery. Analysts see the milestone as direct evidence that the global supply chain's center of gravity is shifting toward AI compute and advanced memory — whoever holds the ecosystem position in AI chipmaking and memory can take the lead in world trade.

4. Data Centers Move to 800V DC Power: The Next-Gen Distribution for High-Density AI

The industry is accelerating adoption of an 800V DC power architecture to meet the surging rack-level power demands of AI compute clusters. Traditional data centers rely on AC distribution with UPS systems; as AI GPU racks climb from tens of kilowatts to well over a hundred, AC line losses and footprint become bottlenecks. A 800V DC bus cuts current dramatically, reducing copper usage and heat while improving end-to-end efficiency — positioning it as the next-generation distribution scheme for high-density AI clusters.

Vendors and cloud providers are already piloting 800V DC architectures paired with solid-state transformers, storage and high-voltage DC equipment, forming a "high-voltage DC + storage" power system. Industry observers liken the shift to the earlier jump from 12V to 48V rack power — and see it as a key lever for AI data centers to cut costs, lift efficiency and reduce carbon intensity.
