⚡ Aug 2 Tech Update: OpenAI's Astra Solves 10 Open Math Problems, Google Rolls Back Earth AI Images, Anthropic Admits Claude Code Bugs

Sunday, August 2, the tech world is buzzing over three big stories: OpenAI publicly confirmed the Astra name for its next major model family, saying an internal build solved ten long-standing open problems in mathematics and theoretical computer science for roughly $2,000 in total token cost; Google hit pause on its AI image-generation feature in Google Earth less than 24 hours after launch, unable to stop what researchers call "grounded deepfakes"; and Anthropic published an engineering postmortem admitting three of its own bugs - not user error - caused a month-long Claude Code performance decline after weeks of denials.

OpenAI Unveils Astra: Ten Math Breakthroughs for About $2,000

According to OpenAI's official blog and The Decoder, on August 1 (Saturday US time), OpenAI published "Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science," officially confirming Astra as the name of its next major model family. The problems "have seen no progress on the main result for at least a decade, and in most cases much longer," spanning eight fields: high-dimensional geometry, coding theory, arithmetic circuit complexity, group theory, operator algebras, quantum complexity, lattice cryptography, and extremal combinatorics.

Headline results include: a construction establishing the existence of non-sofic groups - a central open question in group theory that had resisted proof for decades; a disproof of Connes's rigidity conjecture; the first improvement to the general upper bound on high-dimensional sphere-packing density since 1978; and resolution of Erdős problems 183 (a superexponential lower bound for multicolor triangle Ramsey numbers), 146 and 180. OpenAI says the tokens used to generate all ten solutions would have cost roughly $2,000 at Sol API rates. Humans worked with the model to turn the arguments into a 249-page manuscript, the model formalized each proof in Lean 4, and the openai/ten-proofs repository on GitHub ships machine-checkable certificates plus a walkthrough of the model's reasoning for every result.

"Yes, non-sofic groups exist - this statement is one of many new beautiful results proved by Astra, our next major model." - Sebastien Bubeck, OpenAI's head of mathematics research, August 1

The mathematical community's reaction is enthusiastic but cautious: Fields Medalist Timothy Gowers said he would recommend one of the proofs for publication in Annals of Mathematics without hesitation, and University of Manchester mathematician Thomas Bloom called the constructions "major news." Skeptics pushed back too: Gary Marcus noted the 249-page paper devotes no pages to how the model works or how the proofs were verified, and Noam Brown clarified that Astra has not solved any Millennium Prize Problems. According to The Information, CEO Sam Altman demoed Astra to Washington policymakers this week, and Astra is expected to be among the first models reviewed under the US government's new frontier-AI framework before any public release. No launch date or final naming (speculation centers on GPT-6) has been announced.

Google Earth's AI Image Generator: Rolled Back in Under a Day

According to Forkast and AFP, Google integrated the Nano Banana 2 image-generation model into Google Earth on July 30, letting users generate "visualizations" on top of real satellite, aerial, and 3D-mapping data with a single click. Less than 24 hours later (July 31), Google rolled the feature back. A spokesperson said: "We've seen people sharing screenshots of generated imagery that appear to violate our policies. So we're rolling back this feature in Google Earth while we work on implementing stronger guardrails."

The controversy centered on what researchers call "grounded deepfakes": AI-generated content layered directly onto geographic data that users deeply trust. AFP's tests produced fabricated satellite imagery of an explosion in Paris, a nuclear site in Iran, a bomb crater in Russia, and an ISIS training ground in Syria, plus fake scenes including floods in Bangladesh, a migrant caravan at the US-Mexico border, and a suspicious warehouse in Georgia - one of the states Trump has falsely accused of widespread election fraud. NPR also generated images of Iran's Kharg Island on fire and a flooded US Capitol, events that never happened.

The key lesson: SynthID watermarks only establish provenance; they do not block harmful content - a post-hoc label rather than a functional barrier. Ross Burley, executive director of the London-based Centre for Information Resilience, called the feature "irresponsible," warning that "trust in satellite imagery has taken decades to build and could be irrevocably damaged overnight." The volunteer OSINT project GeoConfirmed also urged Google to reconsider before the rollback. Google says it will reassess once stronger guardrails are in place.

Anthropic Admits: Three of Its Own Bugs Broke Claude Code

According to Fortune, Yahoo Finance, and Startup Fortune, Anthropic published an engineering postmortem (April 23) admitting that three engineering bugs - not user error - caused Claude Code's month-long performance decline, after weeks of pushing back on complaints. Fortune called the backlash a "user revolt," accompanied by a wave of Claude Pro and Max subscription cancellations.

The three bugs: on March 4, Anthropic changed Claude Code's default reasoning effort from high to medium to cut latency (reverted April 7 - "Speed went up. Quality went down."); on March 26, a caching change meant to clear stale thinking from idle sessions had a bug that cleared history on every turn, making the model look forgetful and repetitive while burning through usage limits (fixed April 10); and on April 16, a system prompt capped text between tool calls at 25 words and final responses at 100 words, visibly hurting coding quality (removed April 20).

The fallout was real: a senior AI executive at AMD called Claude "unusable for complex engineering tasks" at the worst stretch. All three issues were marked resolved in Claude Code 2.1.116, and Anthropic says the API was not affected, though the Claude Agent SDK and Claude Cowork were touched by the broader product-layer problems. The bugs are fixed, but as one analysis put it, "the trust repair is still open" - developers want to know the tool will behave the same next week, and that complaints will be treated as signal before the cancellations pile up.
