Ceuta: About 49,000 Migrants Cross from Morocco in 24 Hours, at Least 18 Dead

Spanish officials estimate that roughly 49,000 people have entered Ceuta — Spain's North African enclave — from Morocco by land and sea in the past 24 hours. Videos and images showed thousands of people swimming into the city on Thursday, and local media reported crossings continuing overnight. Officials say at least 18 people have died in recent days while trying to reach the territory. Ceuta, on Morocco's northern coast separated from mainland Spain by the Strait of Gibraltar, has a population of about 85,000 — meaning the one-day influx may exceed half the city's population.

Spain subsequently deployed its armed forces to bolster security in Ceuta and its sister city Melilla, where 300 to 400 crossings were also reported overnight. Officials estimate at least 7,000 of those who entered illegally in the past 24 hours are minors. The two cities form the European Union's only land borders with Africa and have long been a flashpoint in Spanish-Moroccan relations. Earlier this month, Spain's Supreme Court ruled that migrants stopped at sea while trying to reach Ceuta or Melilla cannot be summarily returned to Morocco — a decision seen as contributing to the surge. The last comparable influx came in 2021, when some 8,000 people crossed amid a diplomatic dispute, on nowhere near this scale.

FIFA Crisis: Senior Adviser Cordeiro Resigns in Protest at $20B World Cup Privatization

Carlos Cordeiro — FIFA president Gianni Infantino's senior adviser, a former Goldman Sachs banker and former president of the U.S. Soccer Federation — resigned on Friday in protest at FIFA's plan to sell stakes in World Cup commercial rights to private investors. "I cannot stand by while FIFA considers selling a stake in the World Cup. Let me be clear: I had no involvement in this proposal, and I oppose it unequivocally. It is a bad deal for football," Cordeiro said in a statement. He had often joined Infantino on working visits to meet U.S. President Donald Trump at the White House in recent years.

Infantino's proposal would spin off FIFA's commercial businesses — including the men's and women's World Cups and Club World Cups — into a $20 billion subsidiary, with 20% owned by private investors. FIFA described the "anchor investor" as a New York-based investment firm created by Joshua Kushner, the younger brother of Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner. Cordeiro pushed back, noting FIFA sits on billions of dollars in reserves with no debt and generated $15 billion in revenue over the last four-year cycle tied to the men's World Cup: "Selling a permanent stake in football's most valuable asset to raise $4.2 billion makes little sense. It is mortgaging football's future without any compelling justification."

Opposition is mounting: UEFA voted on Thursday to boycott World Cups if the plan proceeds, and Concacaf, which governs football in North and Central America, said its 41 member associations also rejected the proposal. FIFA needs 106 votes from its 211 member associations to pass it, while UEFA and Concacaf members combined hold 96 votes. On Friday, FIFA issued a statement saying the consultation process had been "disrupted by incorrect media reports," reaffirming: "Nobody is selling football. This is not something FIFA would ever entertain."

France: Russian Commentator Fedorova Ordered to Leave, Assets Frozen

The French government on July 31 ordered Russian TV commentator Xenia Fedorova to leave the country under an "emergency procedure" and announced a six-month freeze on her assets. Fedorova, 45, is the former head of the French arm of RT — the Russian state channel banned in France after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine — and now comments for outlets within French conservative billionaire Vincent Bolloré's media group. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez accused her of relaying Russian propaganda in an attempt to attack "the fundamental interests of the nation." The finance ministry said the decree published on July 31 immediately freezes her assets under a legal mechanism aimed at preventing "new acts of interference." A source close to the case said Fedorova has been placed under house arrest since being notified of the expulsion order.

In her first public comments, Fedorova denounced "censorship" and "political pressure" on X: "Those who celebrate this decision lack foresight, because today it is me, and tomorrow it will be anyone who dares to speak outside the bounds of approved thinking." Her lawyer, Emmanuel Piwnica, told AFP she was the victim of a "relentless campaign," and said both the expulsion order and the asset freeze would be challenged. With France heading toward presidential elections in 2027, disinformation experts have repeatedly warned about Russian influence operations.

Broad Peak Avalanche: Famed Climber Nirmal Purja Among 10 Missing

An avalanche on Pakistan's 8,051-meter Broad Peak has left 10 climbers missing, including legendary Nepali mountaineer Nirmal Purja. The slide struck shortly before 9:30 a.m. local time on July 30 at around 6,600 meters, catching summit-bound climbers; their InReach trackers showed rapid falls of hundreds of meters, with Purja's track showing a fall of about 800 meters. A ground search party has recovered four bodies from the base of the slide. Two of the deceased have been identified: Nadhira Al Harthy of Oman — the first Omani woman to summit Everest in 2019 — and Nepali guide Pur Bahadur Gurung.

Two helicopters departed Pakistan carrying renowned local climbers Sirbaz Khan and Sherzad Karim for an aerial search of the remaining missing. Purja, known as "Nims Dai," has been attempting to become the first person to summit all 14 eight-thousanders three times, and several members of his EliteExped team were caught in the incident.
