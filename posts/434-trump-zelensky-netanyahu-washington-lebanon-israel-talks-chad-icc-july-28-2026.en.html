⚡ July 28 World Affairs: Zelenskyy Meets Trump at White House for Air Defense Talks, Netanyahu Arrives for Iran War Coordination, Lebanon-Israel Rome Peace Talks Set, Chad Quits ICC

1. Washington Summit Day: Trump Hosts Zelenskyy and Netanyahu in Back-to-Back Meetings

On July 28, President Donald Trump held separate White House meetings with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu in a rare diplomatic marathon, bringing the Russia-Ukraine war and the US-Iran war to a single Washington intersection. All three leaders also attended the funeral of the late Sen. Lindsey Graham (R-SC), who died this month at age 71.

Zelenskyy Presses for Patriot Missile Production

Zelenskyy arrived at the Oval Office at 9:30 AM ET, saying before the talks that "peace must be brought closer." His core demand was to firm up Trump's earlier commitment to license joint production of Patriot interceptor missiles on Ukrainian soil.

Russia has sharply escalated ballistic missile attacks on Kyiv in recent weeks, rapidly depleting Ukraine's interceptor stockpiles. "Patriot missiles protect not just Ukraine's skies — they are the most urgently needed weapon," Zelenskyy said. A Raytheon delegation visited Kyiv last week and expressed "readiness to take our partnership to an even higher level."

The two leaders also discussed a joint drone production agreement. Ukraine is on track to produce 6-7 million short-range attack drones this year, while the Pentagon's $1.1 billion production program will have ordered just under 200,000 drones cumulatively by February 2027 — making Ukraine's battlefield expertise strategically valuable to the US.

Zelenskyy also met with 100 US senators. The Senate is expected to vote on a long-pending Russia sanctions bill championed by the late Sen. Graham, which would impose 100% tariffs on goods from the five largest importers of Russian oil and gas, including China and India. Trump made a last-minute intervention to add Iran sanctions to the same bill — a move Zelenskyy called "a great idea."

Netanyahu: Iran War Strategy and the Hormuz Chessboard

At 11:00 AM ET, Trump met with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu — their first face-to-face meeting since launching the joint US-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February, and the eighth such meeting since Trump returned to the White House.

Ahead of the meeting, Trump told reporters: "Without the United States — without me deciding to go in and blow up the nuclear weapons that were being formed — Israel wouldn't exist." He acknowledged "a few differences" with Netanyahu on Iran but said they remain "pretty close overall."

Key agenda items included whether to resume large-scale military strikes on Iran, the next-phase strategy for Strait of Hormuz shipping security, progress in US-Iran indirect negotiations, and the Saudi civilian nuclear deal's附带 normalization-with-Israel condition. Vice President JD Vance — who has recently criticized Israel for dragging the US into the Iran war — also attended the meeting.

Before departing for Washington, Netanyahu said his primary objective was to "understand Trump's ultimate decision," emphasizing that the trajectory of events largely depends on Washington's next move. He stated that the Israel-Iran conflict "will only end when the Iranian regime falls or is so weakened that it abandons its nuclear program."

2. US-Iran Ceasefire Holds for Fourth Day: Trump Says "Good Talks" Underway

The US-Iran military standoff has entered its fourth day without strikes. Trump announced a pause after 13 consecutive nights of bombing, and Iran has reciprocated by halting its own attacks. Trump told reporters aboard Air Force One: "We're having very friendly negotiations" but warned: "If it doesn't work, we go back to doing what we were doing two days ago — very strong military action."

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei denied direct talks with the US, stating: "Mediators may convey messages to us from the American side, but Iran is not conducting any negotiations with the US." He stressed that the Strait of Hormuz remains closed. Iran's armed forces announced they are accelerating missile production and improving precision and combat readiness during the ceasefire.

Regarding the future of the Strait of Hormuz, Oman has presented Iran with a proposal for joint regional management including "voluntary fees" rather than mandatory tolls, which has received backing from Gulf states. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi held separate phone calls with his Saudi and Omani counterparts on strait security. Meanwhile, Saudi Arabia reported intercepting drones launched from Iraqi territory by Iran-backed militias targeting its oil facilities; Iraq has ordered an investigation.

International oil prices fell sharply on ceasefire hopes. Brent crude dropped 8.2% to $87.77/barrel on July 27, while WTI fell 8.21% to $81.98/barrel. US Central Command stated that since reimposing the naval blockade, it has redirected 17 commercial ships, disabled 2 vessels, and boarded 2 others.

3. Lebanon and Israel Set Next Round of Rome Talks for August 4-6

A US State Department official announced late Monday that the next round of US-led direct negotiations between Lebanon and Israel will take place in Rome from August 4-6. The technical working group talks will focus on advancing full implementation of the trilateral framework agreement, including expanding the pilot zone process in southern Lebanon, resolving outstanding border issues, and moving toward a comprehensive peace and security agreement.

The talks build on a trilateral framework signed by Lebanon, Israel, and the United States on June 26. Under the deal, the Lebanese Armed Forces (LAF) assumes sole security control of areas vacated by Israeli forces and certifies that they have been cleared of Hezbollah weapons and infrastructure. Lebanese troops began taking over the first three villages — Froun, Srifa, and Zawtar al-Gharbiya — on July 20.

Significant obstacles remain. Israel has said its forces will stay in a security zone roughly 6 miles (10 km) deep along the frontier as long as Hezbollah remains armed, and the framework sets no timetable for full withdrawal. Hezbollah has rejected the arrangement. Lebanese President Joseph Aoun said Lebanon is committed to implementing the framework but condemned Israel's continued violations — including home demolitions and village razing in southern Lebanon — as unacceptable.

UN Human Rights Chief Volker Türk said the UN has deployed an independent assessment mission to Lebanon, documenting "massive violations of international humanitarian law" since March 2, with some acts possibly constituting "war crimes." Lebanon's Health Ministry reports 4,333 dead and 12,236 wounded since the conflict reignited on March 2.

4. Chad Withdraws from International Criminal Court as US "Dismantle ICC" Campaign Gains Ground

On July 28, Chad's Foreign Ministry formally announced its withdrawal from the Rome Statute, the founding treaty of the International Criminal Court (ICC). In its official communique, Chad said an "in-depth examination" revealed the court's effectiveness has remained "limited and uneven," alleging anti-African bias — noting that its first nine investigations all concerned African countries and that six of the seven people currently in ICC custody are from Africa.

The government called on the African Union and member states to reconsider what it called an "apparently biased system" and to strengthen African judicial mechanisms instead. The withdrawal takes effect in one year.

Chad's exit comes amid an intensified US campaign to dismantle the ICC following the court's issuance of arrest warrants for Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu and former Defense Minister Yoav Gallant over alleged war crimes in Gaza. Secretary of State Marco Rubio earlier this month announced a "comprehensive, whole-of-government campaign" to end what he called the ICC's "illegitimate overreach."

The State Department's Bureau of African Affairs welcomed Chad's decision, saying: "The United States will never surrender its sovereignty to an unaccountable global tribunal." It called on all other ICC members to withdraw from the Rome Statute. Chad made the decision just four days after a phone call in which US Under Assistant Secretary of State for African Affairs Frank Garcia Jr. requested the country review its ICC membership.

Chad's withdrawal follows Venezuela's exit from the ICC and the simultaneous withdrawal of Burkina Faso, Mali, and Niger in June 2026 — marking the most serious membership crisis the ICC has faced since its founding.
