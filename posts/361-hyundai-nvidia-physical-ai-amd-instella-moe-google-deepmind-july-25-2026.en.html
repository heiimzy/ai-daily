Hyundai and Nvidia Formalize 'Physical AI Alliance' with $6.2B Saemangeum Project

Hyundai Motor Group Executive Chair Euisun Chung traveled to Nvidia's headquarters in Santa Clara on July 24 to formalize what the companies are calling a "Physical AI Alliance" — a strategic partnership aimed at merging autonomous driving, humanoid robotics, and AI-powered manufacturing under one umbrella. The upgrade shifts their relationship from "technology adoption to joint innovation."

The centerpiece is the Saemangeum Innovation Hub in North Jeolla Province, South Korea, estimated at 9 trillion won (~$6.2 billion), comprising three major components:

  • Hyperscale AI Data Center (5.8 trillion won): Housing 50,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs for training autonomous driving, humanoid robotics, and software-defined vehicle models
  • Robot Manufacturing Cluster (400 billion won): A 1.12-million-square-meter facility with final assembly, contract manufacturing, and parts production using AGVs and autonomous mobile robots for fully automated logistics
  • Hydrogen Production Base (1 trillion won): Water electrolysis facilities powering an "AI Hydrogen City" demonstration zone

Jensen Huang stated that humanoid robots are "very very close" to industrial reality, and called Hyundai the ideal partner to bring physical AI from labs to factory floors. The two companies also plan to jointly develop a "robot reference platform" open to Korean universities, research institutes, and startups.

"AI will transform every industry, empower every company, and be built by every nation," Huang said. "Korea's world-class manufacturing competitiveness makes it the perfect testbed for physical AI."

AMD Releases Fully Open-Source Instella-MoE: 16B Model Trained Entirely on AMD Hardware

On July 25, AMD announced the release of Instella-MoE, a fully open-source Mixture-of-Experts large language model representing a major step forward in AMD's AI software ecosystem ambitions.

Instella-MoE has 16 billion total parameters with only 2.8 billion active per token, delivering leading performance among similarly sized open-source models. Key architectural innovations include:

  • Gated Multi-head Latent Attention (Gated MLA): Novel attention mechanism reducing inference computation
  • FarSkip-Collective: Optimized distributed training communication efficiency
  • Support for 64K token long-context processing

Importantly, the model was trained from scratch entirely on AMD Instinct MI300X and MI325X GPUs using the ROCm open-source software stack. AMD has released all model weights, training configurations, data mixtures, intermediate checkpoints, and inference code to advance reproducible AI research.

Benchmark results show Instella-MoE-16B-A3B achieving an average score of 76.7 — leading among fully open-source models and surpassing SmolLM3-3B and OLMo-3-7B while activating only 2.8B parameters per token.

Google DeepMind's GenCeption: Turning Video Generation Models into a 'Universal Vision Brain'

Google DeepMind has released GenCeption, a groundbreaking model that repurposes pre-trained video generation models for classic computer vision tasks — depth estimation, image segmentation, and 3D pose estimation — achieving near or above state-of-the-art results on multiple benchmarks.

Built on Alibaba's open-source Wan2.1 video model, GenCeption's simplified architecture enables single-forward-pass prediction of depth maps, surface normals, segmentation masks, and 3D keypoints simultaneously. Key breakthroughs:

  • Remarkable training efficiency: Uses only 7,500 synthetic videos (800 human models × 200 action sequences rendered in Blender) — one-seventh to one-five-hundredth of the data required by specialized models
  • Strong generalization: Despite training only on single-person synthetic data, handles real-world multi-person scenes, animals, and humanoid robots

The model processes 81 frames in 6-10 seconds, with researchers noting room for speed optimization. GenCeption signals that video generation models are evolving from content creation tools toward foundational visual understanding systems.

xAI Confirms Grok 4.6 Pre-Training Complete: Musk Takes Aim at Kimi K3

Elon Musk's xAI is accelerating its model release cadence. As reported on July 25, xAI has confirmed that Grok 4.6 — a 2-trillion-parameter model — has completed initial pre-training, with the even larger Grok 4.7 already on the roadmap weeks behind it.

This release pace is unprecedented in the AI industry:

  • Grok 4.5 (1.5T params): Launched July 8 at $2/M input tokens — 60%+ cheaper than GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8
  • Grok 4.6 (2T params): Pre-training completed July 18; Musk claims it "may exceed Kimi" while running faster and more efficiently
  • Grok 4.7 (estimated 4-6T params): On the roadmap, weeks behind 4.6

xAI's strategy is clear: apply simultaneous pricing and pace pressure on OpenAI and Anthropic while directly targeting Moonshot AI's Kimi K3. Since its July 16 launch, Kimi K3 has triggered a nearly 10% drop in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index in one week and erased approximately $3.3 trillion in semiconductor market value.

Analysts note that while independent benchmarks will determine whether Grok 4.6 truly outperforms Kimi K3, xAI's release cadence alone is pressuring enterprise contract renewals at OpenAI and Anthropic — a cheaper alternative arrives on the market every few weeks.
