⚡ Aug 2 Finance News: US and Japan May Unveil Joint Yen Policy Next Week, Tokyo Steps In Again in New York Hours, Trump's 'Hit Them Very Hard' Iran Warning Triggers $238M Crypto Liquidations, 30-Year Treasury Yield Hits 19-Year High

Yen Defense Enters 'Phase Two': Kyodo Says US and Japan May Unveil a Joint Policy as Early as Next Week, Tokyo Intervenes Again in New York Hours on Friday

The yen defense campaign escalated over the weekend. Kyodo News reported on Saturday (August 1), citing informed sources, that Japan and the United States may unveil a joint policy to address the yen's weakness as early as next week - a warning aimed at speculative bets that have pressured the currency, with the goal of stabilizing markets. On the same day, Nikkei reported that Japanese authorities intervened again in foreign-exchange markets during New York trading hours on Friday - the third round of action this week: central bank data released Friday suggested Japan may have sold as much as $58.97 billion to buy yen on Thursday (Bloomberg estimated around JPY 8.45 trillion, about $52.8 billion), and on Friday Washington joined in person while Tokyo followed up again.

Details of the US Treasury's participation are emerging one by one: according to the Financial Times, citing people familiar with the matter, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York sold euros to buy yen on behalf of the Treasury through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley - the first time since 1998 that Washington has directly bought yen to strengthen it, and the first coordinated US-Japan yen-buying intervention in nearly 30 years (the last direct US support for the yen was in 2011, a G7-coordinated effort). A Reuters photo of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's notepad during the Camp David cabinet meeting showed the words "To Do," followed by "Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil."

Markets responded immediately: the dollar fell to about 157.40 yen at the New York close on Friday, the strongest level for the yen since early May, briefly touching 157.24 intraday - a sharp rebound from near 163.99, its weakest since 1986, earlier this week. Japan's Finance Ministry took the unusual step of posting on X to reassure markets that its monetary authorities have "a broad range of tools to address market liquidity needs," including potential access to the Fed's standing FIMA Repo Facility - a mechanism introduced in 2020 that lets Japan raise dollar liquidity without outright sales of US Treasuries.

Citi FX strategist Osamu Takashima said that with Washington showing willingness to support Japan's currency defense, "it will be difficult for the dollar-yen exchange rate to rise back to 164 yen per dollar in the near future." Some Asian bank traders, however, noted clients are already testing Tokyo's resolve, building yen-selling positions targeting the 162 level. Japan's Finance Ministry will publish official intervention data for the week of July 30 at the end of August, finally revealing the true scale.

Trump's 'Hit Them Very Hard' Iran Warning Shakes Markets: Roughly $238M in Crypto Liquidations in 24 Hours, Bitcoin Loses $63,000

At a Friday cabinet meeting at Camp David, Trump said he is "losing faith" in Tehran and warned the US would be "hitting them very hard," adding that Iran "lies and misinterprets" and that the goal is simply "to win." White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt said "we will win, and Iran will not have a nuclear weapon," while Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell said the Department was "locked and loaded." The Wall Street Journal and CBS reported that US and Israeli officials discussed completing strikes before Monday's market open to limit economic disruption - though no final strike order had been given as of press time.

In the 24/7 crypto market, the hawkish rhetoric triggered an immediate flush. CoinGlass data showed roughly $238 million in leveraged positions liquidated, with longs absorbing the bulk of the damage (about 78%); bitcoin briefly broke below $63,000 to around $62,900 before a partial bounce, down about 2% in 24 hours, with total market cap shrinking to roughly $2.16 trillion. Institutional money is also retreating: US spot bitcoin ETFs recorded net outflows of about $265 million on July 31 (BlackRock's IBIT -$123 million, Fidelity's FBTC -$54.8 million), ending a two-session inflow streak; total ETF assets stand at about $76.29 billion, or 6.04% of bitcoin's market value. Separately, Polymarket traders put the probability of the CLARITY Act becoming law in 2026 at just 27%, with the bipartisan ethics fight stalling ahead of the Senate's August recess.

The risk-off tone is intertwined with the violent moves in Treasuries: the 30-year yield hit a 19-year high, the 10-year climbed above 4.7%, and Iran-driven oil prices keep inflation expectations elevated - interest-rate pressure continues to weigh on rate-sensitive assets like bitcoin, which is simultaneously absorbing a "war risk premium" and "rate hike expectations."

Friday's US Close: Amazon Soars 15.3% to Rescue the Tape, Apple Drops 7.4%, Micron Slides 5.9%, 30-Year Treasury Yield Hits 19-Year High

US stocks closed higher Friday (July 31) after a volatile session, capping a "wild July": the S&P 500 rose 0.7% (+52.09 points) to 7,489.72, the Dow added 0.5% (+276.97) to 52,485.03, and the Nasdaq climbed 1.0% (+251.68) to 25,373.85. Amazon surged 15.32% to $271.58 after a strong report (led by AWS), and Microsoft gained 3.02% to $464.72, reviving AI confidence; Apple fell 7.35% to $308.91 after disappointing results. Memory-chip names whipsawed: Micron went from an early gain of 6.4% to a 5.9% loss, closing at $823.03, with SanDisk down 5.09% and SK Hynix down 3.54% - profit-taking after Thursday's 18% spike collided with rate-hike worries. For the month, the S&P 500 was essentially flat and the Nasdaq fell about 3%, though both remain up roughly 9.4% and 9.2% year to date respectively.

The bond market was the real storm center of the week: the 30-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.27%, touching 5.28% intraday - the highest since July 2007 (a 19-year high); the 10-year yield broke above 4.7% to 4.74%. Drivers include Fed Chair Kevin Warsh refusing to offer forward guidance after the FOMC's 9-3 hold, June PCE still at 3.7% year over year (down from 4.1% in May), and Q2 GDP growing at just a 1.5% annualized rate versus the 2.1% consensus. Warsh's quip that "market participants are learning to play the ball, not the referee" was read by Wall Street as a welcome for higher long-end yields.

Key Events Next Week (August 3-7)

  • Friday (Aug 7): US July nonfarm payrolls - consensus calls for about 91,000 new jobs (vs 57,000 in June), with unemployment edging up to 4.3%; a stronger print would reinforce September hike expectations;
  • Wednesday (Aug 5): US ISM manufacturing PMI and the Treasury's Quarterly Refunding Announcement (QRA) - JPMorgan warns of a roughly $3.7 trillion funding gap over the next four fiscal years, and any wording shift on auction sizes would hit the long end directly;
  • Sunday (Aug 2): OPEC+ JMMC meeting - focus is on whether the group endorses the planned 188k bpd September increase and whether it pauses further hikes;
  • Wednesday (Aug 5): Bank of Japan June meeting minutes (after Governor Ueda hiked 25bp to 1% in June and held in July); China's official PMIs have fallen below the boom-bust line, and Monday/Wednesday RatingDog PMIs plus Friday trade data will test export resilience;
  • Central bank decisions: India's RBI (expected to hold at 5.25%), Brazil's BCB (expected to hold Selic), and Banxico.

The biggest weekend wildcard remains Iran: if US-Israeli strikes land before Monday's open, oil, Treasuries and risk assets will reprice violently again - and if the US-Japan joint policy is unveiled as expected, the yen and carry trades will be the next flashpoint.
