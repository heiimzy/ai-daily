⚡ July 24 Financial Roundup: Global Markets Hit by Perfect Storm — Oil Tops $100, New US Tariffs Take Effect, Asia Stocks Routed, Magnificent 7 Lose $797B

Trump's Section 301 Tariffs on 60 Trading Partners Take Effect

The Trump administration's sweeping new tariffs under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 went into effect at 12:01 AM EDT Friday, imposing 10% to 12.5% duties on imports from 60 trading partners that collectively account for 99.4% of US imports. The new measures replace temporary 10% global tariffs under Section 122 that expired simultaneously, representing the administration's most aggressive effort yet to rebuild Trump's tariff wall after the Supreme Court struck down his reciprocal tariffs in February.

Under the final determination published in the Federal Register, goods from Argentina, Bangladesh, Britain, Canada, India, Mexico, and others that have adopted adequate forced-labor bans face a 10% rate. The European Union and Taiwan face at least 10%; Japan, South Korea, and Switzerland, due to existing trade agreements, face 12.5%. China and 38 other countries receive the maximum 12.5% rate. Exemptions include oil and gas, fertilizers, foodstuffs, and goods already under Section 232 national security tariffs (autos, steel, aluminum, copper). Goods in transit are exempted until July 28.

Trade law experts noted the new framework rests on firmer legal ground than the Section 122 tariffs, but warned it will further escalate global trade friction, raise consumer prices, and compound the inflation shock from surging energy costs.

Brent Crude Surges Past $100 — Houthi Red Sea Attacks Threaten Second Major Oil Chokepoint

Brent crude futures soared 7.04% on Thursday to settle at $100.69 per barrel — the first close above $100 since May 22 — after Iran-backed Houthi rebels struck two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea, opening a dangerous new front in the intensifying Middle East conflict. The international benchmark touched an intraday high of $102, the highest since late May. WTI crude rose 6.17% to $92.19. Brent has surged nearly 40% month-to-date, the biggest monthly gain since March.

The oil shock ignited a fierce sell-off in global bond markets. The US 10-year Treasury yield spiked to 4.7135%, the highest since January 2025 (18-month high). The 30-year yield approached 5.201%, levels not seen since 2007 (19-year high). Germany's 10-year Bund yield surged to 3.2%, the highest since 2011. The yield moves reflect a classic "bond vigilante" protest against the inflation and fiscal risks posed by both the energy crisis and surging US war spending.

Goldman Sachs warned that if disruptions to the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb persist, Brent could rally above $120 per barrel by Q4. RBC Capital Markets painted a more dire scenario, cautioning that oil could challenge the all-time record of $146 set in 2008 if the conflict escalates further.

Asian Markets Routed — KOSPI Triggers Circuit Breaker, Nikkei Drops 1,811 Points, Shanghai Breaks Winning Streak

Friday's Asian trading session witnessed a ferocious sell-off as the triple shocks of surging oil, new US tariffs, and AI spending fears converged. South Korea's KOSPI index plummeted as much as 5.7%, triggering the exchange's "Sidecar" mechanism that halts program selling for five minutes. Samsung Electronics sank 7.6%, SK Hynix plunged 8.3%. The Korea Financial Services Commission announced it would tighten leveraged ETF margin requirements for retail investors, raising the minimum cash deposit from 10 million won to 30 million won, effective July 31.

Japan's Nikkei 225 crashed 1,811.45 points (2.73%) to close at 64,611.15, heading for a third straight weekly decline. Semiconductor and tech shares bore the brunt: Kioxia tumbled 9.5%, SoftBank Group fell 7.4%, Tokyo Electron lost 5.2%, and Advantest dropped 6%.

China's A-share markets ended a four-day winning streak. The Shanghai Composite fell 1.61% to 3,814.20, the Shenzhen Component dropped 2.47% to 13,774.68, and the ChiNext Index slumped 2.65% to 3,480.87. Over 4,900 stocks declined. Total两市 turnover contracted sharply to 1.93 trillion yuan ($265 billion), the lowest in over three months, as investors retreated to the sidelines. Precious metals, power utilities, coal, and pharmaceuticals led the decline, while semiconductor equipment and defense stocks bucked the trend.

Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index fell 1.22%. Australia's S&P/ASX 200, Singapore's Straits Times, and markets across Southeast Asia all traded deep in the red.

Magnificent 7 Lose $797 Billion in a Single Day — Tesla Plunges 14.5%, Alphabet Sinks 7.1%

Wall Street suffered its worst session in a month on Thursday as AI capex anxiety collided with the oil-driven inflation scare. The Magnificent 7 index cratered 4.8%, the biggest one-day drop since the April 2025 Liberation Day tariff turmoil, wiping out $797 billion in market capitalization. The combined index is now down 11% from its May record, erasing over $2 trillion in value.

Tesla bore the brunt of the selling, collapsing 14.52% — its worst day since March 2025. The EV maker reported Q2 capital expenditures of $5.79 billion (up 142% year-over-year) and recorded its first negative free cash flow in two years. CEO Elon Musk called 2026 "a massive capex year" but investors increasingly question the timeline for AI investment payoffs. Alphabet sank 7.13% after raising its 2026 capex forecast to $195-205 billion (from $180-190 billion), turning its cash flow negative for the first time as a public company. While Google Cloud revenue surged 82% to $24.8 billion with margins nearly doubling to 35.6%, the spending acceleration overshadowed the strong operational results.

Other megacaps followed: Amazon dropped 4.6%, Meta fell 3.4%, Microsoft declined 2.2%, and Apple and Nvidia each lost over 1%. The S&P 500 fell 1.21% to 7,408.30, the Nasdaq Composite sank 2.15% to 25,137.69, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 506.93 points (0.97%) to 51,711.65.

Fed Rate Hike Probability Surges — From 12% to 36% in One Week

The combination of surging oil prices and the tightest labor market in decades (initial jobless claims fell to 187,000, the lowest since 1969) has dramatically repriced Federal Reserve expectations. According to the CME FedWatch Tool, markets now assign a 36% probability of a quarter-point rate hike at next week's FOMC meeting (July 28-29), up from below 12% just a week ago. A September hike is over 82% priced in, and December stands at 92%. If delivered, next week's hike would be the Fed's first since July 2023.

The European Central Bank held rates steady at 2.25% on Thursday as expected, but President Christine Lagarde delivered a notably hawkish message, warning that "the inflationary impact of the energy shock has yet to fully materialize." Markets now price a September ECB hike at roughly 70%. Germany's 2-year Schatz yield rose to 2.87%, its highest since 2008.

The dollar index climbed to 101.5, its highest this month. The yen weakened to 163.89 per dollar, near 40-year lows, drawing another verbal intervention warning from Japan's finance minister. The offshore yuan traded at 6.7773. Gold fell below $4,030/oz as higher rate expectations diminished the appeal of non-yielding assets, while silver dropped over 3%.

Intel Shines as Bright Spot — Strongest Revenue Growth in 15 Years

In a rare piece of good news, Intel Corp. delivered its strongest quarterly results in 15 years after Thursday's close. Q2 revenue surged 25% year-over-year, far exceeding analyst expectations, driven by a 59% jump in data center and AI revenue and 31% growth in its foundry business. Q3 revenue guidance of $15.8-16.8 billion comfortably beat the $15.1 billion consensus estimate, sending Intel shares up over 10% in after-hours trading.

CEO Pat Gelsinger said AI is driving "unprecedented" demand for computing power, with supply constraints expected to persist and next year's capex significantly higher than this year's. While Intel's results provided a localized lift to the semiconductor sector, analysts cautioned that the broader market remains hostage to oil prices, tariff developments, and the trajectory of Fed policy.

PBOC Injects 500 Billion Yuan via MLF to Stabilize Liquidity

Amid the escalating external headwinds, the People's Bank of China conducted a 500 billion yuan ($68.7 billion) 1-year Medium-term Lending Facility (MLF) operation on Friday. With 400 billion yuan in MLF maturing this month, the operation represents a net injection of 100 billion yuan in medium-to-long-term liquidity. Analysts said the move aims to replenish bank funding, ease liability cost pressures, and support credit lending — signaling the PBOC's commitment to maintaining stable liquidity conditions despite global market turbulence.

Morgan Stanley warned in its latest note that if oil remains persistently above $100 and trade tensions continue to escalate, global growth forecasts face significant downside risk. The IMF currently projects 3.0% global growth for 2026, the UN 2.7%, and the World Bank 2.5%, but none of these estimates fully incorporate a sustained oil price above $100 scenario.
