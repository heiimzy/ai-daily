⚡ July 30 Financial Super Day: FOMC Countdown, Iran Missile Attack Triggers 7% Oil Surge, KOSPI Loses $2 Trillion in Two Days, Chip Stocks Enter Bear Market

1. FOMC Decision Day: The Most Uncertain Fed Meeting in 30 Years

The Federal Reserve's Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) is set to announce its interest rate decision at 2:00 PM ET on Wednesday, July 29 — arguably the most unpredictable Fed decision in decades. The CME FedWatch tool shows a 30-36% probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike, while all 104 economists surveyed by Reuters expect rates to remain unchanged at 3.50%-3.75%.

"The level of division at this meeting is unprecedented. Market pricing shows roughly 70% hold vs 30% hike — maintaining this wide a spread just 48 hours before decision is extraordinarily rare." — JPMorgan Market Intelligence Desk

Hawks vs Doves

The hawkish camp — led by Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan — argues for immediate action. Logan has publicly stated that "the policy rate should be moderately higher" to better balance the outlook and risks. With inflation exceeding the 2% target for over five years and CPI at 3.5%, the hawks contend that rising Middle East tensions could reignite price pressures.

The dovish camp — represented by Governor Christopher Waller and others — points to June's better-than-expected CPI data (monthly -0.4%, core flat) and argues for patience. Former Fed Governor Stephen Miran told CNBC that the current inflation is "transitory," driven by one-time supply shocks from the Iran conflict.

The wildcard is Chair Kevin Warsh, presiding over only his second FOMC meeting. Citadel Securities stands alone among major Wall Street firms in explicitly predicting a rate hike. Frank Flight, the firm's head of macro strategy, argues: "A surprise hike would emphatically end the forward guidance era, reassert central bank independence, and carry far more informational force than a widely anticipated September move."

Most analysts, however, expect a "hawkish hold" — keeping rates unchanged while signaling a September hike through statement language. Goldman Sachs expects at least one dissenting vote for a hike and predicts the statement will acknowledge geopolitical risks to inflation.

Market Scenario Analysis

JPMorgan's trading desk outlined four scenarios:

  • Hawkish Hold (50% probability) — rate unchanged + hawkish language; S&P 500 moves -0.5% to +0.25%
  • Dovish Hold (20%) — rate unchanged + accommodative tone; S&P 500 +0.5% to +1%
  • Surprise 25bp Hike (25%) — S&P 500 -1.5% to -2%, Nasdaq harder hit
  • Surprise 50bp Hike (5%) — S&P 500 -2% to -4%

2. Iran's Ballistic Missile Strike: Oil Surges 7%

At approximately 5:45 PM ET Tuesday, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched multiple ballistic missiles at the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan, targeting the US Central Command facility. All missiles were intercepted by Patriot air defense systems with no casualties. The US and Saudi Arabia retaliated with airstrikes on Iran-backed militia targets in Iraq.

"We will hit Iran very hard." — President Trump in a Fox News interview

The strike shattered a three-day pause in hostilities — the longest ceasefire since the US-Iran war began in February — and sent oil markets into overdrive:

  • Brent crude surged 6.8% to $89.79/barrel, briefly breaching $90
  • WTI crude jumped 6.7% to $84.60/barrel
  • This reversed a three-day, 16.5% plunge (from $100+ to ~$84) — Brent's worst three-day drop since April 2020

API data showed US crude inventories fell by 3.3 million barrels, well above the 1.3 million draw expected. Iran formally rejected Oman's 50-50 joint management proposal for the Strait of Hormuz, demanding "full control of 50% plus partial control of the opposite lane" or keeping the strait closed. Few vessels are currently transiting the Strait of Hormuz with transponders active. Oil volatility (OVX) hit 57.15 — more than triple the VIX at 18.20.

3. KOSPI Loses $2.18 Trillion in Two Days; Chip Stocks Enter Bear Market

Asian financial markets suffered a historic rout. South Korea's KOSPI index plunged as much as 12.6% intraday on Wednesday before closing down ~6%, extending Tuesday's nearly 11% collapse. The two-day wipeout erased approximately $2.18 trillion in market value, with the index now down nearly 40% from its June peak.

The trigger: SK Hynix's Q2 earnings. Despite record revenue of 79.31 trillion won (+257% YoY) and operating profit of 60.54 trillion won (+557% YoY), the results marginally missed elevated expectations (64.1 trillion won consensus). SK Hynix shares plunged nearly 20% before closing down 9.6%. Samsung Electronics fell as much as 14% before paring losses to 5.2%.

"Today's price action suggests leverage within Korean equities remains high and further unwinding can be expected." — Wee Khoon Chong, Asia-Pacific Macro Strategist at BNY

Finance Minister Koo Yun-cheol publicly apologized to parliament for introducing single-stock leveraged ETFs, saying they "had not been considered carefully enough." The government announced curbs including individual investment limits (capped at 20% of total investment), higher trading costs, and simulated trading requirements.

The carnage spread across Asia:

  • Japan's Nikkei fell 1.6%; Kioxia plunged 13.9%
  • Taiwan's TAIEX dropped 3.5%; TSMC -3.5%, UMC -9.7%
  • Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) fell 26% from June peak, officially entering bear market territory
  • SOX down ~9% this week; AMD -4.4%, Micron -3.7%, KLA -6%+

4. US Stocks Plunge: Dow Loses 852 Points

As of midday Eastern Time, US markets were deep in the red:

  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: -851.54 pts (-1.61%) to 51,895.78 — two stocks (Caterpillar -6.5%, Goldman Sachs -4.6%) drove 72% of the decline
  • S&P 500: -69.12 pts (-0.93%) to 7,359.66, hitting a one-month low
  • Nasdaq Composite: -283 pts (-1.14%) to 24,593.89, now 9.5% below June record high
  • VIX: surged 8.84% to 19.82

Energy was the lone bright spot: XLE +2.3%, Chevron +2.8% on the oil spike. Industrials led declines at -2.5%, with Caterpillar and Vertiv (AI infrastructure, -13.2% on revenue miss) hit hard. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.635%, the 2-year to 4.318%.

5. Microsoft and Meta Earnings: The $4.45 Trillion Test

After the closing bell, two mega-cap tech companies report Q2 results:

  • Microsoft (MSFT): consensus $87.6B revenue, $4.24 EPS; options imply a 6.6% swing
  • Meta Platforms (META): consensus $60.2B revenue, $7.19 EPS; options imply a 7.8% swing

Their combined market cap stands at ~$4.45 trillion. The central question: are the hundreds of billions poured into AI infrastructure translating into meaningful revenue growth? Vertiv's disappointing results have already rattled confidence in the AI investment narrative. Apple and Amazon follow in subsequent days.

"Demand for compute isn't going away — that's why tonight's earnings matter so much. MSFT, META, AMZN, GOOG, and ORCL keep spending hundreds of billions because underinvesting is an existential risk." — Market analyst

6. Europe and Emerging Markets: Growing Divergence

European markets showed relative resilience: Stoxx 600 +0.4%, FTSE 100 +0.8%, supported by strong earnings from Unilever (+8% on sales beat), Mercedes-Benz (+2.9% operating profit +22%). The Eurozone Composite PMI rose to 51.9 in July — the first expansion in four months, above the 50.3 forecast.

In FX, the dollar edged lower (DXY 101.27), the yen firmed to 163.28 on safe-haven demand, and the Australian dollar weakened on softer CPI data. Bitcoin held near $64,300 as crypto markets awaited the Fed decision.

7. What's Next: Three Key Events Remain in Super Week

The FOMC decision is just the opening act of "Super Week":

  • July 30 (Thursday): US Q2 GDP advance estimate (consensus 1.8% annualized); June PCE inflation (headline 3.7%, core 3.3% expected); Bank of England rate decision
  • July 31 (Friday): Bank of Japan rate decision; Apple and Amazon earnings
  • CME FedWatch currently prices an ~80% probability of at least one hike by September

Two-sided risk prevails — a surprise hike or hawkish Warsh could trigger deeper selloffs, while a dovish hold combined with strong mega-cap earnings could spark a relief rally. The only certainty: market volatility and uncertainty are at multi-year extremes.
