"Black Tuesday": The Worst Day for Global Semiconductor Stocks in 2026

July 28, 2026, will go down as "Black Tuesday" for global semiconductor markets. Three shockwaves hit simultaneously — China's mass production of domestic immersion DUV lithography machines, the record-breaking CXMT IPO, and escalating doubts about AI infrastructure financing sustainability — triggering an epic selloff that swept across global chip stocks.

South Korea's KOSPI index crashed 10.84% to close at 6,023.66, triggering its eighth circuit breaker of the year. Samsung Electronics plunged 13.39% to 222,000 won, while SK Hynix tumbled 14.65% to 1.55 million won. Together, the two chip giants account for roughly half of the KOSPI's total market capitalization — their double-digit collapse dragged the entire index into the abyss. The KOSPI briefly fell below the psychologically critical 6,000 level intraday for the first time in three and a half months.

Japan's Kioxia Holdings collapsed 18.3% in a single session, the worst single-stock decline across all three major Asian markets. Taiwan's MediaTek fell nearly 10%, and the Nikkei 225 dropped approximately 4%. In U.S. markets, Nvidia (NVDA) fell 4.99% to $196.51, breaking below the psychologically important $200 level for the first time since November 2025 — erasing approximately $111 billion in single-day market capitalization. AMD plunged 8.15% to $454.62, Micron fell over 7%, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOXX) officially entered bear market territory, now down over 20% from its recent peak.

Foreign investors on the KOSPI recorded net selling of 4.49 trillion won ($3.06 billion) — a record single-day outflow for the year. Retail investors attempted to buy the dip with net purchases of 4.32 trillion won but failed to stem the rout. The tech-heavy KOSDAQ also crashed 7.72%, triggering its own circuit breaker.

"The selloff can be attributed to three factors," said Kim Seok-hwan, analyst at Mirae Asset Securities. "Concerns over China's semiconductor technology catch-up and supply expansion, doubts about AI investment profitability, and forced deleveraging of concentrated positions in Korean semiconductor stocks and leveraged products."

AMD Signs $14B, 15-Year Data Center Deal with Core Scientific — Locking in 2.5 GW of AI Capacity

Amid the market bloodbath, AMD and Core Scientific announced a landmark infrastructure partnership on the same day. The 15-year agreement, with base contracted revenue exceeding $14 billion, is one of the largest infrastructure commitments in AMD's history — signaling that the AI arms race has evolved beyond mere chip competition into a battle for power, land, and purpose-built infrastructure.

Under the initial tranche, Core Scientific will deliver approximately 530 megawatts across five U.S. campuses: Pecos and Hunt County, Texas; Muskogee, Oklahoma; Auburn, Alabama; and Dalton, Georgia. AMD holds direct triple-net leases covering roughly 380 MW, while an additional ~150 MW will support an unnamed Neocloud customer with AMD providing full credit support throughout the 15-year term.

AMD also secured reservation rights for up to an additional 1.9 GW of capacity through late 2028, bringing the total potential footprint to 2.5 GW. The first AMD megawatts at Core Scientific's Pecos facility are expected online in the first half of 2027, with approximately half of the 530 MW delivered by end of 2027.

The capacity will deploy AMD's recently launched Instinct MI455X GPU and Helios rack-scale platform. A single Helios rack integrates 72 MI455X GPUs built on CDNA 5 architecture alongside 18 sixth-generation EPYC "Venice" CPUs, delivering up to 2.9 exaFLOPS of FP4 compute. AMD claims Helios delivers 15% more FP4 compute and 50% more HBM memory than Nvidia's competing Vera Rubin NVL72 system.

AMD's ecosystem is accelerating. The company has previously secured deals with Anthropic (up to $5B investment and 2 GW GPU deployment), Microsoft Azure (Helios deployment confirmed July 20), and now Core Scientific. These accumulating commitments represent a credible alternative supply chain to Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem.

Kimi K3 Goes Fully Open Source: 2.8 Trillion Parameter Model Sparks Global Turmoil

On the evening of July 27, Beijing-based Moonshot AI released the complete weights and technical report for Kimi K3 — a 2.8 trillion parameter Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model — on Hugging Face, along with three key infrastructure technologies: MoonEP, FlashKDA, and AgentEnv. The model topped Hugging Face's trending chart within 30 minutes, setting a record as the fastest-rising release in the platform's history.

Kimi K3 has 2.8 trillion total parameters with 104 billion activated, supports a 100K token context window, and is the world's first open-source model to cross the 3-trillion-parameter threshold. It ranks third globally on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (57.1 points), behind only Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol. On Frontend Code Arena, it scored 1,679 Elo to claim the top spot — the first open-source model ever to surpass all closed-source models on this benchmark. Tesla CEO Elon Musk commented "Impressive" on the评测.

Following the release, at least 18 U.S. companies immediately deployed Kimi K3 commercially, including Nebius, Baseten, and Fireworks. Cognition, the developer of AI coding agent Devin, announced Kimi K3 integration, stating it is "the first open-source model we've tested that approaches frontier performance on FrontierCode 1.1." Huawei's Ascend CANN and Alibaba Cloud's Zhenwu M890 supernode both completed Day-0 adaptation.

The market impact was immediate. Wall Street analysts estimate that the combined valuation expectations of OpenAI and Anthropic evaporated by approximately $314 billion following the Kimi K3 release. Bloomberg called K3 "a force roiling global tech markets." Goldman Sachs noted that the intelligence level of Chinese open-source AI models has reached a critical node for global mass adoption.

On the same day, Nvidia announced a roughly $5 billion strategic investment in Safe Superintelligence (SSI), the AI lab founded by former OpenAI chief scientist Ilya Sutskever. SSI will receive priority access to Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform, increasing its available compute by tenfold within 12 months. The lab had previously relied primarily on Google's TPU chips. The investment is widely seen as Nvidia's defensive bet on next-generation AI safety research amid the disruption caused by open-source models like Kimi K3.

Three Mega-Events, One Signal: The AI Industry's Power Map Is Being Redrawn

Black Tuesday's meltdown, AMD's multi-billion-dollar infrastructure bet, and Kimi K3's open-source earthquake — three events unfolding on the same day — collectively signal that the AI industry has entered a new phase. Computing sovereignty is shifting from unipolar to multipolar. Chip competition has escalated from a pure performance contest to a full-stack ecosystem war. And Chinese open-source AI is transitioning from "follower" to "agenda-setter."

The collapse of South Korea's twin chip giants reflects a market that is pricing in the once-unthinkable risk of a Chinese semiconductor breakthrough. CXMT's blockbuster IPO and China's DUV lithography production represent the first significant failure of U.S. export control strategy. The AMD-Core Scientific deal proves that AI infrastructure competition now extends from chips to power, land, and financing capability. And Kimi K3's open-source release marks the moment when "open-source AI catching up to closed-source AI" stopped being a slogan — a 2.8 trillion parameter open model is forcing the entire industry to rethink business models and value distribution.
