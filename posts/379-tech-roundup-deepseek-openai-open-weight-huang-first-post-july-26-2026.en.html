⚡ July 26 Tech Roundup: DeepSeek Halts $14B Series B, OpenAI Announces First Open-Weight Model Since GPT-2, Jensen Huang's First X Post Backs Open AI

1. DeepSeek Abruptly Halts Series B: CEO Liang Wenfeng Angered by Leaked Meeting Minutes

Chinese AI startup DeepSeek has hit a major roadblock in its second funding round. According to sources familiar with the matter, DeepSeek has verbally notified potential investors that it is putting the round on hold, and investment agreements scheduled to be signed in the near future will not proceed as planned.

The Series B round was expected to raise at least 10 billion RMB (approximately $1.4 billion) at a pre-money valuation of no less than 480 billion RMB (approximately $67 billion) — a significant jump from the first round's roughly $50 billion valuation. In June, DeepSeek completed the most impressive Series A in Chinese tech startup history, raising $7 billion from investors including Tencent and CATL.

CEO Takes Control After Leaked Document Goes Viral

The abrupt halt stems from founder Liang Wenfeng's personal dissatisfaction. Sources report that a document purporting to be a meeting transcript between Liang and investors rapidly spread online, triggering controversy and leading the founder to reassess the fundraising timeline. Insiders stress this is not necessarily the end of the funding round, and DeepSeek may resume the process at a later date.

Meanwhile, DeepSeek has quietly begun IPO preparations, with a potential listing filing as early as this year. Liang Wenfeng's personal wealth has reportedly doubled from approximately $16.7 billion to $36 billion, surpassing Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman to become the world's wealthiest AI model founder.

2. OpenAI Announces First Open-Weight Model Since GPT-2, Signaling Major Strategic Shift

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman today announced that the company will release a "powerful new open-weight language model" with reasoning capabilities in the coming months — its first open-weight release since GPT-2 in 2019, marking a dramatic strategic pivot.

"We plan to release the first open-weight language model since GPT-2," Altman stated. "We had been thinking about it for a long time, but other priorities took over. Now it feels important to do this now." Before release, OpenAI will evaluate the model against its prepared safety framework and host developer events in San Francisco, Europe, and Asia Pacific to gather feedback.

Open Weight ≠ Fully Open Source

It is important to note that open-weight does not equal fully open source. The pre-trained parameters (the "weights" that define how the model responds) will be publicly shared, allowing developers and researchers to download, run locally, fine-tune for specific use cases, or integrate into custom applications. However, training code, datasets, and detailed logs may remain proprietary.

This move brings OpenAI into the open-weight camp alongside Meta (Llama series), Mistral, DeepSeek, and Alibaba. Until now, OpenAI operated exclusively through its closed API model, with ChatGPT and the GPT-5.6 series' model weights never publicly released.

3. Jensen Huang's First X Post: Taking on Hassabis, Championing Open-Weight AI

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang posted his first ever message on X today. Not a product launch, not an earnings report — he shared Nvidia's signed open letter titled "Open Weights and American AI Leadership." Within hours, the post garnered 17.42 million views.

The open letter directly responds to Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis's earlier call for a global AI safety standards body that would require all frontier models — including DeepSeek and Qwen — to pass unified safety evaluations before deployment. Nvidia's letter takes the opposing view: don't impose premature restrictions; open-weight itself is the path to safety.

The Two Camps: 25 Companies Take Sides

Signatories of the open letter include Nvidia, Meta, Microsoft, a16z, Hugging Face, Mistral, Y Combinator, IBM, Dell, Mozilla, Palantir, Perplexity, and 25 others. Notably absent: OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google — the three largest closed-source frontier model providers.

The letter makes three core arguments:

  • Closed source is not inherently safer — proprietary models can also be breached, abused, and fail in ways outsiders cannot detect; concentrating advanced AI in a few closed providers creates systemic single points of failure
  • Distillation is a legitimate technique — using one model's outputs to train another should not be conflated with theft, widely interpreted as a defense of DeepSeek
  • Open weights enable more eyes on security — transparency is safer than black boxes, and open ecosystems drive meaningful AI safety progress

This is fundamentally a battle of business interests, not technical philosophy. Nvidia sells GPUs — more open models means more compute demand. Meta's open-source Llama strategy counters OpenAI's first-mover advantage. Meanwhile, OpenAI and Anthropic derive nearly all revenue from API and subscription access; open-weight releases would dismantle their core competitive moat.

4. Three Events, One Watershed Moment for AI's Open-vs-Closed Divide

These three developments converging on the same day is no coincidence. Together, they signal a structural shift in the AI industry:

  • DeepSeek's funding pause — reveals underlying tensions in China's AI gold rush. A record $7B Series A followed by a $67B valuation for Series B raises questions about AI bubble dynamics, but Liang's decisive action demonstrates founder control over fundraising pace.
  • OpenAI's open-weight pivot — perhaps Sam Altman's most consequential strategic decision yet. In the tug-of-war between closed and open camps, OpenAI choosing to "embrace open" rewrites the competitive rules.
  • Huang's public stance — as the global AI compute霸主, Nvidia's position carries enormous weight. Huang choosing his first ever social media post for this signal shows the battle for AI rule-setting has entered a critical phase.

The AI industry is transitioning from a "capability race" to a "rule-setting battle." Who defines what AI can be deployed, whose standards become global benchmarks, whether open or closed is safer — the answers to these questions will determine the power structure of AI for the next decade.
