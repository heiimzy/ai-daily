⚡ Aug 3 Finance Update: A-Shares Mixed As 3,700+ Stocks Gain, HK Holds With Alibaba Up 6%, Morgan Stanley Turns Overweight on Korea

A-share midday: indices weak, small caps rally — 3,700+ stocks in the green on lighter volume

China's A-share indices opened lower and wobbled through Monday morning, with the STAR 50 leading declines. The divergence between the yellow and white lines was stark: small and mid-cap names outperformed the heavyweights. At the midday close, the Shanghai Composite fell 0.59%, the Shenzhen Component fell 0.45% and the ChiNext fell 0.44% — yet more than 3,700 stocks across the market were trading higher, an emphatically structural session.

Turnover cooled: the combined half-day volume on the Shanghai and Shenzhen exchanges reached RMB 1.37 trillion, down RMB 420.3 billion from the previous session. All-market ETF turnover came in at RMB 300.84 billion for the morning, shrinking RMB 89.32 billion day-on-day, with only about 500 ETFs in the green.

Sector rotation was intense: AI applications stayed hot as Chuanyi Education extended its winning streak to six consecutive limit-ups, while Tianyu Shuke and Hengyin Technology logged two-day streaks and Jihong Co hit the daily limit; the nuclear power complex exploded higher with Jiusheng Electric, Libate, Hejian Intelligent, Rongfa Nuclear Power and China Nuclear Engineering all sealing limit-ups — the sector saw net main-capital inflows of RMB 2.159 billion and 16 limit-up stocks; humanoid-robotics names shone with Zhongda Lide up two days in a row; and the power sector kept climbing with Xinneng Co up two days straight while Shenzhen Nandian A and Huaneng Liaoning hit limit-up. On the downside, memory-chip names slid, with Pucan, GigaDevice, Demingli and Xiangxin Core among the decliners.

Hong Kong midday: three indexes hold higher — Alibaba +6%, MINIMAX +8%, memory names slump

Hong Kong's three major indexes closed the morning in positive territory. The Hang Seng Index edged up 0.04% to 25,657.66, the Hang Seng Tech Index rose 0.74% and the H-shares Index gained 0.13%. Tech names were broadly firmer: Alibaba jumped over 6% and Baidu rose over 3%; AI application plays led the advance with MINIMAX up over 8%; and solar names were active with Flat Glass Group surging more than 10%.

On the other side of the ledger, memory-related names tumbled — the leveraged Southern 2x Hynix product dropped over 14% — echoing the pullback in A-share memory chips as profit-taking and leveraged unwinding continued to ripple out from Friday's record surge in Korea's memory duopoly.

Korea slides ~5% again — and Morgan Stanley upgrades the market to Overweight

After Friday's record 18% single-day surge, Korean stocks reversed hard again on Monday. The KOSPI opened down 3.6% and at one point fell more than 5%, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both dropping about 9% intraday. The KOSDAQ, however, bucked the trend and rose, triggering a buy-side sidecar (program-trading halt) in the morning.

Right into the turmoil, Morgan Stanley upgraded Korean equities from Equal-weight to Overweight, setting a 9,000-point KOSPI target — roughly 36% upside from current levels. The bank notes the KOSPI's forward P/E has fallen to 5.7x, an all-time low; hedge-fund deleveraging is about 75% complete; and leveraged ETF assets have shrunk about 70% from their peak, leaving the positioning structure materially cleaner.

Morgan Stanley sees capital-management moves, HBM4 pricing and the iPhone 18 launch cycle as the three key catalysts for Korea's next leg higher, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix underpinning the valuation recovery and industrials, defense and financials offering tailwinds.

Asia-Pacific stocks retreat as risk aversion cools: US-Iran talks drag oil lower, Treasuries firm

Singapore's The Business Times reported the MSCI broad gauge of Asian shares fell 0.9% intraday as optimism over fresh US-Iran talks cooled risk aversion. Japan's Nikkei 225 slid over 2% and the Topix dropped 2.2%; Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both tumbled over 8%; Australia's S&P/ASX 200 fell 0.2%; Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 0.6% against the trend, while the Shanghai Composite slipped 0.4%.

The pricing logic for risk assets shifted accordingly: oil extended its decline while Treasuries firmed, as markets bet on improving prospects for reopening the Strait of Hormuz and the geopolitical risk premium baked in earlier unwound quickly.

Macro risk is now stacking up: the US ISM manufacturing PMI for July lands at 10:00 a.m. ET tonight (consensus around 54.0 vs 53.3 prior), and Friday brings the July non-farm payrolls report — volatility in risk assets looks set to widen.
