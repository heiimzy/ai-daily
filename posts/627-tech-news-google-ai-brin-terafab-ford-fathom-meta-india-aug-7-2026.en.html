⚡ Aug 7 Tech Briefing: AI Power Shifts Back to Brin as Hassabis Steps Aside, SpaceX Confirms $16.8B Terafab, Ford's Fathom EV Truck Starts at $28,350, Meta Faces 72-Hour Crisis in India

Four big stories have landed in the tech world over the past couple of hours: Google is shifting AI power back to co-founder Sergey Brin as DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis steps aside to become chairman and Alphabet chief scientist; SpaceX and Tesla have officially confirmed the $16.8 billion Terafab chip factory in Texas; Ford has named its new electric pickup Fathom, starting at $28,350; and Meta faces a "72-hour crisis" in India over content moderation that could cost it safe-harbour legal protections. Here is this round's tech briefing.

1. Google's AI Shake-Up: Power Returns to Brin, Hassabis Becomes Chief Scientist

Google is pulling AI authority back toward co-founder Sergey Brin, according to the Financial Times, as Demis Hassabis hands over day-to-day leadership of DeepMind. Hassabis will chair DeepMind and take on a newly created role as chief scientist of Alphabet, focusing on the "big picture" of AGI; DeepMind's chief technology officer Koray Kavukcuoglu will succeed him as CEO.

The reshuffle ripples widely: several core Gemini leaders, including veteran engineer Jeff Dean and Sanjay Ghemawat, are reportedly leaving Google to launch Discovery Loop, a startup focused on machine-learning breakthroughs. Markets reacted sharply — Alphabet shares closed down 4% on Wednesday. Analysts read the move as Google's forceful response to rivals Anthropic and OpenAI, both of which poached a top Google AI researcher this summer and have surged ahead in areas like coding agents.

2. SpaceX and Tesla Confirm Terafab: $16.8B Chip Factory in Texas

SpaceX has made its Terafab chip factory official, confirming a joint investment with Tesla of $16.8 billion for the initial phase in Grimes County, Texas. SpaceX's website describes the facility as "an advanced semiconductor fab that will bridge the divide between current global chip supply and the compute demand of the future," with more than 100 million square feet of manufacturing space planned and at least 3,000 jobs for Grimes and neighboring Brazos County.

Elon Musk called it "the largest and most valuable building on Earth by far." SpaceX filings have hinted total spending could reach $119 billion across a "multi-phase" build-out, and Intel has confirmed it will contribute, though without detailing its stake. The fab will produce chips optimized for edge computing and inference — powering hardware such as Tesla's Optimus robots and self-driving Cybercabs. Notably, a packed county meeting on Wednesday saw hundreds of residents raise concerns over millions in tax breaks and a lack of transparency; SpaceX also pledged to use water from the local Gibbons Creek Reservoir rather than groundwater.

3. Ford Names Its New EV Pickup 'Fathom' — From $28,350

Ford has finally named its sub-$30,000 electric pickup: Fathom. The starting MSRP is $28,350; with the $1,595 destination and delivery fee it lands at $29,945 — just under the $30,000 mark Ford has targeted since it began talking openly about the project in 2025. Fathom is Ford's second all-electric truck after the F-150 Lightning and the first vehicle built on its new "Universal EV Platform" (UEV), which pairs a brand-new assembly-line process with aggressive cost-cutting aimed at Tesla.

The truck seats five with more interior space than a Toyota RAV4, comes with Apple Maps built in, supports Android Auto and Apple CarPlay (though not Apple's next-generation CarPlay Ultra), enables bidirectional power like the Lightning, and is BlueCruise-capable — hands-free highway driving that will likely require a paid subscription. The entry price includes the "standard range" lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery pack; range figures are still under wraps. Ford says the truck will be fully revealed in early 2027 with deliveries starting in fall 2027. The project began as a skunkworks effort led by former Tesla executive Alan Clarke, drawing talent from Rivian, Tesla, and Apple's shuttered car program.

4. Meta's '72-Hour Crisis' in India: Safe-Harbour Protections at Risk

Meta is staring down a "72-hour crisis" in India that could upend its business there. CNBC reports the Indian government has issued a deadline over content-moderation failures; if Meta does not comply, it risks losing safe-harbour protections under Section 79 of India's IT Act — the legal shield that spares platforms from liability for user-generated content. The flashpoint was Meta's restriction of Prime Minister Narendra Modi's Facebook video, alongside government anger over child sexual abuse material (CSAM), deepfakes, and bot accounts. Mark Zuckerberg has apologized to the Indian government over the lapses.

Pressure is mounting: the Ministry of Electronics and IT is reportedly demanding greater transparency into Instagram's algorithm, and scrutiny has widened to include X (Twitter) and Telegram. Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw will meet Meta again, with the full compliance review expected to last 3-4 days. Indian media quote government sources saying "platforms can't run in India on US law" — the bluntest signal yet from New Delhi to Silicon Valley. Losing safe-harbour status would sharply raise Meta's operating costs and legal exposure in the world's largest user market.
