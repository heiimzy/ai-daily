⚡ July 29 Middle East Crisis: US-Saudi Joint Strikes Kill 20 in Iraq, IRGC Intercepts 3 Oil Tankers, Oil Surges 4%

July 29, 2026 — After a brief three-day lull, the Middle East conflict has erupted once again with unprecedented intensity. The United States and Saudi Arabia launched their first-ever joint airstrikes on Iran-aligned forces inside Iraq, killing at least 20 and wounding 32. Simultaneously, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) announced it had intercepted and halted three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude surged more than 4%, sending shockwaves through global energy markets.

1. US-Saudi Joint Strikes: PMF Bases Hit Across 7 Provinces, 20 Killed 32 Wounded

U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) announced late Tuesday that American and Saudi Arabian fighter aircraft had conducted joint precision strikes against Iran-aligned group targets in eastern Iraq. CENTCOM described the operation as "a strong response to over 30 IRGC-directed aerial drone attacks in the last 72 hours" targeting U.S. forces and Saudi energy infrastructure.

Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), an umbrella group of primarily Shiite Iran-backed militias that is officially integrated into the Iraqi military, said in a statement carried by the Iraqi News Agency (INA) that the strikes hit its headquarters and facilities in seven provinces: Baghdad, Wasit, Nineveh, Basra, Kirkuk, Karbala and Diyala. The PMF reported that 20 of its members were killed and 32 wounded, with extensive damage to military vehicles and equipment. Specialized committees are continuing field assessments to determine the full extent of damage.

Saudi Defense Ministry spokesperson Maj. Gen. Turki al-Maliki confirmed the kingdom's participation, invoking Article 51 of the UN Charter on self-defense. Riyadh stated that it "does not seek escalation but will respond to any aggression it faces," and warned of further military action if Iran-backed groups launch new attacks.

The joint operation marks Saudi Arabia's first direct military intervention inside Iraq in coordination with U.S. forces, signifying a major escalation in the kingdom's strategy against Iranian proxy forces.

2. IRGC Strikes 3 Oil Tankers in Strait of Hormuz

The IRGC Navy announced early Wednesday that it had "targeted and stopped" three oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. In a statement, the IRGC said the vessels ignored multiple warnings and continued sailing along what it described as an "unsafe and illegal route," prompting it to strike and halt them. The statement did not disclose the tankers' identities, flag states, ownership, or cargo, nor whether there were any casualties or damage.

The IRGC also warned that "illegal interventions and orders of the US, a child-killing army, will not go unanswered by vessels in the region" and reiterated that it continues to maintain "full control" over the strategic waterway.

The tanker intercepts came just hours after Tehran formally rejected Oman's proposal for a 50-50 joint management arrangement for the strait, signaling a dual-track approach of diplomatic intransigence and military assertiveness.

The Strait of Hormuz, through which approximately 20% of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas transited before the war, has been effectively closed by Iran since the U.S. and Israel launched their campaign on February 28.

3. Iraq PM Calls Emergency Security Meeting: Sovereignty at Stake

Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi convened an emergency National Security Council meeting on Wednesday following the overnight airstrikes. The session comes as Zaidi faces his most severe sovereignty test yet — balancing security cooperation with the United States against the political reality that the PMF is legally part of Iraq's official security forces.

The PMF condemned the strikes as a "highly dangerous escalation" and a violation of Iraq's sovereignty, saying they targeted "official security institutions." The group warned of retaliatory measures.

Zaidi had already ordered Iraqi security agencies to investigate drone attacks on Saudi oil facilities. Iraq's military reiterated its commitment to "preventing Iraqi territory from being used as a route or launching point for any attack targeting brotherly or friendly countries." The prime minister was expected to travel to Saudi Arabia on Thursday for face-to-face consultations.

Analysts note that Zaidi's government is caught between conflicting pressures: restraining Iran-backed militias risks domestic political crisis, while tolerating PMF attacks on Saudi Arabia invites devastating military retaliation.

4. Global Response and Oil Market Turmoil: Brent Surges 4.27%

Oil prices, which had fallen sharply during the brief ceasefire, rebounded dramatically. Brent crude traded 4.27% higher at $87.68 per barrel as of 7:52 AM UAE time, while West Texas Intermediate rose 4.18% to $82.57. The surge reversed part of a 16% three-day rout — the steepest such decline since 2020.

RBC Capital Markets analysts led by Helima Croft wrote in a note to Bloomberg: "We remain exceedingly skeptical that we are on the brink of a major diplomatic breakthrough. The ongoing threat of missiles, mines, drones and Tehran tolls will keep a significant portion of the shipping market on the sidelines."

Earlier Tuesday, Trump said the U.S. and Iran have had "very good talks" and expressed optimism about a potential agreement, but warned he would "finish the job" if diplomacy fails. U.S. Ambassador to the UN Waltz said the pause in strikes was "giving diplomacy some space."

The European Union condemned Houthi threats against Red Sea shipping as "illegal and unacceptable," while the United Nations urged all parties to exercise restraint.

5. Conflict Spreads Across Multiple Fronts: Houthi Red Sea Blockade Intensifies

Beyond the Strait of Hormuz, the conflict continues to expand across additional fronts. Yemen's Houthi militia claimed Wednesday it had fired ballistic missiles at the Saudi-flagged oil tanker NCC Ghazal in the Red Sea, forcing it to turn back. The Houthis announced a maritime embargo on Saudi shipping last week.

According to data from Kpler, Saudi crude supply to Asian buyers via the Suez Canal has more than doubled in the week since the Houthi embargo took effect, rising from approximately 530,000 barrels per day to 1.06 million barrels per day.

The dual threat to both the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb strait places unprecedented pressure on global energy supply chains. Two of the world's most critical maritime chokepoints are now effectively contested or closed simultaneously.

Two senior U.S. officials told Reuters they believe the IRGC is actively seeking to sabotage ceasefire negotiations and the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. The White House has not yet issued a formal statement on Iran's latest ballistic missile attack.
