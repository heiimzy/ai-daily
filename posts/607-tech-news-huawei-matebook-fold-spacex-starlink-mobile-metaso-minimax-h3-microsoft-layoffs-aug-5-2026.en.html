⚡ Aug 5 Tech Update: Huawei Launches MateBook Fold Ultra Master from ¥24,999, SpaceX to Build Terrestrial Network Challenging US Carriers, Metaso Adds MiniMax H3, Microsoft Cuts 605 Redmond Jobs

Over the past hour or two, the global tech industry has delivered another burst of activity: Huawei unveiled its new HarmonyOS foldable PC, the MateBook Fold Ultra Master, priced from ¥24,999; SpaceX used its first post-IPO earnings call to unveil an ambitious plan to build a terrestrial network and directly challenge America's three big wireless carriers, aiming to turn Starlink Mobile into a full mobile operator; Metaso announced it is integrating MiniMax's H3 general video model, pushing per-second video generation pricing down to ¥0.19; Microsoft is reportedly cutting 605 jobs at its Redmond campus; and Chinese makers now hold all five top spots in the global robot vacuum market with a combined share above 70%. Here is this round's tech roundup.

1. Huawei Unveils HarmonyOS Foldable PC MateBook Fold Ultra Master: From ¥24,999, On Sale Aug 14

According to NetEase Tech and other outlets (updated 16:09 on Aug 5, 2026), Huawei released its new HarmonyOS foldable PC, the MateBook Fold Ultra Master, on Aug 5. The device marks a major refresh of Huawei's foldable-PC line, debuting with stylus support and offering three color options, priced from ¥24,999 with sales starting Aug 14. Combining a large unfolded display with portable form factor and HarmonyOS multi-device collaboration, foldable PCs are seen as a key direction for premium mobile productivity; Huawei is accelerating dual-track product development across foldable phones and foldable PCs, further strengthening its premium PC portfolio.

2. SpaceX Plans Terrestrial Network: A Direct Challenge to AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile

According to CLS (Cailianshe) (reported by editor Zhou Ziyi, published 16:02 on Aug 5, 2026), on SpaceX's first earnings call since its IPO, the company detailed plans to build terrestrial infrastructure that complements its satellite internet service and directly competes with America's largest mobile carriers. SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell said the company wants to turn Starlink Mobile into a full-fledged mobile operator and, as the service upgrades, expects to win customers away from AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile.

Today, Starlink Mobile mainly serves as a satellite fallback for phone carriers — in the US, SpaceX partners with T-Mobile on satellite-to-cellular connectivity via the T-Satellite service, which cannot fully replace standard 4G/5G plans. To change that, SpaceX plans to deploy lower-cost, smaller cellular base stations using Starlink antennas. Shotwell said: "We will definitely build terrestrial network infrastructure, so that in addition to the communications capacity provided by satellites, we will deploy the necessary hardware and systems to deliver a true mobile communications service." Musk added that the resulting network will offer better connectivity and higher bandwidth than current cellular operators, without needing the expensive, hard-to-site macro towers. On timing, SpaceX plans to launch next-generation Starlink Mobile satellites in 2027 and begin offering upgraded service by the end of that year — performance it says will be "100 times better" than today's Starlink Mobile. Notably, in May the US FCC approved the transfer of EchoStar spectrum to SpaceX, clearing a key spectrum hurdle for the plan.

3. Metaso Adds MiniMax H3 General Video Model: Video Generation Goes "Per-Second"

According to NetEase Tech (published 15:59 on Aug 5, 2026), Metaso announced on Aug 5 that it is integrating MiniMax's H3 general video model into Metaso AI. Pricing emphasizes pay-as-you-go with no annual subscription lock-in: ¥0.19 per second at 768P and ¥0.29 per second at 2K (the 2K tier uses a diffusion super-resolution model); API pricing matches the web product with official-compatible calls, and ComfyUI workflows are supported. The video-generation LLM race is shifting from per-clip pricing to fine-grained per-second billing. The combination of MiniMax H3 with Metaso's platform — plus the ComfyUI ecosystem — targets creators and developers who want low-cost, programmable video generation, signaling that price competition among Chinese video models is intensifying further.

4. Microsoft to Cut 605 Jobs at Redmond Campus, Effective Sept 4

According to NetEase Tech and other reports (published 15:29 on Aug 5, 2026), Microsoft will lay off 605 employees at its Redmond, Washington campus, effective Sept 4, 2026. As the company's headquarters, the Redmond campus hosts much of Microsoft's cloud and AI engineering work. The move continues Microsoft's pattern of streamlining its organization during the AI transition — reallocating resources toward Azure AI and Copilot while trimming some roles elsewhere. Though 605 people is a small fraction of Microsoft's 200,000+ global workforce, the signal is clear: resource reallocation inside Big Tech's AI arms race continues.

5. Chinese Makers Sweep Top Five in Robot Vacuums: Over 70% Combined Share as iRobot Heads to Bankruptcy

According to NetEase Tech (published 14:00 on Aug 5, 2026), all five of the world's top robot vacuum makers are now Chinese, holding more than 70% of the global market combined, with the industry moving past low-price competition through innovation: Roborock is developing stair-climbing models, Dreame is adding robotic-arm tidying features, and Ecovacs keeps iterating all-in-one base-station mopping solutions — while long-time overseas incumbent iRobot heads toward bankruptcy. Chinese vendors have opened up premium overseas markets with differentiated navigation algorithms, obstacle crossing and automated base stations, drawing strong market attention to the smart-home appliance and service-robot supply chain. By 13:36 on Aug 5, the robot ETF (159039), with nearly 75% humanoid-robot exposure, was up 2.78%. Research firms argue the AI application industry has moved from technology validation into revenue realization, with coding and video generation already achieving commercial closed loops — vertical application vendors with scenario advantages and data moats are set to benefit from both earnings and valuation upside.
