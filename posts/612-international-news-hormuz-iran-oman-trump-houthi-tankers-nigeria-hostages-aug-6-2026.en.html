⚡ Aug 6 World Update: Iran-Oman Hormuz Deal in Final Stages, Trump Says Deal Could Come 'Today or Tomorrow', Houthis Strike Saudi Tankers, Nigeria Frees 308 Hostages

The past hour brought a critical juncture for the Middle East: Iran's foreign ministry announced it has agreed with Oman on the coordinates of a shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, with the deal "in the final stages"; President Donald Trump said an announcement could come "today or tomorrow", though sources cautioned that key details remain unresolved; Iran-backed Houthi fighters struck Saudi tankers as the Red Sea blockade persists; and in Africa, Nigeria said 308 kidnapped citizens were freed in a single night — the country's largest one-day rescue on record. Here is this round's world roundup.

1. Iran: Agreement with Oman on Hormuz Shipping Coordinates, Deal 'In the Final Stages'

Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei announced that Tehran and Muscat have reached agreement on the geographic coordinates of a shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, and that a joint statement is being finalised — provided certain third parties do not interfere. He warned, however, that the agreement with Oman alone would not guarantee safe navigation: "The factors making the Strait of Hormuz insecure still exist on the part of the United States, particularly the naval blockade and other aggressive and threatening actions against Iran and its interests."

Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi later told Iran's official Irna news agency that the new route would be temporary, potentially remaining open for two to four months. Since the US and Israel launched joint strikes on Iran in February, Tehran has largely blocked the strait, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and LNG normally passes. According to Reuters, Iran had sought transit fees of 5–7% of cargo value, Oman is discussing around 3%, and Washington wants no fees at all. Analysts say the deal, if endorsed by the US, would hand Tehran control over ships entering the Gulf — one of the biggest concessions yet to Iran — and could serve as a stepping stone toward a broader ceasefire. In Thursday morning Asian trade, Brent crude eased 0.3% to $79.24 a barrel.

2. Trump: Deal Could Come 'Today or Tomorrow' — Sources Push Back

In live updates reported by CBS News, Trump said the Strait of Hormuz deal could be announced "today or tomorrow" as Iran-Oman talks progress. Al Jazeera also reported that Trump again claimed Washington is in talks with Tehran, saying he "prefers diplomacy to war." Earlier this week, Trump warned that Iran would be "hit very hard" if the strait did not open "very soon."

Sources, however, pushed back against the president's optimism. Reuters cited officials saying important details still had to be agreed and that talk of an imminent deal was premature. Notably, Iran continues to deny direct negotiations with the US, insisting on contact only through intermediaries such as Oman. In June, a US-Iran memorandum of understanding collapsed within days amid tit-for-tat attacks — whether this round holds remains an open question.

3. Houthis Strike Saudi Tankers as Red Sea Blockade Continues

Even as the Hormuz talks advanced, Yemen's Houthi group attacked Saudi tankers. Al Jazeera's live coverage on August 6 confirmed that as Iran and Oman reached agreement on the Hormuz route, "Houthis attack Saudi tankers and Israel bombs Lebanon" continued in parallel. The Houthis have blockaded Saudi ports in the Red Sea since July 20, mirroring the US naval blockade of Iranian ports in the Gulf — putting two of the world's key energy arteries under simultaneous pressure.

"Houthis attack Saudi tankers and Israel bombs Lebanon" — Al Jazeera live blog, Aug 6

Israeli strikes in the region also continued: strikes on southern Lebanon followed the first evacuation warning in weeks, and farmland and homes were reported destroyed in southern Syria. With multiple fronts heating up on the same day, both energy markets and regional diplomacy face added uncertainty.

4. Nigeria: 308 Kidnapped Citizens Freed in One Night, a Record Same-Day Rescue

Nigeria's government announced the rescue of 308 kidnapped citizens in a "coordinated, intelligence-driven" operation. Bayo Onanuga, a special adviser to the president, said President Bola Ahmed Tinubu "warmly welcomes the successful rescue." Of those freed, 163 were abducted from Woro Community in Kaiama Local Government Area, Kwara State, and 145 from Niger State; the hostages were found in the Kainji Lake National Park Forest in New Bussa LGA, Niger State.

The operation was led by Nigeria's National Counter Terrorism Centre, with the armed forces, police and Department of State Services working together — described as "the largest rescue operation ever carried out on the same day by Nigeria's joint security team." Most of the victims were taken during a February 3 attack on communities in Kaiama, in which suspected Boko Haram-linked fighters killed at least 200 people. Tinubu praised the security forces for their "bravery and unwavering commitment." Ransom kidnapping has become a severe crisis in Nigeria: on January 18, 177 people were abducted from three churches in Kaduna State.
