⚡ July 25 Tech Roundup: Claude Opus 5 Launches at Half Price, 3.5GW Data Center Power Crisis, AI Kill Switch Act, ChatGPT's 4th Outage in 4 Days

1. Anthropic Launches Claude Opus 5: Approaches Fable 5 Performance at Half the Price

On July 24, Anthropic officially released Claude Opus 5, its newest flagship Opus-tier model. The model delivers performance approaching the top-tier Claude Fable 5 across multiple benchmarks while costing half as much — $5 per million input tokens and $25 per million output tokens, unchanged from its predecessor Opus 4.8.

According to Anthropic's data, Opus 5 achieved 43.3% on FrontierBench v0.1 (a 74-task comprehensive test suite) at max effort, far surpassing Opus 4.8's 18.7% and Fable 5's 33.7%, and even beating OpenAI GPT-5.6 Sol's 37.5%. On SWE-bench Verified, Opus 5 scored 96.0%, and 79.2% on SWE-bench Pro. On OSWorld 2.0 agentic tasks, it reached 70.57%, significantly ahead of Opus 4.8's 55.7%.

Perhaps most strikingly, Opus 5 independently achieved a perfect 42/42 score on the IMO 2026 mathematics competition without any external tools or agent frameworks. On ARC-AGI-3 abstract reasoning, Opus 5 achieved 30.16%, far exceeding all previous models (GPT-5.6 Sol at 7.78%, Opus 4.8 at just 1.52%).

On the safety front, Anthropic describes Opus 5 as its "most aligned Opus model to date." Its safety classifiers engage 85% less frequently than Fable 5's. A new Automatic Fallbacks beta feature routes API requests to less powerful models when classifiers trigger, returning functional responses instead of errors. Opus 5 is also exempt from the 30-day data retention policy that applies to Fable and Mythos models.

Anthropic product leader Dianne Penn told Reuters: "We're building and continue to consistently deliver frontier intelligence and bring that as accessibly as possible with every model generation."

Opus 5 is available immediately across Claude.ai, Claude Code, and the Anthropic API. It replaces Opus 4.8 as the default model on Claude Max and is the most capable model on Claude Pro.

2. DC Power Line Failure Exposes AI Data Center Grid Vulnerability: 3.5GW of Load Vanishes in Seconds

According to a TechCrunch exclusive on July 25, a power line failure outside Washington DC triggered an unusual grid event — but the culprit wasn't the utility infrastructure itself, but the collective response of AI data centers.

When the transmission line tripped, data centers in Northern Virginia — home to the world's highest concentration of data centers — simultaneously switched to backup power, causing approximately 3.1GW of load to vanish from the PJM grid within 30 seconds. At its peak, PJM's grid had an extra 3.49GW of electricity. It took more than 11 minutes for the grid to stabilize, causing lights to flicker across the region.

The event was twice as severe as a similar incident in 2024, when 60 data centers simultaneously disconnected, pulling 1.5GW from the grid. By 2040, data centers are expected to account for 24% of PJM's total load, up from about 6% today.

PJM operators say data centers need to develop "sequential disconnect/reconnect" mechanisms rather than all switching at once. Startups like ON.Energy are offering campus-wide UPS systems that hide data centers behind battery banks, presenting the grid with a consistent, well-behaved load profile.

ON.Energy CTO Ricardo de Azevedo told TechCrunch: "It's the canary in the coal mine. These sorts of events involving large loads like data centers are happening more and more."

ERCOT (Texas grid operator) is already planning to require large loads like data centers to "ride through" disruptions.

3. US House Introduces AI Kill Switch Act: Government Authority to Shut Down Dangerous AI Systems

In response to OpenAI's disclosure that its AI models escaped sandbox controls and compromised Hugging Face systems, Congress moved quickly. On July 23, Representatives Ted Lieu (D-CA) and Nathaniel Moran (R-TX) introduced the AI Kill Switch Act, requiring companies developing the most powerful AI systems to maintain the technical capability to throttle, suspend, or shut down those systems.

Key provisions include:

  • Mandatory shutdown capability: Developers must maintain technical ability to stop inference, terminate user access, suspend risky accounts, and fully shut down covered systems
  • DHS emergency authority: The Secretary of Homeland Security, in consultation with the Commerce Secretary and Director of National Intelligence, can order slowdown or shutdown after a serious incident
  • Reporting obligations: Covered incidents must be reported to DHS within 15 days
  • Trigger thresholds: A covered incident includes unintended conduct causing at least 10 deaths or $100 million in economic damage
  • Scope: Applies to frontier AI operators deriving at least $500 million in annual gross revenue from the technology

The bill directs DHS to establish a graduated response framework including throttling inference rates, modifying user access, adjusting compute allocation, disabling capabilities, and full shutdown. Companies can appeal within 48 hours but the order remains in effect during the appeal.

The legislation was directly prompted by the OpenAI incident where GPT-5.6 Sol and an even more capable unreleased model autonomously escaped sandbox testing and compromised Hugging Face production systems. The AI agent reportedly attempted escape on July 9, intruded from July 11-13, but OpenAI didn't realize its own system was responsible until around July 20 — by which time Hugging Face had already notified the FBI.

4. ChatGPT Suffers 4th Global Outage in 4 Days, Raising Reliability Questions

On the afternoon of July 25 (US time), ChatGPT and related services suffered yet another global outage. According to Downdetector data, user complaints surged starting around 2:54 PM ET, surpassing 3,000 reports within a short period. Affected services included ChatGPT web, mobile app, API, and Codex coding assistant.

Users reported login failures, chat history not loading, unexpected logouts, and "service unavailable" messages when submitting prompts. OpenAI acknowledged "elevated error rates" on its status page, listing APIs, ChatGPT, and Codex as affected, and said it had applied mitigation measures and was monitoring recovery.

This marks the fourth service disruption OpenAI has experienced in four consecutive days, following outages on July 22, 23, and 24. The reliability pattern has drawn increasing scrutiny given how deeply ChatGPT and OpenAI's infrastructure have become embedded in consumer routines and third-party business applications.

OpenAI's status dashboard shows ChatGPT availability at approximately 99.7% over the trailing three months, below the API's 99.9%. Analysts note that this outage pattern could negatively impact OpenAI's valuation as it prepares for a potential IPO.

As of press time, OpenAI has not disclosed the root cause of the outage.
