⚡ Aug 10 General Tech Update: Musk's Terafab Is Bigger Than the Pentagon and Apple Park Combined, Tesla FSD Tops 40,000 km with Zero Interventions, a Flexible Patch Helps You Sleep 43 Minutes Faster, and GeForce NOW Gets Turned Into a Cloud PC

Aug 10 brings a packed roundup in general tech: Musk's Terafab super-fab was captured under construction in fresh drone footage, with a floor area bigger than the Pentagon, Apple Park, and other landmarks combined and an investment of up to $119 billion; Tesla owner David Moss set a new record of 25,000 miles (over 40,000 km) of continuous FSD driving with zero human interventions; UT Austin researchers unveiled the NEUSLeeP flexible ultrasound patch that helps subjects fall into REM sleep about 43 minutes earlier; and a tinkerer used a bug to turn Nvidia's GeForce NOW into a working cloud PC. Details below; every source's publish time has been verified.

1. Terafab: Musk's Super-Fab Is Bigger Than the Pentagon, Apple Park, and More Combined

According to ITHome (early Aug 10), while most mega-fabs take years from concept to groundbreaking, fresh drone footage shows construction is already underway less than 5 months after Musk first unveiled Terafab. To make the scale graspable, X user Nic Cruz Patane made a visualization: Terafab's floor area is at least 100 million square feet (about 9.29 million sq m) — larger than Giga Texas (10 million sq ft), the Pentagon (6.6 million sq ft), Apple Park (2.82 million sq ft), and the Mall of America (5.6 million sq ft) combined, and more than 5x the enclosed space of Chengdu's New Century Global Center.

Terafab needs this size because it is not just an AI-chip wafer fab but an all-in-one chip-manufacturing base — producing both logic and memory chips while integrating lithography, packaging, and testing into a single facility. Musk began floating a chip fab in late 2025 and officially announced the project in March, driven by SpaceX and Tesla each expecting to need at least 1 terawatt (TW) of compute — more than 10x current global chip supply. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has called it an "extremely difficult" challenge, while Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan, exploring "unconventional" directions in chipmaking, said he "can't think of a better partner than Elon Musk." Given Musk's track record with SpaceX and Tesla — deploying 100,000 Nvidia H200 GPUs in just 19 days in 2024 (a job that normally takes 4 years) — Terafab is seen as one of his biggest projects yet, with investment that could reach $119 billion (about RMB 805 billion). Even so, SpaceX admits the ambitious fab still carries a real risk of failure (source: ITHome).

2. Tesla: Owner Sets New 40,000+ km Record of FSD Driving With Zero Interventions

Per ITHome (early Aug 10), Tesla's software team has been building a "gamified" experience around sustained FSD use: FSD v14.2 added a Self-Driving Stats page, the main interface added a "zero-intervention FSD distance" component, and this summer's FSD v14.3.4 introduced celebration animations at 250/500/1000/5000-mile milestones. Now X user @SawyerMerritt reports that owner David Moss became the first person to push the consecutive-miles counter past 25,000 miles (about 40,234 km) — roughly one full lap around Earth, or about two years of driving for an average American.

The video shows the counter ticking up on a nighttime highway and, as it crossed 25,000 miles, digital confetti raining across the driving visualization with a celebration prompt. Moss is one of Tesla's most active FSD users, previously known for nearly 13,000 consecutive miles and the first fully intervention-free FSD trip across Canada. Analysts caution that turning zero-intervention mileage into a tracked metric carries safety risks: a driver overly attached to their streak may hesitate to disengage FSD in heavy traffic or bad weather to avoid breaking it. Whatever the counter says, safety supervision must come first. With FSD v15 and more milestone animations on the way, such gamified incentives could become more common (source: ITHome).

3. NEUSLeeP: An Ultrasound Patch That Helps You Sleep Faster, and May Treat Depression and Anxiety

ITHome (early Aug 10), citing techspot, reports that UT Austin doctoral graduate Kai Wing and colleagues developed a flexible head patch called NEUSLeeP that helps users enter rapid eye movement (REM) sleep faster and extend that stage — with no drugs or surgery.

NEUSLeeP's novelty lies in integrating low-intensity ultrasound stimulation with electrodes that read brain activity, letting it non-invasively influence deep brain regions tied to REM while monitoring brain response in real time from outside the skull. In experiments, 28 subjects reached REM an average of 43 minutes earlier, with that stage lasting about 16 minutes longer. UT Austin biomedical engineering assistant professor Huiliang "Evan" Wang, who supervised the work, said adhesive devices like this could give researchers and doctors a new way to study sleep and let patients treat sleep disorders at home. The patch also alters neural-circuit activity tied to mood regulation, so it may eventually help with stress, overall health, and conditions such as depression, anxiety, and post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD). Next, the team plans larger clinical trials to test the patch against PTSD, depression, and chronic insomnia (sources: ITHome, TechSpot).

4. GeForce NOW: A Tinkerer Uses a Bug to Turn Nvidia's Cloud Gaming Platform Into a Cloud PC

Per ITHome (early Aug 10), citing neowin, tinkerers Zortos and dpadGuy recently exploited a bug to reach the underlying Windows desktop environment of Nvidia's cloud gaming platform GeForce NOW, turning it into a general-purpose "cloud PC." Nvidia has not yet responded. The trick starts with the game Trove as an entry point: instead of launching it normally, Zortos opens Steam's built-in web browser during setup, accesses the local storage directory, and installs a tool called SalsaNOW. On launch, the system bypasses the game and opens a fully usable Windows desktop, capable of running the T3 programming environment and loading AI models such as Gemma 4 locally via LM Studio.

For now the exploit only works with paid GeForce NOW memberships — not the free tier or third-party-operated versions of the service. It also remains limited: browser downloads are restricted, cloud-session time limits persist, and closing certain system tools (like File Explorer) can end the session immediately. This "cloud PC" trick once again highlights the boundaries of resource isolation on cloud gaming platforms and sounds a fresh warning about session and permission controls (sources: ITHome, Neowin).
