⚡ Aug 11 Market Update: S&P 500 Consolidates Ahead of US CPI, Gold Extends Record Rally, Oil Holds $82, Yen Buying Muted

On August 11, global markets slipped back into a "wait-and-see" mode: the S&P 500's powerful rally stalled as a US-Iran deal failed to materialize on schedule, leaving the index in a tightening range below record highs and all eyes on the upcoming US July CPI report, which will shape the Fed's September decision and Chair Warsh's tone at Jackson Hole. Gold extended into new highs after the soft NFP and is now pressing against the key $4,500 trendline; crude holds firm above $82, with bulls targeting a breakout past $82.55. Yen buying remains muted despite the joint US-Japan intervention, with MUFG and Nomura sizing the operation at roughly ¥14.1 trillion. And Italy's trade surplus widened to €4.2 billion in June as exports accelerated. Details on each item below; all source timestamps have been verified.

1. S&P 500 Rally Stalls: US-Iran Deal Delayed, Market Awaits US July CPI

According to Forexlive (Aug 11, 08:16 GMT), the strong rally in the S&P 500 has stalled since last Wednesday as the US-Iran deal failed to materialize within the expected timeline. Since then, price action has been mostly rangebound, with only last Friday's softer-than-expected NFP report providing some support. That data triggered a dovish repricing in rate expectations, with the probability of a September rate hike falling to 38% from 54% before the release; market pricing has since normalized, with the September hike probability rising back to 48%.

The explanation for this whipsaw: the NFP report contained a significant loss of government jobs, making the headline look much softer than it really was. In fact, the unemployment rate fell further to 4.1%, suggesting the labor market remains on a better trajectory than it has been over the past three years. The next major catalyst is tomorrow's US July CPI report, which will be critical for the September FOMC decision and Fed Chair Warsh's speech at the Jackson Hole symposium. On the technicals, the S&P is stalled around the upper bound of its rising channel; buyers need a break above the record high to extend toward the 8,000 level, while a drop could find initial support near the 7,725 swing low.

2. Gold Extends Record Rally: Nears $4,500 Trendline, US CPI Could Wipe Out Gains

According to Forexlive (Aug 11, 07:11 GMT), gold extended into new highs yesterday after the soft NFP fueled a dovish repricing of Fed rate expectations. As with the equity story, the report wasn't as bad as the headline suggested—the significant loss of government jobs amplified the softness, and the unemployment rate actually fell to 4.1%. Technically, gold has almost reached the major trendline around the $4,500 level, where sellers may step in; a break higher would open the door to the $4,800 target.

The path is not without risk, however. Today's price action may remain mostly rangebound, or we could see some hedging into tomorrow's US CPI that results in weakness. That report will be critical for the September FOMC decision and the Jackson Hole symposium: a hot print could trigger a selloff in gold as traders raise hike bets, while a soft print should reduce the risk of Fed tightening and give gold another boost to extend into fresh highs. After holding comfortably above $4,400, bullion is now pressing against the pivotal $4,500 trendline.

3. Crude Holds Above $82: Bulls Eye Breakout Past $82.55

According to Forexlive (Aug 11, 06:33 GMT), crude futures remain technically bullish after surging from the high-$77s into the low-$82s, although consolidation near the highs shows buyers have yet to secure a clean continuation. Based on CL contract data for August 9–11, crude advanced from an initial close of $78.85 to $82.40—a gain of about $3.55, or roughly 4.5%; the contract printed a low of $77.79 early on August 10 before sharply reversing to a high of $82.55 on August 11.

The immediate outlook remains constructive above $81.90–$82.00, but $82.55 has emerged as the confirmation level bulls must clear to unlock the next move toward $83.00 and potentially $83.40–$83.50. The defining move came on August 10, when crude recovered sharply from its lows. With Middle East tensions and the unresolved US-Iran situation, the geopolitical risk premium remains a central source of support for oil, while upcoming US CPI data will steer the dollar and broader risk appetite.

4. Yen Buying Still Muted: Joint Intervention Hard to Perpetuate, Size Put at ~¥14.1 Trillion

According to Forexlive (Aug 11, 07:42 GMT, citing MUFG and Nomura), despite the joint intervention move by Japan and the US, it doesn't seem to be sticking all that much—USD/JPY is already back on the rise, pushing above 159 this week, though traders may be more cautious about chasing a move above 160 for now. MUFG argues that while the combined effort is significant, it is not likely to perpetuate, at least in terms of market signaling, and that traders still look to sell the yen where possible.

As MUFG put it: "After such a large FX drop in USD/JPY, market participants' appetite for buying the yen could remain muted for now. Certain elements of the market, like retail FX margin traders, were short USD/JPY and could be playing a role in providing renewed yen selling flows. Those short positions have probably been liquidated but returning to a carry strategy at these lower levels may be deemed more attractive again, encouraging renewed USD/JPY buying." Nomura, meanwhile, estimates that Japanese authorities spent roughly ¥14.1 trillion (about $88 billion) in intervention from July 30 to August 3, exceeding the officially confirmed ¥11.7349 trillion of April 30, May 4 and May 6. It also noted, citing FT and Nikkei, that the US may have intervened in EUR/JPY to curb yen weakness without sending a message inconsistent with its strong-dollar policy. Analysts caution that, with the US-Iran conflict unresolved, the yen's fundamentals remain poor, and the intervention is more of a short-term psychological game.

5. Italy's Trade Surplus Widens to €4.2 Billion in June: Exports Accelerate, Led by the EU

According to Forexlive (Aug 11, 08:35 GMT), Italy recorded a €4.23 billion trade surplus in June 2026, with both exports and imports increasing on a monthly and annual basis on seasonally adjusted data. Exports rose 1.6% m/m while imports increased 1.2%; the stronger export performance was driven primarily by trade with EU countries, where exports jumped 6.7%, while shipments to non-EU countries declined 3.6%. On the import side, incoming flows increased from both EU and non-EU markets, rising 1.1% and 1.4%, respectively.

The quarterly picture was stronger still: seasonally adjusted exports rose 2.2% over the three months, while imports recorded a much stronger 5.4% rise. On an annual basis, June exports were 9.8% higher than a year earlier, with imports up 13.2%; export growth was particularly strong within the EU, where outgoing flows rose 15.1% y/y, versus a more moderate 4.1% to non-EU countries. Imports showed the opposite pattern, with flows from non-EU countries jumping 18.7%. Excluding energy, Italy's surplus reached a much larger €9.23 billion, underscoring the continued strength of the country's underlying goods trade. Overall, June's data point to robust growth in Italian trade flows, with exports continuing to expand and the country maintaining a sizable external surplus.
