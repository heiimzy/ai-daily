⚡ July 31 Tech Update: Google DeepMind Launches Gemini Robotics 2 for Full-Body Robot Control, China Unveils Jiuzhang-4 Quantum Computer

Google DeepMind Unveils Gemini Robotics 2: Robots Achieve Full-Body Coordinated Control for the First Time

In the early hours of July 31 at 2:57 AM CST, Google DeepMind officially unveiled Gemini Robotics 2, a next-generation AI model purpose-built for robots. Unlike the previous generation that could only control a robot's upper body, Gemini Robotics 2 achieves full-body coordinated control of humanoid robots for the first time, marking a significant step in bringing Gemini AI technology into the physical world.

In a prerecorded demonstration, DeepMind's new model controlled Apptronik's Apollo humanoid robot through an impressive sequence of coordinated actions — walking across a room, picking up a watering can, and placing it on a shelf — all while autonomously navigating around obstacles. The robot can perform compound actions including walking, squatting, and object manipulation, combined with on-device reasoning for autonomous task planning.

DeepMind simultaneously released three models that work in concert: Gemini Robotics 2 converts camera input and natural language instructions into motor control signals; Gemini Robotics ER 2 acts as the robot's "reasoning system" for multi-step task planning and multi-robot coordination; and On-Device 2 is an edge-optimized version for on-site deployment.

On dexterity, Google demonstrated significant progress: the system achieved a 92% success rate on "unscrewing a lightbulb" tasks. However, success rates remain lower on more complex fine-motor operations like tying garbage bags or sealing Ziplock bags. DeepMind also introduced a new robot safety evaluation benchmark to test whether robots can identify uncertain situations and refuse to execute potentially unsafe instructions. Google stated that Gemini Robotics ER 2 is its safest robot model to date.

"Our goal is to bring AI into the physical world and build an intelligence layer that can be used by all robots," said Carolina Parada, VP of Robotics at DeepMind. However, Kanishka Rao, Director of Robotics at DeepMind, acknowledged that achieving human-level dexterity remains a long road — robots still move slowly and cautiously, with learning efficiency far below that of humans.

Google has partnered with Apptronik, Agile Robots SE, and Boston Dynamics, among others. Gemini Robotics ER 2 will be available through AI Studio for developers, while Gemini Robotics 2 and On-Device 2 will initially be released to early partners and over 100 trusted testers.

China's Jiuzhang-4 Quantum Computer: 3,050 Photons, 10^24x Faster Than Supercomputers

At 2:37 AM CST on July 31, a research team led by Pan Jianwei, Lu Chaoyang, Zhang Qiang, and Liu Naile at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC), in collaboration with multiple domestic research institutes, successfully developed the Jiuzhang-4 — a programmable quantum computing prototype with 1,024 squeezed-state inputs across 8,176 modes, achieving manipulation and detection of 3,050 photons and setting a new world record in photonic quantum information technology. The results have been published in the journal Nature.

The performance is staggering: the world's fastest supercomputer would require approximately 1042 years to solve the specific mathematical problem that Jiuzhang-4 completes in just 25 microseconds — a speedup of roughly 1024 times. The 3,050 photons represent over 3,000 qubits, a more than 10-fold increase over Jiuzhang-3's 255 photons, meaning the computational state space the system can represent and process has grown exponentially.

The biggest technological breakthrough lies in the team's pioneering "programmable spatiotemporal hybrid encoding" architecture. Previously, scaling up photonic quantum computing required adding more optical components, leading to massive equipment size and high photon loss. Jiuzhang-4 enables photons to interfere simultaneously across both time and space dimensions, dramatically improving network connectivity while controlling physical device scale. This breakthrough allows manipulation of 3,050 photons with exponentially improved computational power.

Currently, the Jiuzhang series serves as an extremely powerful special-purpose quantum simulator, excelling at the specific mathematical problem of "Gaussian boson sampling." This problem has near-term applications in image recognition and graph theory, and longer-term potential for generating bosonic error-correcting codes — a key building block for future fault-tolerant universal quantum computers. Jiuzhang-4's dual advantages in scale and low loss lay a solid foundation for building "trillion-quantum-mode 3D cluster states" and future fault-tolerant photonic quantum computing hardware.

However, achieving truly universal quantum computing will still require manipulating millions of qubits with error correction capabilities. Pan's team notes this will need continuous iteration on existing prototypes — Jiuzhang-4 represents a major milestone on this long journey.

Industry Perspective: AI Goes Physical, Quantum Breaks Computing Boundaries

While covering different domains, the two major tech stories from the early hours of July 31 point to the same trend — AI and computing are accelerating their transition from the digital to the physical world.

Google's Gemini Robotics 2 launch signals that AI is no longer confined to chatbots inside screens — it is beginning to acquire a "body." Combined with OpenAI's partnership with Figure AI and Tesla's Optimus progress, 2026 is shaping up as the pivotal year when humanoid robots move from laboratories toward industrialization. By positioning itself as the provider of a universal robotic intelligence system rather than building its own hardware, Google appears to be aiming for an "Android of robotics" strategy — providing the underlying intelligence layer to empower all robot manufacturers.

Meanwhile, Jiuzhang-4 demonstrates China's continued leadership in quantum computing. From the original Jiuzhang in 2020 to today's Jiuzhang-4, Chinese teams have maintained a consistent world record in photonic quantum computing. While quantum computers cannot yet replace classical computers for everyday tasks, the quantum advantage on specific problems like boson sampling is now measured in astronomical factors. This foreshadows a potential paradigm shift in computing power for AI training, drug discovery, and materials science in the years ahead.
