⚡ Aug 9 Tech Roundup: OpenAI Acquires Presentation Startup NextSlide, X Rolls Out Original-Content Rewards, Perseverance Set to Break an Off-World Driving Record, DeepMind WeatherNext Adds a Day of Hurricane Warning

Over the past few hours, several threads across general tech moved in parallel: OpenAI quietly acquired the presentation startup NextSlide and brought its team onto ChatGPT work; X (formerly Twitter) is sunsetting its Revenue Sharing program to launch "Original Content Rewards," a creator program that prizes originality; NASA's Perseverance rover is set to crack over 45 km of driving on Mars, breaking Opportunity's record for the most distance driven on another world, with about 90% of its miles now autonomous; and Google DeepMind and Google Research's WeatherNext model, published in Nature, gives forecasters roughly an extra day of hurricane lead time. Here is a source-verified deep dive into each.

1. OpenAI Acquires NextSlide: A Presentation Team Enters ChatGPT

TechCrunch reports that OpenAI has brought the presentation startup NextSlide under its wing, with its team now working on ChatGPT. NextSlide's website currently carries a note from founder Ahmed Beshry describing the product as one "that could turn prompts, notes, documents, or research into a polished, editable presentation," with the ultimate goal of making visual communication more accessible and helping more people express their ideas clearly.

On LinkedIn, Beshry wrote that after joining OpenAI the team will "continue pursuing the same mission: building AI products that help people create, communicate, and turn their ideas into meaningful work." Financial terms weren't disclosed — but Beshry said the announcement is coming "a few months late," as the deal actually closed earlier this year. He previously co-founded Caper AI, a smart-cart / cashier-less checkout startup acquired by Instacart in 2021.

On its own this looks like a modest, talent-driven acquisition, but it maps a broader trend: workplace productivity surfaces like decks, documents, and notes are increasingly being folded into the great AI players' own models. When generation and editing stop being scarce, the real differentiator is whether content can flow from idea to polished output inside one assistant. For ChatGPT users, NextSlide's abilities may soon translate into a built-in "turn these bullets into a deck" feature.

2. X Sunsets Revenue Sharing: Original Content Rewards Prizes Originality

On the platform side, X — the social site fully owned by Elon Musk's SpaceX — is winding down its existing Revenue Sharing program and replacing it with something called "Original Content Rewards." It will stop taking new Revenue Sharing participants; existing participants keep earning through September 7, and from September 8 they can apply to the new program.

The entry bar is largely unchanged: creators still need an X Premium subscription, at least 500 verified followers, and 500,000 Home Timeline impressions from verified users over 90 days. The real shift is the stress on originality. Original content includes reporting and analysis, photos and videos created by the poster, and memes or graphics they designed themselves. Commentary counts too — though "if your content regularly incorporates material created by others, you'll need to contribute meaningful original value to qualify."

By contrast, posts that simply copy another account, re-uploaded content, or reposts "without meaningful transformation" do not qualify. X's Allegra Jacchia explained on the platform that the old program "had reached a point where its incentives were misaligned." "Creators should focus on bringing net-new content to the platform instead of maximizing payouts," she said. "We could have kept adding more rules and exceptions, but the better decision was to start fresh and build a program that rewards originality from day one."

3. Perseverance Breaks an Off-World Driving Record: About 90% Autonomous

The self-driving benchmark in space comes from NASA: Perseverance will push past 45.1 billion Kn (28 miles) next week, breaking the record for the most distance driven by any vehicle on another world, a record previously held by the long-lived Opportunity rover, which lost contact with NASA in 2018. What's remarkable: it covered that distance in about a third of the time.

As with Earth's self-driving cars, Perseverance's navigation cameras image the terrain in real time, and an onboard computer runs algorithms to calculate the safest route. Mars offers no clear maps, lanes, or street signs, and it is full of boulders and sandy slopes — but at least the rover doesn't deal with other traffic. By contrast, the twin-esque Curiosity rover carries similar imaging but an older onboard computer, some chips still dating to the 1990s, so only about 10% of its driving is autonomous; in over a decade and a half it has driven 38 km. Perseverance's modern Vision Compute Element lets it sense and run its computing while its wheels turn, letting roughly 90% of its distance be autonomous — even though its top wheel speed is just ~150 meters per hour. The tell is clear: onboard computing generation gaps decide how much of the "let the machine drive" we genuinely trust.

4. DeepMind WeatherNext: About a Day More Hurricane Warning

Finally, the crossroads of AI and hard science: Google DeepMind and Google Research's WeatherNext model, detailed in a study published in Nature, shows "unprecedented" hurricane and cyclone prediction accuracy, giving forecasters roughly a day more lead time than current models. The model's landmark test came in October 2025, when Caribbean data posed a tough question — would a growing storm end up in the weak closer to Jamaica, or hit land? WeatherNext chose a second path, and five days before landfall predicted with 80% confidence that the system would strike the Jamaica coast (May in Category 5). The coming "Hurricane Melissa" indeed brought catastrophic flooding and landslides to the island — and earlier, NASA's record safety net let coastal communities get out ahead of the threat.

The research trick: train the model on both weather and cyclones. "We don't have much cyclone data, but we have a lot of weather data," says lead Ferran Alet, "so we trained a model to be good at weather as well as cyclones." This matters because extreme events are inherently rare, an area where AI forecasting has previously struggled. Extra lead time is genuinely golden for evacuations and staging supplies — "even a few hours can make a difference," says Mike Brennan, director of the US National Hurricane Center, "and the ability to push forecast accuracy out as much as a day is really valuable." Historically that gain would represent a decade of work; today's model brings it much closer to hand.

5. What to Watch

In the next 24 hours, keep an eye on: whether OpenAI ships NextSlide's capabilities as an in-ChatGPT "turn this into a deck" has quickly; how X's Original Content Rewards resets the incentive landscape and hammers repost accounts; whether Perseverance's record pushes JPL to raise autonomous autonomy in next-gen Mars missions; and how the WeatherNext study feeds into the global conversation about AI dashboard day-to-day forecasting. Together these threads underline a shared point: whether the tool is a rover contends on Mars or an assistant on your screen, the real frontier is no longer "what the machine could do" — it's "how much we'll trust it to do on its own." That day keeps getting closer.
