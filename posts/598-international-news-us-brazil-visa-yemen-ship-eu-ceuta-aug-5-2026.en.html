⚡ August 5 World News: US Revokes Brazil Ambassador's Visa, Indian-Flagged Ship Sinks Off Yemen, EU Praises Spain's Ceuta Response

Over the past few hours, major news broke on three fronts at once: Western Hemisphere diplomacy, Middle East shipping, and Europe's borders. The Trump administration revoked the visa of Brazil's ambassador to the United States, pushing US-Brazil relations to a new low ahead of Brazil's election; an Indian-flagged cargo ship was hit by a projectile off the coast of Yemen and sank, renewing alarm over Red Sea shipping safety; and EU interior ministers unusually united in praising Spain's "swift response" to the Ceuta migrant crisis, saying Europe had "passed" the border test. Here is this round's world news roundup.

1. US-Brazil spat: Washington revokes the ambassador's visa in retaliation for denied visas for US diplomats

According to The Associated Press (Washington, August 4, 2026, 20:27 UTC), the Trump administration has revoked the visa of Brazil's ambassador to the United States, Maria Luiza Ribeiro Viotti, in retaliation for Brazil's denial last month of visas for two American diplomats. The two were Riley Barnes, the assistant secretary of state for democracy, human rights and labor, and one of his top aides — after reports emerged that the pair planned to criticize Lula's government. Washington denied that account, saying the two had planned to visit Brasilia on July 27-30 for routine discussions on "election integrity," religious freedom and freedom of expression.

The State Department called the decision a reciprocal response to Brazil's actions and said the step had been delayed several times to give Brazilian President Lula room to back down — something he did not do. One State Department official stressed that Ambassador Viotti is not being expelled, making the visa revocation more of a diplomatic gesture than a break in ties. The friction is long-running: the Trump and Lula administrations have clashed over Venezuela and Cuba, and Lula has blamed Secretary of State Marco Rubio for some of the actions. Notably, the 80-year-old Lula, seeking a fourth term, will face Sen. Flávio Bolsonaro, son of the former president, whose family has close ties to the Trump administration — so the visa episode is widely read in Brasilia as a signal about how Washington views Brazil's election.

2. Red Sea shipping hit again: Indian-flagged vessel sinks off Yemen, all 14 crew rescued

According to BBC (updated August 4, 2026, 21:33 UTC), the Indian-flagged vessel MSV Faize Noore Oliya capsized and sank after being hit by a projectile off the coast of Yemen. India's Shipping Minister Sarbananda Sonowal confirmed that all 14 people on board — 13 of them Indian nationals — were safely rescued, condemned the "unprovoked attack," and said steps would be taken to ensure the safety of seafarers in the region. India's ministry of external affairs later called attacks on commercial shipping in the region "deeply worrisome," insisting that free and unimpeded navigation through international waterways must be upheld under international law.

It is not clear who struck the ship, but Yemen's transportation ministry — part of the Saudi-backed, internationally recognized government — accused Iran-backed Houthi fighters of carrying out the attack using a boat laden with explosives; the Houthis have not commented. This is the latest in a spate of attacks on vessels in the Red Sea, an alternative waterway some tankers have used since Iran blocked the Strait of Hormuz in February. The Houthis announced a blockade on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea ports on July 20 and have since targeted Saudi tankers; the UK Maritime Trade Operations agency has recorded several attacks over the past week. Analysts note that while Tuesday's hopes of a US-Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz pushed oil prices to a three-week low, the shipping threat on the Red Sea side keeps building, and the security of global energy and trade routes is far from assured.

3. EU backs Spain: ministers praise 'swift response' to Ceuta crisis, Europe 'passes' the test

According to BBC (updated August 4, 2026, 21:46 UTC), EU interior ministers meeting on Tuesday praised Spain's "swift response" to the Ceuta migrant crisis. Spain's Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska told the meeting that around 72,000 migrants crossed into the Spanish territory in North Africa between July 30 and 31, and that an estimated 70,000 have since been returned to Morocco. At least 75 people died on the Spanish side of the border, while 11 deaths were reported in Morocco.

The meeting had been expected to be tense: Italy called images from Ceuta "shocking" and suspended its Schengen agreement with Spain on Friday — which, since the two countries share no land border, effectively applies to flights — with Finland and Denmark backing the move and Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babiš urging a temporary suspension of Spain's Schengen membership; Italy, Denmark and 20 other nations later sent a letter expressing concern. But Irish Justice Minister Jim O'Callaghan, who chaired Tuesday's meeting, said the EU stood in solidarity with Spain and that migration required "a united European response." EU commissioner Magnus Brunner called the episode a "test" of European resilience and border security that Europe had successfully "passed," adding that the EU had offered Spain emergency financial support to strengthen its borders. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez had earlier accused some European governments of attacking Spain "driven by prejudice, fake news, ignorance, or political interest," pointing out that Ceuta is not part of the Schengen area.
