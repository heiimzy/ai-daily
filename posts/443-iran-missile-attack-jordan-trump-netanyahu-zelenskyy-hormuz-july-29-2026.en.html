⚡ Iran Fires Ballistic Missiles at US Base in Jordan, Breaking Fragile Truce; Trump Hosts Netanyahu and Zelenskyy Back-to-Back; Oman Pushes Hormuz Joint Management Plan

The Middle East saw a dramatic escalation on Tuesday as Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) launched multiple ballistic missiles at a US military base in Jordan, shattering a four-day pause in hostilities. The attack came on the same day President Donald Trump held back-to-back White House meetings with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, making July 28 one of the most consequential days of the ongoing Iran war.

Iranian Missile Strike on US Base in Jordan Ends Fragile Ceasefire

US Central Command (CENTCOM) reported that at 5:45 PM ET on Tuesday, IRGC forces fired multiple ballistic missiles from Iranian territory in what it described as an "attempted surprise attack" on American forces stationed in the Middle East. All incoming missiles were successfully intercepted. Axios reported, citing a US official, that the target was the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base near Azraq in northeastern Jordan — a critical US air hub that was struck by Iranian missiles on July 17, killing two American service members.

"All Iranian missiles were successfully intercepted. US forces remain vigilant and at a high state of readiness," CENTCOM stated on X. The attack marked the first direct Iranian strike on US forces since Trump ordered a pause in American airstrikes on Friday, July 24, to give diplomacy another chance.

The pause had followed nearly two weeks of nightly US strikes against Iranian military targets, which were met by Iranian drone and ballistic missile attacks on US bases across Gulf states. The tit-for-tat cycle halted on Friday after Trump claimed Tehran had requested talks — though Iran has consistently denied engaging in direct negotiations with Washington.

Oil prices ticked higher after the attack, with Brent crude rising 1.13% to $83/barrel and WTI climbing 4.11% to $83/barrel, paring Monday's sharp 8% decline triggered by ceasefire hopes.

Trump Hosts Netanyahu, Zelenskyy in Landmark White House Double-Header

Netanyahu: 90-Minute Closed-Door Meeting Called 'Excellent'

Netanyahu arrived at the White House at 11 AM ET (15:00 GMT) for his first face-to-face meeting with Trump since the US and Israel jointly launched their war on Iran on February 28. The meeting — attended by Vice President JD Vance, Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, and other senior officials — lasted approximately 90 minutes and was closed to the press.

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt described the talks as "positive and productive." Netanyahu was more effusive, posting a video calling it "one of the best conversations I've ever had with President Trump" and emphasizing "full partnership, mutual support, and an understanding of the shared goal of ensuring that Iran does not obtain a nuclear weapon."

Tensions had flared publicly just hours before the meeting. Trump told Fox News he did not need Netanyahu to brief him on Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site, saying: "I don't need Bibi to tell me that. Bibi's telling me that because he wants me to stay involved." He dismissed the site as "not a big problem" but threatened to destroy it if no deal is reached. Despite pre-meeting reports that Netanyahu planned to present intelligence on the underground facility, Israeli officials later confirmed that Pickaxe Mountain was not discussed during the talks.

The meeting revealed the deep strategic divide between the two leaders on Iran policy. Trump is under domestic pressure to end an unpopular war ahead of November midterm elections, while Netanyahu — facing his own reelection on October 27 against former IDF Chief Gadi Eisenkot — has pushed for continued military pressure on Iran. Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz stated bluntly before the meeting that Israel "very much wants to attack energy targets in Iran but the United States does not allow it at the moment."

Also not discussed: the potential US sale of F-35 fighter jets to Turkey, which Netanyahu opposes, and Palestinian statehood.

Zelenskyy Presses for Patriots, Discusses Peace Process Revival

Zelenskyy also met with Trump at the White House on Tuesday, with Patriot missile interceptors at the top of his agenda. Ukraine faces daily Russian missile and drone barrages, but the Pentagon revealed that $400 million in authorized Ukraine aid would not be fully disbursed until fiscal year 2029 — drawing bipartisan criticism.

After the meeting, Zelenskyy said he discussed licenses for Patriot interceptor production and "revitalizing the diplomatic process" to end Russia's war. He later visited Capitol Hill for a meeting with all 100 US senators. Trump had promised at the NATO summit to grant Ukraine a license to produce Patriot interceptors domestically, though ramping up production will take months.

Relations between Trump and Zelenskyy have warmed as Ukraine has gained battlefield momentum, including deep strikes on Russia's oil infrastructure. Trump has slashed his peace deadline for Putin from 50 days to 10-12 days, saying "I already know the answer."

"The president and I discussed licenses for Patriot interceptor production and several other ideas that could help," Zelenskyy said. "We also spoke about diplomacy — it's important that the diplomatic process be reinvigorated."

US and Saudi Jets Strike Iran-Backed Targets in Iraq

In a coordinated operation on Tuesday, US and Saudi fighter aircraft struck multiple "terrorist logistics and weapons sites across Iraq" used by Iran-aligned militias. The operation came after more than 30 drone attacks were launched against Saudi Arabia over the prior 72 hours, many targeting Saudi Aramco oil facilities. Saudi air defenses had intercepted drones launched from Iraq toward Riyadh and the Eastern Province.

Yemen's Houthi movement also claimed attacks on Saudi oil tankers, keeping Red Sea shipping lanes under threat. CENTCOM stated that the strikes were conducted "at the Commander in Chief's direction" as part of ongoing operations to degrade Iran's ability to threaten regional security.

Strait of Hormuz: Oman Proposes Joint Management, Iran Digs In

On the diplomatic front, Oman has formally presented a joint Strait of Hormuz management plan backed by Gulf states. The proposal centers on a "voluntary fee" mechanism — commercial vessels pay a fee voluntarily upon transit, with funds jointly managed by Iran, Oman, and other stakeholders. The goal is to prevent Iran from unilaterally controlling the world's most critical energy chokepoint.

Iran's position remains uncompromising. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Tehran will take "any necessary action" to maintain control over the strait, and the foreign ministry reiterated that Iran "has not requested talks with the US in the past 16-17 days." Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi held separate phone calls with his Saudi and Omani counterparts on Tuesday evening, emphasizing the need to "strengthen cooperation and advance joint diplomatic efforts."

Omani-Iranian technical talks in Tehran over the weekend reportedly "made progress" though no final deal was reached. The New York Times reports that Iran's leadership views the current situation as a manageable stalemate between peace and all-out war, using oil prices and Hormuz access as leverage against Trump. Brent crude is currently trading in the $82-84 range, reflecting continued uncertainty.

Senate Advances Russia-Iran Sanctions as Graham Mourned

The US Senate moved forward with a bipartisan Russia-Iran sanctions bill, with sponsors calling it the best way to honor the legacy of the late Senator Lindsey Graham. The South Carolina Republican — one of Washington's most hawkish voices on Iran — died recently at age 71.

Trump, Netanyahu, and Zelenskyy all attended Graham's memorial service in Washington on Tuesday. Trump eulogized Graham as "extremely hawkish," noting he "never saw a war that he didn't like." The rare convergence of the three wartime leaders underscored the deeply interconnected nature of the current global geopolitical landscape — from the Iran war to the Ukraine conflict, from Hormuz to the Black Sea.

Separately, the Israeli military reported intercepting a drone near its border with Jordan on Tuesday, with the origin under investigation. Iran also executed two men on charges related to January protests, signaling no let-up in domestic repression.

The coming days will be critical: the next round of Lebanon-Israel talks is set for Rome on August 4-6, while US-Iran messaging through Omani and Qatari mediators continues. Whether the fragile diplomatic window can survive Tuesday's missile attack remains the central question facing the Middle East.
