⚡ Aug 3 World Update: Israel Publicly Balks at Gaza Disarmament Deal, Ukraine-Russia Drone and Energy War Escalates, Russian Glide Bombs Strike Zaporizhzhia

Monday's international stage was pulled in two directions. On one side, Trump's push for a "full Hamas disarmament" deal met with an unusually public Israeli rebuke, leaving the Gaza ceasefire process swinging between disarmament and withdrawal. On the other, the drone and energy war between Russia and Ukraine escalated once again, as Kyiv kept striking Russian logistics targets and Zelenskyy urged the West to speed up Patriot deliveries. Here is this roundup.

1. Israel Publicly Balks: No Withdrawal Until Hamas Truly Disarms, Weapons Must Be Destroyed, Qatar and Turkey Rejected as Monitors

On July 30, President Donald Trump announced that his Board of Peace had reached a "historic agreement" for the full disarmament of Hamas and all other armed groups in Gaza, calling it a major milestone in his 20-point plan. But on August 3, Doron Spielman, a spokesperson for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office, told the Associated Press that Israel had shared "serious security concerns" with the White House about the deal — the first public Israeli response since Trump announced the agreement.

Spielman said Israel's intelligence assessment is that Hamas remains committed to rebuilding its military rather than genuinely demilitarizing — still recruiting fighters, restoring arms and rebuilding its military system. He stressed that the IDF — which controls more than 60% of Gaza — would not withdraw before Hamas truly disarms: "If we redeploy before Hamas is truly disarmed, it will quickly expand its presence throughout Gaza, rebuild its terrorist infrastructure, again threaten Israeli communities, and seek to carry out another Oct. 7-style attack."

According to Haaretz, Israel relayed three reservations to Board of Peace member and former British Prime Minister Tony Blair: it wants to retain the right to conduct military operations in areas to be handed to international forces; it insists confiscated Hamas weapons be destroyed rather than stored by the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG); and it rejects Qatar and Turkey taking part in verifying the roadmap's stages. Meanwhile, Israeli air raids hit Gaza for a second consecutive night, killing at least 13 people including children, according to Palestinian officials; the military said it targeted militants.

2. Ukraine-Russia Drone and Energy Escalation: Kyiv Strikes Russian Logistics, Zelenskyy Calls for More Patriot

Russia's largest online retailer Wildberries confirmed on August 3 that a Ukrainian drone strike had hit its Vladimir region warehouse, where a fire broke out and all personnel were evacuated with no casualties. It was the second straight night of Ukrainian strikes on Wildberries facilities — a day earlier, drones hit a warehouse in the Samara region, about 800 km from the border. In recent weeks Ukraine has struck around a dozen Wildberries warehouses across multiple Russian regions, accusing the company of supporting Moscow's military logistics and selling dual-use goods.

Russia's Defense Ministry said on August 3 its air defenses intercepted and destroyed 131 Ukrainian drones overnight. Ukraine's drone commander, Robert Brovdi, claimed his units had struck four Russian oil refineries and the Taman marine terminal over four days, destroying 12 fuel storage tanks. The Ukrainian Air Force said Russia launched 181 strike drones overnight; Zelenskyy said that over the past week Moscow fired about 1,900 drones, nearly 1,600 aerial bombs and 144 missiles of various types.

Facing persistent ballistic-missile pressure, Zelenskyy again urged the West to speed up Patriot deliveries: "The world has Patriot missiles. What matters now is for our partners to make the political decision — the decision to provide the necessary supplies." In what appeared to be a retaliatory move, Russia struck the Kyiv-region Rozetka warehouse with two drones on August 2 — a facility that had been hit a day earlier in an attack that killed one person and wounded at least six.

3. Russian Glide Bombs Kill One in Zaporizhzhia as the Front Stays Stuck

On August 3, Russian forces dropped glide bombs on Zaporizhzhia in southern Ukraine, killing at least one person and wounding dozens. Over the past week, Russian glide bomb and missile strikes on Zaporizhzhia and elsewhere have sharply increased. That same day, explosions were reported across Crimea, with the Russia-installed head of the peninsula, Sergei Aksyonov, saying the overnight raids killed three people and wounded two others, and traffic on the Crimean Bridge was temporarily suspended.

Analysts note that Russia has made little to no territorial gains along the 1,200-kilometer front this year, turning instead to long-range strikes on Ukrainian cities and infrastructure, while Ukraine keeps sending drones deep into Russia to hit refineries and logistics. Battlefield escalation running alongside diplomacy has become a defining feature of the current landscape — whether the Gaza "disarmament-for-withdrawal" roadmap can survive and when the Russia-Ukraine energy strikes cool will shape the outlook for the Middle East and European security in the coming weeks.
