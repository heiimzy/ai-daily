1. Kyoto Fusioneering Wins US Grants to Build Fuel-Breeding Device at Oak Ridge

According to TechCrunch (published Aug 11, 15:00 UTC), Kyoto Fusioneering, a Japanese fusion supply-chain specialist, has secured grants from the US Department of Energy and the state of Tennessee to build a fusion fuel-breeding prototype called Unity-3 at Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL). The company has raised about $121 million in committed capital, according to FusionX, and exclusively told TechCrunch it is moving its US headquarters to Oak Ridge. Fusion reactors need vast supporting infrastructure to run for long, and fuel-cycle technology is widely seen as a bottleneck: a recent Fusion Industry Association survey found that over half of fusion startups plan to work with external suppliers like Kyoto Fusioneering. Unity-3 will test a technology known as a breeding blanket: a liquid-lithium blanket that absorbs heat and neutrons, with the lithium atoms splitting into helium and tritium—a hydrogen isotope that is key fuel for many reactor designs. The tritium is separated and sent to the reactor while heat is extracted for power. The device will also validate a range of blanket materials, giving startups including Realta Fusion, Thea Energy, Type One Energy and Xcimer Energy experimental data to replace computer-model assumptions.

2. Manus Announces Split from Meta, Resumes Independent Operation

According to IT Home (published Aug 11, 14:48 GMT), Manus announced in an official statement that it is leaving Meta and resuming operations as an independent company, continuing to serve millions of users worldwide. To comply with regulatory requirements in certain jurisdictions, data generated by some users on or after December 29, 2025 will be deleted between 08:00 Beijing time on August 23 and August 24, 2026. Affected users can back up data until 07:59 on August 23 and restore it from 08:00 on August 25 through official tools, with return rewards promised afterward. Between the backup cutoff and the restore window, affected accounts will be inaccessible. After independence, Manus says it will store data in the United States and Singapore. For context, Meta's roughly $2 billion acquisition of Manus last year stunned the industry, after which China's National Development and Reform Commission prohibited the foreign acquisition of the Manus project in accordance with regulations, and the Ministry of Commerce said the deal must comply with Chinese law. Users who registered via Apple or Facebook accounts should watch for in-app notifications.

3. Apple iOS 27 Beta 5 Hints at iCloud+ Changes, Possible Paid Tiers

According to IT Home (published Aug 11, 14:55 GMT), Apple released the fifth developer beta of iOS 27 today, and users have spotted new prompts related to iCloud+ subscriptions and HomeKit Secure Video that may hint at future charges for premium features. Under current rules, the 50GB iCloud+ plan supports one HomeKit Secure Video camera, the 200GB plan supports up to five, and plans from 2TB to 12TB have no limit. In beta 5, however, a 2TB subscriber found the HomeKit Secure Camera settings page only showing support for five cameras, while a screenshot shared by user Stephen Robles showed a prompt reading "Your iCloud+ plan includes video summaries for 15 cameras" for a user on the top-tier $59.99-per-month 12TB plan. The changes could be a beta bug or a test of new service policy; Apple has not confirmed any change to HomeKit Secure Video entitlements. CEO Tim Cook has repeatedly hinted that some Apple Intelligence features may eventually carry fees, and the Siri AI allowance scheme for iOS 27 has yet to be finalized.

4. Ulefone Launches Armor 22 Pro and 24 Pro Rugged Phones, Up to 22,000mAh

According to IT Home (published Aug 11, 15:03 GMT), Ulefone has unveiled two rugged smartphones, the Armor 22 Pro and Armor 24 Pro, both powered by the MediaTek Helio G200 chipset with eSIM support. The Armor 22 Pro is 15mm thick, features a 64MP main camera plus a 64MP night-vision lens, a 6.58-inch FHD+ display with 120Hz refresh, 8GB RAM, 128GB/256GB storage, a 6,600mAh battery and Android 16, priced from $239.99. The Armor 24 Pro is the endurance/performance version: a 22,000mAh battery, 12GB RAM, a 6.78-inch FHD+ 120Hz display, 66W wired charging and 10W reverse charging, plus support for the uSmart expansion interface, priced at $359.99. Both models continue Ulefone's focus on the extreme-durability niche, targeting outdoor, engineering and emergency-use users.

5. Bumble Drops Its Women-Message-First Rule, Extends Replies to 72 Hours

According to TechCrunch (published Aug 11, 13:59 UTC), dating app Bumble announced Tuesday that anyone in a match can now send the first message, officially ending the women-message-first requirement that defined the company for more than a decade. Reply windows are also extended from 24 to 72 hours to ease the pressure of constant app-checking. Founder and CEO Whitney Wolfe Herd framed the change as evolution, not retreat: "While women making the first move was a radical idea, being women-first was never about prescribing just one way to connect." She cited survey data showing 66% of women surveyed prefer men to send the first message and that more than half of members said the longer window improved their experience. Bumble had begun loosening the rule in 2024 with its "Opening Moves" feature. The update arrives as the company works to turn around its business: last week's Q2 earnings showed revenue down 15.2% year over year to $210.5 million, with paying customers expected to decline in Q3. Bumble is also exploring a swipe-free future, more in-person events and AI features to win over Gen Z.
