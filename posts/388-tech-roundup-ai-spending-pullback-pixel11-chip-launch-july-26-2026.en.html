⚡ July 26 Tech Roundup: Corporate America Rations AI, Google Pixel 11 Hikes Price on RAM Crisis, Moody's Warns on AI Capex, China Launches GPU Compute Satellite

1. WSJ: Corporate America Pulls Back on AI Spending — Uber Burned Annual Budget in 4 Months, Companies Switch to Chinese Models

The Wall Street Journal reported July 26 that Corporate America is undergoing an unprecedented AI spending pullback. After two years of FOMO-driven investment, executives are finally asking the devastatingly simple question: where is the revenue?

According to WSJ's investigation, several major companies saw their AI bills double or even triple in the past year. Uber exhausted its entire annual AI budget by March — just four months into the year. Microsoft canceled most internal Claude Code licenses, pushing employees toward its own coding assistant. Meta has urged employees not to use AI tools unnecessarily. Tesla imposed a $200-per-employee-per-week spending cap on third-party AI products.

An even more notable trend is the mass migration toward cheaper AI models — many of them built in China. UBS estimates training costs for leading Chinese AI models amount to just one-tenth of their US counterparts. Cursor (recently acquired by SpaceX for $60 billion) noted that most routine tasks simply don't need the most powerful and expensive models.

"We don't need Mythos or Fable. We need something good enough." — Curt Meinhold, North Carolina-based tech executive, explaining why he chose DeepSeek over Anthropic's flagship models

According to OpenRouter, the five most popular AI models over the past month were all Chinese. Moonshot's Kimi K3 saw 930,000+ downloads in its first week, with US downloads surging 387%. This trend is reshaping the AI competitive landscape from "performance-first" to "value-first."

2. Google Confirms Pixel 11 Price Hike: Global RAM Crisis Drives 1GB Cost from $2.80 to $12

Google's VP of Devices and Services, Shakil Barkat, confirmed in an interview with 9to5Google that the upcoming Pixel 11 series — launching August 12 — will see price increases across the board. The cause: a global RAM shortage crisis.

"There's never been an increase in memory prices like the world's going through right now," Barkat said bluntly. Citing Morgan Stanley data, he noted that 1GB of RAM has surged from $2.80 in 2025 to $12 in 2026 — a more than sixfold increase. The crisis, dubbed "RAMageddon," is being driven by AI data centers absorbing the world's memory supply.

According to leaks, the entry-level Pixel 11 Pro will drop from 16GB to 12GB of RAM, but storage doubles from 128GB to 256GB to soften the blow. Prices are expected to rise $100 across the lineup: the Pixel 11 starts at an estimated $899, with the Pixel 11 Pro XL and Pixel 11 Pro Fold each seeing $100 increases.

Google emphasized that it's not alone — Apple, Nintendo, Microsoft, and Roku have all raised prices due to surging memory costs. Barkat revealed Google is making a "dedicated effort" to optimize Android's memory efficiency, aiming to reduce future devices' physical RAM requirements through software.

"The economics have fundamentally shifted and we're not immune to that." — Shakil Barkat, Google VP of Devices and Services

3. Moody's Warns: AI Infrastructure Arms Race Threatens Big Tech Credit Ratings

Moody's Ratings published a research note warning that the race to build AI infrastructure is straining the finances of six hyperscalers: Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, Oracle, and CoreWeave. The shift from asset-light software models to capital-intensive infrastructure is creating unprecedented financial pressure.

Key figures from the report:

  • 2026 capex: Combined $785 billion
  • 2027 capex: Approaching $1 trillion
  • Direct debt: ~$460 billion across the six companies
  • Lease liabilities: $1.2 trillion total, with $820 billion for data centers not yet constructed
  • Alphabet: Announced an $85 billion equity sale — even the world's most cash-rich companies are selling stock

Moody's specifically highlighted the "circular AI ecosystem" problem: tech giants pour billions into AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic, which then spend that money on cloud services from the same investors. This circular flow makes it difficult to assess genuine demand.

Oracle's credit rating stands at Baa2 with a negative outlook — just two notches above junk status. CoreWeave is already in Ba3 high-yield territory. While Microsoft, Amazon, and others face no immediate downgrade risk, Moody's warned that investors must closely monitor whether these companies can generate adequate returns on their AI investments.

4. China's Lijian-1 Rocket Successfully Launches Five Satellites, Including First In-Orbit Domestic GPU Compute Payload

On July 24 at 7:33 AM Beijing time, the Lijian-1 (Kinetica-1) Y15 launch vehicle successfully lifted off from the Dongfeng Commercial Aerospace Innovation Zone, deploying five satellites into precise orbits. This was the 15th flight of the Lijian-1 and the 16th Lijian-series launch — the only Chinese commercial rocket to have deployed over 100 satellites (now 110) into space.

The mission's highlight was the "Jitianxing A-04" (Yunjian Muxi) satellite, carrying a domestically-produced GPU compute payload from Shanghai-based Muxi (沐曦) — marking the first in-orbit reliability verification of a Chinese GPU in real space conditions.

Key technological breakthroughs of the "Yunjian Muxi" satellite:

  • Domestic GPU in space: Carries a Muxi GPU-based compute payload, enabling real-time on-orbit processing — the satellite can "think," not just "see"
  • AI design + 3D printing: Satellite main structure uses AI-generated cellular structures plus 3D printing, reducing component count by 80% and weight by 30%
  • Embodied AI satellite model: First in-orbit test of Zhejiang Lab's embodied intelligence satellite model for autonomous health management and multi-satellite collaborative control
  • Three-Body Computing Constellation: Serves as a node in the thousand-satellite space computing infrastructure, validating the "compute in space, satellite interconnect, model in orbit" vision

The mission also carried an onboard router and laser communication terminal — important steps toward building a space-based intelligent computing network. China's "Star Pivot Plan" aims to construct a thousand-satellite-scale space computing network, creating an integrated space-ground AI computing infrastructure.
