I. US-Iran War Day 144: Congress Divided as Fighting Intensifies

Senate 49-47 Rejects War Powers Resolution as House and Senate Split

The US Senate voted 49-47 on Thursday to block a Democratic-led joint resolution that would have required President Donald Trump to obtain congressional authorization for continued hostilities against Iran, marking a rare public split between the two chambers on the Iran war. Hours earlier, the House of Representatives passed its own war powers resolution 214-208, with four Republicans crossing party lines to join Democrats in directing Trump to halt military action against Iran unless Congress authorizes it.

In the Senate vote, Republican Senator Susan Collins of Maine voted with Democrats in favor, while Democrat John Fetterman of Pennsylvania sided with Republicans against. Four Republican senators — Murkowski, Paul, Britt, and McConnell — did not vote. While the House resolution is largely symbolic and cannot legally force the president's hand, the 214-208 vote sends a strong signal that Trump's congressional support is eroding as the war enters its fifth month with no end in sight.

Iran Launches Retaliatory Drone and Missile Strikes on US Bases in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait

On Friday (July 24), Iran's military announced a wave of drone and missile attacks against American military facilities in Bahrain, Jordan, and Kuwait, in what Tehran described as an "eye for an eye" response to the 13th consecutive night of US strikes on Iranian territory. Iran's army said it used Arash kamikaze drones to target fuel depots, large equipment warehouses, and barracks at Isa Air Base in Bahrain. In Jordan, the army claimed it struck aircraft hangars, aviation maintenance hangars, and a barracks at Al-Azraq base. In Kuwait, Iranian forces separately attacked US equipment warehouses at Camp Udairi and US personnel positions at Camp Doha and Camp Arifjan.

The Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) separately claimed it struck Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait, destroying an ammunition depot and inflicting US casualties, and "significantly damaged" a US Fifth Fleet surveillance tower in Bahrain. The IRGC also claimed to have hit an Amazon data center in Bahrain, though neither Amazon nor Bahraini authorities have commented. As of press time, neither the US, Jordan, nor Kuwait had independently confirmed Iran's claimed battle damage.

Jordan Intercepts 7 Missiles and 6 Drones; Erbil Airport Hit

Jordan's military said Friday its air defenses intercepted seven Iranian missiles and six drones during the day's attacks, with no casualties or material damage reported. In Iraq's Kurdistan region, Kurdish counter-terrorism forces said the US-led coalition shot down five explosive-laden drones over Erbil between 9:32 and 9:45 a.m., causing no casualties. Erbil International Airport temporarily suspended flights. Bahrain's Interior Ministry activated warning sirens multiple times, urging citizens to head to the nearest shelters.

Trump "Close to Decision" on Massive Attack; Frozen Iranian Funds to Cover Ship Damages

President Trump told Axios on Thursday that he is "very close to a decision" on launching a massive attack on Iran "bigger than anything before." On Truth Social, he announced that effective immediately, "any and all damages done to Ships, Cargo, or anything related thereto, will be paid for by Iranian Money that the United States has in its possession, and controls." Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi condemned the move as an "incendiary precedent," warning that "once governments normalize confiscation, no one's assets are safe."

The human and financial toll continues to mount. US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth previously disclosed that the war has cost $37.5 billion since hostilities resumed on June 27. Iran's health ministry reports 55 killed and 629 wounded on the Iranian side. Friday morning saw US missiles strike an IRGC naval headquarters in the Caspian Sea province of Gilan, while four people were killed and five wounded near Ahvaz, and two injured in Bandar Abbas.

Brent Crude Retreats from $100; UN Urges Evacuation of 6,000 Stranded Seafarers

After Brent crude surged 7% to break above $100 on Thursday following Houthi attacks on two Saudi oil tankers, prices retreated about 3% to around $97 on Friday. Only one tanker successfully transited the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, the lowest level since May 7. UN human rights chief Volker Turk urged all conflict parties to help evacuate and repatriate approximately 6,000 seafarers stranded on vessels in the strait, stressing that critical supplies must be delivered. Germany's defense ministry announced it would withdraw two naval vessels from the Red Sea, concluding they are no longer required for a potential mine-clearing mission in the Strait of Hormuz.

II. Israel Announces Major West Bank Military Operation

The Israel Defense Forces announced Friday it was preparing to launch a "large-scale counterterrorism operation" in the occupied West Bank following deadly clashes near Nablus in which two Israelis and four Palestinians were killed. The military completed a "targeted operation" at a Nablus hospital, arresting two wounded Palestinians, and canceled all soldier leave across the West Bank. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz issued a five-point directive: demolish the attacker's home; conduct intensive operations in "terror hotbed" villages including weapons seizures and work permit revocations; reinforce IDF forces throughout the West Bank; establish new checkpoints; and accelerate the legalization of settler farms and establishment of new outposts. According to the Palestinian health ministry, 87 Palestinians have been killed in the West Bank in 2026.

III. EU Agrees 21st Round of Russia Sanctions

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen announced Thursday that EU member states had reached a political agreement on the 21st package of sanctions against Russia. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas called it the "largest sanctions package in four years," targeting 218 entities and individuals, over 100 banks and crypto operators, 40+ shadow fleet vessels, and multiple oil refineries in Russia and Belarus. For the first time, the EU will sanction vessels assisting Russia's shadow fleet and suspend the oil price cap dynamic mechanism for one year. Russia's permanent mission to the EU responded that the restrictions would further worsen the EU's own socioeconomic problems and that Moscow would respond "effectively and adequately." Notably, the final package was significantly weakened from the European Commission's original proposal.

IV. Ukraine-Russia War: Massive Ukrainian Drone Attack, Russian Missile Hits Kyiv Arms Exhibition

The Ukraine-Russia front saw significant escalation on Friday. Ukraine launched one of its largest drone attacks of the year overnight Thursday-Friday, with Russia's defense ministry claiming to have shot down 571 drones across 15 regions, Crimea, and the Black Sea. Targets included: Wildberries logistics centers near St. Petersburg catching fire (the fifth such attack in a week, with cumulative losses estimated at $4.2 billion), the Avitek missile manufacturing plant in Kirov hit by a Ukrainian Flamingo cruise missile (confirmed by President Zelensky as a strike on a "key Russian military enterprise"), and a Wildberries sorting center in occupied Simferopol. St. Petersburg's Pulkovo Airport reported approximately 50 flight delays.

In a rare daytime attack, Russian ballistic missiles struck a defense industry exhibition in Kyiv on Friday, killing at least 10 people and wounding more than 100. Zelensky denounced it as a "deliberate strike against representatives of Ukraine's defense industry." Meanwhile, Zelensky announced an agreement with US defense contractor Raytheon to jointly produce Patriot air defense missiles. On the diplomatic front, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio met with Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov on the sidelines of the ASEAN Foreign Ministers' Meeting in Manila to discuss Ukraine. Zelensky is planning a visit to Washington next week for talks with President Trump.

V. NATO Chief Warns of Russia-China-Iran-North Korea Axis

NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte told Anadolu Agency that while Russia remains NATO's biggest threat, Moscow is now acting in coordination with China, Iran, and North Korea. Rutte warned that China could possess 1,000 nuclear warheads by 2030, saying "we must not be naive about China." He stressed that NATO must rapidly increase defense industrial production capacity and armed forces personnel. Separately, a NATO official warned that the alliance "will defend Bulgaria against any action by Iran" after Sofia moved toward allowing US aerial refueling aircraft to support Middle East operations.

VI. Romanian F-16 Shoots Down Drone Violating Airspace

Romanian President Nicusor Dan confirmed Friday that a Romanian F-16 fighter jet shot down a drone that breached the country's airspace at approximately 11:00 a.m. local time. The president said the area was uninhabited, allowing the pilot to engage without risk. This is the latest in a series of drone incursions into NATO member airspace amid the widening Middle East conflict.
