⚡ July 24 Tech Roundup: ByteDance Launches China's First AI IDE, Stripe to Acquire OpenRouter for $10B, Tencent Restructures AI R&D, ChatGPT Image Gen Goes Free

🚀 ByteDance Releases China's First Native AI IDE: Trae

On July 24, ByteDance officially launched Trae domestic edition — touted as "China's first AI-native integrated development environment (AI IDE)." The tool comes equipped with the Doubao-1.5-pro model and supports switching to the full-capability versions of DeepSeek R1 and V3, marking a significant milestone for AI-powered coding tools in China.

Positioned as an "intelligent collaborative AI IDE," Trae's core features include code completion, code understanding, bug fixing, and natural-language-to-code generation. The standout feature is its new Builder mode: developers describe requirements in natural language, and Trae automatically builds the project framework, iteratively refining it to produce production-ready code. This dramatically lowers the programming barrier, enabling even non-professional developers to participate in application development.

Trae was initially launched in overseas markets with GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet built in. The domestic edition has been optimized for Chinese developers' usage patterns and will soon support custom model integration, allowing users to connect their preferred large model APIs. The IDE is currently available on macOS (10.15+) and Windows (10/11), with a Linux version in development. This launch signals ByteDance's aggressive entry into the AI development tools space, going head-to-head with GitHub Copilot, Cursor, and other international products.

💰 Stripe in Talks to Acquire AI Model Hub OpenRouter for ~$10 Billion

According to a Wall Street Journal report on July 24, fintech giant Stripe is in discussions to acquire OpenRouter, an AI model aggregation platform, in a deal that could value the startup at approximately $10 billion. Multiple other large tech companies are also eyeing the acquisition, and while a deal could be announced soon, negotiations remain subject to change.

Founded in 2023 by former OpenSea founder Alex Atallah, OpenRouter acts as a "model router" — an aggregation platform connecting over 400 large language models, including OpenAI's GPT series, Anthropic's Claude series, and open-source models like DeepSeek and Kimi. Developers and enterprises can access all models through a single API, comparing and switching between providers without individual integrations. In its most recent funding round in May, OpenRouter was valued at just $1.3 billion — meaning its valuation has jumped nearly 8x in under three months.

While the Stripe-OpenRouter deal may seem like a cross-industry play, it aligns with Stripe's strategic expansion into AI infrastructure. OpenAI chose Stripe to handle ChatGPT Plus subscriptions back in 2023; today, every company on the Forbes AI 50 list that accepts online payments runs on Stripe, including Anthropic, Midjourney, and Cohere. At its Stripe Sessions conference in April, the company launched 288 products, nearly all focused on building economic infrastructure for AI — including Link Wallet for AI agents, streaming payments for real-time token billing, and the MPP protocol for machine-to-machine micropayments. Co-founder and CEO Patrick Collison has predicted that AI agents will dominate the majority of online transactions in the future.

🏢 Tencent Merges Multimodal and LLM Teams into Unified Foundation Model Department

On July 23, Tencent announced the merger of its Hunyuan multimodal model division and large language model division into a newly established Foundation Model Department, under the unified leadership of Tencent's Chief AI Scientist Yao Shunyu. The reorganization aims to break down silos between different technical directions, reduce cross-team coordination costs, and concentrate resources on exploring the upper limits of full-modal AI intelligence.

According to an organizational restructuring notice from Tencent's Technology Engineering Group (TEG), the new department will focus on frontier technology exploration and foundational model R&D, including model architecture, data and training strategies, reinforcement learning, and agent capabilities. It will also build an AI PaaS middleware layer to empower product innovation and business applications both inside and outside the company.

The previous LLM and multimodal divisions have been dissolved, with their teams and operations consolidated under Yao Shunyu, who reports to TEG President Lu Shan. This restructuring follows a pattern: in December 2025, Yao was appointed Chief AI Scientist in the CEO/President's Office; in March 2026, the nearly decade-old Tencent AI Lab was dissolved and its core researchers absorbed into the Hunyuan team. Now, by bringing multimodal and language R&D under one roof, Tencent is signaling its intent to converge conversational AI with visual, audio, and video generation capabilities into a unified technical foundation.

Industry analysts note that as Chinese AI competition enters its "full-modal, real-world application" phase, Tencent's restructuring sends a clear signal of its commitment to concentrate resources on foundational model development and compete with global leaders.

🖼️ ChatGPT Native Image Generation Rolls Out to Free Users

OpenAI has rolled out native AI image generation in ChatGPT to all free users. The feature, previously limited to Plus, Pro, and Team subscribers, has been highly popular since its launch on March 26, with its "Ghibli-style" image generation going viral on social media globally.

The feature's popularity has put significant strain on OpenAI's GPU resources. CEO Sam Altman noted on X that GPUs were "melting" from the heavy usage, prompting temporary rate limits while the company works on efficiency improvements. Free-tier ChatGPT users will receive 3 image generations per day.

However, the feature has also sparked copyright controversy. Studio Ghibli fans and supporters of filmmaker Hayao Miyazaki have criticized the AI's imitation of Ghibli's distinctive art style as borderline plagiarism. Miyazaki, in an old interview clip, stated: "I feel strongly disgusted. I would never use this technology in my work. I strongly believe this is an insult to life itself." OpenAI has not formally responded to these concerns.

The expansion to free users signals OpenAI's intent to grow its user base and model usage while deepening its commitment to AI image generation. Analysts believe that image generation capabilities are transitioning from a paid barrier to a universally accessible tool, accelerating the democratization of AI-powered creative tools.
