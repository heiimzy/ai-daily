⚡ July 26 Financial Roundup: Trump Halts Iran Strikes Sending Oil Crashing, Super Week Pitches Fed Against Big Tech Earnings, 'TACO' Meme Takes Over Wall Street, Bond Market Under Worst Stress Since 2007

1. Breaking: Trump Orders Halt to Iran Airstrikes, Oil Plunges 3%+

In a dramatic reversal on July 24, President Donald Trump ordered a halt to 13 consecutive days of U.S. airstrikes on Iran. The decision triggered a violent selloff in oil markets during Asian trading on July 26: WTI crude crashed 3.86% to $86.62/barrel, while Brent crude sank 3.13% to $89.56/barrel, completely unwinding the rally that had briefly pushed prices above $100.

According to Axios and Xinhua, just hours before the halt, an Omani delegation arrived in Tehran to negotiate the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Regional sources say talks are making progress, with a preliminary agreement possible by this weekend. Qatar also announced on July 26 that it would fully resume maritime navigation, ending a weeks-long shipping ban.

White House sources cited two key reasons for the decision: creating space for diplomatic efforts, and a growing recognition that limited airstrikes were unlikely to force Iranian concessions, while a full-scale ground war carried costs Washington was unwilling to bear.

"We're talking to the Iranians right now. I think they're getting more and more serious as the days go by. We're locked and loaded and ready to go, but we're talking to them." — President Trump, July 24

2. 'TACO' — The New Market Meme: Trump Always Chickens Out

A new acronym is making the rounds on Wall Street trading desks: TACO, short for "Trump Always Chickens Out." It reflects the market's growing skepticism toward Trump's repeated military threats that never fully materialize.

Past threats that fizzled include warnings to "bomb Iran back to the Stone Age," targeting Iran's bridges and power plants, and even declaring a dedicated "bridges and power plants week." Traders note that TACO is a key reason oil prices haven't surged even higher during this conflict — the market increasingly discounts Trump's escalation rhetoric.

However, risks remain elevated. Houthi forces (Ansar Allah) launched missiles and drones at Saudi Aramco facilities in Jizan and Yanbu on Sunday, threatening to close the Bab al-Mandeb Strait — which, combined with the Hormuz disruption, could affect over 8 million barrels of daily oil shipments.

3. Super Week: FOMC + Big Tech Earnings + GDP + PCE

This week is arguably the most consequential trading period of 2026. Here's the calendar:

Wednesday July 29 — FOMC Decision + Microsoft & Meta Earnings

The Federal Reserve will announce its rate decision Wednesday. Consensus expects the federal funds rate to remain at 3.50-3.75%, but CME FedWatch数据显示 the probability of a hike has surged from 14% a week ago to 33-38%. This is Chairman Kevin Warsh's second meeting since taking office.

Key things to watch:

  • The hawkish camp: The June dot plot showed 9 of 18 FOMC participants expect at least one hike this year. Dallas Fed's Lorie Logan, Cleveland's Beth Hammack, and Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari have all signaled openness to moving sooner. Markets anticipate 2-4 possible dissents.
  • Warsh's communication style: The new Chair has already shortened the policy statement and explicitly rejects forward guidance. In congressional testimony, he stated the Committee has "zero tolerance for persistently elevated inflation."
  • Market pricing: All 76 economists surveyed by Bloomberg expect a hold. But Barclays and some traders say a surprise hike can't be ruled out. September is now fully priced for a hike.

After the bell Wednesday, Microsoft (MSFT) and Meta (META) report Q2 earnings. After Alphabet raised its 2026 capex guidance by 8% to $195-205 billion and saw its stock tumble 7.8%, and Tesla plunged 17.8% on margin concerns, the market's scrutiny of AI investment returns has never been sharper.

Thursday July 30 — Q2 GDP + PCE + Apple & Amazon Earnings

On the data front:

  • Q2 GDP (Advance): The Atlanta Fed's GDPNow model estimates just 1.3% growth, while Bloomberg's consensus is 2.5%. That massive gap alone guarantees volatility. Q1 came in at 2.1%.
  • June PCE Inflation: Core PCE expected at +0.17-0.19% MoM, with the YoY rate cooling to 3.3% from 3.4%. Because software prices carry a larger weight in the PCE basket than CPI, core PCE is likely to prove stickier.

After Thursday's close, Apple (AAPL) and Amazon (AMZN) report. Combined, the four reporting mega-caps represent over $10 trillion in market value, roughly 20% of the S&P 500.

Friday July 31 — Bank of Japan + Eurozone CPI

The BoJ decision is expected to hold rates steady, but October hike odds have risen above 80%. Eurozone July CPI is expected to tick back above 3% from June's 2.8%.

4. Bond Market Under Siege: 30-Year Yield Above 5% for Longest Streak Since 2007

The bond market is flashing red. A combination of oil-driven inflation expectations and an avalanche of AI-related corporate debt issuance has triggered a deep selloff in Treasuries:

  • 10-year yield: Hit 4.71%, an 18-month high
  • 30-year yield: Has now closed above 5% for 14 consecutive trading days — the longest streak since the 2007 subprime crisis
  • 2-year yield: Climbed to ~4.35%

Dustin Reid, Chief Fixed Income Strategist at Mackenzie Investments, warned that inflation is the single biggest headwind for long-dated bonds: "If inflation stays elevated for an extended period, investors will demand higher yields as compensation." Alexander Payne at Vanguard added: "Investors will have plenty of opportunities to deploy capital into long-duration bonds at higher yields."

According to Goldman Sachs, AI-related debt issuance has reached $489 billion year-to-date in 2026, up 50% from all of 2025, with 60% coming from non-hyperscaler companies. The six largest tech firms (Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia, Meta, Oracle) now carry a combined debt stock approaching $500 billion.

5. Capital Economics Warns: Foreign Buying Frenzy Signals Risk for S&P 500

In a report published July 26, Capital Economics warned that foreign investors now own more than 21% of the U.S. equity market, up from just 6% in 1997. Chief Economic Adviser John Higgins noted that historically, surges in foreign net purchases of U.S. equities have coincided with powerful S&P 500 rallies — followed by significant corrections.

The firm cited the dot-com bubble, the Global Financial Crisis, and the 2022 market decline as precedents, adding that "the latest increase in overseas purchases has been much larger than during those earlier episodes." Capital Economics attributes the current wave to AI enthusiasm and warns that "if and when the AI bubble bursts, this trend is likely to reverse," potentially leaving U.S. equities underperforming international markets.

7. Outlook: Three Scenarios for the Week Ahead

Scenario 1: Hawkish Hold (Most Likely). The Fed holds rates but strikes a hawkish tone, emphasizing that inflation remains elevated and the Middle East poses upside risks. Markets read this as paving the way for a September hike. Tech stocks remain under pressure while energy and defense benefit. This is the consensus call among Wall Street strategists.

Scenario 2: Surprise Hike (Tail Risk). If Warsh delivers a 25bp hike, risk assets sell off aggressively. Growth stocks get hit hardest, and Microsoft/Meta earnings face unprecedented scrutiny. The dollar strengthens, emerging markets weaken.

Scenario 3: Dovish Hold (Least Likely). The Fed acknowledges cooling inflation and signals the hiking cycle is done. Tech stocks rally sharply, but given Warsh's hawkish reputation, this scenario has the lowest probability.

No matter which scenario plays out, this week is shaping up to be one of the most volatile trading periods of 2026. As Goldman Sachs' trading desk noted: "There are almost no buy orders, and tech earnings have failed to become the stabilizing force many hoped for."
