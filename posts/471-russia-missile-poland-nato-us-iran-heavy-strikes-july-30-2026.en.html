⚡ July 30 World Affairs: Russian Missile Suspected of Entering Polish Airspace — NATO's Article 5 Tested; US Launches Massive Retaliatory Strikes on Iran; Saudi Arabia Joins Combat for the First Time; Kuwait Chinese Facility Hit, 1 Dead

1. Russian Missile Suspected of Breaching Polish Airspace — NATO Confronts Potential Article 5 Scenario

In the early hours of July 30, Russia launched a massive missile and drone attack on Ukraine, firing more than 70 missiles and over 280 attack drones across the country. Air raid sirens wailed across Ukraine as the capital Kyiv, the western city of Lviv, Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, and other regions came under fire. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky had warned the previous day that Russia was preparing a massive strike.

During the assault, Polish military radar detected an unidentified object moving westward inside Polish airspace at 3:40 AM local time. Poland scrambled F-16 fighter jets to intercept, but the object disappeared from radar at 3:46 AM. A Mi-24 helicopter later located a 10-meter-wide (33-foot) crater in an undeveloped area near the village of Tarnawa-Kolonia in Lublin Province, approximately 90 kilometers (55 miles) from the Ukrainian border.

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk told an emergency government meeting: "All indications suggest that it was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, but we want to be 100% certain about the type of missile and who launched it." He confirmed NATO commanders had been briefed. Ukraine's Acting Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha stated directly that a "Russian Kh-101 cruise missile crossed into Poland as part of Russia's massive strike against Ukraine, violating NATO airspace."

Defense Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz revealed that approximately 20 objects were detected near Polish airspace overnight. This incident marks one of the most serious NATO airspace violations since the start of the Russia-Ukraine war, following the November 2022 incident where a Ukrainian air defense missile accidentally landed in Poland, killing two.

Analysts warn that if the missile is confirmed to have deliberately entered NATO territory, it will test the alliance's collective defense pledge under Article 5. Poland previously triggered Article 4 consultations in September 2025 after 19 Russian drones entered its airspace. This more serious incident could further escalate NATO's response posture.

2. US Launches 'Heavy Wave' of Retaliatory Strikes on Iran — IRGC Vows to 'Punish the Aggressor Today'

At 8 PM ET Wednesday (00:00 GMT Thursday), the US Central Command (CENTCOM) announced it had completed a "heavy wave of strikes against Iran" lasting approximately two hours. CENTCOM said its forces struck "dozens of Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) targets in Iran, including military command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and defense sites, and maritime capabilities."

The strikes were a direct retaliation for Iran's ballistic missile attack on a US base in Jordan the previous day. President Donald Trump had promised to hit Iran "very hard," telling Fox News he would "beat the f***ing s*** out of them," and later telling reporters at the White House: "It's our turn to hit them. They know it's coming. They asked us not to do it."

Casualties and damage: Iran's Fars News Agency reported that a US missile struck a residential building on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, killing a couple and their two-year-old child. Two other children aged 7 and 9 were wounded. Explosions were reported across multiple Iranian provinces — including Ahvaz and Abadan in Khuzestan, three cities in Bushehr province, and Kazerun in Fars province — with power outages reported in parts of Ahvaz.

IRGC threatens retaliation: The IRGC announced Thursday that "with the help of God Almighty, the aggressor will be punished today," warning that countries aiding the US would also face a "harsh response." The IRGC also declared it maintains "full control" of the Strait of Hormuz, stating that "a stranger who has come from thousands of kilometers away will not be allowed to interfere."

3. Saudi Arabia Joins US in First Publicly Announced Joint Military Action Against Iranian Proxies in Iraq

Prior to the direct strikes on Iran, the US and Saudi Arabia conducted their first publicly announced joint military operation on July 29 — striking Iranian-backed militia bases across seven provinces in eastern Iraq. This marks the first time Riyadh has publicly participated in military action alongside Washington since the war began on February 28.

Iraq's Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF), a coalition of Shiite Iran-backed armed groups, reported at least 20 fighters killed and 32 wounded, with six Iranian advisers also killed. Saudi Defense Minister Khalid bin Salman subsequently met with US Vice President JD Vance in Washington, urging the Trump administration not to escalate further — warning that attacking Yemen's Houthis or launching additional strikes in Iraq would open the door to "major unknown risks."

4. War Spreads to Egypt and Kuwait

The conflict is expanding at an alarming pace across the Middle East:

  • Kuwait: An Iranian missile struck a building belonging to a Chinese company in northern Kuwait, killing one worker. Kuwait's Defense Ministry confirmed the attack.
  • Egypt: Drone strikes ignited fires on two LNG vessels at Egypt's Damietta port — including a US-owned floating storage facility and a Greek-owned tanker. Egypt's Cabinet confirmed a drone caused the fire but did not assign responsibility. This is the first time Egyptian infrastructure has been directly attacked in the conflict.
  • Jordan: Jordanian air defenses intercepted and destroyed five Iranian missiles targeting the kingdom Thursday morning.

5. Strait of Hormuz Standoff Intensifies, Oil Markets in Turmoil

Iran's deputy foreign minister confirmed Tehran has rejected Oman's 50-50 proposal for joint management of the Strait of Hormuz, insisting the waterway will "never return to its prewar situation." The IRGC announced it had interdicted three oil tankers in the strait. In response, the US Treasury imposed new sanctions on two Iranian firms linked to an IRGC-backed scheme allegedly extorting commercial vessels transiting the waterway.

Brent crude surged 7.3% to above $88 per barrel, while West Texas Intermediate topped $84, as the renewed fighting raised fears of prolonged disruption to global oil supplies. Saudi Arabia simultaneously faces a Houthi naval blockade in the Red Sea, with the Yemeni rebels considering fees on commercial shipping.

The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) estimated this week that the US military has fewer than 1,000 Patriot interceptor missiles and fewer than 250 THAAD interceptors remaining — a dwindling arsenal that raises serious questions about sustainability. Meanwhile, a senior US official acknowledged the administration is not actively pursuing diplomatic talks, saying "any resumption in direct diplomacy was not imminent."

Five months since the US and Israel launched strikes on Iran on February 28 — a war President Trump initially claimed would last "only 4 to 5 weeks" — there appears to be no end in sight.
