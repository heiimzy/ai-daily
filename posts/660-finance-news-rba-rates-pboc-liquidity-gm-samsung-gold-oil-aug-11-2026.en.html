⚡ Aug 11 Finance Update: RBA Holds at 4.35%, PBoC Pauses Liquidity, GM Exits Indiana Battery JV, Gold Holds Above $4,400

Several notable moves surfaced in global financial markets around midday on August 11: Australia's RBA held its key cash rate at 4.35% for a second straight meeting, betting weak housing and rising unemployment will cool inflation; China's PBoC paused short-term liquidity injections, the first such pause since June; GM is reportedly selling its 50% stake in the Indiana battery joint venture to partner Samsung SDI, further shrinking its in-house battery strategy; and with oil and Treasury yields both rebounding and September rate-hike odds back above 50%, gold held above $4,400 an ounce as markets await U.S. July CPI. Details below; all source publication times verified.

1. RBA Holds for a Second Straight Meeting: Cash Rate Steady at 4.35%, Inflation Still Too High

According to Jin Shi Data (reporting at 12:46 Beijing time on Aug 11, citing the RBA statement) and Seeking Alpha (04:48 GMT on Aug 11), the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) held its key cash rate target at 4.35% on Tuesday (Aug 11), a second consecutive hold. Although inflation remains well above target, the board chose to keep policy steady to assess how the situation evolves. The decision matched broad expectations — traders had priced in roughly a 97% chance of a hold before the announcement.

The statement shows the RBA has raised the cash rate target three times since the start of the year, financial conditions are tighter than before, and the economy appears to be slowing "as expected." The bank also warned that disruptions to global oil supply are directly pushing up inflation; while the impact of Middle East conflict has so far been smaller than expected, headline inflation remains too high. The RBA's forecasts point to inflation staying above 2.5% until early 2028, leaving room for further hikes if needed.

Another key consideration is the rapid deterioration of the housing market: prices have begun to fall after three hikes in the first half, and the downturn has accelerated since the federal budget tightened tax breaks for property investors, threatening household consumption and momentum. The RBA said it "will not hesitate to take further action to bring inflation sustainably back to target, including further increases in the cash rate target" if upside risks materialize. Australia's four major banks generally believe the RBA has finished its tightening cycle and will pause before pivoting to easing. Markets were watching Governor Michele Bullock's afternoon press conference for further guidance.

2. PBoC Pauses Short-Term Liquidity Injections: First Time Since June, a Clearer Tightening Signal

According to Seeking Alpha (04:54 GMT on Aug 11) and Jin Shi Data, China's central bank (PBoC) paused short-term liquidity injections on Aug 11 — the first time since June it did not inject short-term funds into the banking system via open-market operations. As a window into China's monetary policy stance, the move was read by the market as a signal of marginally tighter short-term liquidity, and also reflects the central bank's balancing act between defending the exchange rate and managing bond-market risks.

Against a backdrop of fragile domestic demand and external uncertainty, the PBoC had previously used tools such as reverse repos to keep the short end of the funding market relatively loose; the pause means short-end rate volatility could rise, and expectations about liquidity conditions are being adjusted accordingly. Analysts argue the pause is more of a phased operational change than a reversal of the easing cycle — if the recovery loses momentum, the central bank retains tools to add support again. The move stands in sharp contrast to the RBA's hold, underscoring that the "difficult balance" central banks face between inflation and slowing growth remains the core pricing driver in financial markets.

3. GM Reportedly Sells 50% of Indiana Battery JV: Samsung SDI to Take Full Ownership

According to Reuters (04:30 GMT on Aug 11) and Bloomberg (05:00 GMT on Aug 11), GM is reportedly selling its 50% stake in the Indiana (New Carlisle) battery joint venture to partner Samsung SDI, which would give Samsung SDI full ownership of the venture. Yonhap News and MNI Market News further reported that GM and Samsung SDI also signed a new battery development agreement the same day, to jointly develop prismatic EV batteries and extend their strategic partnership; Seeking Alpha (06:08 GMT on Aug 11) followed up under the headline "GM exits Indiana battery JV as Samsung SDI takes full ownership."

The move marks a further retreat from GM's in-house battery strategy: facing slowing EV demand growth and price-war pressure, GM has noticeably slowed aggressive expansion of battery and vehicle capacity, leaning more on partnerships and external supply to control capital spending and focus resources on models and software that can reach profitability sooner. For Samsung SDI, taking sole control of this U.S. plant helps lock in GM as a major customer and expand its share of the North American EV battery market. The parallel moves — selling the stake while continuing to co-develop new batteries — also reflect how intertwined, and simultaneously competitive, automakers and battery makers have become.

4. Gold Holds Above $4,400 Against a "Triple Headwind": Markets Await U.S. July CPI

According to FXStreet (04:50 GMT on Aug 11) and Moneycontrol (01:15 GMT on Aug 11), gold held above $4,400 an ounce despite a "triple headwind" of rebounding oil prices, higher Treasury yields, and markets again pricing a better-than-50% chance of a Fed rate hike in September. Traders are turning their attention to the upcoming U.S. July consumer price index (CPI) — before the data lands, gold is consolidating at high levels with little directional conviction.

FXStreet notes that geopolitical risk (especially around Iran and the Strait of Hormuz) and the inflation data will be the key variables determining gold's next move. Analysts say that if July CPI shows sticky inflation and strengthens expectations of further central-bank tightening, gold could face near-term pullback pressure; conversely, if inflation cools, safe-haven demand and easing expectations could push prices toward a higher platform. Central-bank buying and geopolitical hedging flows continue to underpin the metal, and the tug-of-war around the $4,400 level is building momentum for the next trend.

5. Big Picture: Central-Bank Divergence, Automaker Retrenchment, and the Precious-Metals Game

This hour's finance news runs along three threads: central-bank divergence — the RBA holding for a second straight meeting while the PBoC pauses short-term liquidity for the first time in six weeks, showing major central banks still treading cautiously and at different paces between high inflation and slowing growth; industrial capital in motion — GM selling its battery JV stake and retreating from in-house production, reflecting the EV supply chain's rebalancing under cooling demand and cost pressure; and the precious-metals vs. rates game — gold holding above $4,400 against the triple headwind of oil, yields and rate-hike bets, with markets holding their breath for U.S. July CPI.
