⚡ Aug 3 Finance Update: Oil Dark Pool Crashes Over 6% After Trump Cancels Iran Strikes, Super NFP Week Begins

This weekend, the biggest driver of global financial markets was not an economic data point but a Middle East statement. US President Donald Trump announced late Saturday (Aug 1) that he had cancelled a planned new round of military strikes on Iran, saying the parties had reached an agreed framework - including the immediate, complete and total reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and an end to Iran's nuclear threat. The news sent oil's dark pool into a tailspin: Brent crashed more than 6% to $84.91 a barrel and WTI fell over 5% to $81.94, while Middle East equity markets caught a breather with Saudi Arabia's TASI index closing up 1.1% on Aug 2. Iran, however, quickly denied any deal, calling Trump's claim "a new lie" and reports of a Hormuz reopening "pure rumor." Meanwhile, the US July jobs report lands this Friday (Aug 7) - a super data week plus a busy earnings calendar now sets the stage for a directional test across global assets.

Overnight Flip: Trump Cancels Iran Strikes, Oil Dark Pool Crashes 6%+

According to Xinhua, Trump said in a social media post on Aug 1 that the US was "locked and loaded" against Iran, with a level of military deterrence "not seen since World War II." Nevertheless, he said Iran and other Middle Eastern countries had just asked Washington to hold off any attack because an agreement framework had been reached. Based on that request, Trump said he agreed to cancel the attack - subject to being able to rapidly finalize a deal - and noted that Israel joined him in the commitment. Just days earlier, US media had reported that the military had been ordered to prepare a "most ferocious" bombing campaign against Iranian energy infrastructure over the weekend, possibly lasting 10 to 14 days, aimed at forcing Tehran's capitulation.

Markets priced the de-escalation instantly: as tensions eased, crude's dark pool plunged - Brent crashed more than 6% to $84.91 a barrel and NYMEX crude dropped over 5% to $81.94. Context matters: Brent had closed the week above $90, versus below $72 at the start of the month - a roughly 22% monthly gain that made oil the best-performing major asset class in July. At dark-pool prices, the geopolitical risk premium is being squeezed out fast. Middle East equities rallied in sympathy, with Saudi Arabia's TASI up 1.1%. Analysts point to two forces behind the cancellation: Iran betting that Gulf states battered by the war would lobby Trump to hold back, and the White House facing high political risk from another strike campaign with midterm elections approaching.

Iran Denies Any Deal: Hormuz Reopening Remains the Wild Card

For now, the "deal" is largely a one-sided narrative. Iran's Mehr News Agency on Aug 2 quoted a military official calling Trump's claim that Iran asked to stop the attack "a new lie," adding that Iranian forces remain "at the highest state of readiness, prepared for every possibility." Iran's Fars News Agency, citing sources, said the plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz was pure rumor - no agreement exists, and as long as the US maintains hostile actions, the strait will stay closed, with ships only allowed through published routes and with permission from the IRGC Navy.

"Whether he continues aggression or retreats, our forces are at the highest state of readiness, prepared for everything. If confrontation is unavoidable, the battlefield will decide everything, and everyone will see who holds the power and who has the final say." - Iranian military official

The Strait of Hormuz carries roughly one-fifth of the world's crude and LNG. A US-Iran memorandum of understanding in June briefly restored some traffic before collapsing in early July. The EIA's forecast, built on that June deal, is now clearly outdated; analysts broadly expect the strait to remain constrained until commercial tanker traffic normalizes and both sides publicly endorse the same framework. That means when Asian markets open on Monday (Aug 3), the direction of the oil futures gap will be the market's first vote on how credible this "framework" really is.

Super NFP Week: July Jobs Report, ISM and Big Earnings Crowd the Calendar

Beyond the Middle East, this week's macro calendar is heavy. At 8:30 a.m. ET on Friday (Aug 7), the Bureau of Labor Statistics releases the July Nonfarm Payrolls report. Consensus expects about +91,000 new jobs and the unemployment rate rising to 4.3% from 4.2% - versus just +57,000 in June, a clear slowdown. TradingKey notes that if both payrolls and wage growth beat expectations, it would strengthen the case for further Fed tightening, lifting Treasury yields and the dollar while pressuring richly valued tech and gold; a big miss would cut hike bets, though recession fears would also cap equity upside.

Leading indicators arrive first: July ISM Manufacturing PMI on Monday (Aug 3) at 10:00 a.m. ET (consensus ~54.0 vs 53.3 prior), JOLTS job openings and factory orders on Tuesday (Aug 4), ADP private payrolls and ISM Services PMI on Wednesday (Aug 5), and Challenger layoffs plus jobless claims on Thursday (Aug 6). In money markets, the Fed on July 30 held rates at 3.50%-3.75% for a fifth straight meeting, but with three rare dissents favoring a hike; the probability of a September hike has fallen from fully priced-in before the meeting to roughly 68%, and the "expectations gap" in NFP and ISM will decide the next move. Earnings season is also busy: Palantir (Monday), AMD and SpaceX (Tuesday), SanDisk, Western Digital, Caterpillar, Disney and Uber are among the names reporting, testing AI demand and commercial-space valuations.

Bitcoin Holds ~$63K: Waiting for a Macro Catalyst

Crypto chose to sit on its hands. Over the past day, Bitcoin traded in a narrow $62,500-$63,100 range, down about 2.5% since July 26, and still roughly 50% below its record high of $126,080 set on October 6, 2025. Sentiment is already fragile: this week's Coldcard hardware-wallet vulnerability, weekend ETF outflows, and controversy around two August forks have traders selling strength rather than chasing. Bitcoin News argues the crypto-macro link runs through inflation - if oil stays elevated on Hormuz uncertainty, it complicates the Fed's rate path, the very variable digital assets are most sensitive to. The next key checkpoint: US July CPI on August 12.
