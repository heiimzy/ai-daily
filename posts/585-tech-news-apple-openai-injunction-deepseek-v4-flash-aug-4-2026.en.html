⚡ Aug 4 Tech Update: Apple Seeks Preliminary Injunction Against OpenAI as the Trade Secrets Battle Escalates; DeepSeek V4 Flash Recovers After an API Capacity Crunch

On the afternoon of August 4, two stories arrived from opposite corners of tech: Apple asked a U.S. judge for a preliminary injunction barring two former employees and OpenAI from accessing, using or disclosing alleged confidential information in its trade secrets case, prompting a lengthy rebuttal from OpenAI insisting Apple has it wrong; meanwhile, DeepSeek-V4-Flash hit an API capacity crunch from "unprecedented" traffic before its official confirmation that the issue was resolved. Here is the roundup.

1. Apple Seeks a Preliminary Injunction Against OpenAI in Its Trade Secrets Case

According to Reuters (republished via MarketScreener at 13:55 Beijing time on Aug 4) and Gelonghui (14:07), Apple on Monday (Aug 3, U.S. time) asked a U.S. judge for a preliminary injunction barring two former employees and OpenAI from accessing, acquiring, using or disclosing alleged confidential information as it moves ahead with its trade secrets lawsuit. Apple sued OpenAI and the two former employees — both now working at OpenAI — on July 10, alleging misappropriation of its trade secrets to benefit the ChatGPT-owner's push into consumer hardware.

Per the Reuters-cited filings, Apple also filed a concurrent motion on Monday seeking expedited discovery, including the production of documents relating to the defendants' alleged access to Apple's proprietary and trade secret information. Apple asked the judge to order depositions of the two named defendants — former Apple senior systems electrical engineer Chang Liu and former Apple vice president of product design for iPhone and Apple Watch Tang Yew Tan — as well as OpenAI employee Yu-Ting Peng and an unnamed OpenAI employee who previously worked at Apple, plus corporate representatives of OpenAI and io Products. "Apple will be irreparably harmed absent a preliminary injunction," the company said in its filing.

2. OpenAI Fires Back: "Apple Is Getting This Wrong" — the Injunction Is Unnecessary

Reuters noted that OpenAI responded in a blog post late Monday: "Apple's request for a preliminary injunction is both based on false information and completely unnecessary because we do not have, nor want, any of their trade secrets." According to 9to5Mac (Aug 4), OpenAI's post — titled "Apple is getting this wrong" — goes into detail: it says Apple's outside lawyers emailed the wrong person after confusing two Asian last names, that a claimed conversation with OpenAI's general counsel "never happened," and that Apple shifts blame to "residual access" while hiding its own poor management of departing employees' system access. OpenAI also defended Tang Yew Tan, noting he served Apple for more than 24 years and was among its most innovative leaders, and that the team "do not want, and must not use, any confidential information from other companies."

The underlying dispute centers on OpenAI's hardware ambitions: in 2025 OpenAI acquired io Products, the hardware startup co-founded by former Apple design chief Jony Ive, for about $6.5 billion, and brought former Apple iPhone design chief Tang Yew Tan aboard as chief hardware officer. Analysts widely believe OpenAI is developing its own AI device — possibly a phone or another hardware form factor — that could steer consumers away from Apple's best-selling iPhone, which is why Apple is pursuing the case and the injunction so aggressively. Separately, The Information (via ifanr/36Kr, Aug 4 morning) reported that some former Apple employees say confidential documents lingered in their personal iCloud accounts and were "accidentally" synced rather than deliberately downloaded; Apple responded that the conduct alleged in the complaint is unrelated to iCloud, adding another wrinkle to the case.

3. DeepSeek V4 Flash's API Hits a Capacity Crunch From Record Traffic — and Recovers

According to Gelonghui (citing Yicai/OpenCode, around 13:30 on Aug 4), OpenCode — the overseas open-source AI coding agent platform — posted that DeepSeek-V4-Flash was experiencing capacity shortages due to "unprecedented" access volume, with users likely to hit errors while the team worked on urgent fixes. Some users reported the official API was nearly unusable in the morning, comparing it to the launch-day load on Kimi K3 — a sign that Chinese models have caught up in capability, but that availability and capacity still need work.

DeepSeek's official account then confirmed: the V4-Flash API experienced degraded performance this morning and the issue has now been resolved, with service restored. The "happy problem" tracks V4-Flash's recent surge in popularity: per the latest OpenRouter data, for the week of July 28 – August 3, DeepSeek-V4-Flash topped the global LLM call-volume leaderboard with roughly 7.1 trillion tokens, logging about 1.02 trillion tokens on Aug 3 alone; total global LLM call volume was about 56.8 trillion tokens, with Chinese models at 28.13 trillion — a 14th consecutive week ahead of the U.S. The cost-efficient "small cup" model is now living through a genuine stress test from overwhelming traffic.

From Apple and OpenAI trading blows over AI-hardware trade secrets, to DeepSeek-V4-Flash weathering a capacity storm after topping global call volume — Aug 4 gave big-company drama and open-source-AI reality checks in equal measure. The former is a fight over who controls the next generation of AI devices; the latter is proof that Chinese foundation models have reached the center of the global stage, with engineering reliability now the next test.
