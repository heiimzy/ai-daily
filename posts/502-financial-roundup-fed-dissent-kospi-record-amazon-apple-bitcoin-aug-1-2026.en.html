⚡ Aug 1 Financial Roundup: Three Fed Officials Push for Rate Hikes, Korea's KOSPI Soars 18% in Record Rally, Amazon Jumps 15% While Apple Loses $372.7B, Bitcoin Hits 3-Week Low

Federal Reserve: Three Officials Publicly Demand an Immediate Rate Hike as Divisions Deepen

Divisions inside the Federal Reserve over the path of interest rates burst into the open on Friday (July 31). Reuters (15:24 GMT) reported that three officials who dissented against the July meeting's decision to hold rates steady have now publicly argued that an immediate hike is needed to curb inflation; Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack is among them, according to Crain's Cleveland Business (12:31 GMT). Bloomberg (16:33 GMT) followed up, noting the hawks believe inflation remains too high and that delaying action only makes policy more reactive. Richmond Fed President Thomas Barkin told The Wall Street Journal on Friday that it is a "close call" whether rates are high enough, and voiced skepticism that the labor market has genuinely strengthened (WSJ exclusive, 16:49 GMT; Sina Finance, 16:54 GMT). Markets are voting with their money: Kalshi prediction markets now price a 59% chance of a September rate hike (The Crypto Times, 16:01 GMT), the 30-year Treasury yield hit its highest level since 2007 on inflation concerns (Crypto Briefing, 16:07 GMT), and the cost of hedging a deeper bond selloff is soaring (Bloomberg, 15:56 GMT). Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's first major press conference also left markets puzzled over how the central bank actually measures inflation (Fortune, 16:01 GMT).

South Korea: KOSPI Soars About 18% in the Biggest Single-Day Gain in Its History

Korean stocks delivered a historic session on Friday. The benchmark KOSPI index surged roughly 18% to settle near 6,590, the largest one-day gain since the index was created (Businesskorea closing report; The Korea Times: "KOSPI rockets 18% in record-breaking rally"). The memory duopoly led the charge: SK Hynix jumped about 30% and Samsung Electronics rose nearly 30% (KED Global, 12:20 GMT; Barron's, 13:53 GMT). The week was a wild ride for the index — after back-to-back circuit breakers and panic selling on July 29, the KOSPI violently rebounded once Microsoft's earnings calmed AI-bubble fears (Tech Times; Startup Fortune). Foreign buying was the main driver of the rebound. Barron's cautions, however, that a 30% single-day surge in SK Hynix is mixed up with leveraged trading and retail sentiment, and the index's heavy dependence on a handful of AI memory names leaves structural risks on the table.

US Stocks: S&P 500 Heads for First July Decline Since 2014 as Amazon and Apple Go in Opposite Directions

US stocks closed a choppy session on Friday, ending a July defined by divergence: the S&P 500 is on track for its first July decline since 2014 (MarketWatch, 16:38 GMT). Mega-cap earnings went in opposite directions — Amazon's Q2 cloud and AI results blew past expectations, sending shares up as much as 15.2% intraday, the biggest single-day gain since 2012 (Sohu Finance, 15:05 GMT), with AWS's AI growth described as putting Amazon "at the top of the cloud wars" (Investor's Business Daily, 17:03 GMT); Apple, by contrast, plunged more than 9% on supply-shortage warnings, erasing roughly $372.7 billion in market value — the largest one-day market-cap loss in US stock market history (Sohu Finance, 15:26 GMT; CNBC reported Apple down 9% and Amazon up 14%), with reports pointing to supply constraints hurting its outlook (East Bay Times). Elsewhere, Roblox tumbled nearly 29% after disappointing earnings (Business Upturn, 16:25 GMT), and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gave back a 5% intraday gain to turn negative as SanDisk, Micron and others faded (Sina Finance, 15:48 GMT). Reuters (16:29 GMT) noted that rate uncertainty offset Amazon's boost, leaving US stocks without a clear direction.

Bitcoin: Drops Below $63,000 to a Three-Week Low; Over 94,000 Traders Liquidated

Crypto markets came under pressure on Friday: bitcoin fell below $63,000, sliding to a three-week low (Forbes, 16:25 GMT; Moomoo reported "bitcoin falls below $63,000" at 15:43 GMT). Volatile AI stocks dragged on crypto sentiment (Crypto Briefing, 16:31 GMT), and more than 94,000 traders were liquidated over 24 hours (Crypto Briefing, 16:39 GMT). The contrast worth noting: institutions are still buying the dip. US spot bitcoin ETFs saw $233 million in net inflows, with BlackRock's IBIT contributing about $183 million — nearly 80% of the total (Bitcoin News, 16:47 GMT). Meanwhile Strategy, the bitcoin treasury heavyweight, announced plans for a $5 billion raise to keep accumulating after earnings (Pluang, 16:51 GMT), with CEO Michael Saylor listing AI's "capital suction" as one of five headwinds facing bitcoin (TradingView, 16:32 GMT).

Market Wrap: Brazil's B3 Halted Mid-Session, US Hints at Yen Intervention, New York Sues Kalshi, Taiwan Q2 Grows Nearly 13%

Brazil's B3 exchange suffered a widespread trading halt at Friday's open before resuming (Bloomberg, 16:10 GMT). In FX, sources said the US Treasury has informed banks it may intervene if the yen moves too far (Reuters, 16:14 GMT). In regulation, New York's attorney general sued prediction-market platform Kalshi, alleging it operates an illegal gambling business (The Seattle Times, 17:01 GMT). Asian data impressed: Taiwan's Q2 GDP grew nearly 13% year on year, powered by AI demand (ASHARQ Al-Awsat, 15:44 GMT); in Europe, the FTSE 100 posted its biggest monthly rise since February (Reuters, 16:26 GMT). Overall, policy divisions, geopolitical risk and crowded AI trades are feeding a visible pickup in global volatility — traders are bracing for an "August storm," with Goldman Sachs positioning for a volatility rebound (hstong, 15:20 GMT).
