⚡ July 31 Tech Roundup: Anthropic Drops Claude Opus 4.7 Tops SWE-bench, Google Ships Gemini 3.6 Flash & 3.5 Flash-Lite, SpaceX Starship V3 Flies for the First Time

Anthropic's Midnight Surprise: Claude Opus 4.7 Goes Live

In the early hours of July 31, Anthropic quietly released Claude Opus 4.7 — no press conference, no trailer, just a brief update on its official blog. But benchmarking data and developer reaction quickly turned this "silent launch" into the day's biggest AI headline.

Claude Opus 4.7 achieved an 82.3% score on SWE-bench Verified, substantially surpassing Opus 4.5's 77.2% and leaving OpenAI's GPT-5 at 74% behind. Anthropic's internal testing shows the model can autonomously execute programming tasks for over 7 continuous hours without human intervention — no drift, no crashes, no hallucination spirals.

On the agent front, Opus 4.7's Computer Use capability saw a qualitative leap. It can now smoothly open browsers, fill forms, compare data, download files, compile spreadsheets, and send emails as an end-to-end workflow. One developer tasked it with opening 10 e-commerce pages, comparing prices and reviews, and compiling results into an Excel sheet — it finished in 11 minutes.

The model supports a genuinely usable 2 million token context window, maintaining 99.4% recall on Needle-in-a-Haystack tests. Anthropic reports the model's factual error rate dropped approximately 38% compared to Opus 4.5, with the model now frequently volunteering "I'm not sure" or "this needs verification" — a sign of maturity rather than weakness.

However, API pricing remains unchanged, and multimodal capabilities saw little improvement — Anthropic clearly prioritized reasoning and agent scenarios this cycle.

Google Unveils Gemini 3.6 Flash and 3.5 Flash-Lite: Efficiency First

Nearly simultaneously with Anthropic, Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite in the early hours of July 31, while announcing that Gemini 4 pre-training has already commenced. Notably, the much-anticipated Gemini 3.5 Pro remains delayed due to coding capabilities not meeting internal benchmarks.

Gemini 3.6 Flash is positioned as a "default workhorse model" suited for coding, knowledge processing, multimodal tasks, and AI agents. Official data shows 17% lower output token usage compared to Gemini 3.5 Flash, with up to 65% token reduction in certain agent workflows. Output pricing has also decreased, and overall performance in code generation, multimodal understanding, and knowledge tasks exceeds the previous generation.

Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite targets high-throughput scenarios with generation speeds reaching 350 output tokens per second at extremely low cost. It is designed for document processing, search, classification, batch inference, and OCR. For SaaS products, AI office tools, and chatbot services, Flash-Lite is likely to become the new default choice.

This release signals a strategic shift from "parameter competition" to "efficiency competition." The market is no longer asking "who scores highest on benchmarks" but rather "how many requests per day," "what's the token cost," and "is latency low enough for agents." Google has also confirmed that Gemini 4 training is already underway.

SpaceX Starship V3 Completes First Flight Despite Engine Anomaly

On Friday, July 31, SpaceX launched its 12th Starship from Boca Chica, Texas — the first flight of the V3 version of both the Starship upper stage and Super Heavy booster, as well as the inaugural launch from a newly designed launch mount built for the more powerful rocket.

The 40+ story vehicle lifted off at 5:30 PM Central Time. The Super Heavy booster successfully landed in the Gulf of Mexico approximately 6 minutes after launch. The Starship upper stage, despite losing one of its six engines, reached suborbital cruise and executed a controlled landing in the Indian Ocean roughly an hour later. During the flight, the payload deployment mechanism released 20 simulated Starlink satellites and two real satellites to scan the thermal protection shield during descent.

This test flight carries enormous significance for SpaceX. The company has invested over $15 billion in Starship development, and its highly anticipated IPO — expected to value the company at up to $1.75 trillion — is just three weeks away. Starship's success is critical for SpaceX's Starlink satellite internet business and orbital data center plans, both of which depend on Starship's massive payload capacity.

The V3 upgrade features a complete redesign of Super Heavy's 33 Raptor engines for greater thrust through weight reduction, along with optimized upper stage propulsion for long-duration missions including in-orbit refueling capabilities — a key technical path for NASA's Artemis lunar program.

Industry Analysis: Three Tech Giants, One Day, Converging on Practical AI and Space

Anthropic, Google, and SpaceX all announced major product updates within hours of each other, reflecting the intensifying competition across the tech landscape. The common thread is a focus on practical utility over spectacle — Anthropic highlights 7-hour continuous coding, Google emphasizes token efficiency and cost reduction, and SpaceX demonstrates Starship's commercial readiness.

In the AI model space, the market is shifting from "benchmark racing" to "deployment competition." Claude Opus 4.7 and Gemini 3.6 Flash represent two distinct paths: Anthropic pursues deep reasoning and agent capabilities, while Google pursues low cost and high efficiency. Both are trying to answer the same question — how AI transitions from "a chatbot" to "a collaborative productivity partner."

In space, SpaceX is attempting to slash the cost of human space activity by an order of magnitude. The V3's successful first flight means Starship is one step closer to commercial operations — with implications far beyond launch services, from global satellite internet to orbital manufacturing and lunar exploration.
