⚡ July 28 Geopolitical Update: US-Iran Reach 60-Day MoU Framework to Reopen Strait of Hormuz; Trump Slashes Ukraine Peace Deadline to 10-12 Days; Netanyahu Secretly Departs for Washington; Putin Declares 'Fateful Moment' for Russia

1. Breakthrough in US-Iran Talks: 60-Day MoU Framework Agreed, Strait of Hormuz to Reopen

In a major diplomatic development, US and Iranian negotiators have reached a framework agreement on a 60-day memorandum of understanding (MoU) that would extend the ceasefire and launch comprehensive negotiations on Iran's nuclear program, CCTV reported early Tuesday, citing US officials. The agreement still requires final approval from President Donald Trump.

US officials said the terms were largely finalized by July 26, but both sides still need high-level sign-off. Iran has indicated it has received the necessary authorization and is prepared to sign, though Iranian officials have not yet confirmed this. Negotiators have briefed Trump on the final terms, but the president has not immediately approved them, saying he "needs a few days to consider."

Key Provisions of the Draft MoU

  • Strait of Hormuz passage: Unrestricted shipping through the strait with no tolls or interference. Iran must clear all naval mines within 30 days.
  • Naval blockade: The US will gradually lift its maritime blockade of Iranian ports, restoring normal commercial traffic.
  • Nuclear non-proliferation: Iran commits not to develop nuclear weapons. The 60-day negotiation period will prioritize disposal of Iran's highly enriched uranium and enrichment activities.
  • Sanctions relief: The US will discuss sanctions relief, unfreezing Iranian overseas assets, and establishing mechanisms for humanitarian aid access.

This breakthrough comes on the third day of a US pause in bombing operations against Iran. Over the preceding 13 nights, the US struck more than 13,000 targets across Iran, while Iran retaliated with ballistic missiles and drones against US bases in Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, and Iraq. The Pentagon has confirmed at least 14 US service members killed and 482 wounded in the conflict.

Speaking to Axios on Sunday, Trump revealed that Iran had "asked very nicely" for a pause in strikes and a meeting. But he warned that if diplomatic efforts fail, the US would resume "very powerful military action." The White House stressed that the US military buildup in the Middle East continues and readiness has not been reduced.

"We're having very deep talks with Iran. If it doesn't work out, we go back to very powerful military action. There's not a lot of time — it'll either get done quickly, or it won't get done at all." — Trump to Axios

2. Trump Tightens Screws on Russia: Ukraine Peace Deadline Cut from 50 to 10-12 Days

President Trump announced Monday that he is dramatically shortening the 50-day deadline he gave Russian President Vladimir Putin for a Ukraine peace deal to just "10 or 12 days," signaling mounting frustration with the Kremlin leader. The announcement came during Trump's meeting with UK Prime Minister Keir Starmer at his Turnberry golf resort in Scotland.

"We thought we had that settled numerous times, and then President Putin goes out and starts launching rockets into some city like Kyiv and kills a lot of people in a nursing home," Trump said at a joint press conference. "So I'm going to reduce that 50 days that I gave him to a lesser number because I think I already know the answer — what is going to happen."

Trump said the new 10- to 12-day deadline begins July 28. "There's no reason in waiting. It's 50 days, I wanted to be generous, but we just don't see any progress being made." Asked whether he still wanted to meet Putin, Trump replied: "I'm not so interested in talking anymore. He talks with such nice conversations, such respectful and nice conversations, and then people died the following night."

The original 50-day ultimatum, issued July 14, threatened "severe tariffs" on Russia and secondary sanctions on buyers of Russian oil unless Moscow agreed to peace terms. Dmitry Medvedev, deputy chairman of Russia's Security Council, warned that the move could push Moscow and Washington closer to direct conflict.

According to Reuters, AP, BBC and multiple other outlets, Trump made the announcement after meeting with Starmer and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. Ukrainian presidential chief of staff Andriy Yermak praised Trump's stance, saying "Putin respects only power."

3. Netanyahu Secretly Departs for Washington Amid Iran Security Concerns

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu left Israel under a cloak of secrecy Sunday night for a White House meeting with President Trump, Israeli media reported. Citing security concerns stemming from Iran, Channel 12 revealed that extraordinary precautions were taken for the trip — Netanyahu's first foreign visit in five months.

According to the report, Netanyahu's plane took off from the Nevatim Military Air Base near Beersheba rather than from Ben-Gurion International Airport in Tel Aviv — a security measure against potential Iranian threats. The departure time and location were kept confidential, contrary to standard procedures. No media personnel accompanied the prime minister on the flight, and the Prime Minister's Office issued no statement about the departure.

A pre-recorded video statement by Netanyahu was released with a delay. In it, he said the White House meeting would cover all agenda items, including Iran. The timing is particularly significant: Trump is scheduled to meet both Netanyahu and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky on the same day, highlighting the deep interconnection between the Middle East and Eastern European conflict theaters.

Israel has growing concerns about any US-Iran deal that might compromise its security interests. Netanyahu's coalition government includes far-right factions calling for more aggressive action against Iran, and reports indicate Israeli intelligence believes Iran has moved thousands of centrifuges to the underground Pickaxe Mountain facility.

4. Putin Addresses United Russia Congress: Russia at a 'Fateful Moment'

Russian President Vladimir Putin told the United Russia party congress on Tuesday that the country is at a "fateful moment," asserting that the West cannot defeat Russia on the battlefield and therefore is attempting to destabilize Russia's domestic political situation — but will fail.

"Russia will firmly defend its fundamental interests," Putin said. He acknowledged that Russia has adjusted some development goals in response to changing circumstances but insisted that all strategic plans will be fully realized. The security of the country and its citizens, as well as the inviolability of Russia's borders, will be guaranteed.

Putin also stated that State Duma elections will be held within the prescribed timeframe and in strict accordance with the law. He said he would task relevant agencies with ensuring the security of the vote and preventing external manipulation attempts.

On the same day, President Zelensky announced that Ukrainian forces had struck two Russian oil refineries in a long-range precision operation — the Slavyansky refinery in Krasnodar Krai (about 300 km from the front line) and another refinery in Yaroslavl Oblast. "We will continue operations to weaken Russia's ability to wage war," Zelensky said.

The Ukrainian Air Force reported that Russia launched 8 missiles and 142 drones at Ukraine overnight July 27-28, with Ukrainian air defense and electronic warfare units intercepting 7 missiles and 125 drones. Kyiv's military administration said the attack injured two people. Russia, meanwhile, claimed that Ukrainian forces had launched more than 60 drone attacks on the Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Power Plant and the surrounding city in the past 24 hours.

5. Market Reaction: Brent Crude Plunges 9% as Diplomatic Hopes Rise

The US-Iran diplomatic pause triggered a sharp sell-off in oil markets. Brent crude futures fell 8.7% to settle at $88.36 per barrel — the lowest close since July 17 — while US WTI crude dropped 7.5% to $82.61 per barrel.

The decline came after Brent briefly surged above $100 last week as fears of a full-scale conflict and the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz gripped markets. The MoU framework raises prospects for reopening the critical waterway, through which about 20% of global oil passes.

However, analysts caution that the situation remains highly fragile. Trump has not yet approved the MoU, and Iran has not officially confirmed the framework. The strait's current transit volume has collapsed from 57 vessels per day before the crisis to just 3. Even if a deal is signed, restoring normal operations will take weeks. The International Energy Agency (IEA) previously warned that a prolonged closure of the strait could create a supply gap of 17 million barrels per day — 17% of global demand.

Outlook: A Geopolitical Super Tuesday

July 28 is shaping up as a geopolitical super-Tuesday: Trump meets Netanyahu and Zelensky in back-to-back White House sessions; the US-Iran MoU awaits Trump's final decision; and the clock starts ticking on the new 10-12 day deadline for Russia. Three major diplomatic tracks converging on a single day means the next 48 hours will be pivotal in determining the direction of global geopolitics.
