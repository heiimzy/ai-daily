Breaking: US and Israel Plan Joint Strikes on Iran's Energy Sites — Bombing Could Last Through the Weekend

The US-Iran conflict is escalating sharply again. At 21:20 GMT, The Times of Israel's live blog cited CBS News reporting that the United States and Israel are planning a bombing campaign against energy infrastructure targets inside Iran, with strikes possible throughout the weekend. Citing sources, the report said the two sides discussed wrapping up the strikes before financial markets open on Monday over concerns about economic fallout, though no endpoint was agreed; CBS also noted that President Trump has yet to give the final go-ahead. Just 35 minutes later (21:55 GMT), the Times of Israel cited an exclusive Wall Street Journal report: Trump has ordered another wave of strikes against Iran with the explicit aim of forcing the Islamic Republic's surrender. Citing US officials, the WSJ report says the bombing is expected to last several days and could begin as soon as this weekend. Taken together, the two reports show Washington's military pressure on Tehran moving into a new phase — from "warning retaliation" toward "pressure to capitulate."

Trump Slams 'Dishonorable' and 'Dishonest' Iran: 'We Just Want to Win' — Iran Must Never Get a Nuclear Weapon

At 21:34 GMT, The Times of Israel reported that Trump, speaking at a cabinet meeting at Camp David, sharply attacked what he called Iran's "dishonorable" conduct during negotiations. Asked by reporters how he planned to revive last month's memorandum of understanding with Iran — which was supposed to end the war in less than four months — Trump replied "We just want to win," offering little indication he is interested in a diplomatic settlement. He reiterated: "They cannot have a nuclear weapon. Iran will not have a nuclear weapon." Asked whether Americans should be prepared for the back-and-forth strikes between the US and Iran to continue, Trump responded "A little bit," adding, "It would be foolish to say no. You always have to keep your guard up." The report also noted that Iran claims to have hit two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and launched drones at American facilities in Kuwait. The trajectory of this conflict is now a top variable shaping both the US midterm elections and global oil prices.

Venezuela: Government and Part of the Opposition to Hold US-Backed Talks Aimed at a Path to Elections

Al Jazeera reported at 20:28 GMT that Venezuela's government and a segment of the opposition will kick off US-backed talks on Saturday aimed at paving the way for eventual elections. Notably absent will be the country's most prominent opposition figure, Nobel Peace Prize winner Maria Corina Machado, who has not been invited to attend. The talks come nearly seven months after the US military's abduction of Venezuelan President Nicolas Maduro. Under Washington's influence, interim President Delcy Rodriguez has already made several key concessions, including opening the state-owned oil industry to foreign companies. The opposition delegation will be led by Dinorah Figuera, who headed an opposition bloc in the National Assembly elected in 2015 and returned from exile in Spain in June; she is set to push for overhauling the electoral council and top court, long filled with Maduro loyalists, to ensure free and fair elections. Leading the government side is National Assembly president Jorge Rodriguez, Delcy's brother, who has said he will use the talks to push for more US sanctions relief. Machado, who resurfaced in Oslo in December to receive the Nobel Peace Prize, said last week she would not obstruct any "real progress" and would judge success on whether democratic institutions are restored, political prisoners freed and elections scheduled.

UN Warns: Powerful El Nino Is 'Adding Fuel to a Planet Already on Fire'

Al Jazeera reported at 20:38 GMT that the World Meteorological Organization (WMO) released its latest update on Friday, warning that a rapidly intensifying El Nino event will dominate global weather patterns in the coming months, heightening the risk of heatwaves, drought and flooding around the world. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres said: "El Nino is not just on our doorstep, it is inside the house and turning up the heat. This is only a warm-up act. El Nino is strengthening, adding fuel to a planet already on fire." Central Pacific sea-surface temperatures are projected to soar more than 2.9 degrees Celsius above normal, and the WMO projects above-normal temperatures across nearly all land areas, with the strongest signals across Africa, southern Europe, the Indian subcontinent, East Asia and South America. Guterres urged nations to fast-track climate action and halt new coal, oil and gas projects, calling fossil fuel expansion the core driver amplifying the crisis. WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo stressed: "Forecasts in themselves do not prevent hazards; people do. The decisions we make today will shape the impacts we experience tomorrow."

UK Arrests Turkish Man Accused of Funneling Money and Supplies to Hamas

The Times of Israel reported at 21:28 GMT that a Turkish man has been arrested in the United Kingdom on charges alleging he conspired to funnel money and supplies to Hamas through his senior role in what prosecutors say was a sham global humanitarian organization. Mohammad Yousef Hasna, 45, of Istanbul, has been ordered held pending extradition proceedings before he can be brought to New York. US Attorney Jamie McDonald in Manhattan said Hasna worked closely with Hamas's senior leadership to deliver supplies, food and funding under the guise of humanitarian aid. The case is the latest example of Washington's ongoing pursuit of Hamas's overseas financing network, and it underscores the uneasy reality that Western governments are keeping up the pressure on Hamas even as Gaza ceasefire negotiations continue.
