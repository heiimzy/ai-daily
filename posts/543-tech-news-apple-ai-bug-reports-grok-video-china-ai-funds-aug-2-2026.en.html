⚡ Aug 2 Tech Update: Apple Cracks Down on AI-Generated Bug Reports, xAI's Grok Imagine Video 1.5 Goes Native 1080p, Chinese VCs Return to AI Fundraising

Sunday, August 2 - several stories outside the spotlight all point in the same direction: AI is rewriting how the tech industry operates. Apple, swamped by AI-generated vulnerability reports, is capping submissions and adding a cool-off period; xAI upgraded its Grok video model to native 1080p text-to-video; Chinese venture firms are rushing back to raise AI and robotics funds after a three-year low; and the New York Times, citing Epoch AI, reports that AI chip deployments are doubling every nine months.

Apple Imposes Limits: AI-Generated Bug Reports Overwhelm Security Teams

According to a Financial Times report on August 2, Apple has restricted the number of potentially dangerous bugs researchers can submit to its internal security team, as it faces a deluge of reports from people using AI models to flag alleged risks. Under Apple's updated Security Bounty guidelines, researchers who repeatedly submit ineligible findings - including issues discovered by AI without proper human validation - can be paused from submitting for 180 days; more than two paused periods can lead to permanent removal.

This is not Apple's problem alone - it is an industry-wide "AI noise" crisis. Bugcrowd, the platform whose clients include OpenAI, Motorola and T-Mobile, said submissions more than quadrupled during a three-week period in March, with most turning out to be false positives or low-quality AI-generated findings. curl creator Daniel Stenberg called it an "explosion in AI slop reports" when he suspended the paid program in January; Nextcloud followed in April, citing "a massive increase of low-quality reports." Sophos CISO Ross McKerchar told the FT that the influx of poor-quality reports is "quickly becoming a major problem."

"The rules are quietly changing: drafting with a model is fine, but validate the finding yourself before you file - or you may lose access to a lucrative channel." - industry consensus, per the Financial Times, August 2

Why it matters: Meta, Microsoft, Apple and Crypto.com collectively paid researchers at least $58 million in 2025. When a company the size of Apple writes "AI-assisted reports" into its penalty clauses, the industry's default rules are being rewritten - the burden of validation is shifting from platforms back to submitters.

xAI Rolls Out Grok Imagine Video 1.5: Native 1080p Text-to-Video

According to Cryptobriefing, xAI released Grok Imagine Video 1.5 on August 1, a key upgrade to Grok's video generation: native 1080p text-to-video, where both text and image prompts can generate high-definition video directly; support for up to seven image references to keep character or environment consistency, and up to three audio inputs for voice consistency across scenes.

The new features are live on Grok's web and mobile apps and the xAI API, starting with SuperGrok Heavy and SuperGrok Plus subscribers in the US. The upgrade lands as the AI video race heats up - ByteDance's Seedance 2.5 and MiniMax H3 just ignited a Chinese AI video battle last week, and xAI's native 1080p generation with multi-reference consistency is a direct response to two of the industry's toughest pain points: character consistency and voice consistency.

Chinese VCs Return to AI and Robotics Fundraising After Three-Year Low

A separate Financial Times report on August 2 says Chinese venture firms are rushing back to fundraising after three years of record lows, with LPs redirecting capital toward domestic tech, AI and robotics champions. The report says the rebound follows mega-rounds including DeepSeek's $7.4 billion raise and Moonshot AI's $3.5 billion round at a $35 billion valuation.

Analysts see this as China's private-market spigot reopening around embodied AI and semiconductors, reshuffling how Chinese AI startups compete with US frontier labs on compute, talent and valuation. For the global AI landscape, the return of Chinese capital means the "China AI" narrative is getting fresh ammunition on the funding front.

Epoch AI: AI Chip Deployments Doubling Every Nine Months

According to the New York Times, citing research group Epoch AI, AI chip deployments are projected to double every nine months in the coming years - a pace far beyond any historical expansion of computing power. Doubling every nine months compounds to roughly a tenfold increase every two and a half years, which helps explain the recent wave of mega-infrastructure deals: reported Nvidia-OpenAI financing talks, Microsoft Azure's continued expansion, and gigawatt-scale data centers worldwide.

Epoch AI's projection also points to a deeper shift: the bottleneck for AI progress is moving from "not enough chips" to "not enough power, capital and engineering." If the forecast holds, compute growth over the next two years will be driven mainly by energy supply and capital spending rather than semiconductor capacity alone.
