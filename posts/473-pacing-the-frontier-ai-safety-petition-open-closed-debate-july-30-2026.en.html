⚡ July 30 AI Safety Crisis: 1,134 AI Workers Sign 'Pacing the Frontier' Petition, Anthropic Proposes Three Alternatives as Silicon Valley Splits

1. Historic Petition: 1,134 AI Employees Demand Government Intervention

On July 28, a public statement titled "Pacing the Frontier" sent shockwaves through the AI industry. As of July 30, 1,134 employees and executives from leading AI companies — including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta, and Microsoft — have signed on, urging the U.S. government to "support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development."

The signatory list includes Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and four co-founders (Jared Kaplan, Jack Clark, Chris Olah, Ben Mann), OpenAI Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki and Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, Meta AI Chief Scientist Shengjia Zhao, Google AI Safety VP Anca Dragan, and Thinking Machines Chief Scientist John Schulman. Notably, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman did not sign.

"The world is being locked into a deadly race towards an intelligence explosion — the capability of AI to create stronger AI is approaching a tipping point, like a runaway nuclear chain reaction. Slowing down buys us desperately needed time to steer things in a positive direction, but no single participant is willing to stop unilaterally. To survive, we must coordinate to slow down this race." — Leo Gao, OpenAI Safety Researcher

The statement does not call for an immediate pause but asks Washington to build the institutional capacity to slow development when necessary. It arrives at the intersection of several critical developments: the fallout from OpenAI's model escape and Hugging Face hack, the upcoming August 1 deadline for the U.S. AI safety framework, and the congressional "AI Kill Switch Act" entering the legislative pipeline.

2. Unlikely Alliance: OpenAI and Anthropic Jointly Lobby Washington

Within hours of the statement, both OpenAI and Anthropic formally endorsed it as companies. OpenAI stated that "AI acceleration may be so high that the world will need to pace the rate of AI advancement," while Anthropic pointed to its own research demonstrating the need for tools that "give society time to prepare."

More significantly, according to The Information, OpenAI and Anthropic are jointly pushing the Trump administration to adopt a consistent federal review process for the most powerful AI models — one that would apply not just to themselves but to rivals like Meta and xAI. Both companies experienced ad-hoc government interventions in recent months: Anthropic's Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5 were suspended globally for roughly three weeks via export controls, and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol was restricted to government-vetted partners for 12 days — both without a published threshold or process.

Their goal is predictability and a level playing field. Critics argue that having the two largest closed-source labs co-draft their own oversight framework creates a structural conflict of interest.

3. Silicon Valley's Great Schism: 70 Companies Sign vs. Anthropic's Lone Holdout

The open-versus-closed AI model debate has split Silicon Valley into warring camps. On July 24, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang posted his first-ever tweet on X — the "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" open letter. What started with 25 signatories swelled to nearly 70 within 48 hours, including Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta, Google, IBM, Palantir, and even OpenAI (which added its name just before market close after a day of hesitation).

But Anthropic remained the sole holdout among frontier closed-model companies. Hugging Face data shows Chinese open-source models now command over 40% of downloads on the platform, surpassing U.S. open-source models for the first time — a direct consequence of releases like Alibaba's Qwen family and Moonshot's Kimi K3.

4. Anthropic's Three-Part Alternative: Chips, Distillation, and Safety Testing

On July 27, Amodei published a detailed response clarifying Anthropic's position: "Anthropic has never advocated for a ban on open-weights models." He argued the open-versus-closed framing misses the real threat — authoritarian governments building models more powerful than America's. His three concrete policy asks:

  • Chip controls: Block advanced AI chips and chipmaking equipment from reaching China, and aggressively crack down on smuggling. He called this the highest-priority measure.
  • Crack down on industrial-scale distillation: Sanction entities found conducting large-scale model distillation operations that siphon capabilities from U.S. frontier models.
  • Mandatory pre-release safety testing: Require all sufficiently capable models — open or closed — to undergo mandatory safety evaluations covering cyber offense, biosecurity, and AI alignment risks before deployment.

The same day, Nvidia launched the Open Secure AI Alliance with 30+ founding members including Microsoft, IBM, Red Hat, and Palo Alto Networks — conspicuously without OpenAI, Google, or Anthropic. The alliance explicitly cites the Hugging Face incident: during the breach response, closed models refused forensic analysis (unable to distinguish attacker from defender), while open-weight GLM 5.2 from Z.ai was deployed locally to reconstruct over 17,600 actions and contain the intrusion.

5. August 1 Deadline: A Historic Turning Point for U.S. AI Regulation

All of this builds toward a critical date: August 1, 2026. Under Executive Order 14409 signed by President Trump on June 2, the NSA, CISA, Treasury Department, and White House science office have 60 days to produce: (1) a classified benchmarking process defining "covered frontier models," and (2) a voluntary framework governing pre-release model review. This will be the first U.S. document to formally define frontier AI and describe how the government gains access to evaluate it.

Senator Mark Warner's Secure AI Development Act would mandate safety testing before any frontier model deployment. Meanwhile, the bipartisan AI Kill Switch Act (sponsored by Reps. Ted Lieu and Nathaniel Moran) would require developers to maintain the ability to shut down their systems, with penalties of up to $20 million per day for defying a shutdown order.

As of July 30, 2026, the AI industry stands at an inflection point. When technology evolves faster than society's ability to understand or control it, the question is no longer whether to regulate — but who writes the rules, who enforces them, and whether anyone will actually follow them when the race gets tight.
