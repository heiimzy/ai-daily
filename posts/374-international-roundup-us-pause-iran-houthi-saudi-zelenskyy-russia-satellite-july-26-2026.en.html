⚡ July 26 International Roundup: Trump Orders Pause on Iran Strikes — First Quiet Night in Two Weeks, Houthi Missile Barrage Hits Saudi Aramco Refineries in Jizan and Yanbu, Zelenskyy Reveals Russian Satellite Intel for Iran Targeting US Bases, Ukraine Drone Strikes Hit Siberian Refinery 2,000 km Inside Russia

1. Trump Orders Pause on Iran Strikes: First Peaceful Night in 13 Days

Iran experienced its first night without American bombing in two weeks on Friday. Axios reported Saturday, citing two sources familiar with the matter, that President Donald Trump directed the military not to conduct new strikes against Iran on Friday, ending a streak of 13 consecutive nights of escalating attacks.

"It seems that our dear Iran had a peaceful night last night," Iran Health Ministry spokesman Hossein Kermanpour posted on X. Iranian state media confirmed no new explosions or attacks were reported within the country's borders overnight.

According to Axios, Trump typically approved daily strike packages submitted by the military each afternoon. But on Friday, after receiving a new operational plan, he did not authorize it and instead instructed the military to stand down for the day. It remains unclear whether this is a one-day pause or signals a major shift in military posture. The Pentagon and the White House did not return requests for comment.

Hours before the pause, an Omani delegation arrived in Tehran for talks on the future of navigation through the Strait of Hormuz. Two regional sources familiar with the negotiations said progress had been made and that Oman and Iran could reach an agreement by the weekend, after which Trump would decide whether to accept the terms. Speaking at the White House on Friday, Trump said Iran was getting "more and more serious" in talks but added: "We're locked and loaded. We're ready to go."

"There's a military exit where we just keep going just the way we are, and we can even make it a heavier dose, and it's knocking out everything they have. Or there's a smarter strategy that you make a deal." — President Donald Trump

2. Houthi Missile Barrage Hits Saudi Aramco: New Red Sea Front Erupts

While Iran itself enjoyed a quiet night, the Red Sea theater saw a major escalation. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced Saturday that they had launched coordinated missile and drone strikes targeting Saudi Aramco facilities in the cities of Jizan and Yanbu in retaliation for Saudi airstrikes on the Yemeni port city of Hodeidah.

Houthi military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree said in a statement that the first operation used "dozens of ballistic missiles and drones" targeting "sensitive Aramco-affiliated facilities" in Jizan, while a second operation struck similar targets in Yanbu with ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and drones. The group claimed both operations were "successful" and had achieved "precision, direct hits."

Video verified by Reuters showed a large column of black smoke rising from the direction of the Aramco refinery in Jizan. NASA's FIRMS/VIIRS satellite fire detection system detected multiple heat hotspots at the Jizan refinery location. Two Asia-based trading sources said they had been informed of potential damage to fuel and oil storage sites at Jizan.

In Yanbu, two ballistic missiles aimed at oil installations were intercepted by a US-made Patriot battery operated by Greek military personnel under an agreement with Riyadh, Greek security sources said. Yanbu is Saudi Arabia's main Red Sea oil export terminal, loading millions of barrels per day, while Jizan hosts a 400,000 barrel-per-day refinery near the Yemeni border.

Saudi civil defense issued emergency alerts early Saturday for Jizan province and Yanbu governorate, advising residents to stay away from windows and seek shelter. The alerts were lifted shortly afterward with authorities declaring the danger had passed. Saudi authorities did not immediately comment on casualties or specific damage.

This escalation marks the effective collapse of the 2022 Yemen ceasefire, as the Houthis have fully joined the wider war their Iranian allies have been waging against the US and its partners. Houthi leader Abdul Malik al-Houthi had previously warned that all Saudi oil facilities could become targets, and the group's maritime blockade of Saudi shipping remains in effect.

3. Zelenskyy Publishes Evidence of Russian Satellite Intel Helping Iran Strike US Bases

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy issued a major accusation Saturday, stating that Russia is actively providing satellite imagery to Iran to help target US military installations in the Middle East. Zelenskyy said he would share the evidence with the United States.

"Since the beginning of July, we have recorded active Russian satellite surveillance of Gulf states and US military facilities located there," Zelenskyy wrote on X. "These images subsequently appear in Iran. At the same time, there is a clear correlation between Russia's satellite imagery of these sites and Iranian strikes — both before the attacks, in preparation for them, and afterward, to assess the damage inflicted."

Citing Ukrainian intelligence data, Zelenskyy detailed that between July 19 and 20 alone, four air bases fell within the area of interest of Russian satellites — two in Bahrain, one in Jordan, and one in Kuwait. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth acknowledged during a congressional hearing this week that adversary alignment was occurring, though Trump contradicted him days later, saying on Truth Social that both Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin had assured him they were not involved.

Eighteen American service members are believed to have been killed since the start of the Iran war, including three in a recent Iranian attack on Jordan's Muwaffaq Salti Air Base and one during a controlled demolition of an Iranian drone in Iraq. Nearly 100 US service members have been injured in multiple Iranian airstrikes on bases across the region.

Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov denied providing intelligence to Tehran but acknowledged that Moscow has supplied military equipment to Iran under long-standing military alliance agreements.

4. Ukraine Strikes Tyumen Refinery 2,000 km Inside Russia, Hits Iranian Vessel in Caspian

Ukrainian forces successfully struck a Russian oil refinery in Tyumen in western Siberia, a plant in Kirov producing projectile components, a logistics site in Yekaterinburg, and a Russian warship in the Caspian Sea, President Zelenskyy confirmed Saturday.

"We also achieved very strong results with long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea, including vessels used in military cargo shipments involving Iran," Zelenskyy said on X. Ukraine's Security Service said the latest attacks involved strikes on Lukoil's Filanovsky oil platform in the Caspian Sea and the Port Olya 2 and Begey cargo vessels believed to be involved in military cargo shipments between Russia and Iran.

Iran's Foreign Ministry confirmed that a Ukrainian attack struck an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea, causing an explosion that killed one sailor and injured another. Tehran described the attack as an "act of aggression" and said it would defend its national security interests.

The Tyumen refinery, located more than 2,000 kilometers from Ukraine, was hit by Ukrainian drones sparking a fire. The attack underscores the expanding reach of Ukraine's long-range drone campaign against Russian energy infrastructure, which has already caused fuel shortages and put pressure on President Vladimir Putin.

In other developments, Moscow-installed officials in the partially occupied Zaporizhzhia region said Ukrainian attacks on holiday camps killed 12 people including four children, while Ukrainian authorities in Sumy reported three killed by a Russian drone strike.

5. Romania Shoots Down Second Russian Drone with F-16 in Two Days

Romania announced Saturday that it had "safely shot down" a second drone breaching its national airspace in as many days, in an unpopulated area near the Ukrainian border. President Nicusor Dan confirmed on social media that the drone was "shot down by a Romanian pilot from an F-16 aircraft" at 8:30 AM local time, 10 km west of Sfantu Gheorghe in the Danube Delta.

The interception follows Friday's first-ever shootdown by Romanian forces of a drone confirmed by prosecutors to be Russian. Romania, which shares a 650 km land border with Ukraine, has recorded approximately 30 Russian drone airspace breaches since Moscow's full-scale invasion began in 2022.

In May, a Russian drone crashed into an apartment building in the southeastern Romanian town of Galati near the Ukrainian border, injuring two people — the first time a strike hit a densely populated area in a NATO country and caused injuries during the war. Romanian army Chief of Staff General Gheorghita Vlad said Friday that Russian attacks around the Ukrainian port of Odesa had increased ahead of the harvest season, further pressuring key Black Sea energy and trade routes.
