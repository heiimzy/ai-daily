⚡ Aug 2 Finance News: US and Japan Intervene in Yen for First Time in 28 Years, Bessent's $5-10B Note Exposed, Bitcoin Drops Below $63K

This weekend delivered the most dramatic moment in global currency markets in nearly three decades: the U.S. Treasury unusually stepped in to buy yen on Friday (July 31), joining Japanese authorities in a coordinated defense of the currency - the first direct joint US-Japan yen support since the 1998 Asian financial crisis. Adding to the drama, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was photographed at a live Camp David cabinet meeting with a handwritten to-do note reading "Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil." The yen rallied more than 6% in two days, climbing from a 40-year low of 163.99 to 157.40, while the ripple effects of carry-trade unwinding hit crypto, with bitcoin briefly falling below $63,000.

US Treasury Buys Yen for First Time in 28 Years: NY Fed Sells Euros, Buys Yen

According to the Financial Times, citing three people familiar with the matter, the Federal Reserve Bank of New York executed a "sell euros, buy yen" operation on behalf of the U.S. Treasury on July 31, conducted through Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley. It is the first time Washington has directly purchased yen to support the currency since June 17, 1998 - when the New York Fed deployed $833 million - although the US joined G7-coordinated intervention in 2011 after the Great East Japan Earthquake, in the opposite direction, to curb excessive yen strength.

The night before, the New York Fed had conducted the customary "rate check" - asking major banks for live yen quotes - which markets read as a precursor to official intervention. Reuters earlier reported that the Treasury had told several banks on Friday that it might intervene in the yen market that day and to "stand ready." Japan's Finance Ministry also posted on X in the early hours of Saturday that it could tap the Federal Reserve's standing FIMA Repo Facility - pledging US Treasuries for temporary dollar liquidity - if needed.

Treasury Secretary Bessent had told Fox Business that the yen is "deeply undervalued" and that excessive volatility is unhealthy. The veteran currency trader who once led Soros Fund Management's yen-shorting campaign in 2012 - pocketing over a billion dollars in three months - has now personally come to the yen's rescue.

Bessent's Note Exposed: $5-10 Billion Yen Purchase Planned, Fear of Japan Dumping Treasuries

During a live-streamed Trump cabinet meeting at Camp David on Friday, with a wall of photographers behind him, Bessent's notepad conspicuously read: "To Do: Buy Japanese Yen (JPY), $5-10 bil." A Reuters photographer captured the note, and the image circled the globe - the most direct "smoking gun" evidence of US intervention.

Analysts point to a deeper motive: protecting US Treasuries. With US long-term yields hitting their highest since the subprime crisis this week on the Iran war and confidence questions around new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, a massive Japanese Treasury sell-off to fund intervention would pour fuel on the fire. Treasury International Capital (TIC) data shows Japan sold $66.75 billion of US Treasuries in May, roughly matching its intervention operations. During the "Japan bond storm" triggered by Prime Minister Takaichi's fiscal policy in January, Citi warned the turmoil could trigger as much as $130 billion in Treasury selling - exactly what Washington wants to avoid this time.

Japan's Thursday Intervention Estimated at ¥8.45 Trillion: Possibly Its Largest Ever

Japan struck first. Bloomberg estimates, based on Bank of Japan account data released Friday and broker forecasts, that the government and BOJ bought yen and sold dollars on July 30 (Thursday) to the tune of about ¥8.45 trillion (roughly $52.8 billion) - possibly Japan's largest single-day intervention on record; CME data shows yen trading volume on Thursday spiked to its highest in nearly 12 years. Reuters also reported that South Korea coordinated by selling dollars for its own currency the same day.

According to Cailian Press calculations, combined with the ¥11.7 trillion (about $74.3 billion) spent on intervention from late April to late May, Japan's cumulative 2026 intervention now totals about ¥18 trillion (about $114.3 billion) - surpassing the previous annual record of ¥15.3 trillion set in 2024. The effect was immediate: USD/JPY, which touched 163.99 on July 23 - its weakest since 1986 - was yanked back more than 6% in two days, settling near 157.40 on Friday, its strongest level since early May.

Ripple Effects: Carry-Trade Unwinding Sends Bitcoin Below $63,000

The yen's surge hit global risk assets first. For years, investors borrowed ultra-cheap yen to fund higher-yielding investments - the classic carry trade - and a sudden yen rally forces leveraged traders to liquidate risk assets quickly to repay yen loans, which drove bitcoin below $63,000, trading around $63,034, down 1.25% over 24 hours; OKX data showed BTC at about $62,999 in the early hours of August 2 Beijing time. US stocks, meanwhile, finished Friday higher on tech strength: the Nasdaq gained 1%, the S&P 500 rose 0.7%, and the Dow added 0.53%.

Evercore ISI strategists caution that intervention without narrowing US-Japan rate differentials tends to be "relatively short-lived." Markets now treat 160 as the key technical level for USD/JPY - if the yen weakens again, another intervention window could stay open into Monday.

BOJ Holds at 1% But Turns Hawkish: September Hike Bets Rise

On Friday, the Bank of Japan kept its policy rate at 1% in an 8-1 vote, with the sole dissenter - board member Hajime Takata - calling for a 25bp hike to 1.25%. The bank trimmed its core inflation forecast for FY2026 to 2.5%, but warned that core inflation could accelerate to "clearly above 2%" in the second half of FY2026, driven by wage pass-through to prices, higher oil costs and yen weakness.

Governor Kazuo Ueda struck a distinctly hawkish tone in his post-meeting press conference: he called "upside price risks" a core consideration for policy and said the BOJ would "very likely accelerate the pace of hikes" if the economy and prices move in line with forecasts while financial conditions stay overly accommodative. CICC Research sees the next hike most likely in October, but says September (the September 17-18 meeting) is possible if the yen weakens further and price pressures build. Notably, Bessent took to social media on Friday to say he looks forward to meeting Ueda at the G20 finance ministers meeting in Asheville, North Carolina (August 31 - September 1) - the second half of the US-Japan currency showdown is just beginning.
