⚡ July 25 Financial Roundup: Brent Retreats to $97 but Posts 13% Weekly Gain, Trump Visits Fed to Push Rate Cuts, Intel Q2 Earnings Beat with AI Revenue Surging 59%

1. Oil Markets: Brent Falls from $100 but Weekly Gain Still Exceeds 10%

International oil prices retreated on Friday (July 24) from Thursday's highs. Brent crude futures settled at $97.10/barrel, down 3.57%, while WTI crude fell to $89.05/barrel. However, Brent is still up over 10% for the week after surging 7% on Thursday—its first close above $100 since May, when Houthi forces attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea.

Friday's decline was driven primarily by profit-taking rather than fundamental improvement. Rystad Energy analyst Janiv Shah noted that ongoing Middle East tensions combined with CPC pipeline outages are squeezing supply from multiple directions simultaneously. The Strait of Hormuz disruption appears to be nearing peak levels seen in March.

JPMorgan warned in a research note that each additional month of supply disruption would add $7-8 per barrel to Brent, pushing monthly average prices to around $114 if disruptions extend to three months. Goldman Sachs maintains a Q4 base case of $80/barrel but cautioned Brent could exceed $120 if Hormuz disruptions persist and risks spread across the Bab el-Mandeb and Suez routes.

Kpler ship-tracking data showed daily tanker transits through the Strait of Hormuz holding steady at three per day—far below pre-war levels. The Bab el-Mandeb strait saw 32 commercial vessel transits on July 23, up from 26 the day before. UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo commented that "ships are still moving—it's not a complete blockade as some feared—but the situation remains far from normal."

2. Fed Policy: Trump Personally Visits Fed to Push Rate Cuts, Yet Hike Probability Surges to 35%

In an extraordinary move on Thursday (July 24), President Donald Trump personally visited Federal Reserve headquarters to inspect a $2.5 billion building renovation project and pressure Fed Chair Jerome Powell for lower interest rates. The visibly tense interaction came after Trump had called Powell a "numbskull" earlier in the week for failing to heed White House demands for aggressive rate cuts.

Paradoxically, markets are now pricing in a higher probability of a rate hike. CME FedWatch data shows the probability of a July 29 FOMC rate hike has more than tripled from 10.7% a week ago to 34.7%. This reversal is driven by two factors: surging oil prices fueling inflation expectations, and Core PCE data showing the inflationary effects of the Iran war extend well beyond the energy sector.

Treasury yields spiked across the curve: the 10-year yield hit 4.7135% (18-month high), the 30-year yield rose to 5.19% (near 2007 levels), and the 2-year yield reached 4.37% (highest since February 2025). TD Securities' Gennadiy Goldberg noted that even if the Fed holds steady next week, the yield surge itself reflects a fundamental repricing of inflation and rate hike risks.

The dollar index rose to three-week highs on safe-haven demand. The yen continued its slide to 163.77 per dollar, approaching 40-year lows, drawing warnings from both the U.S. Treasury and Japan's finance minister about excessive currency volatility.

3. Intel Q2 Earnings: Revenue Up 25%—Fastest Growth in 15 Years, AI Business Surges 59%

Chip giant Intel reported blockbuster Q2 2026 results after Thursday's close (July 23 ET), beating on every metric. Revenue hit $16.1 billion, far exceeding the $14.43 billion consensus estimate, representing 25% year-over-year growth—the fastest in nearly 15 years. Non-GAAP EPS was $0.42, double the $0.21 expected. Non-GAAP gross margin reached 41.8%, up 12.1 percentage points year-over-year.

By segment: Data Center & AI (DCAI) revenue surged 59% to $6.3 billion—the quarter's standout performer. Client Computing & Physical AI (CCPG) revenue grew 13% to $8.9 billion. Intel Foundry revenue rose 31% to $5.8 billion. CEO Lip-Bu Tan stated that AI-related revenue grew over 70% year-over-year, and demand continues to outpace supply.

Q3 guidance also impressed: revenue forecast of $15.8-16.8 billion (midpoint $16.3B), well above the $15.1B consensus. 18A process output exceeded targets, yields continue improving, and 14A is ahead of schedule with PDK 0.9 on track for October. The company raised 2026 CapEx to over $20 billion. Shares surged over 13% in after-hours trading, though they later settled to $96.82 (-3.41%) on Friday.

4. Wall Street Selloff: S&P 500 Drops 1.2%, Nasdaq 2.2% as AI Capex Fears Mount

U.S. stocks suffered a broad selloff on Thursday. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 506.93 points (-0.97%) to 51,711.65, the S&P 500 dropped 90.66 points (-1.21%) to 7,408.30, and the Nasdaq Composite plunged 553.21 points (-2.15%) to 25,137.69. The CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) rose to 18.7, briefly touching 20.3—its highest in nearly a month.

The Magnificent Seven index plunged 4.78%, its worst single-day drop since April 2025. Alphabet (Google) fell 7.13%, and Tesla cratered 14.52%—both following disappointing earnings that raised red flags about AI spending efficiency. Alphabet raised its 2026 CapEx to $195-205 billion and reported negative free cash flow. Tesla also reported negative FCF of $1.1 billion. The central investor concern: when will massive AI infrastructure investments begin producing adequate returns?

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 0.5%, but Intel's after-hours earnings provided a bright spot. Oracle gained after securing a nearly $7 billion Pentagon software licensing deal. American Express and Verizon also reported earnings beats, offering potential support for Friday's session.

Gold edged up 0.1% to $4,051/oz on Friday after a 2% decline the previous day, as rising real yields weighed on non-yielding assets while Middle East避险需求provided a floor. Silver traded around $57.63.

In FX markets, the dollar held near three-week highs. The yen remained under pressure at 163.77 per dollar, within striking distance of 40-year lows. EUR/USD drifted near 1.08 after mixed Eurozone PMI data—Germany's composite PMI returned to expansion (50.3) after four months while France remained in contraction territory. UK retail sales fell 0.3% month-over-month, weighing on sterling. The Chinese yuan traded in a narrow range around 7.25, supported by the PBOC's 500 billion yuan MLF operation.

6. China Markets: A-Share Indices Mixed, Tech Rotation Drives STAR 50 Rally

China's A-share markets saw mild declines on Friday, with the Shanghai Composite slipping 0.33% to lose the 3,600 level, the Shenzhen Component down 0.22%, and the ChiNext off 0.23%.
