Google Quickly Withdraws Google Earth AI Image Tool: It Lasted Just One Day

Google's new AI image-generation feature in Google Earth lasted only one day. On Thursday, the company launched a feature that let users edit real satellite, aerial and 3D imagery in Google Earth with text prompts, powered by its Nano Banana 2 image-generation model. "For the first time, you can generate custom images using Google Earth's satellite, aerial, and 3D imagery alongside Nano Banana, which creates concepts grounded in the real world," wrote Bryan Horowitz, Google Earth's product manager, in a company blog post. But within hours, users were producing strikingly realistic fake scenes — investigator Henk van Ess deliberately generated images adding refugees near the Mexican border and a bomb crater beside a hospital in Gaza, while others fabricated fake disaster sites and bogus "satellite images" of Singapore. Critics noted that Google Earth is one of the most trusted sources of visual evidence for journalists and researchers, and that the tool essentially enabled the creation and spread of misinformation. Google initially responded that generated images carried a digital watermark and that it prevented "image creation on harmful topics," but a day later it reversed course, acknowledging that "people uniquely trust Google Earth for a reliable view of the world," and pulled the feature. Ars Technica, in a piece titled "What on earth is Google doing?", said the swift launch-and-retreat exposed the risks of rushing AI features to market.

Reuters Exclusive: Chinese Military-Linked Researchers Used OpenAI and Anthropic Outputs to Train Defense AI

A Reuters review found that Chinese military-linked researchers used outputs from leading US AI models built by OpenAI and Anthropic to train domestic AI systems and advance China's defense capabilities. The review covered more than 80 Chinese academic papers and patents, including research compiled by the Washington-based Jamestown Foundation and shared exclusively with the news agency. The documents show widespread use of a technique known as "model distillation," in which outputs from a powerful AI system are used to train smaller, specialized models that can be deployed locally without the enormous computing requirements of building frontier models from scratch. In one case, researchers at PLA Unit 96941 — a military intelligence and cyber-warfare unit in Beijing — published a paper describing the use of OpenAI's GPT-3.5 to process sensitive military source code: because third-party models were unsuitable for handling classified information, they used GPT-3.5 to summarize the code and trained a domestic model on those summaries to run entirely within Chinese military networks. The dispute centers on "unauthorised capability extraction" rather than distillation itself, a widely used industry practice, and it has emerged as a major flashpoint ahead of US-China talks on AI governance and safety. US officials have accused some Chinese entities of using distillation to extract capabilities from American models, potentially undermining export controls and infringing intellectual property; China has rejected the accusations, saying Washington is pursuing AI "hegemonism." Sunny Cheung, a Jamestown fellow who analyzed over 60 of the papers, said Chinese military scientists are systematically capturing the reasoning steps of Western models to adapt them for surveillance, cyber warfare and tactical decision-making. Neither OpenAI nor the Chinese institutions responded to requests for comment.

Unitree Finalizes STAR Market IPO Arrangements: Online Subscription on Aug 10, 171 Employees to Invest 271.5 Million Yuan

Humanoid-robot leader Unitree has taken a key step toward its public listing. Sina Finance reported that Unitree has finalized its issuance arrangements on Shanghai's STAR Market, with 171 employees planning to invest a combined 271.5 million yuan (about $38 million) in follow-on subscription, and that the online subscription period opens on Monday, August 10. The STAR Market's new-share pipeline remains active — on August 1, other new listings including Chaochun Yingcai (Ultra-Pure Materials) and Guo Yi Company opened for subscription the same day. Unitree, known globally for its H1 and G1 humanoid robots, is widely seen as a top contender for the title of "first humanoid-robot stock," and the pricing and institutional bookbuilding around its listing have become a focus of market attention.

CXMT's LPDDR6 Enters Final Stage of R&D Validation: 12,800 Mbps Data Rate, Targeting World-First Mass Production

China's domestic memory chip sector has logged another breakthrough. Yicai (China Business Network), Wallstreetcn and MyDrivers all reported in the early hours of Saturday that CXMT, China's leading DRAM maker, has brought its next-generation low-power memory LPDDR6 close to the end of R&D validation: according to MyDrivers, the data rate reaches 12,800 Mbps (12.8 Gbps), with CXMT racing against Korean giants Samsung and SK Hynix and aiming for the world's first mass production. Sina Mobile described the development as CXMT "racing Korean giants" with LPDDR6 validation nearing completion, while Wallstreetcn called it a "key breakthrough." LPDDR6 is the next-generation memory standard for AI smartphones and on-device large models, delivering significantly higher speed and energy efficiency than LPDDR5X and serving as a key enabler of on-device AI computing. CXMT has been on a powerful run — its market value recently surpassed 4 trillion yuan for the first time — and with this push into high-end memory standards, the "catch-up curve" of China's memory industry continues to climb.
