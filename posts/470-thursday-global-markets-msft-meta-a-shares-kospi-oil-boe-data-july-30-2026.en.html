⚡ July 30 Thursday Global Markets: Microsoft's 8.8% Surge Lifts Futures, A-Share Tech Rout Deepens, Korea Limits Leveraged ETFs, Brent Breaks $92

1. US Futures Stabilize After Microsoft's Blockbuster Earnings

US equity futures edged higher in early Thursday trading as Microsoft's blowout quarterly results provided a much-needed anchor after Wednesday's brutal selloff. As of 9:00 AM ET:

  • S&P 500 futures: +0.21%
  • Nasdaq 100 futures: +0.50%
  • Dow Jones futures: +0.07% (34 points)

The relief rally comes after the Dow's worst session since April 2025 — a 1,153-point (2.19%) plunge. The Nasdaq Composite fell 1.74%, closing more than 10% below its intraday record and officially entering correction territory. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index dropped 5.3% for its fifth consecutive daily decline, its longest losing streak since December.

"Microsoft's solid earnings have provided a dose of relief and given the market fresh hope that the tech trade still has legs," said Tim Waterer at KCM Trade. "Sentiment appears to be stabilizing for now, though traders will be watching the rest of the mega-cap reports closely."

Microsoft vs Meta: A Tale of Two AI Strategies

Microsoft (MSFT) surged 8.8% in after-hours trading — the primary driver of Thursday's futures rebound. Q4 revenue reached $90.01 billion (+18% YoY), crushing estimates of $87.62 billion. Azure cloud revenue grew 43%, with full-year cloud revenue surpassing $100 billion for the first time. The company lowered its FY2027 capex guidance from $190 billion to $175 billion (~8% below prior estimates), signaling capital discipline that investors rewarded handsomely.

Meta (META) slumped ~7% after hours despite Q2 revenue of $60.8 billion (+28%, a record high). Net income fell 14% to $15.8 billion, and free cash flow collapsed 91% to just $784 million — the lowest in four years. Q3 revenue guidance of $61-64 billion (midpoint $62.5B) missed analyst estimates of $63.17 billion. The company raised its FY capex floor from $125B to $130B. The message from markets was clear: show returns on AI spending, not just spending itself.

Stephen Evans, CIO at Pave Finance, summarized the divergence: "This is ultimately a tale of two AI investment strategies. One company is increasing profits while spending heavily, while the other is allowing those costs to eat into its bottom line."

Qualcomm After-Hours: Mobile Chip Demand at 2021 Lows

Qualcomm fell 5% in extended trading. Q3 revenue came in at $9.95 billion (-4% YoY), with mobile chip revenue plunging 20% to $5.1 billion — its lowest since 2021 — battered by a ~300% YoY surge in memory chip prices. Net income dropped 25%. The company announced a "double-digit percentage" price increase effective September 1 but still issued below-consensus Q4 guidance.

2. China A-Shares: Tech Rout Deepens, Consumer Staples Rally

Chinese equities closed broadly lower on Thursday as the tech selloff intensified while defensive consumer staples rotated higher. Key indices at close:

  • Shanghai Composite: -0.62%
  • Shenzhen Component: -2.73% to 13,141
  • ChiNext (Growth Enterprise): -3.97% to 3,180, breaking below the 3,200 mark
  • STAR 50 (Kechuang): -6.34% to 1,572

Consumer staples were the day's standout winner. Baijiu (Chinese spirits) stocks surged, with Shede Spirits hitting the daily limit up. Kweichow Moutai, Gujing Gongjiu, and Jiugui Liquor all rallied. The catalyst: Beijing and Shanghai Moutai direct-sale stores raised Feitian Moutai prices to 1,719 yuan per bottle, above the iMart platform price of 1,639 yuan, reinforcing the valuation recovery thesis for the baijiu sector.

The AI/tech crash was brutal. CPO (co-packaged optics) concepts collapsed: Dongshan Precision, Accelink Technologies, and Cambridge Industries all hit limit-down. End-side AI names: Rockchip Electronics hit limit-down, while StarChip and Aojie Technology fell over 13%. The semiconductor equipment ETF plunged over 4%. IC design and foundry names across the board — Zhongke Feice, Hua Hong Hongli — suffered heavy losses. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology's announcement of an upcoming "AI + Software" action plan failed to stem the panic.

ICBC and CCB hit all-time highs as capital rotated into high-dividend, low-valuation state-owned banks — a classic risk-off signal in Chinese markets.

3. KOSPI Rebounds After Government Cracks Down on Leveraged ETFs

South Korea's KOSPI index rebounded 2.76% on Thursday, staging a dramatic recovery after two consecutive sessions of circuit-breaker level crashes that wiped out trillions in market value. The index had plunged a cumulative 16% over Monday and Tuesday.

Two factors fueled the turnaround:

Government emergency measures — The Ministry of Economy and Finance, Bank of Korea, and financial regulators held emergency talks Wednesday evening, announcing new restrictions on retail participation in leveraged ETFs. Measures include setting maximum limits on leveraged ETF holdings as a percentage of total investor assets and raising transaction costs. The KOSPI had fallen approximately 40% from its June peak, with leveraged ETF liquidations acting as the primary accelerant.

Samsung's record earnings — Samsung Electronics reported Q2 revenue of 171.5 trillion won (+130% YoY) and operating profit of 89.49 trillion won (+1,813.83% YoY), both well above consensus. Samsung shares surged 7.91% on the day. SK Hynix rose 2% after its own record earnings. Samsung's foundry prices have increased ~15%, while TSMC raised advanced process prices by 5-10% — semiconductor manufacturing is shifting from demand-driven to supply-driven pricing.

Meanwhile, Samsung Electro-Mechanics announced a 30% MLCC price increase effective August 1, and Japan's Murata (Taiyo Yuden) will raise prices from September 1 — indicating that the consumer electronics supply chain inflation wave is spreading from memory to passive components.

4. Middle East: US Resumes Iran Strikes, Brent Pushes Past $92

The fragile Middle East truce shattered overnight as US Central Command launched fresh airstrikes against Iranian targets Wednesday evening, hitting the southwestern city of Abadan and the strategic Qeshm Island. The strikes were in direct retaliation for Iran's ballistic missile attack on US forces in Jordan a day earlier.

Brent crude continued its rally, climbing 2.1% to $92.63 per barrel on Thursday. WTI crude traded above $85. On Wednesday, Brent had already surged 7.91% to $90.74 after Trump vowed to "hit Iran hard."

President Trump, in a Fox News interview, warned of further escalation and revealed the US is considering a "significant military campaign" against Iran's missile capabilities. Sources indicate the Pentagon is preparing plans for an intensive two-week air operation targeting Iran's missile infrastructure.

Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman met urgently with Trump and Vice President Vance, delivering a message from Crown Prince MBS about the rapidly deteriorating regional situation. Houthi blockade actions have already damaged Saudi Aramco's Abqaiq facility and forced the shutdown of the Jazan refinery.

The oil price surge introduces a new variable into the global inflation calculus — one that will directly influence whether the Fed resumes hiking in September. RBC Capital Markets analysts note that sustained Brent prices above $90 for more than a month would force the Fed's hand.

5. Super Thursday: PCE Data, Bank of England, US GDP All Due

Thursday shapes up as the most critical data day of the week, with three major macro events converging:

US June PCE Inflation (released 8:30 AM ET) — The Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Consensus expects headline PCE to remain at 3.7% YoY, with core at 3.3%. Any upside surprise would dramatically strengthen the case for a September rate hike. Fed Chair Warsh has stated the 2% inflation target has "no flexibility."

US Q2 GDP (Preliminary) — Expected at 2.1% annualized, unchanged from Q1. This reading will test the resilience of the US economy under elevated rates and geopolitical pressure.

Bank of England Rate Decision — Expected to hold rates steady, but any hawkish signals from the BoE would add to the global bond selloff. The UK 10-year yield is already at 5.04%.

Weekly Jobless Claims — Expected at 225,000, providing a real-time read on labor market health.

Later Thursday, Amazon, Apple, and Coinbase will report earnings after the close — rounding out one of the heaviest earnings weeks on record.

6. European Earnings Deluge: Shell Profit Doubles, BMW/SocGen/Sanofi/Schneider All Beat

European earnings season reached a crescendo on Thursday, with blue-chip companies delivering a wave of beats:

  • Shell: Q2 adjusted earnings of $9.84 billion — more than double the $4.26 billion from a year ago, and the best quarterly profit since Q2 2022. The Iran war-driven oil price surge is pouring record cash into Big Oil's coffers.
  • BMW: Automotive profit beat expectations despite China demand headwinds.
  • Societe Generale: Raised full-year profitability targets and announced a €1.5 billion ($1.7B) share buyback.
  • Sanofi: Profit growth beat estimates; raised full-year guidance.
  • BBVA: Q2 net income exceeded analyst forecasts.
  • Schneider Electric: Raised both sales and profit guidance.
  • ING Groep: Q2 profit topped analyst estimates.
  • Anheuser-Busch InBev: Volume growth narrowly missed expectations.
  • Adidas: Missed estimates.

European equity markets were mixed: the pan-European Stoxx 600 edged up 0.16%, France's CAC 40 rose 0.56%, while the UK's FTSE 100 and Germany's DAX traded marginally lower. Energy and mining sectors led gains, while autos underperformed.

Looking ahead, JPMorgan noted in its latest report that the AI value chain is undergoing a critical divergence: "Companies selling chips and equipment continue to thrive, while the cloud hyperscalers bearing massive AI investments are seeing their stocks and free cash flow come under pressure." The market's central question has shifted from "Will AI change the world?" to "Can AI make money fast enough?" — and this earnings season is providing the answer.
