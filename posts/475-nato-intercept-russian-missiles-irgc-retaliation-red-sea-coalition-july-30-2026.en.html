⚡ July 30 Evening World Affairs: NATO Weighs Shooting Down Russian Missiles in Historic Policy Shift, IRGC Vows Retaliation After US Strikes Kill 3 Including Toddler, Saudi Arabia Announces 50-Nation Red Sea Coalition

1. NATO Considers Intercepting Russian Missiles Approaching Allied Borders: Landmark Policy Shift

Polish Deputy Foreign Minister Andrzej Szejna revealed on Thursday that NATO is actively analyzing options to shoot down Russian missiles approaching the borders of alliance member states. The statement signals what would be one of the most significant shifts in NATO's defensive posture since its founding in 1949.

"The alliance is analyzing various concepts, including shooting down such missiles when they are very close to NATO borders," Szejna told journalists. "But this would have to be done with the consent of the Ukrainian side and with due regard to international consequences — then NATO missiles would hit Russian missiles outside their territory."

The announcement came hours after a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile crashed into a field in eastern Poland's Lublin province near the village of Tarnawa-Kolonia, leaving a massive crater approximately 10 meters wide and 5 meters deep. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said all indications point to a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, with fragment analysis ongoing.

"We were prepared to shoot down the missile if it had continued its flight." — Polish PM Donald Tusk at an emergency government meeting

According to the Polish military's operational command, the timeline unfolded as follows:

  • 3:40 AM (local time) — Polish radar detected an unidentified object moving westward inside Polish airspace. An F-16 fighter was scrambled.
  • 3:46 AM — Radar signal disappeared. The six-minute window was insufficient to complete NATO peacetime rules of engagement requiring visual identification and command authorization before firing.
  • ~4:00 AM — Residents reported a loud explosion. Police found a crater in a field approximately 2 km from the nearest homes.

Military analysts noted that the Kh-101 cruise missile, flying at 700-900 km/h at treetop altitude, covered the distance before Poland's authorization architecture could complete its decision cycle. This marks the most serious Russian munition incursion into NATO territory since the war began in 2022, with the missile penetrating approximately 90-100 km into Poland before crashing.

NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe General Alexus G. Grynkewich said the alliance would "continue to take all necessary measures to defend NATO territory." Two NATO F-16s, a NATO tanker aircraft, and a Polish early warning aircraft were deployed in response.

2. IRGC Vows to 'Punish the Aggressor Today' After US Strikes Kill 3 on Qeshm Island

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) vowed through state media on Thursday to "punish the aggressor today," responding to a fresh wave of heavy US airstrikes across Iran.

The US Central Command (CENTCOM) said it completed a two-hour "heavy wave of strikes" against Iran from 8:00 PM to 10:00 PM ET Wednesday (00:00-02:00 GMT Thursday). The strikes targeted "dozens of Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets in Iran, including military command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and defense sites, and maritime capabilities."

Iranian state media reported that a US missile struck a residential area on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, killing at least three people including a two-year-old child. Explosions were also reported in Fars and Khuzestan provinces. The IRGC statement warned that countries aiding the United States would also face a "harsh response."

President Donald Trump responded during a Fox News interview: "It's our turn and we'll see if we get there with an agreement at some point, but we're going to hit them very hard." He said he had been briefed on the drone strike on a US-owned gas tanker at Egypt's Damietta port.

Separately, the IRGC Navy announced it had intercepted and detained three "violating" oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, accusing them of using unauthorized routes. The IRGC asserted it maintains "full control" of the strategic waterway.

Jordan intercepted Iranian missiles for the second consecutive day. The Jordanian Armed Forces shot down five ballistic missiles launched from Iran, with no casualties reported. Saudi Arabia's Foreign Ministry "strongly condemned" Iran's continued attacks on Jordan, calling them a violation of international law and good neighborly principles.

3. Saudi Arabia to Announce 50-Nation Red Sea Coalition

Saudi Arabia is moving to form an international coalition of approximately 50 nations to protect Red Sea shipping from Houthi attacks, sources told Reuters and Al-Monitor. The coalition could be formally announced as early as Thursday, headquartered in Riyadh under Saudi command.

Red Sea littoral states Egypt, Sudan, Djibouti, and Somalia have already confirmed participation. Invitations have been extended to the United States, Pakistan, Turkey, and European nations including France, Germany, and Italy. However, sources said Washington's participation remains uncertain — "everyone is waiting for the US to show its hand. Without real assets, this initiative won't take off."

Houthis Consider Red Sea Transit Fees

After declaring a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia on July 20, Yemen's Houthi rebels have repeatedly attacked Saudi oil tankers. New reports indicate the Houthis are considering imposing transit fees on commercial shipping in the Red Sea — mirroring Iran's approach in the Strait of Hormuz. Yemen's Information Minister Moammar al-Eryani described the plan as "a dangerous escalation aimed at transforming one of the world's most strategic maritime corridors into a permanent source of funding for the militia's military and terrorist activities."

Since July 20, the Houthis have claimed multiple attacks on Saudi vessels. Saudi warplanes have conducted retaliatory airstrikes on Houthi-held Hodeidah port. The crisis has forced Saudi Arabia to redirect much of its oil exports from the Hormuz route to the Red Sea port of Yanbu — which now falls within Houthi strike range. In peacetime, the Strait of Hormuz handles approximately 20% of global oil and gas supplies; the current conflict has disrupted an estimated 25% of world energy supplies.

4. IRGC Claims Destruction of US Radar Systems in Kuwait

The IRGC announced on Thursday that it had successfully destroyed multiple US radar systems in Kuwait as part of its latest retaliatory operations. The statement said targets included an early warning radar and tactical radar complex near Ali al-Salem Airbase, as well as a radar system on Kuwait's Bubiyan Island — all rendered "inoperable."

This follows an Iranian missile strike on a Chinese company building in northern Kuwait that killed one worker. Kuwait's Defense Ministry has confirmed the incident. Analysts say the conflict is expanding across the Persian Gulf region at an unprecedented pace.

5. UN Warns of Widening Conflict; Pakistan Mediates

UN Secretary-General António Guterres, through deputy spokesperson Farhan Haq, urged the US and Iran to stop fighting and return to negotiations. "The Secretary-General has been worried for some time that if the parties do not return to negotiations, you could have a widening of the conflict," Haq said, noting that UN-backed mediation efforts led by Pakistan, Qatar and other countries remain active.

Pakistan's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Tahir Andrabi said Islamabad is making "utmost" efforts to bring both sides back to full talks under the memorandum of understanding, with progress reported on Strait of Hormuz discussions.

"Once the door to negotiations has been opened, it should not be closed. As long as there is hope for peace, it should not be abandoned." — Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi at the SCO Foreign Ministers' Meeting

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi met with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi on the sidelines of the SCO meeting in Kyrgyzstan, emphasizing that the memorandum of understanding signed a month ago represents a valuable outcome of multilateral mediation efforts and should not be discarded.

6. US Military Tightens OpSec: Frontline Troops May Lose Phones

In a Reuters exclusive, US Central Command Admiral Brad Cooper warned in an internal memo that Iran is using news reports and photos/videos uploaded by US troops to social media to assess missile strike damage in real time. Some troops stationed at forward bases in Jordan have been notified that smartphones may be confiscated in the coming days.

Pentagon data shows US military casualties since the February 28 campaign stand at 642, including 18 killed. The Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS) estimates the US has fewer than 1,000 Patriot interceptors and fewer than 250 THAAD interceptors remaining. War spending has reached $37.5 billion.

On the domestic political front, Politico reports growing unease among Republicans over the war's political toll. Rising fuel prices, US casualties, and the conflict's uncertain trajectory pose a challenge for President Trump and his party ahead of the November midterm elections.
