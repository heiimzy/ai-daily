⚡ July 31 Financial Roundup: Gold Breaks Above $4,100, Strategy Posts $8.2B Quarterly Loss, Indian Oil's First Loss in 15 Years, Oil Inches Higher

Gold: Spot Price Reclaims the $4,100 Level

Spot gold broke back above the $4,100-per-ounce mark around midday on Friday (July 31). A Yahoo Finance market report updated at 12:34 GMT said gold had "finally broken above $4,100," linking the move to the U.S. pause in airstrikes overnight. The metal had been under pressure earlier in the session: at 8:05 a.m. ET, spot gold was at $4,056.13, down 0.63% and $25.57 below the prior close of $4,081.70 — before buyers staged a rapid comeback, reclaiming the $4,100 round number in a swift rally of roughly $50. Hours earlier (around 09:03 GMT), gold had been hovering near $4,062. Over a longer horizon, gold traded at $3,284.65 a year ago, up 23.49% over the past 12 months; it remains about a quarter below its 52-week high of $5,477.79.

Strategy: Former MicroStrategy Posts $8.2B Quarterly Loss, Still Holds 843,000+ Bitcoin

The Q2 earnings fallout from Strategy — the bitcoin treasury company led by Michael Saylor and formerly known as MicroStrategy — continued to dominate crypto-market conversation on Friday. A report published by qz.com at 13:00 GMT confirmed that Strategy recorded an $8.2 billion loss in the second quarter as the bitcoin price declined. Yahoo Finance noted the dramatic swing: a year ago the company was sitting on roughly $10 billion in profits; now it has flipped to an $8.2 billion loss in the span of one year. Benzinga reported that Strategy still holds more than 843,000 bitcoin, making it the largest corporate bitcoin holder in the world. Despite the ugly headline numbers, MSTR shares actually rose about 4.4% — investors are more focused on the "bitcoin proxy" stock's next accumulation moves and dividend policy, with some analysts expecting it may sell more bitcoin to fund dividend payments.

India: IOC Swings to First Quarterly Loss in 15 Years as Refining Margins Collapse

Indian Oil Corporation (IOC), India's largest refiner, reported a net loss of Rs 2,661 crore (about $310 million) for the first quarter of fiscal 2026-27 — its first quarterly loss in 15 years — even as revenue grew 26.2% year on year. Reuters, reporting at 12:55 GMT, attributed the loss to surging crude costs from the Iran war squeezing refining margins. NDTV Profit noted the loss still came in sharply better than the worst-case street estimate. CNBC TV18 reported that IOC swung to a loss despite higher fuel sales, underscoring just how severely refining margins have collapsed. BPCL and HPCL, two other state-owned Indian refiners, posted losses earlier this month as well — the impact of high oil prices on India's energy system is becoming fully visible, and markets are watching whether the government will step in with fuel subsidies or other relief measures.

Crude Oil: Prices Inch Higher After Iran Strikes US Bases; Hormuz Uncertainty Lingers

Oil prices moved higher on Friday: OilPrice.com live quotes showed WTI crude at $85.23 per barrel, up $1.64 (+1.96%), and Brent at $90.41 per barrel, up $1.38 (+1.55%). The trigger was Iran's strikes on US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain (reported by OilPrice.com at 12:30 GMT), which reignited the geopolitical risk premium. A companion market analysis published at 12:00 GMT noted that uncertainty over the Strait of Hormuz continues to keep oil markets on edge. The BBC reported that UK petrol prices have climbed to their highest level since the Iran war began. At the same time, markets are watching the partial recovery of Hormuz transits — shipping traffic through the strait is picking up again, which is offsetting some of the war premium and keeping the advance choppy rather than one-directional.

Market Wrap: Futures Rise, Crypto Pulls Back, Eurozone Inflation Rebounds

US stock futures rose on Friday as investors digested mega-cap tech earnings: Amazon's better-than-expected cloud results lifted risk appetite (Investor's Business Daily, 12:00 GMT), while Apple stayed under pressure on weak revenue guidance, with CEO Tim Cook flagging the "increasing impact" of the memory shortage (Yahoo Finance, 13:04 GMT). Cryptocurrencies pulled back in the morning session: Yahoo Personal Finance (13:06 GMT) reported bitcoin and ethereum prices retreating early in the day. On the macro front, Eurostat reported earlier in the day that eurozone inflation rebounded to 2.9% in July, above the prior reading, driven by persistently high energy prices — firming expectations for a possible ECB rate hike in September. European stocks had already hit record highs, extending their outperformance versus the US. Notably, safe-haven gold and risk assets such as US stock futures rallied together, reflecting intensifying two-way bets amid geopolitical conflict and policy uncertainty.
