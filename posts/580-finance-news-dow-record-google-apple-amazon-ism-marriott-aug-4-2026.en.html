⚡ Aug 4 Finance Update: Dow Closes at a Record High, Google Overtakes Apple to Become World's No. 2, Amazon Tops $3 Trillion, ISM Manufacturing PMI Hits a 4-Year High

In the early hours of August 4 Beijing time, US stocks got August off to a strong start on Monday (US time, August 3) as easing US-Iran tensions sent oil prices down more than 5% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at a record high while the Nasdaq jumped over 2%. At the individual-stock level, Google's parent Alphabet overtook Apple to become the world's second-most-valuable company, and Amazon's market value topped $3 trillion for the first time. On the macro front, the July ISM manufacturing PMI jumped to 55.6, its highest since May 2022. Here is this finance roundup.

1. Strong US Open: Dow Closes at a Record High, Nasdaq Rises Over 2%

On Monday, August 3 (US time), all three major US indexes closed higher: the Dow rose 693.38 points (+1.32%) to a record closing high of 53,178.41; the S&P 500 gained 110.78 points (+1.48%) to 7,600.50; and the Nasdaq Composite climbed 540.04 points (+2.13%) to 25,913.90. After a volatile July, risk appetite staged a clear recovery on the first trading day of August — the S&P 500 posted a two-month high, the Dow a 3.5-week high and the Nasdaq 100 rose 1.78%.

The core catalyst was easing geopolitical risk: President Trump canceled a planned strike on Iran over the weekend and said talks would resume on Monday, sending international oil prices sharply lower — WTI fell 5.1% to $80.34 a barrel and Brent dropped 4.7% to $83.77. That soothed inflation fears and pushed the 10-year Treasury yield down about 5 basis points to 4.68%. Large-cap tech led the advance: Meta rose over 6%, Microsoft and Alphabet each gained about 5%, Amazon climbed about 4.6% to an all-time high, and Nvidia rose about 3%; the communication services sector was the session's strongest. New York Fed President John Williams also struck a reassuring tone, saying inflation pressures would ease gradually and that rates are well positioned.

2. Google Overtakes Apple as World's No. 2; Amazon Clears $3 Trillion for the First Time

On Monday, Alphabet, Google's parent, rose about 3.4% to a market cap of roughly $4.51 trillion, officially overtaking Apple (about $4.48 trillion, down 0.7% on the day) to become the world's second-most-valuable listed company; Nvidia held the top spot at roughly $5 trillion. Apple fell 0.7% on the day, dropping its valuation to $4.48 trillion and slipping to third globally — the divergence between the AI narrative and consumer-electronics prospects is reshuffling the global market-cap pecking order.

The same day, Amazon rose about 4% to a record high, with its market value crossing $3 trillion for the first time, joining Nvidia, Apple, Microsoft and Alphabet in the exclusive "$3 trillion club." It took Amazon just over two years to go from $2 trillion (June 2024) to $3 trillion. Friday's Q2 report showed AWS growing at its fastest pace in more than four years, and the company raised its full-year capital spending guidance — AI and cloud demand are the main engines behind the sustained rally, prompting a wave of Wall Street price-target hikes.

3. July ISM Manufacturing PMI Rises to 55.6, a Four-Year High

At 10:00 a.m. ET on Monday (10:00 p.m. Beijing time), the ISM reported its July manufacturing PMI at 55.6, up 2.3 percentage points from June's 53.3, above the 54.0 consensus and the highest since May 2022 (55.9) — the seventh straight month of manufacturing expansion and the 21st consecutive month of overall economic growth. The sub-indices were broadly stronger: new orders at 56.7 (seven straight months of expansion), production at 58.5 — the best since November 2021 — and the employment index at 52.8, back in expansion for the first time in 33 months (its highest since August 2022). The prices index eased to 71.1 from June's 73.0 but remains elevated, and new export orders returned to expansion at 53.0.

But the strong data also carry a warning: the ISM report noted some firms compared current price volatility to the Covid era, and sticky inflation worries could add pressure on the Fed to hike in September — rate futures currently price roughly a 63%-64.5% chance of a 25 bp hike at the September 15-16 FOMC meeting (the funds rate stands at 3.50%-3.75%). Analysts note manufacturers are front-loading orders to avoid supply disruptions and higher costs, while strong AI-related investment has partly offset the impact of tariffs, and low inventories leave room for further expansion.

4. Marriott's Mixed Q2: EPS Beat but Revenue Miss, Shares Slide Over 7%

Before Monday's open, Marriott International (Nasdaq: MAR) reported Q2 with adjusted EPS of $3.19, up 20.4% year over year and about 4.2% above the consensus; however, revenue of $7.07 billion, up 4.8% year over year, came in about 2.6% below estimates, and worldwide RevPAR rose just 3.4%, reflecting solid US/Canada growth offset by the Middle East conflict dampening international travel. Soft Q3 profit guidance was the biggest drag on the session.

Shares of the hotel operator fell more than 7% to about $345.95, with volume at 1.6x the 200-day average; Stifel cut its price target to $343 from $365 on the same day. The market's message is clear: at a valuation near 39x earnings, investors are focused on demand trends, RevPAR growth and the hit to travel from Middle East risk — not an EPS beat in isolation. "A profit beat is only the entry ticket; the growth engine and forward guidance are where pricing power is won."

5. After Hours and the Week Ahead: Palantir Jumps 12%, AMD and SpaceX's First Report, Friday Non-farm

On the after-hours earnings front, Palantir (Nasdaq: PLTR) confirmed Q2 results: revenue of $1.935 billion, up 93% year over year, adjusted EPS of $0.41 and net income of $1.062 billion, all beating estimates. The company raised its full-year outlook for the third straight quarter to $8.15 billion (up about 82%), with U.S. commercial revenue expected to grow at least 134%, and lifted adjusted operating income and free cash flow guidance. Shares jumped about 12%-13% after hours — a sharp contrast to earlier "sell the news" fears and a vivid sign of resilience in AI application-layer demand.

This is a dense week: on Tuesday (August 4) both AMD and SpaceX (its first report since going public) report after the close, offering fresh reads on AI data center chip demand and commercial-space commercialization, with McDonald's, Costco and Disney also due. On the data front, Tuesday's June JOLTS job openings and Wednesday's ADP payrolls tee up Friday's (August 7) July US employment report — a Reuters poll points to roughly 83,000-88,000 jobs added and a 4.3% unemployment rate. With US-Iran diplomacy seesawing between "talks and no talks," earnings and jobs data will have to bear much of the market's pricing weight.

From the Dow's record close and a four-year high in the ISM manufacturing PMI, to Google passing Apple and Amazon joining the $3 trillion club — Monday's strong open was both a direct repricing of easing US-Iran tensions and a reflection of the market rebalancing between the "AI narrative" and "sticky inflation." The jobs report and mega-cap tech earnings in the coming days will decide whether this rally has legs.
