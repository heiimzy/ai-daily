⚡ Aug 10 World Update: Drone Strike Kills 13 in Tatarstan, Israel Demolishes Homes in Southern Lebanon, Kashmir Vote Shrinks, Thai Ex-MP Confesses to Shooting on YouTube

Several tense threads converge on the international stage this Monday morning: a massive Ukrainian drone attack on Russia's Tatarstan kills at least 13 people and injures 39 in Nizhnekamsk, in what Moscow calls one of the deadliest strikes on Russian soil since the war began; meanwhile Israel bulldozes homes in two southern Lebanese villages even as US-mediated talks are under way; Pakistan-administered Kashmir's third-phase legislative election shrinks sharply with only four of 11 constituencies voting on schedule after unrest has killed more than 50; and in Thailand, a former MP shoots a former police official, then dials into a YouTube livestream to confess. Here are the details, with all source publication times verified.

1. Ukrainian Drone Strike on Tatarstan: 13 Dead, 39 Injured in Nizhnekamsk

According to BBC News (published Aug 10, 08:50 GMT), a Ukrainian drone attack on the Russian republic of Tatarstan killed at least 13 people, including one child, and injured 39 more — described as one of the deadliest single attacks on Russia since Moscow launched its full-scale war on Ukraine.

The strikes hit Nizhnekamsk, more than 1,100 km from the Ukrainian border and home to several major oil refineries and a large petrochemical company. Unverified social-media footage shows plumes of smoke and flames rising into the sky amid air-raid sirens. Moscow confirmed the attack targeted both industrial facilities and residential areas, saying "civilian individuals who were not involved in any military operations were present," according to Russia's Investigative Committee. Tatarstan leader Rustam Minnikhanov offered condolences, and Nizhnekamsk mayor Radmir Belayev called it "a tremendous pain for all of us," with a mourning period declared. Russia said its air defences shot down more than 450 drones overnight. Kyiv had not commented on the attack as of publication (Source: BBC).

2. Israel Demolishes Homes in Southern Lebanon Amid US-Mediated Talks

According to Al Jazeera (news footage, published around 09:20 GMT on Aug 10), Israeli earthmovers are demolishing homes in the southern Lebanese villages of Zawtar al-Sharqiyah and Zawtar al-Gharbiyah while tanks advance through the area — despite US-mediated negotiations between Lebanon and Israel.

Witnesses described anguished locals watching their homes being razed. The continued ground operations and demolitions in the south, even as external mediation appears to advance, highlight a stark gap between diplomatic progress and on-the-ground realities, further straining an already tense humanitarian and security situation along the border (Source: Al Jazeera).

3. Pakistan-Administered Kashmir: Third-Phase Vote Shrinks to 4 of 11 Constituencies

According to Al Jazeera (by Abid Hussain from Islamabad, published around 08:05 GMT), voting is under way in the third and final phase of Pakistan-administered Kashmir's (Azad Jammu and Kashmir, AJK) Legislative Assembly elections, but only four of the 11 constituencies originally scheduled are going to the polls because of unrest.

More than 800 polling booths in the Bagh and Haveli districts of Poonch division opened at 8am Monday, with about 460,000 people registered. AJK Prime Minister Faisal Mumtaz Rathore (PPP) is defending his seat in Haveli, while Bagh features two former prime ministers — Sardar Tanveer Ilyas (Istehkam-e-Pakistan) against Sardar Attique Ahmed Khan (Muslim Conference). The remaining seven constituencies (five in Poonch, two in Sudhnoti) were postponed last week over security concerns, with a new date to be announced. The postponement followed weeks of protests by the Jammu Kashmir Joint Awami Action Committee (JAAC), which demands the abolition of 12 seats reserved for displaced Kashmiris living elsewhere in Pakistan. Violence during JAAC-led protests has killed more than 50 people, according to residents and rights groups; the government has not shared an official toll but said at least seven police and paramilitary personnel were killed. JAAC, which has called for a boycott of the elections, was banned in June under "antiterrorism" laws, and international media have been barred from reporting in the region. Turnout in the first two phases (46% and 53%) was below the 60%+ recorded in 2021 and 2016, and an intermittent internet shutdown around Poonch has stretched beyond 50 days (Source: Al Jazeera).

4. Thai Ex-MP Shoots Former Police Official, Then Confesses on YouTube Livestream

According to BBC News (published Aug 10, 09:51 GMT, marked "13 minutes ago"), former Thai MP Chalong Riewrang allegedly shot dead a local official — former police officer Thongchai Yenprasert — in Nonthaburi province, just north of Bangkok.

Chalong reportedly dialled into a YouTube livestream and told the shocked host, political pundit Anchalee Paireerak, that he had shot Thongchai outside his office. Thongchai, hit in the neck, died in hospital; his driver was shot in the arm and hand. Chalong was arrested but then allowed to hold a press conference at a police station, where he calmly explained events while smoking a cigarette — saying Thongchai was "angry" at him (without explaining why) and that he had not intended to shoot his "good friend." Local media said the two had a personal dispute; Chalong claimed he had only wanted to talk and that Thongchai then "pushed me…" before the account cut off. The bizarre "kill-then-confess-live" episode has stunned Thailand (Source: BBC).
