⚡ August 1 Finance News: South Korea July Exports +62.8% to Record Second-High, Bessent's To-Do List Shows $5-10B Yen Buy Plan, Brent Up 20% in July, India Price Hikes Loom, US Faces Jobs & Earnings Week

South Korea's July Exports +62.8% to $98.89 Billion, Semiconductors Top $40 Billion for Second Straight Month

South Korea's trade data for July, released by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy on Saturday (August 1), showed exports surging 62.8% year-on-year to $98.89 billion — the second-highest monthly figure on record and comfortably above the +59.0% expected in a Reuters poll. Imports rose 29.7%, leaving a trade surplus of $30.3 billion (Yonhap, 08:28 Beijing time; Newspim 08:25). Semiconductors remained the dominant engine: chip exports jumped 178.8% year-on-year and topped $40 billion for a second consecutive month (Asia Economy 08:13; Edaily 08:53), as the memory-chip supercycle continues to power Korea's bellwether trade gauge. By market, shipments to the US stayed strong while exports to China recovered on AI-chip demand; it was the fifth straight month exports exceeded $80 billion. Analysts note that despite the violent KOSPI swings this week (including a historic one-day surge of around 18%), the resilience of export data shows the AI-compute-driven semiconductor upcycle is far from over — an important support for Q3 GDP growth and the won.

Reuters Exclusive: Bessent's 'To-Do List' Shows Plan to Buy $5-10 Billion of Yen

A photo of US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's handwritten "to-do list" from a Camp David meeting appears to show he is considering buying $5 billion to $10 billion worth of yen, according to an exclusive Reuters report (Reuters via Devdiscourse, 06:56 Beijing time; The Guardian followed at 08:43). The list, which includes the line "Buy Yen $5-10bn," reinforces market speculation of coordinated US-Japan FX intervention. The Guardian noted that while such a size would be far smaller than the Bank of Japan's own interventions, direct yen purchases by the US Treasury itself would be extremely rare — arguably historic — since Washington almost never intervenes directly in the dollar-yen rate. Over the past week, the yen repeatedly faced suspected intervention as it approached the 160 level; Japanese media reported officials spent roughly ¥8.45 trillion on Thursday (July 30) and stepped in again on Friday, while the Treasury warned banks it might intervene in the dollar-yen rate (WSJ 01:49; Wall Street CN 06:15). By 08:50 Beijing time, the dollar-yen was trading near 156, notably stronger than before the interventions.

Oil: Brent Rises More Than 20% in July, Then Retreats on Hormuz Transit News

Crude wrapped up July with a striking monthly gain: Brent jumped more than 20% over the month — the strongest monthly performance since March — while the WTI-Brent spread widened sharply on concerns about Strait of Hormuz shipping disruptions (TechStock², 19:26 Beijing time; Reuters settlement wrap-up 11:00). But oil gave back some gains at the start of August: reports of improving transit conditions through the Strait of Hormuz, alongside profit-taking, pushed both Brent and WTI off their highs (IndexBox, 08:32 Beijing time). Earlier in the week, news that Iran had stopped vessels transiting Hormuz briefly sent prices to multi-month highs, underscoring how sensitive the market remains to Middle East supply disruptions. Oil had also spiked on US-Iran escalation earlier in July before swinging on ceasefire hopes. Analysts say that as long as transit risks at Hormuz are not fully resolved, elevated volatility in crude is likely to persist, with OPEC+ supply policy and Middle East developments set to be the next key drivers in August.

Indian Firms Plan Fresh Price Hikes, Testing the Inflation Outlook

Bloomberg reports that Indian companies are preparing another round of price increases, which would test the central bank's inflation outlook (Bloomberg, 07:00 Beijing time). Citing corporate surveys, the report says multiple Indian FMCG, food and energy firms are ready to raise prices over the coming weeks amid high crude and raw-material costs and logistics pressure; domestic petrol and diesel prices have already risen by Rs 7 per litre over 11 days. The Reserve Bank of India has kept core inflation within its target band, but the lagged pass-through of energy costs is now showing up. Economists warn that if the price hikes broaden, India's CPI could drift back toward the 4% upper end of the policy target, shrinking room for rate cuts this year. The plans also illustrate the shared predicament of emerging markets under imported inflation — Asian economies highly sensitive to oil are reassessing their price outlooks in the aftermath of the US-Iran conflict.

US Stocks Ahead: July Jobs Report and a Big Earnings Week

After a turbulent July (the S&P 500 slipped for the month — its first July decline since 2014 — as the momentum trade suffered its biggest wipeout since 2000), US stocks face two heavy catalysts next week: the July nonfarm payrolls report and a slate of big tech and consumer earnings (Reuters Wall St Week Ahead, 05:43 Beijing time; republished by Investing.com at 09:00). With the market "teetering," strategists say the jobs data will directly shape whether the Fed holds or resumes hikes at its September meeting — June PCE inflation is still running at 3.7%, and several Fed officials publicly argued for a hike this week. On earnings, results from major tech names next week will test whether AI capital spending can keep supporting valuations. Reuters cites strategists saying a strong payrolls print and solid earnings could help stocks stabilize; otherwise, a second round of momentum unwinding could hit markets again. Investors should brace for volatility around the payrolls release on Friday (August 7).
