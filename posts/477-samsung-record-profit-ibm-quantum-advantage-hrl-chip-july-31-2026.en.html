⚡ July 31 Tech Roundup: Samsung Q2 Profit Soars 1,814% to All-Time High, IBM Verifies Quantum Advantage, HRL Builds Self-Running Quantum Chip

📊 Samsung Posts Record Q2 Results: Operating Profit Hits 89.5T Won ($62B), AI Chip Demand Driving Unprecedented Memory Boom

On July 30, Samsung Electronics announced second-quarter 2026 financial results that shattered records across the board. The company posted an operating profit of 89.5 trillion won ($62 billion) for the April-June period — a staggering 1,814% surge year-over-year and a 56% increase from the prior quarter. Revenue reached 171.5 trillion won ($118.7 billion), up 130% year-over-year, also an all-time high.

The earnings were overwhelmingly driven by Samsung's Device Solutions (DS) division, which oversees the semiconductor business. The DS division generated approximately 127.5 trillion won in revenue and 89.2 trillion won in operating profit — essentially the entire company's profit. Record-breaking sales volumes in both DRAM and NAND flash memory, fueled by surging AI server demand for memory chips, powered the division's performance.

"The AI infrastructure buildout is still paying its suppliers handsomely, and Samsung expects more to come, pointing to strong memory demand in the second half as agentic AI adds another layer of appetite for its chips." — Josh Gilbert, Lead Analyst for APAC at eToro

HBM4 Ramps Up, HBM4E Samples Ship First

Samsung revealed it has significantly ramped up shipments of its sixth-generation High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4) and became the industry's first to ship HBM4E samples to major customers. HBM4E is an enhanced version designed to power advanced AI processors including Nvidia's Vera Rubin platform. Third-quarter HBM revenue is projected to more than triple from the previous quarter, with HBM4E expected to account for well over 60% of total HBM revenue in the second half of 2026.

Memory Shortage to Persist Through 2028

During the earnings call, Samsung issued a stark warning: despite aggressive capacity expansion, AI-driven memory demand is growing faster than the industry can add supply. Given the more than 3.5-year lead time from new fab construction to wafer production, Samsung expects the supply-demand imbalance to intensify in 2027 and persist into 2028. The company has already completed long-term supply agreements with five major global data center customers and is in final-stage talks with five additional ones.

800 Billion Won Investment in Chip Startups and Robotics

On the same day, Samsung's board approved two venture funds totaling 800 billion won ($560 million) — 500 billion won for semiconductor startup investments and 300 billion won for Device eXperience (DX) future technologies. Samsung also elevated its robotics business directly under the CEO, creating an RX Business Office and appointing former Boston Dynamics strategist Lee Dong-gun to lead strategy. The company plans to build a robot pilot line in Gumi, initially targeting manufacturing and logistics before expanding into multipurpose humanoids and consumer applications.

Foundry AI Revenue Share Jumps to 30%

Samsung Foundry reported that AI/HPC customer revenue share has jumped from the high-teens percentage last year to over 30% this year. The company will begin mass production of second-generation 2nm mobile chips in the second half and is manufacturing Nvidia's Grok3 LPU. Samsung's first Taylor, Texas fab will come online this year, with a second fab breaking ground by year-end targeting 2030 production. Advanced process lines (8nm and below) are running at near-full capacity.

🔬 IBM and University of Chicago Achieve Verifiable Quantum Advantage: 70 Logical Qubits Crack Classically Intractable Problem in 15 Minutes

On July 30, IBM and researchers from the University of Chicago announced a landmark breakthrough in quantum computing: a verifiable demonstration of quantum advantage using 70 logical qubits, completing a computation that would be infeasible for classical supercomputers.

In their paper "Sampling hard circuits with verifiably high fidelity," the team proposed a novel construction of encoded quantum circuits. Traditional Random Circuit Sampling (RCS) benchmarks can demonstrate quantum superiority but make verification itself computationally prohibitive. IBM's team designed a structured alternative to RCS that preserves the same hardness criteria while enabling error detection during the computation — effectively allowing the quantum computer to both win the race and provide video replay proving it didn't cheat.

Technical Breakthrough Details

In one of the world's largest error correction demonstrations to date, the team executed 70 logical qubits, running 2,415 logical two-qubit operations and 468 logical T gates — key metrics quantifying quantum circuit complexity. Because the circuit was encoded, effective logical error rates were 10 times lower than physical error rates, maintaining remarkable fidelity even at these large gate counts. The quantum computation completed in approximately 15 minutes, while leading classical simulation approaches faced prohibitive runtimes.

"We are now firmly in the quantum advantage era. We have demonstrated a quantum computation beyond the practical reach of classical computers that establishes, with statistical confidence, a lower bound on how faithfully it was executed. This milestone gives scientists, developers, and businesses a new foundation for trusting quantum computers as they scale to problems far beyond what we can achieve classically." — Jay Gambetta, Director of IBM Research and IBM Fellow

IBM also announced parallel quantum advantage demonstrations with partners Algorithmiq and Qedma. All circuits and results have been openly released on the Quantum Advantage Tracker.

⚙️ HRL Labs Publishes Self-Running Silicon Quantum Chip in Nature — IBM Had Already Acquired It Six Days Earlier

On July 29, HRL Laboratories published a groundbreaking result in Nature: a silicon quantum processor that effectively runs itself. The Malibu-based research lab, jointly owned by Boeing and General Motors, demonstrated an 18-qubit silicon spin quantum processor whose controller operates at 4 kelvin (approximately -269°C), inside the cryostat alongside the qubits themselves—rather than in room-temperature electronics racks.

The custom chip achieved control errors ten times lower than any previous demonstration with this type of qubit, with each operation taking under a microsecond. When the team added more qubits to its error-correcting code, the error rate fell approximately fivefold — the "error suppression" effect every practical quantum computer will depend on.

Solving the Wiring Bottleneck

Every quantum computer faces a fundamental scaling trap: qubits sit near absolute zero while their control electronics remain at room temperature, each requiring dedicated signal lines running into the cold. Scaling to the millions of qubits a useful machine needs would create an impossible wiring tangle. HRL's solution embeds the controller in the cold — a custom chip at 4K generates all control signals for 18 qubits and, crucially, runs error correction autonomously with no real-time help from room-temperature electronics. HRL says this is a world first.

IBM's Foresight

The most intriguing detail is the timeline: IBM signed an agreement to acquire HRL on July 23 — six days before the Nature paper was public. IBM's quantum roadmap has bet on superconducting qubits, with its large-scale machine Starling due in 2029. HRL's silicon spin qubits represent a rival approach. Acquiring HRL serves as both a hedge and a strategic complement — both qubit types use silicon substrates, so HRL's control chips, interconnects, and packaging benefit IBM regardless of which architecture ultimately prevails.

"The technologies that enabled conventional computing weren't just the highest-performing, they were the ones that could be manufactured cheaply and at scale. We think quantum computing will follow a similar path. Our goal is to build these powerful computers using standard microchip production lines and fit each one inside a single refrigerator. This approach will keep production costs low and make the technology affordable enough to tackle a much wider variety of business and scientific problems." — Rob Vasquez, CEO of HRL Laboratories

IBM did not disclose the acquisition price. The deal is expected to close by the end of September. Boeing and GM will continue working with IBM after the transaction.
