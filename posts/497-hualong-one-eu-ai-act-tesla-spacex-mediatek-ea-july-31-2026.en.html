China's State Council Approves 8 Nuclear Power Units, Hualong One 2.0 Demo Projects Finalized

China National Nuclear Corporation (CNNC) announced on July 31 that the State Council has approved a total of eight nuclear power units across four projects: Zhejiang Jinqimen Phase II, Guangdong Taipingling Phase III, Liaoning Zhuanghe Phase I, and Shandong Laiyang Phase I. The meeting stressed that nuclear units must be built and operated to the world's highest safety standards, with full-chain, full-domain safety oversight to guarantee zero safety incidents.

Of the eight units, six — Jinqimen Phase II, Taipingling Phase III and Zhuanghe Phase I — will use Hualong One technology, and Jinqimen Phase II and Taipingling Phase III have been designated Hualong One 2.0 demonstration projects. Hualong One is a million-kilowatt-class Generation III pressurized water reactor developed by CNNC and CGN from more than 40 years of Chinese nuclear R&D and operations experience, often dubbed China's "national calling card." The 2.0 version is an advanced "Gen III-plus" design that incorporates operating feedback, adopts Chinese standards and indigenous software, and features optimized active and passive safety systems plus intelligent control and modular construction — sharply reducing project scope while improving safety, performance and economics. It is seen as a landmark step in moving Chinese nuclear technology from "running alongside" global leaders to "leading in key areas."

To date, 10 Hualong One units are in commercial operation at home and abroad, with another 37 approved and under construction — the largest fleet of Generation III reactors in the world. A single unit generates nearly 10 billion kWh per year, enough to power a mid-sized city of over one million people for a year, equivalent to cutting more than 3 million tonnes of standard coal and over 9 million tonnes of CO2 emissions annually. The program has also driven a supply chain of nearly 6,000 upstream and downstream companies.

EU AI Act Transparency Rules Take Effect Tomorrow: Chatbots Must Disclose They Are AI

According to China Central Television, the European Commission announced on July 31 that starting August 2, the Commission's AI Office and member-state authorities will jointly begin enforcing the EU AI Act's transparency requirements. Under the new rules, interactive AI systems such as chatbots must clearly inform users they are interacting with AI rather than a human. Deepfake images, videos and audio generated or edited by AI must be labeled, and AI-generated or modified content must carry machine-readable markings for identification and tracing.

The Commission said the measures aim to reduce deception and manipulation, help the public make more informed judgments, and give businesses clearer compliance requirements and implementation paths. Violations of transparency obligations can draw administrative fines of up to €7.5 million, or for companies, up to 1% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher. Breaches of prohibited AI practices can be fined up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover.

The same day, the Commission published the first list of more than 180 institutions that have signed the AI-Generated Content Transparency Code of Practice, including Google, Microsoft, OpenAI, Amazon, Anthropic, Mistral AI and IBM — while Meta declined to join. Generative AI systems already in use before August 2, 2026 get a transition period until December 2, 2026. This is another key milestone for the world's first comprehensive AI regulation: the ban on unacceptable-risk practices such as social scoring and real-time biometric surveillance took effect in February 2025, and obligations for standalone high-risk AI systems will apply from December 2027.

WSJ Exclusive: Tesla Weighs Sale of China Business for Potential SpaceX Merger — Musk Swiftly Denies

The Wall Street Journal published an exclusive report on July 31 saying Tesla is weighing a sale of its China business to clear the way for a potential merger with SpaceX. The report quickly drew market attention, with Tesla (TSLA) shares jumping in pre-market trading as merger speculation heated up.

Elon Musk, however, immediately responded on social media, calling the report "absurdly fake news" and saying the topic "never even came up in a discussion." Barron's reported the same day that Tesla-SpaceX merger speculation is intensifying while Musk tries to cool it off. Tesla's Shanghai Gigafactory is one of its most important production bases worldwide, which is why the China business question has triggered widespread discussion. As of now, neither Tesla nor SpaceX has officially confirmed the WSJ report, and the story remains in a "rumor plus denial" phase — worth watching as it develops.

MediaTek Reports H1 2026 Results: Net Profit NT$48.49 Billion, Down 15.2% YoY

MediaTek released its 2026 first-half results on July 31: revenue of NT$301.33 billion (about RMB 62.95 billion), down 0.8% year over year; gross profit of NT$139.36 billion (about RMB 29.11 billion), down 5.64%, with gross margin of 46.25%, down 2.4 percentage points; net profit attributable to shareholders of NT$48.49 billion (about RMB 10.13 billion), down 15.2% YoY; and basic EPS of NT$30.44 (about RMB 6.4).

On the balance sheet, as of June 30, 2026, MediaTek had total assets of NT$787.44 billion (about RMB 164.50 billion) and total liabilities of NT$354.81 billion (about RMB 74.12 billion), for a debt-to-asset ratio of 45.06% — a moderate leverage level. As a major player in smartphone chips and AI edge computing, MediaTek's first-half profit decline reflects soft smartphone demand and intensifying competition. The market will now focus on the ramp-up of its flagship Dimensity products and on-device AI chips in the second half.

EA Confirms: $55 Billion Privatization Deal Closes Next Week

According to Engadget, Electronic Arts (EA) will complete its $55 billion (about RMB 398 billion) go-private transaction by August 4, officially becoming a private company. The deal is one of the largest acquisitions in gaming industry history and marks the delisting of the publisher behind blockbuster franchises such as FIFA (EA Sports FC), Apex Legends and The Sims.

Once private, EA will no longer face quarterly public-market earnings pressure, giving it more flexibility for long-cycle investments in game subscriptions and AI-assisted development. Industry watchers see the deal as part of accelerating consolidation in gaming — with development costs climbing and top IPs concentrating, major publishers are increasingly choosing the "privatization plus long-termism" path.
