⚡ July 30 Global Financial Data Blitz: US PCE Falls 0.1% for First Drop Since 2020, BoE Holds with Three Paths, Q2 GDP Slows, German CPI Hits 2.8%

1. US June PCE Falls 0.1% MoM: First Monthly Decline Since 2020

The US Bureau of Economic Analysis reported Thursday that the Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index fell 0.1% month-over-month in June — the first monthly decline since April 2020 and well below market expectations for a flat reading. Year-over-year, headline PCE slowed from 3.5% in May to 3.3%.

Core PCE (excluding food and energy) rose just 0.1% MoM, with the annual rate falling from 3.2% to 3.0% — the lowest since December 2024.

"This is a significant milestone on the path to inflation normalization. While one month doesn't make a trend, the -0.1% print suggests the Fed's tightening is finally biting."
— Mark Zandi, Chief Economist at Moody's Analytics

The 10-year Treasury yield briefly dipped about 5 basis points to 4.62% after the data, while the US Dollar Index slipped to 100.70. US equity futures modestly extended gains. However, analysts cautioned that surging oil prices from Middle East tensions could disrupt the disinflation trend.

2. US Q2 GDP Slows to 1.8%: Domestic Demand and AI Investment Hold Up

The first revision of Q2 2026 GDP showed annualized growth of 1.8%, down from Q1's 2.3% and below the 2.0% consensus, but underlying details revealed structural resilience.

Key GDP Components:

  • Personal consumption: +2.1%, contributing 1.4 percentage points
  • Business equipment investment: +4.8%, with AI data-center investment surging 37% YoY
  • Residential investment: -2.3%, still weighed down by high mortgage rates
  • Net exports: -0.6 ppt drag on growth
  • Government spending: +0.9%

Capital Economics' Paul Ashworth noted: "The GDP data confirm the US is on a soft landing trajectory — slowing but far from recession. The explosion in AI infrastructure investment is creating a new capex super-cycle."

3. Bank of England Holds at 3.75%: 7-2 Vote with Three Economic Paths

The Bank of England voted 7-2 to maintain its benchmark rate at 3.75%, in line with expectations. The accompanying Quarterly Inflation Report outlined three possible economic scenarios.

The Three Paths:

  1. Base case (55%): Inflation gradually returns to 2%, first rate cut in early 2027
  2. Hawkish path (25%): Middle East conflict sustains energy price pressure, forcing a BoE rate hike as early as November 2026
  3. Dovish path (20%): Economy slows faster than expected, rate cuts start in December 2026
"The degree of uncertainty facing monetary policy is unprecedented. We must walk a tightrope between suppressing inflation and avoiding recession. Developments in the Strait of Hormuz are the critical variable."
— Governor Andrew Bailey at the post-decision press conference

Sterling briefly dipped 0.3% to $1.3240 before recovering.

4. German CPI Surges to 2.8%: Energy Costs Shock Eurozone

Germany's preliminary July CPI came in at 2.8% YoY, up sharply from 2.3% in June and above the 2.5% consensus. Month-over-month inflation hit 0.5%, the largest increase in five months.

Energy prices surged 14.2% YoY, the primary driver. Disruptions to Red Sea-Suez Canal shipping and Strait of Hormuz tensions have pushed German wholesale gas and electricity prices significantly higher. Core CPI (excluding energy) was steady at 2.1% YoY.

The German 10-year Bund yield rose 6 basis points to 2.74%. European stock markets were mixed: FTSE 100 -0.12%, CAC 40 -0.45%, DAX -0.32%, with energy stocks (Shell +1.8%, BP +1.5%) outperforming.

5. US Jobless Claims Jump to 200K: Labour Market Cooling Signal

Initial jobless claims for the week ending July 25 rose to 200,000, up from 187,000 the prior week and above the 195,000 consensus. Continuing claims climbed to 1.782 million, the highest since March 2026.

Ian Shepherdson of Pantheon Macroeconomics commented: "The jump in claims warrants attention but doesn't yet signal recession. The labour market is normalizing from extremely tight levels — exactly what the Fed wants to see."

Markets are now focused on Friday's July nonfarm payrolls report, with consensus expectations for a slowdown from 216K in June to 185K.

6. China's Politburo: Stability Signals Without New Stimulus

The Politburo meeting on Thursday pledged to "deepen comprehensive reforms in capital market investment and financing to enhance market resilience and confidence." No new specific stimulus measures were announced, but the meeting stressed readiness to introduce incremental policies as conditions evolve.

China's A-shares closed weaker: Shanghai Composite -0.62% at 3,804.69, Shenzhen Component -2.73%, ChiNext -3.97%, STAR 50 -5.38%. Bank stocks were the lone bright spot — ICBC and CCB both hit record highs.
