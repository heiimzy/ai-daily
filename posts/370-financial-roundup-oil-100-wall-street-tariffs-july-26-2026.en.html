⚡ July 26 Financial Roundup: Brent Crosses $100 Then Retreats to $97, Wall Street Mixed With Chip Rout, Trump's 60-Nation Tariffs Take Effect

1. Oil Markets: Brent Breaches $100, Posts 10% Weekly Gain

Oil markets experienced extreme volatility this week. Brent crude surged to an intraday high of $102 on Thursday (July 23), closing at $100.69 — the first time above $100 since late May. But Friday (July 24) brought a sharp reversal: Pakistani officials revealed that China was pushing to restart US-Iran peace negotiations, and Brent plunged 3.9% to settle at $96.78.

Despite Friday's pullback, Brent still gained 9.85% for the week, while WTI crude rose 9.21%. Since July 10, Brent has surged 26.9%, reflecting the massive war premium from escalating Middle East tensions.

The immediate catalyst was the Houthi opening of a second front in the Red Sea. On July 25, Yemen's Houthi movement claimed responsibility for missile strikes targeting Saudi Aramco facilities in Jizan and Yanbu — the first direct assault on a Saudi refinery complex in four years. This followed the Houthis' July 20 announcement of a naval blockade on Saudi shipping and their July 22 attack on two Saudi oil tankers, the Encelia and Layla.

Analysts note that Strait of Hormuz crossings have plunged roughly 60%, and Red Sea shipping is now threatened simultaneously — both global energy chokepoints under pressure simultaneously. JPMorgan warned each additional month of disruption adds $7-8/barrel to Brent, potentially pushing monthly averages to $114 if disruptions extend to three months.

2. Wall Street: Dow Bucks the Trend, Nasdaq Falls for Second Week

Friday's (July 24) US stock market close was mixed:

  • Dow Jones Industrial Average: +235.60 pts (+0.46%) to 51,947.25, led by Apple's 3.5% gain
  • S&P 500: +0.05% to 7,411.98, posting its second straight weekly loss (-0.6%)
  • Nasdaq Composite: -161.87 pts (-0.64%) to 24,975.82, weekly loss of 2.1%

The semiconductor sector was hammered: Intel plunged nearly 8% despite strong Q2 earnings (buy-the-rumor sell-the-news); Micron Technology fell 7%; Broadcom dropped 2.7%. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) fell 3%, bringing its month-to-date losses to over 17%.

Three headwinds weighed on sentiment: ① Surging oil prices stoking inflation fears; ② AI capex anxiety — Alphabet raised its 2026 capex guidance to $195-205 billion, sending free cash flow to negative $5.9 billion; ③ Trump's new 60-nation tariffs adding global trade uncertainty.

3. Trump Tariffs: Section 301 Replaces Expired Section 122 on 60 Nations

On July 25, the US Section 122 global 10% tariff expired, and the Trump administration immediately replaced it with Section 301 "forced labor" tariffs covering 60 largest trading partners (59 countries + EU), with rates of 10% and 12.5%.

This marks the Trump administration's third tariff reconstruction after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs in February. The New York Times noted these tariffs arrive while the US-Iran war enters its fifth month with Brent crude at ~$97 — the global economy is in a far weaker position than on "Liberation Day" 2025, when oil was around $70.

ING economist Carsten Brzeski said: "These new trade tensions come at a moment in which the global economy, due to higher energy prices, is in a weaker spot than on Liberation Day last year." Canada has signaled retaliation, and pharmaceutical imports are explicitly threatened with future tariffs.

4. Bond Market & Fed: Rate Hike Odds Surge

The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.67% on Friday after hitting 4.71%, its highest since January 2025. The 30-year yield held near 5.16%, just off a 19-year high of 5.201%.

Oil-driven inflation expectations have repriced rate probabilities dramatically: the chance of a Fed rate hike at next week's FOMC meeting (July 28-29) surged from 10% to 36% in just one week, while September hike odds hit 80%. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh — a Trump appointee who has publicly emphasized AI's deflationary effects — now faces an energy shock and a still-tight labor market that severely constrain his policy options.

The European Central Bank held rates at 2.25% on Thursday but September hike odds sit around 70%. Germany's July PMI returned to expansion for the first time in four months, and France's contraction narrowed, offering positive signals for the Eurozone.

5. Asia Pacific & Emerging Markets: Mixed Picture

Japan's Nikkei 225 fell for the week, with the yen sinking to 163.84 per dollar — a 40-year low. Japan's Cabinet Office warned Friday that companies are passing on Iran war-related cost increases faster than during the 2022 Russia-Ukraine energy shock.

South Korea's KOSPI came under heavy pressure; semiconductor exports benefit from AI demand but chip stocks were battered by the global sell-off. The Philippine peso hit a record low of 61.847 per dollar, as the country — which declared a national energy emergency in March — continues to suffer from high oil prices.

The IMF's July 2026 World Economic Outlook projects global growth at 3.0% with inflation at 4.7%, warning that prolonged Middle East disruption could cut growth to 2.1%.

6. Cryptocurrencies: Risk-Off Spillover

Bitcoin fell to approximately $64,800 (-1.8%), while Ether dropped to around $1,870 (-3%), tracking the broader risk-off sentiment from tech stocks. Bitcoin had briefly surged above $68,000 earlier in the week on bipartisan CLARITY Act progress, but the gains were erased by the broader market downturn.

Next week's key events: ① Microsoft, Meta, and Amazon Q2 earnings — the AI capex vs. revenue debate continues; ② Fed July FOMC decision (July 28-29); ③ US-Iran developments and Pakistan/China-brokered diplomatic efforts.
