⚡ July 27 European Open: Stocks Rally as Oil Breaks $91, Airlines Surge 3.7%, Korea Reverses, Fed Divide Widens

Monday, July 27 marks the opening of Super Week in global financial markets, with three converging narratives driving cross-asset repricing: the US-Iran weekend ceasefire fueling risk-on sentiment, US durable goods orders rebounding to confirm economic resilience, and deepening internal divisions at the Federal Reserve ahead of Wednesday's FOMC decision. European equities opened broadly higher, but KOSPI's dramatic intraday reversal from +1.7% to -1.1% serves as a cautionary signal that market fragility remains just below the surface.

1. Europe Opens Higher: Airlines Soar on Oil Slump, Energy Stocks Weigh

European equity markets rallied across the board Monday, with the pan-European Stoxx 600 rising 0.68% to 648.98 in early trading. The sharp drop in crude prices triggered a powerful sector rotation — airline stocks surged while energy majors slumped.

Index snapshot (midday European session):

  • Stoxx 600: +0.68% at 648.98, led by financials and technology
  • CAC 40 (France): +0.46% at 8,410.86, luxury goods stabilizing
  • DAX (Germany): +0.6%, supported by improved manufacturing sentiment
  • FTSE 100 (UK): +0.1%, held back by energy sector weight

Sector rotation highlights:

  • Airlines & Travel (+2.4%) — the biggest beneficiaries of falling oil. Lufthansa surged 3.7%, IAG (British Airways parent) climbed 3.7%, and Ryanair gained over 3%. "Every $10 drop in oil saves the airline industry roughly $20 billion in annual fuel costs," noted a UBS analyst. Lower fuel costs combined with peak summer travel demand are driving a sharp upgrade in airline earnings expectations
  • Energy & Utilities (-2%) — BP, Shell, and TotalEnergies tracked crude lower, though some investors argue the "supply premium" still justifies holding energy exposure. Utilities fell in sympathy with lower bond yields
  • Defense steady — ongoing geopolitical uncertainty continues to support European defense names such as Rheinmetall and Thales

Europe's own macro calendar this week is formidable: the Bank of England delivers its rate decision Thursday (all 70 economists surveyed expect a hold at 3.75%), Eurozone Q2 GDP preliminary reading is due Friday, and Germany's July IFO business climate index is also on today's docket.

2. Oil Breaks Below $91: War Premium Evaporates, Structural Risks Remain

Brent crude traded in the $90.88-$91.50 range during the European session Monday, down approximately 6% on the day and hitting an intraday low of $90.88. WTI crude fell in tandem, trading at $84-$85, down over 5%. This represents crude's largest single-day decline since June.

Three forces driving the crash:

  • Military pause takes hold: The US halted its 13-day bombing campaign Friday evening, and Iran reciprocated by suspending retaliatory strikes. US Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz said Trump wants to "give negotiations a little bit of room," and a senior Iranian official confirmed to Reuters that Tehran will halt attacks as long as Washington does the same
  • Hormuz negotiations restart: Iran and Oman are in talks over a shipping management mechanism for the Strait of Hormuz, with Tehran proposing to manage vessel transit in exchange for a "service fee." While the US and other Gulf states are unlikely to accept Iran's control over the waterway, the mere fact of negotiations has reduced immediate supply disruption expectations
  • Position squaring: Brent had rallied over 10% last week, and the ceasefire news triggered massive long liquidation. CFTC data shows speculative net long crude positions declined roughly 12% last week

But structural risks are far from resolved. Transit through the Strait of Hormuz remains at just 40% of pre-war levels, with over 500 vessels anchored at both ends. Houthi attacks on Saudi Aramco's Jizan refinery and Yanbu terminal continue — Greek-operated Patriot systems intercepted ballistic missiles over Saudi territory on Saturday for the first time, demonstrating that the conflict continues to spill over. Goldman Sachs maintains its bullish view, arguing that even if negotiations succeed, full supply normalization will take weeks or months.

3. US Durable Goods Rebound 1.6%: Another Data Point Confirming Economic Resilience

The US Commerce Department reported Monday that June durable goods orders rose 1.6% month-over-month, matching consensus expectations exactly and recovering sharply from May's steep 4.5% decline. Core durable goods (excluding transportation) rose 0.9%, above the 0.8% forecast. The key business investment proxy — non-defense capital goods orders excluding aircraft — surged 1.4%, well ahead of the 1.3% expected.

Three key takeaways from the data:

  • Capex is not collapsing: Despite elevated interest rates (fed funds at 3.50-3.75%) and geopolitical turmoil, American businesses continue to invest in equipment and machinery. Computer and machinery orders led the advance
  • Fed gains policy breathing room: Strong economic data reduces the urgency to cut rates, while falling oil eases imported inflation pressure — this gives the Fed more justification to hold steady. Citigroup economist Veronica Clark noted: "If subsequent data shows limited pass-through from higher energy prices, policymakers may be comfortable extending the hold"
  • "Soft landing" narrative reinforced: Manufacturing recovery combined with last week's cooler CPI (June headline at 3.5%, core at 2.6%) creates a theoretically favorable backdrop for risk assets. However, credit markets remain cautious about this combination

Following durable goods, the week's data calendar includes: Tuesday's Conference Board Consumer Confidence and trade balance, Wednesday's FOMC rate decision, Thursday's Q2 GDP advance reading and June core PCE, and Friday's Bank of Japan decision.

4. Fed Divide Deepens: At Least Two Governors May Dissent

The FOMC's two-day July meeting kicks off Tuesday (July 28). This decision is shaping up as the most suspenseful since 2023 — not just about the rate outcome, but about the internal governance of Chair Kevin Warsh's Fed.

The current pricing landscape:

  • Economists unanimously predict a hold: All 104 economists surveyed by Reuters expect rates to remain at 3.50-3.75%. EY-Parthenon's Gregory Daco described the chance of a July hike as "negligible"
  • Rate futures price 36% odds of a hike: Fed funds futures show a roughly 36% implied probability of a 25bp hike, up from just 13% a week ago. This gap between economist consensus and market pricing means any decision will trigger significant repricing
  • Dissenting votes possible: Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan and Governor Beth Hammack have both publicly signaled support for higher rates. Both hold voting seats this year. Former Fed Governor Larry Meyer expects both to vote against a hold

Chair Warsh reiterated his "price stability" commitment in recent congressional testimony but has refused to offer forward guidance, stating at the June press conference that "forward guidance is not appropriate" in the current environment. This leaves markets with virtually no directional signals heading into the decision, amplifying sensitivity to any surprises.

The September meeting path is equally important. Lower oil has temporarily reduced the urgency to hike, but FedWatch still shows an approximately 80% probability of a September hike — markets expect a delay, not a cancellation. The 2-year Treasury yield at 4.33% already exceeds the upper bound of the fed funds rate (3.75%), reflecting bond market pricing for higher rates ahead.

5. KOSPI's Dramatic Reversal: From +1.73% to -1.10% as Foreign Investors Take Profits

South Korea's KOSPI index delivered the most dramatic reversal in Asian trading Monday — opening 1.73% higher at 6,806 before sliding steadily to close at -1.10%, erasing all early gains. The move underscores that even with massive AI infrastructure catalysts, valuation pressure in foreign-dominated Korean equities remains a formidable headwind.

Deeply divided sector performance:

  • Naver surged 7.71% — Nvidia agreed to purchase $1 billion in newly issued Naver shares (204,500 won each), acquiring a 4.5% stake to become the third-largest shareholder. Naver simultaneously announced a $10 billion AI data center partnership with Nvidia and Brookfield
  • Samsung Electronics -0.70% — despite announcing a $200+ billion AI chip manufacturing MOU with Broadcom (sub-2nm process), foreign investors took profits ahead of earnings. The stock opened nearly 3% higher before giving up all gains
  • SK Hynix -1.25% — down ahead of its Q2 earnings report this week. ADR premium over domestic shares also created arbitrage pressure
  • KOSDAQ +1.77% — in stark contrast to KOSPI, the tech-heavy junior market continued to attract buying in biotech, robotics, and AI names. Rainbow Robotics +7.41%, Alteogen +4.33%

Foreign investors net sold approximately 500 billion won in KOSPI stocks during the morning session, directly triggering the reversal. Trader attention now shifts to Samsung and SK Hynix quarterly results, which will determine whether AI chip demand growth justifies current valuations or whether a broader correction is due.

7. Singapore MAS Unexpectedly Tightens: Asian Monetary Policy Divergence Accelerates

The Monetary Authority of Singapore surprised markets Monday by further tightening policy, raising the slope of the Singapore dollar's Nominal Effective Exchange Rate (NEER) policy band. This marks Singapore's seventh tightening operation since October 2021.

Singapore uses the exchange rate rather than interest rates as its primary policy tool. MAS stated that core inflation remains above historical averages and the economy is near potential, warranting a "very slight" increase in the policy band's appreciation slope to ensure continued price stability.

The SGD strengthened approximately 0.3% immediately after the announcement, extending its daily gain to 0.5%. MAS's unexpected move may signal to regional central banks — particularly the Bank of Korea and Bank Indonesia — that with oil still above historical averages and inflation pressures unresolved, Asian monetary policymakers may be more hawkish than markets currently pricing.

Super Week Day 1: Cautious Optimism

The first day of Super Week closes with "cautious optimism" as the dominant theme. Oil's collapse provides a "comfort illusion" of inflation relief, but KOSPI's reversal and the Fed's internal divisions serve as stark reminders that structural risks remain fully in play. European risk assets are up but constrained by energy sector weakness, and US futures gains are measured rather than euphoric.

The next three days will sequentially digest: Tuesday's US Consumer Confidence (expected 101.5) and 2/5-year Treasury auctions, Wednesday's FOMC decision and Warsh press conference, Thursday's Apple/Amazon earnings plus Q2 GDP and core PCE. Each node is a potential catalyst for significant repricing. Until uncertainty peaks, reducing leverage, diversifying across asset classes, and maintaining cash flexibility remains the prudent strategy.
