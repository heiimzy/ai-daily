⚡ July 30 International Politics: US Launches Retaliatory Strikes on Iran, Drone Hits US-Owned Tanker at Egypt Port, Iran Rejects Hormuz Proposal, Russia Pummels Kyiv with Ballistic Missiles

I. US Launches 'Powerful Response' Strikes on Iran — Residential Area Hit on Qeshm Island

The United States military confirmed Wednesday evening it had begun launching strikes against Iranian targets, in what U.S. Central Command described as "a powerful response to yesterday's attempted Iranian attacks on U.S. forces based in the Middle East." The operation ends a brief pause in hostilities that began Friday when Trump suspended a 13-night bombing campaign.

President Donald Trump, speaking at the Oval Office before the strikes, vowed: "We're going to be hitting them very hard because it's our turn to hit them. They know it's coming. They asked us not to do it." Trump said he had been briefed on the explosion at Egypt's Mediterranean port of Damietta involving a U.S.-owned gas storage vessel.

Shortly after the strikes began, multiple explosions were reported across southern Iran. Iranian state-aligned Tasnim News Agency reported that a missile strike hit a residential area on Iran's Qeshm Island, a strategic island in the Strait of Hormuz. Multiple locations across southern Iran reported explosions, with air defenses placed on high alert.

The strikes represent a major escalation after several days of relative calm. Trump had suspended intensive bombing last Friday, with the U.S. saying it had successfully struck Iranian weapons and military sites. However, Iran's Revolutionary Guards launched ballistic missiles at U.S. bases in Jordan on Tuesday and claimed to have struck three tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, shattering the brief truce. Axios reported, citing a U.S. official, that this is the beginning of a new wave of strikes.

II. War Reaches Egypt for the First Time: Drone Strikes US-Owned LNG Tanker at Damietta Port

The Middle East conflict took a dangerous new turn as war reached Egypt for the first time. A drone struck the U.S.-owned floating liquefied natural gas storage vessel Energos Winter at Egypt's Mediterranean port of Damietta on Wednesday, sparking a fire that spread to a second Greek-owned tanker.

British maritime security firm Ambrey confirmed the attack. Egypt's petroleum ministry acknowledged a fire at the port but did not mention a drone strike, saying the blaze was quickly brought under control with no casualties. However, three trading sources familiar with the incident confirmed to Reuters that a drone strike caused the fire.

Trump confirmed he had been briefed on the Damietta incident. Asked whether Iran was responsible, he replied: "It's a little more of the same. It's going to be straightening out. In the meantime, we're going to be hitting them very hard."

Egypt, a close U.S. ally and key regional mediator, is one of the few countries in the Middle East that had been spared direct military action during the five-month war. If confirmed as an Iranian or Iranian-proxy strike, this would mark a significant widening of the conflict — extending the front lines from the Persian Gulf and Red Sea to the Mediterranean.

Egyptian Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty spoke with U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff to discuss de-escalation efforts, emphasizing the need to uphold international law, state sovereignty, and territorial integrity.

Meanwhile, the U.S. Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control announced fresh sanctions targeting Persian Gulf Marine Insurance Company, HormuzSafe Marine Services Authority, 10 entities involved in transporting Iranian oil — including six China-based firms — and eight tankers. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. "will not allow Iran to hold global commerce hostage" or use international shipping to finance the IRGC.

III. Iran Formally Rejects Omani Strait of Hormuz Joint-Control Proposal

Iran's Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi confirmed in a state television interview that Iran has formally rejected Oman's proposal for joint management of the Strait of Hormuz. The Omani plan, backed by Gulf states, would have divided the strait into two routes under joint administration.

Gharibabadi said the 50-50 arrangement "would not serve Iran's interests," though Tehran considers Oman a "valuable neighbor." Iran offered a counterproposal allowing temporary vessel transit through its territorial waters but emphasized that Tehran's policy is for the strait to "never return to its prewar situation" of free navigation.

The IRGC Navy said it continues to maintain full control over the strait and will respond to "unlawful U.S. military interference." The Pentagon has advised ships to use routes closer to the Omani coast to avoid Iranian-controlled waters.

The Strait of Hormuz handles approximately 20% of global oil shipments. Iran has progressively tightened its grip on the waterway since the war began in February, effectively leaving the channel semi-closed. The International Maritime Organization has warned that transit through the strait has become "too dangerous" due to continued Iranian targeting of tankers. Brent crude surged more than 8% in response to the renewed hostilities, pushing back above $90 per barrel.

IV. Russia Unleashes Massive Ballistic Missile Barrage Across Ukraine — Residential Area Hit in Kryvyi Rih, Children Feared Trapped

In a separate major escalation on Europe's eastern front, Russia launched a massive coordinated attack using dozens of ballistic missiles and drones across Ukraine in the early hours of July 30. Air raid alerts were issued across nearly all of Ukraine's regions.

Multiple explosions were heard in Kyiv beginning at 1:18 a.m. local time, followed by a second wave around 3:50 a.m. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko confirmed at least one fatality in the capital, with fires breaking out at non-residential properties in the Sviatoshynskyi and Obolon districts after debris from intercepted missiles fell on the sites.

In Kryvyi Rih, President Zelensky's hometown, a missile struck a residential area. Local media reported that many people, including children, were believed to be trapped beneath the rubble of a destroyed home. Casualty figures were not immediately available. Dnipro, Poltava, and Vinnytsia also faced ballistic missile threats. Poland scrambled fighter jets to protect its airspace in response.

The attack came just hours after President Zelensky warned the public that Russia was preparing a "massive attack." In a post on X on July 29, Zelensky wrote: "The Russians prepared a massive attack several days ago, and there is a high probability that the strike will be carried out tonight. Please, in all regions of Ukraine, pay attention to air raid alerts today and take care of yourselves." Thousands of residents sheltered in Kyiv's metro stations overnight.

The timing is notable: Zelensky had just met with President Trump at the White House on July 28 to discuss Patriot interceptor supplies and reviving the Ukraine-Russia peace process. Both leaders called the meeting "productive." Yet just two days later, Russia responded with a massive missile barrage — signaling that Moscow will not be constrained by U.S.-Ukraine diplomatic efforts.

Russian President Vladimir Putin stated on July 3 that large-scale missile attacks against Ukraine "must continue." As Russian ground advances have slowed, Moscow has increasingly relied on long-range missile and drone strikes to deplete Ukraine's air defense capabilities and civilian morale. However, Ukraine's air defense systems — particularly the Patriot batteries capable of intercepting ballistic missiles — are facing critical ammunition shortages.

Ukrainian and U.S. officials had previously signaled that a new round of peace proposals, including a potential aerial ceasefire, was being prepared. This massive attack may significantly complicate those diplomatic efforts. Putin appears to be sending a clear signal: even as Trump attempts to broker peace, Russia has no intention of making concessions on the battlefield.
