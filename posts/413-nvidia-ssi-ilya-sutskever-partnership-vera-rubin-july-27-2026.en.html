⚡ NVIDIA Strategic Investment in Ilya Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence: Vera Rubin Platform Delivers 10x Compute Boost, Challenging Google TPU Dominance

Monday, July 27 — NVIDIA (NASDAQ: NVDA) announced a long-term strategic partnership with Safe Superintelligence Inc. (SSI), the AI lab founded by former OpenAI co-founder and chief scientist Ilya Sutskever, along with an additional investment in the company. Under the agreement, SSI will gain access to NVIDIA's next-generation flagship Vera Rubin computing platform, expanding its available compute capacity by approximately an order of magnitude. The deal not only locks in one of the world's most prestigious AI research teams for NVIDIA's hardware ecosystem but also signals a strategic pivot for SSI, which previously relied heavily on Google's TPU infrastructure.

I. Deal Details: 10x Compute Boost, Jensen Huang Says "Excited for New Breakthroughs"

According to the joint announcement, NVIDIA has made an additional investment in SSI (specific terms undisclosed) while granting priority access to the Vera Rubin platform — NVIDIA's most powerful AI computing architecture to date, unveiled at the GTC conference in March 2026. The NVL72 rack pairs 72 Rubin GPUs with 36 Vera CPUs connected via NVLink 6, training large mixture-of-experts models with a quarter of the GPUs that the previous-generation Blackwell required, while delivering up to 10x the inference throughput per watt at a tenth of the cost per token.

NVIDIA founder and CEO Jensen Huang said in a statement:

"Ilya has pioneered fundamental breakthroughs at the foundation of modern AI, beginning with AlexNet. We are excited to see what new breakthroughs SSI will discover powered by our Vera Rubin platform." — Jensen Huang, Founder and CEO of NVIDIA

Sutskever responded:

"We have research that is worthy of scaling up, and having access to a big NVIDIA computer will let us do so. We're incredibly proud to be partnering with Jensen and the NVIDIA team, and we are confident that our big bet on the Vera Rubin platform will take us to the next level." — Ilya Sutskever, Cofounder and CEO of SSI

NVIDIA said it entered the partnership after obtaining rare access into SSI's closely guarded research, and decided to accelerate the lab's next stage of growth. The two companies will also collaborate on the technical advancement of NVIDIA's current and future compute platforms, leveraging SSI's unique insights into the future of AI.

II. SSI: The Rise of the World's Most Secretive AI Lab

Safe Superintelligence Inc. was founded in 2024 by Sutskever, Daniel Gross (former head of Apple AI), and Daniel Levy. Headquartered in Palo Alto and Tel Aviv, the company is positioned as "the world's first straight-shot SSI lab" with one goal and one product: safe superintelligence.

Sutskever is one of the most influential figures in AI — his research contributions span AlexNet, AlphaGo, Sequence-to-Sequence learning, and the GPT model family, and he led the research behind OpenAI's o1 reasoning model. He departed OpenAI in 2024 to found SSI.

SSI Funding History:

  • September 2024: Raised $1 billion at a $5 billion valuation, led by a16z, Sequoia Capital, DST Global, and SV Angel
  • April 2025: Raised $2 billion at a $32 billion valuation (6.4x increase), led by Greenoaks Capital with $500 million, joined by a16z, Lightspeed, DST Global, and strategic investors NVIDIA and Alphabet
  • July 2026: NVIDIA makes additional investment; terms not disclosed

Notably, SSI previously relied primarily on Google Cloud's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) for its model training. In April 2025, Google Cloud signed an agreement to provide SSI with TPU chip access — the first time Google had opened its internally reserved TPUs at scale to an external organization. The new NVIDIA partnership means SSI is now building a dual-source compute strategy, avoiding single-vendor dependence.

III. NVIDIA's "Double Lock" Strategy on Former OpenAI Core Teams

This is not NVIDIA's first investment in an AI lab founded by former OpenAI core members. In March 2026, NVIDIA announced a similar partnership with Thinking Machines Lab, founded by Mira Murati (former OpenAI CTO), committing at least one gigawatt of Vera Rubin systems with deployment targeted for early 2027, along with an undisclosed investment. Thinking Machines Lab shipped its first model, Inkling, earlier this month, trained entirely on NVIDIA hardware.

From OpenAI's former chief scientist to its former CTO, NVIDIA is systematically locking in the world's most elite AI research talent. Analysts point to a clear strategic intent:

  • Locking compute demand: Top AI labs' hunger for compute is nearly infinite. NVIDIA uses a combination of equity investment and compute guarantees to ensure these labs prioritize NVIDIA hardware
  • Binding top talent: Early insight into frontier research helps NVIDIA optimize next-generation chip architecture
  • Building a moat: Over the past 16 months, NVIDIA has committed approximately $90 billion across more than 145 AI companies, establishing multi-layered dominance through financial instruments, hardware capacity, and energy access

Meanwhile, NVIDIA is also in talks with OpenAI to guarantee up to $250 billion in financing for a data center project, helping OpenAI lease SoftBank's 10-gigawatt Ohio data center hub. Jensen Huang's "trillion-token factory" vision is accelerating — the center of gravity in AI is shifting from model training to inference, with data centers transforming into AI engines processing unstructured data.

IV. Industry Impact: AI Compute Arms Race Enters a New Phase

This partnership comes at a pivotal moment for the AI industry. The exponential growth in frontier model training costs and compute demand is reshaping global technology capital flows:

  • Google TPU vs. NVIDIA GPU: SSI was a flagship Google TPU customer. Its pivot to NVIDIA's Vera Rubin platform signals that even the most advanced AI labs refuse to bet their compute future on a single vendor
  • Financialization of AI infrastructure: NVIDIA has evolved from "selling chips" to "building ecosystems," using investments, guarantees, and revenue-sharing to deeply embed itself in AI infrastructure financing
  • Sovereign AI race: NVIDIA's recent global moves — $1 billion investment in Korea's NAVER, a $500 billion AI factory partnership with SK Group, and sovereign fund partnerships in the UAE — are building a global AI compute network

Gartner data shows that global tech company AI infrastructure spending has reached approximately $1 trillion in 2026, up 34.7% year-over-year. As the core supplier of AI compute, NVIDIA's strategy is evolving from pure chip manufacturing to acting as the "general contractor" of AI infrastructure.

V. Vera Rubin Platform: Technical Deep Dive

Vera Rubin, NVIDIA's flagship computing platform unveiled at GTC 2026, is described by Jensen Huang as the "beginning of NVIDIA's largest infrastructure build in history":

  • Vera CPU: NVIDIA's custom Arm-based processor, competing directly with Intel, AMD, and cloud giants' custom silicon
  • Rubin GPU: Next-generation graphics processor paired with HBM4 high-bandwidth memory (supplied by SK Hynix, Samsung, and Micron)
  • NVL72 Rack: 72 Rubin GPUs + 36 Vera CPUs connected via NVLink 6
  • Performance: Trains large MoE models with one-quarter the GPUs of Blackwell; delivers up to 10x inference throughput per watt at one-tenth the cost per token
  • Groq 3 LPX: Inference acceleration rack with 256 LPU processors, boosting inference throughput per megawatt by up to 35x when paired with Vera Rubin
  • Production timeline: Systems began shipping to partners in the second half of 2026; SSI's expansion depends on this delivery ramp

For SSI, access to the Vera Rubin platform means not just a scale increase in compute, but the ability to explore much larger model architectures at lower cost. However, independent analysts note that NVIDIA's published performance figures are self-reported — the baselines for "10x improvement" claims have not been fully disclosed, and real-world results will need to be validated in actual deployment environments.
