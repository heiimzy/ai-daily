Middle East: Trump Announces Hamas Disarmament Deal; Israel's Far-Right Minister First to Object

US President Donald Trump announced late Thursday that Hamas has agreed to disarm as part of the second phase of his 20-point Gaza peace plan. Senior Hamas official Bassem Naim confirmed on X that the group had agreed to the deal after what he called "a fierce negotiation battle," adding that "the ball is now in Israel's court." The Associated Press published the full text of the roadmap on Friday: it reaffirms the parties' commitment to Trump's comprehensive peace plan, enshrines the principle of "One Authority, One Law, One Weapon," establishes a new Gaza administration (NCAG) to take over civilian institutions and public services, and tasks an international verification committee with monitoring each phase. The administrative and financial transition could take up to three years, with supplier-related commitments capped at $400 million; Hamas must surrender heavy weapons and dismantle its tunnel network in a process that could take 200 to 350 days.

Developments in the past few hours show the hurdles ahead: Hamas official Ghazi Hamad told Reuters the group will hand over its weapons to the new Gaza administration set up by Trump's Board of Peace, but only if Israel withdraws its troops; Israeli National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir became the first Israeli official to speak publicly on the deal, calling it "not acceptable" and insisting that targeted killings of Hamas militants must continue; an Israeli airstrike in central Gaza killed at least one person. The Israeli government has not yet formally responded to the agreement.

US: Uyghur Forced-Labor Blacklist Grows by 43 Chinese Companies to 187

The US Department of Homeland Security (DHS) announced Thursday that it was adding 43 Chinese companies to the Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) Entity List, along with technical updates to two existing entries, bringing the total to 187 entities — a roughly 30% increase. Beginning Friday (July 31), US Customs and Border Protection (CBP) will apply a presumption that goods produced by the newly listed companies are tied to forced labor, barring them from the US market unless importers can rebut that determination. The additions span aluminum, apparel, copper, cotton, tomatoes and related downstream products.

DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the move is aimed at keeping forced-labor-linked products out of US supply chains while protecting American workers from "unfair competition." Since the UFLPA took effect, CBP has denied entry to more than 24,300 shipments valued at nearly $1 billion.

South China Sea: China Rejects Philippine Baselines Claim, Says Scarborough Shoal Is Its Territory

China's foreign ministry issued a statement on Friday declaring that Huangyan Dao (Scarborough Shoal) is China's "inherent territory" and that Beijing neither recognizes nor accepts the 2016 South China Sea arbitration ruling, which favored the Philippines. The statement comes after the Philippines filed its extended continental shelf claim in the West Philippine Sea with the UN panel on continental shelf limits and notified the UN of its baselines around Bajo de Masinloc, the Filipino name for the shoal. Beijing also criticized the Philippines' Maritime Zones Act as unlawfully incorporating Chinese-claimed features into Philippine maritime zones, and the China Coast Guard said it had taken law enforcement measures against multiple Philippine vessels on Friday.

China urged Manila to stop what it called "infringement and provocation" around Huangyan Dao, warning it would respond "resolutely" to actions it considers harmful to its sovereignty. The shoal has long been one of the most sensitive flashpoints in China-Philippines maritime disputes.

China-EU: SCMP Exclusive Says EU Foreign Policy Chief Kallas Expected to Visit Beijing

The South China Morning Post exclusively reported on Friday that EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas is expected to visit Beijing, with the date yet to be confirmed. The trip would revolve around the bilateral "strategic dialogue," an annual format in which the two sides discuss foreign and security policy. According to insiders cited by the SCMP, high on the EU agenda will be China's relations with Russia, the ongoing crises in the Middle East and the tense situation in the Taiwan Strait. A previous encounter was described as tense from the outset, with Kallas pressing Wang Yi over China's support for Russia. If confirmed, it would be a high-level contact at a time of some of the deepest strains in China-EU relations in years.

Commodities: Vitol, Cargill and Glencore Cut Ties with Radiant World

Bloomberg News reported that three of the world's largest commodity traders have pulled back from iron-ore trader Radiant World: Vitol and Cargill have ceased trading with the company, while Glencore has halted new business. The move follows concerns that invoices or other documents Radiant World provided to its banks were invalid or falsified. At least two creditors are reviewing their exposure: Italy's biggest bank Intesa Sanpaolo has booked provisions on an exposure worth €200 million ($230 million), saying the position is now largely covered with no impact on its 2026 net profit guidance, and Jefferies' Point Bonita fund is also assessing risk. Radiant World denied the allegations, saying its business continues to operate normally and that the claims are "inaccurate and unsubstantiated."
