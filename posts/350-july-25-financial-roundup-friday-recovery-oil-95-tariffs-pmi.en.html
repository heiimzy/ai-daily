⚡ July 25 Financial Roundup: US Stocks Rebound with Dow +0.7%, Brent Retreats from $100 to $95, New Trump 301 Tariffs on 60 Economies Take Effect

1. US Stocks Stabilize Friday: Dow +0.7%, S&P +0.5% After Thursday's Meltdown

Wall Street staged a modest recovery on Friday after the Magnificent Seven suffered their worst session since April 2025. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 234.86 points (+0.45%) to close at 51,946.51, after gaining as much as 0.7% intraday. The S&P 500 advanced 0.5%, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite was roughly flat as chip stocks continued to weigh.

Despite Friday's bounce, all three major indexes posted weekly losses. The Nasdaq fell ~1.5% for the week, the S&P 500 lost ~0.8%, and the Magnificent Seven index tumbled 4.8% — its worst weekly performance since the Liberation Day tariff turmoil in April 2025. The selloff was driven by mounting concerns over AI infrastructure spending returns, compounded by a surge in oil prices tied to escalating Middle East conflict.

Sector rotation was evident: energy stocks gained 0.56% on elevated crude prices, financials benefited from rising bond yields, and defensive sectors attracted safe-haven flows. The CNN Fear & Greed index slipped into "Fear" territory, reflecting the cautious turn in investor sentiment. S&P 500 futures were little changed in after-hours trading.

2. Brent Crude Retreats 5% from $100: Oil Rally Pauses but Weekly Gains Still Top 10%

Oil prices pulled back sharply on Friday after touching the psychologically important $100 milestone. Brent crude futures fell about 5% to trade below $95 per barrel, retreating from Thursday's high above $100 — the first breach since May. WTI crude eased 0.4% to $91.85 per barrel. Despite the pullback, Brent is still up over 10% for the week and nearly 40% for the month.

The retreat was driven by profit-taking rather than fundamental improvement. Thursday's 7% surge followed Houthi attacks on two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea, marking an escalation of the Middle East conflict that now threatens both the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb — the world's two most critical energy transit chokepoints.

Logistics firm Flexport warned that the Strait of Hormuz has "reverted to full war conditions," with daily vessel transits collapsing 66% to just 13 ships versus a normal 88. RBC Capital Markets warned that in a full escalation scenario, oil could surpass the December 2022 high of $128 and potentially challenge the all-time record of $146 set in 2008. Goldman Sachs forecasts oil above $120 in Q4 if shipping disruptions persist.

"Until recently, investors had largely assumed that tensions in the Middle East would eventually subside. But with crude oil climbing above $90 a barrel, markets are beginning to recognize the situation warrants closer attention." — Yugo Tsuboi, Chief Strategist, Daiwa Securities

3. Trump's New 301 Tariffs Go Live: 10-12.5% Duties on 60 Economies

A sweeping new tariff regime took effect at 12:01 AM ET Friday, July 24, as the Trump administration implemented Section 301 tariffs targeting roughly 60 economies. The new duties, ranging from 10% to 12.5%, replace the expiring 10% global tariff and cover approximately 99.4% of U.S. imports.

The rate structure divides trading partners into two tiers: roughly 10 economies (including Japan and South Korea) that have established forced labor prohibition laws face 10% tariffs; the remaining economies (including China, Hong Kong, the EU, UK, Canada, Mexico, India, Brazil, and Taiwan) face the higher 12.5% rate. Energy products, certain foodstuffs, and critical minerals are exempted.

The new tariffs drew sharp international backlash. Australian Trade Minister Farrell called them "completely unjustified." New Zealand Trade Minister McClay expressed disappointment. EU Foreign Affairs Chief Kallas said the tariffs are "not really grounded." Brazil's government rejected them as "arbitrary and unjustified." Japan confirmed it is seeking assurance that the U.S. will not exceed the 2025 tariff agreement.

Analysts warn that the combination of new tariffs and surging oil prices will compound global inflationary pressures, further complicating central bank policy decisions worldwide.

4. US July PMI Hits 8-Month High: Composite at 53.6, World Cup Boost Drives Services

S&P Global's flash July PMI data released Friday surprised to the upside across the board. The Composite PMI rose to 53.6 from 51.9 in June, marking an 8-month high. The Services PMI surged to 53.6 (vs. 51.2 prior, beating the 51.0 estimate), while Manufacturing PMI edged down to 53.8 from 53.9 (slightly below the 54.5 estimate but still firmly in expansion territory).

The report highlighted that the acceleration in services activity was largely driven by the 2026 FIFA World Cup, with the U.S. as a host nation attracting record international tourist arrivals. Hotel, restaurant, and transportation sectors saw significant boosts. On the manufacturing side, while overall growth moderated slightly, AI-related equipment orders continued to show robust momentum.

Chris Williamson, Chief Business Economist at S&P Global Market Intelligence, noted: "July data point to the economy moving into the second half of the year with a solid growth momentum. The services sector is performing particularly well thanks to World Cup-related demand. However, the survey may not fully capture the impact of the recent Middle East escalation and oil price surge, making the coming months' readings particularly important."

5. Rate Hike Expectations Soar: 38% Probability for July FOMC, 82% for September

Despite Friday's equity market rebound, the bond market continued to signal heightened inflation and rate hike risks. CME FedWatch data shows the probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the July 29 FOMC meeting has risen to approximately 38%, up from just 10.7% a week ago. The probability for a September rate hike has surged to 82%, compared to under 53% last week.

Treasury yields continued climbing: the 2-year yield rose to 4.37% (17-month high), the 10-year yield reached 4.71% (18-month high), and the 30-year yield approached 5.201% — just shy of a 19-year high. The Bloomberg Global Bond Index average yield surged to 3.68%, the highest since the 2008 financial crisis.

The surging oil price is now feeding through to consumers. AAA data shows the U.S. national average gasoline price reached $4 per gallon, a one-month high. "Risks remain skewed towards tightening," wrote Seema Shah, Chief Global Strategist at Principal Asset Management. TD Securities rates strategist Gennadiy Goldberg noted that even if the Fed holds rates steady next week, the yield surge itself reflects a market-driven repricing of inflation risk.

In currency markets, the dollar index edged lower on Friday but held near its three-week high at 101.45. The yen remained under pressure at 163.78, within striking distance of 40-year lows. The Indian rupee hit a fresh record low of 97/USD, and the Philippine peso also set a new record low of 61.8/USD, highlighting emerging market vulnerability to surging energy costs.

6. Asia Markets Rout: KOSPI Crashes 6%, Nikkei -2.8%, Taiwan Drops 1,195 Points

The selloff that gripped Wall Street on Thursday swept through Asian markets on Friday. South Korea's KOSPI index plunged about 6%, with Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix both falling more than 8%. The Korea Financial Services Commission's announcement of higher cash deposit requirements for leveraged ETFs starting July 31 added to the selling pressure.

Japan's Nikkei 225 tumbled as much as 1,800 points (-2.8%) in its third consecutive weekly decline. Taiwan's Weighted Index suffered its 9th largest point drop in history, falling 1,195 points, with the Taiwan dollar weakening 9.2 cents to 32.358. Hong Kong's Hang Seng fell 1.3%, and China's Shanghai Composite lost 1.61% with trading volume falling below 2 trillion yuan.

Australia's S&P/ASX 200 declined 0.93%. Curiously, the South Korean won was a rare bright spot, strengthening to 1,463 per USD — a three-month high, reflecting divergent dynamics among Asian economies navigating the global risk-off environment. Market attention now turns to a packed week of central bank meetings: the Fed (July 29), Bank of Japan (July 31), and Bank of England (August 6).
