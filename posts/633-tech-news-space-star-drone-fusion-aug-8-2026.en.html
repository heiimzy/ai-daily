⚡ Aug 8 Tech Roundup: Sequoia China Backs Satellite Maker in $297M Round, Malaysia-Cambodia Deepen AI & Drone Defense Ties, Dongwei H1 Profits Jump, ORNL & Type One Energy Push Fusion Fuel Cycle

Over the past few hours, the tech world has seen fresh moves spanning space, defense and clean energy: Sequoia China joined an approximately $297M round in commercial satellite company Space Star Technology, doubling down on satellite manufacturing; Malaysia and Cambodia agreed to deepen defense cooperation with a strong focus on artificial intelligence and drone technology; Dongwei Technology posted a 133% jump in H1 net profit with equipment export orders up over 150%, underscoring the semiconductor equipment boom; and the U.S. Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) signed a new pact with Type One Energy to advance fusion fuel cycle innovations. Below we break down each story, with publication times verified.

1. Sequoia China Adds to Commercial Space: Space Star Technology Raises ~$297M

According to Tech in Asia, commercial space and satellite manufacturer Space Star Technology has closed a roughly $297 million round, with Sequoia China participating. At a time when private space firms have faced heavy cash burn and industry consolidation, a bet by a top-tier venture capital firm reads as a vote for the "satellites at scale" thesis — companies trying to move from one-off custom builds toward mass-producible constellations. Commercial space is one of the hardest-tech segments to watch, riding together the waves of AI, communications and remote-sensing demand, while China's low-earth-orbit constellation plans and reusable-rocket advances keep foreign investors attentive.

Notably, the round arrives just as multiple Chinese LEO constellations accelerate deployment. Sequoia China's continued footprint in the space sector signals renewed momentum for private space investment, though the specific valuation and equity terms have not been disclosed. For observers, the rebound in domestic space venture funding reflects both maturing supply chains and a fresh influx of capital ammunition for a full "rocket-satellite-application" race.

2. Malaysia & Cambodia Deepen Defense Ties, Centered on AI and Drone Technology

As reported by Malay Mail, Malaysia and Cambodia have agreed to deepen their defense cooperation, with a strong focus on artificial intelligence, drone technology, and broader defense-industry collaboration. The move extends years of security dialogue between the two countries within ASEAN and the surrounding South China Sea context, and places "AI + drones" at the center of the regional defense agenda. As the line between consumer drones and military unmanned systems blurs, several Southeast Asian nations are racing to build local drone and counter-drone capabilities, making intra-ASEAN technology and capacity synergy increasingly practical.

Such cooperation typically spans joint exercises, intelligence sharing and defense procurement. Explicitly placing "AI technology" on the agenda signals that autonomous systems, target recognition and data analytics will play a decision-making role in next-generation defense. For industry players, rising Southeast Asian defense spending combined with localization demand could open new export opportunities for drone and AI-security vendors.

3. Dongwei Technology H1 Net Profit Jumps 133%; Equipment Export Orders Up Over 150%

Reported by finance.biggo, Dongwei Technology posted its 2026 H1 results: net profit surged 133% year over year, while semiconductor equipment export orders rose more than 150%. Against a backdrop of global AI infrastructure buildout and mature-node capacity expansion, semiconductor equipment demand remains buoyant, and Chinese equipment makers are enjoying high growth on a "domestic substitution + export" double engine. Rising orders often signal revenue recognized later on, so investors anticipate strong second-half delivery — while also keeping an eye on inventory cycles and export controls on valuations.

4. ORNL & Type One Energy Sign Pact to Advance Fusion Fuel Cycle

Per Newswise, the U.S. Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL) has signed a new agreement with fusion company Type One Energy to jointly advance fusion fuel cycle and other innovative technologies. The fusion fuel cycle — covering procurement, handling and back-end recovery of deuterium-tritium fuel — is viewed as a key enabler for making commercial fusion power plants a reality. ORNL holds deep expertise in nuclear fuel and materials, while Type One Energy is focused on stellarator-pathway demonstration devices. Together they underscore that fusion is moving beyond "ignition experiments" into engineered fuel management.

The fusion industry is currently racing on which pathway reaches commercial power first, and partnerships between public energy labs and private ventures have become a mainstream model. This particular agreement hasn't disclosed dollar figures or milestones, but the direction is clear: the fusion race is no longer only about whether a plasma "lights up," but also about the harder back-end question of how fuel cycles sustainably. For clean-energy investors, this is a technological milestone worth following carefully.
