⚡ July 31 Tech Roundup: OpenAI Slashes GPT-5.6 Prices by Up to 80%, Naver TV Shuts Down After 14 Years, Montage Unveils CXL 3.2 Memory Chip

OpenAI Cuts GPT-5.6 Prices: Luna Down 80%, Sol Gains a 2.5x Fast Mode

On July 31, OpenAI announced price cuts across its GPT-5.6 API lineup, just three weeks after the models became generally available on July 9. The steepest reduction hits GPT-5.6 Luna, the cost-efficiency model: API pricing drops 80% immediately, with input falling to $0.20 per million tokens and output to $1.20 per million tokens. The balanced GPT-5.6 Terra gets a 20% cut, bringing input to $2 per million tokens and output to $12 per million tokens.

The flagship GPT-5.6 Sol keeps its price unchanged but gains a new Fast mode in the API: up to 2.5x the inference speed of the standard mode at twice the price, with identical model capability. Requests that previously used Priority Processing will automatically migrate to Fast mode. Credit calculations for ChatGPT Work and Codex are also being adjusted — with Luna and Terra now cheaper, users will burn through their quotas more slowly. Free and Go users can access Terra, while Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise users can choose between Terra and Luna.

Analysts note the move comes as Chinese startup Moonshot pushes its Kimi K3 model and enterprise customers grow increasingly cost-sensitive. OpenAI said its strategy remains focused on improving capability and efficiency in tandem, so "each generation of AI can do more work at lower cost."

Self-Optimizing AI: GPT-5.6 Sol Cuts Its Own Inference Costs by 20%

A day before the price cut, OpenAI revealed a striking technical milestone: GPT-5.6 Sol had been used to optimize its own inference stack. Production GPU kernel optimization reduced overall serving costs by 20%, while speculative decoding improvements lifted token-generation efficiency by more than 15%. Those efficiency gains were passed straight to developers in the form of lower prices just one day later.

Beyond the model itself, OpenAI said GPT-5.6 also gained efficiency improvements in inference systems, context management and agent workflows, letting it accomplish more with fewer compute resources. Developers were quick to note the irony: Kimi K3, which had attracted developers with its aggressive pricing, now faces a GPT-5.6 Luna that has gone even cheaper — the model price war has spread from open-weight rivals to closed flagship APIs.

Naver TV Shuts Down After 14 Years, Content Shifts to Short Drama and Live Streaming

South Korean internet giant Naver has announced it will close Naver TV, its video platform of 14 years, pivoting its content business to the short-form platform Clip and the live-streaming service Chzzk. According to The Korea Herald, Naver TV will terminate service at 3 p.m. on September 30, 2026, with existing long-form content migrating to Clip. Starting September 10, Naver will run a creator profile migration process so Naver TV creators can continue their work on Clip.

Launched in 2012 as "Naver TV Cast" and rebranded as Naver TV in 2017, the platform once served as one of South Korea's most important video destinations for dramas, variety shows, music videos and individual creators. Its closure reflects a deep shift in Korean content consumption: according to Spoonlabs data based on the Biglu short-drama platform, domestic short-drama viewing time surpassed 2 million hours last year, while Clip's daily average users topped 11 million in June. Naver said it will "strengthen content competitiveness by centering on Clip and Chzzk" — grafting long-form video onto a short-form core, then layering in Chzzk's gaming live-streams and sponsorship/advertising monetization.

Montage Technology Starts Trial Production of CXL 3.2 Memory Expansion Controller

On July 31, Montage Technology (688008.SH) announced it has begun industry-first trial production of a CXL 3.2 memory expansion controller (MXC) chip. Targeting memory expansion and pooling for AI, cloud computing and data centers, the chip supports CXL Type 3 CXL.mem and CXL.io protocols, and is designed on PCIe 6.x and CXL 3.2 with data rates up to 64 GT/s.

The chip integrates dual DDR5 memory controllers supporting memory up to 8000 MT/s, converting host-side CXL memory requests into DDR access commands in real time to break through traditional server memory capacity limits. It also integrates PCIe 6.x PHY, DDR5 PHY, dual RISC-V processor subsystems and rich management interfaces, enabling PCIe standard add-in cards (AIC), EDSFF and other CXL memory expansion form factors. Montage's CXL 3.2 MXC has already been adopted by major memory module makers including Samsung and SK hynix for their next-generation CXL products, with initial validation complete, and supports mainstream platforms such as Intel Xeon and AMD EPYC.

"Starting trial production of the CXL 3.2 MXC chip ahead of the industry is an important milestone for Montage in driving CXL technology innovation and industrialization," said Stephen Tai, President of Montage Technology. "Facing the ever-growing memory demands of the AI era, we provide customers with memory expansion solutions combining high bandwidth, large capacity and high reliability." Montage will showcase the chip at CXL DevCon on August 3 and FMS (August 4-6) in Santa Clara. With AI model training driving explosive demand for memory bandwidth, CXL memory pooling is emerging as a key architecture for next-generation data centers — and Montage's early production ramp puts a Chinese memory-interface chipmaker in a more assertive position in the AI infrastructure race.
