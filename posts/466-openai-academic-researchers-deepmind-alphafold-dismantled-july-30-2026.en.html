⚡ July 30 Tech Double Feature: OpenAI Offers Free GPT-5.6 to 100,000 Scientists; DeepMind Disbands Nobel-Winning AlphaFold Team

I. OpenAI Launches ChatGPT for Academic Researchers: Free GPT-5.6 Sol Pro for 100,000 Scientists

On July 29, 2026, OpenAI officially announced the "ChatGPT for Academic Researchers" program, offering free access to its frontier AI models — including the flagship GPT-5.6 Sol Pro — to 100,000 researchers worldwide. The program represents OpenAI's largest academic support initiative to date, backed by a total commitment of over $250 million through 2027.

Program Details

  • Scale: The first cohort of 10,000 researchers will begin accessing this summer, expanding to 100,000 by 2027
  • Launch partners: Institute for Advanced Study (IAS) and École normale supérieure (ENS) are already onboard
  • Free resources: Participants receive the equivalent of a $200/month ChatGPT Pro subscription, including GPT-5.6 Sol Pro, ChatGPT Work, and the Codex coding environment
  • Enhanced features: Higher usage limits, larger context windows, and an expanded Deep Research capability
  • Collaboration: Each approved researcher can invite up to 4 colleagues from their institution

Privacy & Security

OpenAI emphasized that researchers' workspaces come with enterprise-grade privacy and security protections, and data will not be used for model training by default. Eligible research areas span genomic analysis, protein modeling, literature reviews, grant writing, and scientific publishing across biology, chemistry, computer science, engineering, mathematics, and physics.

Soaring Demand for AI in Research

OpenAI disclosed that approximately 1.3 million people use ChatGPT weekly for advanced science and mathematics, generating about 8.4 million messages. In mathematics, a growing number of published papers now acknowledge ChatGPT's contributions. Over the past six months, AI has evolved from occasional use on isolated problems to a regular fixture in mathematical research workflows.

"We believe the benefits of frontier AI should not be concentrated in a few companies and well-resourced labs. Scientific progress depends on researchers asking the right questions, testing new ideas, and building on what others have discovered." — OpenAI Official Statement

Competitive Landscape

The announcement follows Anthropic's launch of Claude Science on July 1, 2026, which offers AI research tools to all paid subscribers without credential verification. Unlike Anthropic's open-access model, OpenAI's program requires institutional affiliation verification through SheerID and a recent publication record — highlighting the diverging approaches between the two leading AI companies in the academic market.

Notably, the program explicitly excludes weight-level access for AI safety researchers — participants can only query the model via API inference endpoints, without access to model weights or training data. The AI safety community has long argued that independent safety evaluation requires weight-level access.

II. Google DeepMind Disbands Nobel-Winning AlphaFold Team: Major Strategic Pivot

Google DeepMind has dismantled its Nobel Prize-winning AlphaFold team, with most original paper authors reassigned over the past year, according to a report from Caixin on July 30. The move signals a major strategic shift at the world's premier AI research institution.

AlphaFold's Historic Legacy

AlphaFold, DeepMind's AI system for protein structure prediction, earned its developers Demis Hassabis and John Jumper the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for "breaking protein structure prediction through artificial intelligence." Hailed as a "landmark achievement of AI-driven scientific progress," the system has been used by millions of researchers worldwide for drug discovery, disease research, and synthetic biology. AlphaFold single-handedly compressed what once took years or decades of protein structure determination into mere minutes.

Behind the Dismantling

Internal sources indicate the restructuring stems from DeepMind's shifting strategic priorities. As the AI competitive landscape evolves at breakneck speed, DeepMind is redirecting more resources toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) frontier research rather than single-domain biological applications. AlphaFold, as a fully validated technology whose research phase is largely complete, is gradually having its maintenance and iteration responsibilities transferred to other Google divisions.

DeepMind also faces intense pressure from the global AI talent war. With competitors like OpenAI, Anthropic, and xAI aggressively poaching top researchers with lavish compensation packages, reconfiguring how its elite research talent is deployed has become an existential necessity for maintaining competitiveness.

Industry Reaction

The dismantling of the AlphaFold team sent shockwaves through the bioinformatics community. Many researchers expressed their regret on social media, calling it "the end of an era." However, analysts note that AlphaFold's core technology has been fully open-sourced, with its algorithms and model weights already widely deployed — ensuring the technology's continued impact even after the original team's dissolution.

"AlphaFold wasn't just DeepMind's achievement — it was a milestone for all of science. The team may be gone, but AlphaFold's technical legacy will forever change structural biology." — A computational biologist's reflection

The restructuring has also raised concerns about the concentration of AI research directions at major labs. Critics warn that when the AGI race becomes the sole focus of every AI giant, "pure science" projects like AlphaFold — requiring long-term investment without immediate commercial returns — may find it increasingly difficult to secure resources.
