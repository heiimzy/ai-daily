⚡ Aug 3 World Update: Trump Says US-Iran Talks Reshape Today After Shelving 'Largest Since WWII' Strike, Israel Sidelined Feels 'Abandoned by an Ally'

The conflict between the United States, Israel and Iran hit a pivotal turning point this week. US President Donald Trump announced on Sunday that the planned new round of large-scale strikes on Iran had been called off — replaced instead by US-Iran negotiations set to resume Monday afternoon. The operation, described as the "largest since World War II," was shelved at the last minute, while Israel, a key ally fighting alongside Washington, was reportedly left out of the decision-making for hours. Whether the Middle East will actually cool down remains highly uncertain.

I. A last-minute reversal: 'Largest since WWII' strike canceled

Trump posted on Truth Social on Saturday that the US was prepared to strike "the Islamic Republic of Iran at levels of Military Terror, Strength, and Power not seen since World War II," warning that if America hit hard enough, Iran would eventually "not be able to take it." And yet, a day later, he announced he was agreeing to cancel the operation.

"We have just been asked by Iran, and other Middle Eastern Countries, to hold off any attack in that the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to," Trump wrote. He stressed that any deal must include the "Immediate, Complete and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT — and an end to Iran's nuclear threat."

"It would have been a massive attack, the biggest since World War II, and it would have been disastrous for them. They didn't want us to do it." — Trump to reporters aboard Air Force One.

Multiple US media outlets, citing sources, said Washington had been planning a joint bombing campaign over the weekend, with Iranian refineries and power plants among the possible targets. Before the pause was announced, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman had called Trump directly, emphasizing the need to "prioritize dialogue to de-escalate tensions in the Middle East."

🟢 Iran: Oman talks in 'final stage,' but strait won't return to pre-war status

The Iran-region negotiations with Oman on managing the Strait of Hormuz have been officially confirmed to be in their "final stage." Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi told the cabinet that the talks were advancing well and "getting close to completion." Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei confirmed to state media that a deal centers on securing a new route that is "neither the northern nor the southern route, one that respects the sovereign rights of both sides and protects both nations' security."

Tehran, however, repeatedly stressed that any route agreement does not mean the strait would reopen, and that its future management must be led by Iran. "The strait will not return to its pre-conflict status; the United States is responsible for its closure," Baqaei said. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps also denied that any final deal had been reached, signaling that this critical issue remains a live tug of war.

🎭 Israel's awkward position: learned of the strike cancelation via Trump's social media

One of the biggest ripples from the shelved strikes is Israel's marginalization. Israeli officials said they were excluded from the decision-making for hours before the US decided to cancel the planned strike, and had no idea the cancellation was coming.

"For several hours, we were in complete informational vacuum and uncertainty. President Trump put us in the fog." — senior Israeli official.

Even Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and Defense Minister Yoav Katz reportedly learned of the US decision through Trump's posts — including through Trump's appearance on CBS. The stonewalling has left Israeli officials "extremely frustrated" and instead, them and their to a high-alert defense posture, as Trump has previously called off strikes only to resume military action. For Trump, the pressure appears to be to get the deal — and Israel has become just one card in his US-Iran talks.

🕊 Gaza, war raging: Turkey lashes 'expansionist' approach, strikes kill dozens

While Middle East peace efforts appear to be advancing, Gaza remains far from calm. Israeli jets carried out a second straight day of strikes on Gaza City, Deir al-Balah and Khan Younis on Sunday, with Palestinian health officials putting the death toll from the two days at at least 20 dead — the highest single-day toll in weeks.

Turkey's Foreign Ministry then roared, slamming Israeli PM Netanyahu's "expansionist approach" and claiming his "sole objective is to displace Palestinians from their homeland and prevent stability in the region," urging the international community to adopt a "more determined, consistent and robust stance." Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen, meanwhile, said plainly that "no deal has been reached to stop the attacks" on Gaza.
