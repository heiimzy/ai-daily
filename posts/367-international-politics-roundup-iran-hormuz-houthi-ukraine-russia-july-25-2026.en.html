US-Iran: First Night Without Airstrikes in Two Weeks as Trump Says Talks 'Most Serious'

For the first time in 13 consecutive nights, Iran reported no US airstrikes overnight into Saturday, July 25. Iranian Health Ministry spokesman Hossein Kermanpour posted on X that "Iran had a peaceful night," while US Central Command did not announce new strikes — a notable departure from its daily pattern over the past two weeks.

Speaking at the White House on Friday, President Donald Trump revealed that the US and Iran are engaged in "by far the most serious" talks since the conflict began. "We are talking to them. I think they're being serious... they are being by far the most serious that we've seen them, but that doesn't mean we get there," Trump told reporters. He said he had not yet decided whether to order "major" strikes, describing a negotiated agreement as "the smarter strategy."

According to The New York Times, Trump met with senior advisors on Friday to discuss potentially intensifying military operations, but no final decision has been made. Trump outlined two paths: "a military exit where we just keep going... or there's a smarter strategy that you make a deal."

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi accused the United States of trying to force open a new route through the Strait of Hormuz, bypassing Iranian authorities, in violation of the June Memorandum of Understanding. He reiterated that Tehran has the right to set transit routes and mechanisms for the strategic waterway.

Iran's IRGC Stops 4 Ships in Hormuz as Omani Delegation Arrives in Tehran

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) announced Saturday that its naval forces stopped four vessels attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz via "unauthorized and unsafe routes" over the past 24 hours. The ships were warned with warning shots and "altered course," according to a statement carried by state television. The IRGC has previously warned that any vessels attempting to cross using unapproved routes face a "forceful response."

Meanwhile, a diplomatic delegation from Oman arrived in Tehran late Friday for talks with Iranian officials on managing shipping traffic through the critical waterway. Oman controls the strategic strait's southern coastline and has historically served as the principal intermediary between Tehran and Washington. Iranian Foreign Minister Araghchi held a phone call with his Omani counterpart Badr Albusaidi, Iran's state-run IRNA news agency reported.

The diplomatic development comes as the US military disabled a second commercial vessel attempting to breach the American blockade of Iranian ports. US Central Command spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins said the M/T Lavine was disabled in the Gulf of Oman after the crew attempted to run the blockade at least four times and ignored repeated warnings. US forces fired into the vessel's engine room.

Houthis Launch Missile and Drone Attacks on Saudi Aramco Facilities

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced Saturday that they had fired missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia in retaliation for Saudi airstrikes on the strategic Red Sea port city of Hodeida and Kamaran Island. Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree, the Houthi military spokesman, said the attacks targeted Aramco oil facilities in the Red Sea cities of Yanbu and Jizan.

Saudi Arabia's Civil Defense confirmed that air raid sirens sounded multiple times early Saturday in both cities. Greek security sources reported that Saudi air defense systems intercepted two ballistic missiles from Yemen targeting oil refineries in Yanbu. Saudi authorities later declared the danger had passed.

"In response to this blatant and criminal aggression, the Yemeni armed forces carried out two qualitative military operations," a Houthi statement said, claiming direct hits on Aramco facilities. The group pledged to maintain its naval blockade of Saudi Arabia and warned it was prepared to expand military operations.

The escalation marks a dangerous new front in the Middle East conflict, adding Red Sea shipping routes to the existing crisis in the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude oil briefly surged above $100 per barrel this week — the highest since May — before settling around $96 on Friday. US gasoline prices averaged $4.11 per gallon, according to AAA.

Zelensky Warns of Massive Russian Attack Within 48 Hours; Ukraine Strikes Deep Inside Russia

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky warned Friday that Russian forces have prepared missiles for a massive attack on Ukraine within 48 hours. "The strike could come today: preliminary information indicates it may happen within 48 hours," Zelensky said in an evening address, urging Ukrainians to pay close attention to air raid alerts.

The warning comes at a critical diplomatic juncture, with Zelensky scheduled to meet President Trump in Washington on Tuesday, July 28. White House officials confirmed the meeting, during which US and Ukrainian officials will discuss renewed peace proposals including a possible air ceasefire.

On Saturday, Zelensky confirmed that Ukrainian forces carried out strikes deep inside Russian territory on Friday night, targeting a weapons facility in Kirov, an oil refinery in Tyumen, a logistics facility in Yekaterinburg, and a fuel depot in Rostov-on-Don. "We also achieved very strong results with long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea — including vessels used in military cargo shipments involving Iran, as well as a warship," Zelensky said, releasing video footage of the strikes.

The warning followed a Russian missile strike on the Kyiv region on Friday that killed at least 10 people and injured approximately 100 at a defense industry exhibition. Latvia confirmed its consulate in Slovyansk was damaged in the attacks. EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said the strike "underscores Moscow's utter disregard for international law."

The United Nations reported this week that 1,396 civilians were killed and 7,978 injured in Ukraine during the first half of 2026 — a 37% increase year-on-year and nearly double the casualty rate of the first half of 2024.

Russian Reconnaissance Aircraft Violates Japanese Airspace

Japan's Defense Ministry announced Friday that a Russian military IL-20 reconnaissance aircraft intruded into Japanese territorial airspace over the disputed Northern Territories — islands seized by the former Soviet Union at the end of World War II that are claimed by Japan. An Air Self-Defense Force fighter was scrambled in response.

The Russian aircraft flew from the continent to the Pacific Ocean via the Sea of Okhotsk, traveled south to waters off Miyagi Prefecture, then returned to the Sea of Japan via the Sea of Okhotsk. A ministry official noted that Japan is currently in a situation where it is virtually unable to exercise jurisdiction over the disputed islands, adding that the territorial issue must be resolved through diplomatic negotiations. The territorial dispute has prevented Tokyo and Moscow from concluding a formal peace treaty ending WWII hostilities.

Other International Developments

Trump Adds Abraham Accords Condition to Saudi Nuclear Deal

President Trump abruptly attached a new condition to the US-Saudi civilian nuclear agreement less than 24 hours after it was signed, demanding Riyadh join the Abraham Accords, CNN reported Friday. The reversal blindsided both the Energy Department and Riyadh, according to sources.

UN Chief Guterres Visits Syria's Golan Heights

UN Secretary-General António Guterres made his first visit to Damascus in 17 years and traveled to the Golan Heights, calling for international support for Syria.

ICC Removes Karim Khan as Prosecutor

The International Criminal Court's member states voted in an unprecedented decision to remove Chief Prosecutor Karim Khan from his post over sexual misconduct allegations.
