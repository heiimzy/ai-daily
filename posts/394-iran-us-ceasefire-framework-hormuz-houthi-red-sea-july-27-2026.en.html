⚡ July 27 International Roundup: US-Iran Ceasefire Framework Draft Exposed — 60-Day Truce, Lifting Blockade, IRGC Says 'Low' Chance of War Restart, Houthis Strike Saudi Refinery, Ukraine Hits Iranian Ship in Caspian

I. Breakthrough: Details of US-Iran Ceasefire Framework Emerge

In a major diplomatic breakthrough early on July 27, Iran's Mizan News Agency — affiliated with the country's judiciary — disclosed a preliminary draft of the US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) framework, as reported by Xinhua News Agency. This marks the closest the two sides have come to a diplomatic resolution since President Trump declared the ceasefire "no longer in effect" on July 8, triggering 13 consecutive nights of US airstrikes on Iran.

According to the disclosed framework document, the United States commits to withdrawing its military forces from areas surrounding Iran and lifting the naval blockade on Iranian ports. Iran's National Security Council member Boroujerdi confirmed on July 27 that under the preliminary draft agreement, the US will commit to a 60-day comprehensive ceasefire across all fronts in the first phase, "especially in Lebanon." Boroujerdi also confirmed that the release of a significant portion of Iran's frozen assets and the end of the maritime blockade are part of the agreement.

"Iran's diplomatic institutions and state system will not make decisions based on Trump's social media posts. What matters is the final outcome of negotiations. Any document that reaches the signing stage will be formulated within the framework of Iran's systemic red lines and aimed at protecting national interests and the rights of the people," Boroujerdi emphasized.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy official Mohammad Akbarzadeh delivered an even clearer de-escalation signal on July 27. Speaking to the public in Zanjan province, he stated that the possibility of war restarting with the United States is "low," though Iran's armed forces remain "fully loaded" and prepared for defense. Akbarzadeh also criticized the US for "strategic failure" on the Strait of Hormuz issue: "They claimed they could open the Strait of Hormuz, but after this passage was blocked, they tried everything and were helpless."

Tabriz Airport Reopens: Signal of Civil Aviation Recovery

Iran's Civil Aviation Organization spokesperson announced on July 27 that Tabriz International Airport — which had been damaged in recent skirmishes — has been repaired and reopened. Tabriz is Iran's third busiest international airport, serving 9 international destinations. The previous day, Iran's Civil Aviation Organization announced that 10 airports nationwide would extend operating hours to 24/7 service, including Tehran's Imam Khomeini International Airport and Mehrabad Airport. So far, 20 Iranian airports have resumed operations since the interim ceasefire took effect.

II. Red Sea New Front: Houthis Launch Full-Scale Attack on Saudi Aramco Facilities, Greece Enters the Fight

Despite the emerging ceasefire曙光 between Washington and Tehran, fighting continues to rage on the Red Sea front. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi forces launched a comprehensive strike on July 24-25 against two of Saudi Arabia's most strategically important targets — the Jizan Refinery and the Yanbu oil terminal, both owned by Saudi state oil giant Aramco.

Video footage verified by Reuters showed a massive column of smoke rising from the Jizan refinery. Trading sources in Asia reported that fuel and oil storage facilities in Jizan may have been severely damaged, with six major explosions recorded at the site. The Jizan refinery processes 400,000 barrels of crude oil per day, making it a core energy asset for Saudi Arabia on the Red Sea coast.

At the Yanbu oil terminal — Saudi Arabia's primary west coast export gateway and the kingdom's key alternative route bypassing the Strait of Hormuz — two ballistic missiles fired by the Houthis were intercepted by a Patriot air defense system. Notably, the Patriot battery was operated not by US forces but by Greek military personnel stationed in Saudi Arabia under a bilateral defense agreement. The Greek Air Force media office confirmed that surface-to-air missile systems operated by Greek crews downed two ballistic missiles and one drone over Yanbu. This marks the first confirmed instance of Greek forces directly engaging in combat operations since the 2026 US-Iran conflict began.

Middle East analysts warn that the Houthi strikes represent a formal expansion of the US-Iran war into the Red Sea theater. If the Houthis proceed to blockade the Bab al-Mandeb Strait — the southern entrance to the Red Sea — both of the world's most critical oil chokepoints (Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb) could be simultaneously obstructed, triggering a global supply chain crisis of unprecedented scale. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, in an interview on July 27, stated that "there is no military solution to the situation in Yemen and the Bab al-Mandeb," and offered to mediate talks between the Houthis and Riyadh.

III. War Spreads to the Caspian: Ukraine Strikes Iranian Ship, Tehran Threatens Retaliation

In a dramatic escalation, Ukrainian forces struck an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea in the early hours of July 25, killing one sailor and wounding another. This marks the first publicly confirmed direct military strike by Ukraine against Iranian targets. Iran's Foreign Ministry strongly condemned the attack as a violation of the UN Charter's fundamental principles and an "act of aggression" that threatens the security of Caspian littoral states. Iran summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Tehran to convey its protest.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy claimed responsibility on X, stating that Ukraine had achieved "very strong results" with long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea, hitting "vessels used in military cargo shipments involving Iran, as well as a warship." Ukraine's Security Service (SBU) later confirmed that the targeted vessels were carrying military cargo between Iran and Russia.

Iran Weighs Ballistic Missile Strike on Kyiv

According to UK's i Paper citing Iranian sources, the IRGC Aerospace Force is actively considering a retaliatory ballistic missile strike against a strategic target in Kyiv. Iran's Khorramshahr-4 medium-range ballistic missile has a stated range of approximately 2,000 km (1,250 miles), theoretically sufficient to reach Kyiv (about 1,850 km from Tabriz). Analysts warn that such a strike would formally merge the US-Iran war with the Russia-Ukraine conflict, creating an unprecedented entanglement of the two deadliest active wars.

IV. Netanyahu to Visit Washington Monday: Israel Factor Re-enters the Arena

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced on July 27 that he will visit Washington on Monday (July 28) at President Trump's invitation. In a post on X, Netanyahu stated: "I will discuss all pending issues with President Trump, including the situation in Iran." Analysts note that Israel has refrained from directly entering the latest round of US-Iran hostilities, but Netanyahu's upcoming visit signals that Jerusalem may exert significant influence on the negotiating process. Israel has consistently opposed any agreement that allows Iran to retain its nuclear infrastructure.

As of press time, the US and Iran have gone two consecutive nights without military exchanges — the longest period of calm since July 11. US Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz confirmed to Fox News on July 27 that Trump's decision to pause airstrikes was intended to "give talks some space." However, Waltz emphasized that "all military options remain firmly on the table."
