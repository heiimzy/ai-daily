⚡ Aug 6 Tech Update: X's Nikita Bier Steps Down, Gmail Drops Third-Party 'Send As', Windows 11 Perf Analyzer Gets AI, 2200V GaN, China Confirms the Glueball

Over the past hour, big-company moves, software updates and a landmark physics breakthrough all landed at once: X head of product Nikita Bier announced he is stepping down to an advisor role; Google said Gmail will drop third-party "Send as" from January 2027; Microsoft is testing AI and MCP inside its Windows performance analyzer; Power Integrations unveiled the highest commercial-voltage gallium-based technology at 2200V; and China's BESIII collaboration reported the first confirmation of the glueball — a discovery worthy of the physics textbooks. Here is this round's tech roundup.

1. X: Head of Product Nikita Bier Steps Down, Moves to Advisor Role

On August 5, Nikita Bier, X's head of product, announced he is leaving the role and will transition to an advisor. "Time to pass the torch and demote myself back to my natural state: a poster," he wrote. The announcement comes just over a month after he celebrated his first anniversary at the company, and only weeks after Elon Musk's merged SpaceX completed its public listing.

During his tenure, Bier said "pretty much every area" of X was rebuilt — including the feed, the Android app, chat and notifications. He specifically called out the long-delayed, recently launched X Money feature (Musk had once expected it before the end of 2024). X's "next leadership team" will include Benji Taylor on design, Jonah Katz on iOS and Mridul Singhai leading product. Bier, a co-founder of startups tbh (acquired by Meta) and Gas (acquired by Discord), now returns to his roots as "a poster."

2. Google: Gmail Drops Third-Party "Send as" in January 2027

Google announced that from January 2027 Gmail will stop supporting the "Send as" feature for third-party email addresses — users will no longer be able to send mail as Yahoo, Outlook or a custom-domain external address from Gmail's web or mobile apps. Google has updated its help documentation and published the timeline, confirming the change does not affect Google Workspace aliases or other Gmail addresses the user owns.

Google said the move keeps Gmail "secure, smart and easy to use" by retiring legacy features that require heavy maintenance. The change also covers Gmailify and web-based POP fetching. The news sparked wide discussion on Reddit: many power users rely on Gmail as the unified inbox for their personal-domain mailboxes and argue the move nudges them toward paid Google Workspace subscriptions (or paid custom-domain services like Apple iCloud+). Affected users can still manage multiple accounts via native mobile clients or desktop apps like Thunderbird and Outlook over IMAP/POP — and they have a wide runway before the change takes effect.

3. Microsoft: Windows Performance Analyzer Gets AI + MCP

Windows Latest reported on August 5 that Microsoft is testing AI integration in Windows Performance Analyzer (WPA), using the Model Context Protocol (MCP) to let developers diagnose Windows 11 performance issues in natural language. WPA is Microsoft's tool for analyzing Windows performance — tracking data, processes, call stacks and CPU/disk/memory/scheduling activity, typically used to find the root cause of slowness, latency and regressions.

With AI woven in, developers no longer need to parse tables and charts item by item. They can ask GitHub Copilot questions such as "why is CPU spiking" or "what is hitting the disk," and let the AI generate a summary from WPA data. Microsoft says it already uses the AI-assisted WPA internally to investigate Windows issues, and it should help app, driver and hardware developers identify input latency, regressions, memory pressure and scheduling anomalies. The company is also optimizing memory usage across Windows 11 and its apps to improve performance on machines with 8GB or more of RAM.

4. Power Integrations: 2200V PowiGaN — the Highest Commercial Voltage GaN

On August 5 Power Integrations announced that its PowiGaN gallium technology now supports a rated 2200V — the highest voltage tolerance for gallium nitride in commercial use. The move supports higher-voltage bus architectures, enabling power designs for future data centers as well as electric vehicles, solar photovoltaics and battery energy storage systems.

President and CEO Jennifer Lloyd said the milestone "provides ample voltage margin for emerging high-voltage systems while enabling the high switching frequencies needed to maximize power density," extending GaN into voltage ranges traditionally served by silicon carbide (SiC) and offering a compelling high-frequency alternative for solar, HVDC transmission and advanced industrial power conversion. For data centers and renewable infrastructure, that means next-gen power supplies can be smaller, more efficient and higher power.

5. First Confirmation of the Glueball: China's BESIII Collaboration

According to the Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of High Energy, on August 6 the BESIII collaboration at the Beijing Electron-Positron Collider announced at ICHEP 2026, held in Brazil, that after 15 years of work it has built the full evidence chain confirming the existence of glueballs — solving a mystery that has confounded physics for nearly half a century. It provides a decisive validation of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) and demonstrates the existence of a new kind of matter built purely from force itself.

Atomic nuclei are made of protons and neutrons, which are in turn made of quarks. Quarks are held together by gluons carrying the strong force, much as photons carry electromagnetism — but while photons do not attract each other, gluons do. That means gluons can glom onto one another to form a brand-new particle: the glueball. For fifty years experiments failed to find one, and whether the glueball exists has been a key test of QCD. BESIII produces large numbers of J/psi particles and searches their decays for the glueball candidate X(2370); after measuring its quantum numbers to match lattice-QCD predictions, the team has now pinned down its most important signature — the "flavor singlet" property. Since ordinary particles carry different flavor quarks while a glueball is made purely of gluons and carries no flavor info, X(2,370)'s identity is finally settled: it is the glueball physicists had sought for nearly five decades.

The work was led by a team including Professor Jin Shan of Nanjing University and Researcher Huang Yanping of the CAS Institute of High Energy Physics. Since its major upgrade in 2008, the BESIII detector has collected more than 10 billion J/psi events, making it a world leader in the charmonium/tau-charm energy region. ICHEP is held every two years, and an extra plenary talk is reserved only for truly major results — the glueball discovery clearly qualifies.
