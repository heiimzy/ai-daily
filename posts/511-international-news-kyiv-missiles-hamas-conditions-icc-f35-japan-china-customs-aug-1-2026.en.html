Russian Missiles Strike Kyiv Overnight: At Least 3 Killed and 15 Wounded as Explosions Rock the Capital

Russia launched another missile attack on Ukraine's heartland as the war enters a new week. Al Jazeera reported at around 02:06 GMT that Russian missiles hit Ukraine's capital Kyiv in the early hours of Saturday, with the city's mayor saying the strikes killed at least three people and wounded 15 others. Witnesses told the Reuters news agency that the attack triggered more than a dozen explosions that reverberated across the city, and one apartment building was partially collapsed and caught fire after being hit, with residents briefly trapped. Ukrainian authorities had earlier issued air-raid warnings that multiple missiles were approaching the capital. The overnight barrage — the latest in a series of nighttime strikes on Kyiv amid Ukraine's repeated appeals for more Western air defenses — has further raised tensions on the front line.

Hamas Sets Conditions for Disarmament: Israeli Withdrawal from Gaza Required; Israel Strikes Gaza the Same Day

Less than a day after President Trump announced that Hamas had agreed to "complete disarmament," tensions in Gaza flared again. Al Jazeera reported at 00:48 GMT that Israel launched attacks across Gaza one day after Hamas agreed to disarm. The IDF said the strikes targeted terrorists: a Hamas operative who kidnapped Noa Argamani and Avinatan Or on October 7 was killed in an air strike, and a Hamas commander who had held hostages Romi Gonen and Noa Marciano was killed in a separate attack; Gaza officials reported that two children were killed in the strikes; and the IDF struck a mosque in Gaza City, saying it was used for weapons storage. Meanwhile, NHK reported at 01:14 GMT that Hamas issued a statement insisting that disarmament is conditional — including on the withdrawal of Israeli forces from Gaza. The statement contrasts sharply with the deal announced by Trump, casting fresh doubt on the ceasefire prospects.

Trump: Rubio Is Working to Bring the ICC 'to Heel' to 'Defend Bibi and Others'

The Times of Israel reported at 00:46 GMT that at a cabinet meeting at the Camp David presidential retreat, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio lashed out at the International Criminal Court, saying it "has made themselves illegitimate because they claim that even if you're not a member of that court, we can come after you." "We tried to get them to reform and listen to us, they wouldn't do it. They're very arrogant. So we've now begun an effort to really try to bring them to heel," he added. President Donald Trump then clarified that Rubio was not advancing the initiative to defend him — the ICC is not currently seeking to prosecute the US president — saying, "He's trying to defend Bibi and various other people," referring to Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. Rubio also celebrated the administration's brokering of agreements between Israel and Lebanon as well as the disarmament deal between the Board of Peace and Hamas. The remarks came several weeks after Rubio unveiled the administration's effort to "dismantle" the ICC. Neither the United States nor Israel has ever been a member of the court, and Trump's hostility toward it dates back to his first term.

US F-35B Crashes in California: Pilot Ejects and Is Expected to Survive

Al Jazeera, citing the Associated Press, reported at 01:55 GMT that a US Marine Corps F-35B Lightning II stealth fighter crashed in California. The jet went down on Friday near the Miramar airbase in San Diego. "The pilot ejected, was transported to a local medical facility in stable condition for evaluation and treatment of non-life-threatening injuries," the Marine Corps said in a statement. Aerial video showed a plume of black smoke rising from the wreckage in a dirt field. The F-35B is the short-takeoff/vertical-landing variant of the fifth-generation fighter and a workhorse of the Marine Corps; the cause of the crash remains under investigation.

Survey: About 60% of Japanese Firms in China Are Satisfied, but Customs Inspections Are Lengthening

NHK reported at 02:06 GMT that a survey of Japanese companies operating in China found that roughly 60 percent said they are satisfied with the business environment. At the same time, however, amid steadily tightening export controls on dual-use items, customs inspections are taking noticeably longer even for general cargo, affecting business operations. With US-China tech competition and export restrictions continuing to tighten, Japanese companies face new pressure at the customs and supply-chain level — longer checks are beginning to affect ordinary trade goods, not just high-tech equipment, making it a fresh variable in Japan-China economic ties.
