⚡ July 26 International Roundup: Iran Summons Ukraine Diplomat Over Caspian Vessel Attack, Romania F-16 Shoots Down Second Russian Drone, US Blockade Stats Revealed, Israel Announces Major West Bank Settlement Expansion

1. Iran Summons Ukraine's Charge d'Affaires Over Caspian Sea Vessel Attack

Iran on Saturday summoned Ukraine's charge d'affaires in Tehran to lodge a strong protest over what it claims was a Ukrainian military attack on an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea that killed one sailor and injured another. According to Iranian state media IRIB, the Ukrainian diplomat was summoned by Manouchehr Moradi, assistant foreign minister and director general for Eurasia, who described the attack as a "hostile and criminal act." Moradi stressed that Iran would firmly defend its national security and interests, warning that attacks against Iranian citizens "will not go unanswered."

Iran condemned the incident as an act of aggression and called on the UN Security Council, the European Union, and the international community to hold those responsible accountable. Ukraine's SBU security service confirmed the strikes targeted the Filanovsky oil platform (belonging to Russia's Lukoil) in the Caspian Sea, as well as the Port Olya 2 and Begey cargo vessels believed to be involved in military cargo shipments between Russia and Iran.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on X that Ukraine achieved "very strong results" with long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea, including against "vessels used in military cargo shipments involving Iran, as well as a warship." He did not specifically identify the vessel cited by Tehran.

2. Iran's FM Araghchi and EU's Kallas Discuss Caspian Attack, Hormuz Tensions

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi and EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas held a phone call Saturday to discuss the Caspian Sea vessel attack and tensions in the Strait of Hormuz. Araghchi strongly condemned the attack and urged a "decisive response" from the UN Security Council and the EU. Both officials emphasized the need for diplomatic initiatives to manage tensions and prevent further escalation in the region.

3. CENTCOM: US Naval Blockade Against Iran Remains in Full Effect

The US military's Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed Saturday that the naval blockade against Iran remains in full effect, releasing detailed statistics for the first time. As of July 25, US forces had redirected 12 commercial vessels attempting to breach the blockade, disabled two that failed to comply, and boarded two others for verification. CENTCOM reported completing a verification boarding of the Comoros-flagged oil tanker M/T Charminar in the Arabian Sea earlier Saturday, after which the vessel continued its journey. US forces also disabled the Mozambique-flagged M/T Lavine in the Gulf of Oman on Friday after its crew repeatedly attempted to violate the blockade and ignored multiple warnings.

4. Ukraine Strike on Occupied Zaporizhzhia Holiday Camp Kills 11, Including 4 Children

Moscow-installed officials say Ukrainian forces attacked holiday camps in the partly Russian-occupied Zaporizhzhia region overnight, killing 11 people and wounding 14. Kremlin-installed regional head Yevgeny Balitsky said four children were among the eight people who died at the resorts. "The enemy saw and understood who it was targeting," he said on Telegram. Ukraine did not immediately comment on the attack. Separately, in Ukraine's north-eastern Sumy region, a Russian drone strike on a "civilian facility" killed three drivers for the private postal service Nova Poshta. Russia's defense ministry said it targeted a Ukrainian drone "storage and launch site" in Sumy.

5. Romania F-16 Shoots Down Second Russian Drone in Two Days

Romania announced Saturday that it had safely shot down a second drone breaching its national airspace — the second consecutive day of such incidents. President Nicusor Dan said the drone was shot down "by a Romanian pilot from an F-16 aircraft" at 8:30 AM local time, 10 km west of Sfantu Gheorghe in the Danube Delta. The defense ministry said radar detected the airspace breach at 8:22 AM, and two F-16 fighter jets were scrambled. Romania shares a 650 km land border with Ukraine and has reported approximately 30 Russian drone airspace breaches since Russia's full-scale invasion began in 2022.

6. Israel Approves 763 New Settlement Units, West Bank Violence Intensifies

Israel has announced plans to build 763 new settlement units in the occupied West Bank, a threefold expansion of the Eli settlement. The decision comes amid a sharp escalation in West Bank violence following a deadly clash in the village of Tal that left four Palestinians and two Israeli soldiers dead. Israeli forces subsequently arrested approximately 70 Palestinian residents and conducted extensive raids across the region. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu vowed "powerful action," including demolishing the homes of Palestinian assailants and accelerating settlement construction. Israeli police also arrested nine protesters at a Tel Aviv rally against settler violence in the West Bank. Human Rights Watch called for immediate steps to prevent further escalation, criticizing the lack of protective measures for Palestinians amid rising settler attacks.

7. Houthi-Saudi Tit-for-Tat Escalates: Red Sea Conflict Intensifies

Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthi rebels said Saturday they carried out coordinated missile and drone strikes on Saudi Aramco energy facilities in Jizan and Yanbu, in retaliation for Saudi-led coalition airstrikes on the Houthi-held Red Sea port of Hodeidah. The Saudi-backed internationally recognized Yemeni government's air force struck Houthi missile and rocket launch platforms and weapons depots in the Marib and al-Jawf provinces, killing several Houthi members including missile specialists. The escalation follows the Houthis' announcement of a maritime ban on Saudi-linked shipping last week and claimed attacks on two Saudi oil tankers on Thursday. Although a UN-mediated truce expired in 2022, a fragile de facto ceasefire had largely held until this latest round of escalation raised fresh concerns over Red Sea shipping security and regional stability.
