⚡ Aug 3 Tech Update: Naver Teams Up With Nvidia & Brookfield on an AI Factory, Fangjing's A1 Round Crosses $1B, Crealight Secures 10M DSP Chips

Monday afternoon brought a flurry of fresh moves across the global AI infrastructure and homegrown-chip landscape. In South Korea, Naver for the first time published the detailed contract structure for the first phase of its AI Factory, with Nvidia set to invest $1 billion in equity and Brookfield preparing to put up to $9 billion into an SPV to procure GPUs. In China, Fangjing Technology, focused on separated-architecture intelligent computing systems, closed an A1 round that pushed its valuation past the $1 billion (RMB) mark, officially earning "unicorn" status. Meanwhile, with AI optical interconnect demand exploding, Crealight locked in roughly 10 million DSP chips for 2027 to seize a key supply-chain bottleneck. Here is this roundup of general tech news.

1. Naver AI Factory Phase 1: Nvidia to Inject $1B, Brookfield Up to $9B via SPV

South Korean internet giant Naver disclosed the detailed contract structure for the first phase of its AI Factory on August 3. According to Naver's reference material on "NAVER AI Factory Phase 1," Nvidia will make a $1 billion (about KRW 1.4 trillion) equity investment in Naver, while Brookfield plans to invest up to $9 billion by setting up an SPV to acquire the GPUs and data-center equipment needed to run the AI Factory.

Under this structure, Naver will set up a subsidiary to fully oversee AI Factory operations and its overall AI computing business. Brookfield's SPV will directly raise and deploy up to $9 billion to buy GPUs and data-center assets. Naver's AI Factory operating company will procure computing resources from the SPV and pay on a usage basis, easing the financial burden of building its own massive infrastructure; Naver is also considering taking a minority stake in the SPV to lock in stable compute supply. Naver has named Brookfield its exclusive preferred negotiation partner for 12 weeks; if talks conclude within that window, Brookfield will be confirmed as the final financing partner.

On scale, Naver aims to build gigawatt (GW)-level infrastructure: 55 MW in the first half of next year, a cumulative 100 MW by the end of next year, and a cumulative 200 MW by 2028. Nvidia will join as the primary GPU supplier, while Naver leads data-center site selection, construction and operations. A Naver spokesperson said the company would start with the "Gak Sejong" hyperscale data center and gradually expand to Europe, the Middle East and beyond, while cautioning that terms and schedules could shift as detailed contracts are finalized.

2. Fangjing Technology Closes A1 Round, Valuing It Above $1B as a Unicorn

On August 3, Fangjing Technology, a Chinese developer of the next generation of AI computing systems, announced the completion of an A1 round that lifts its valuation above RMB 10 billion (about $1.4 billion), formally entering the "unicorn" club. The round was co-led by Xu Hui Capital, the state capital platform of Shanghai's Xuhui District, and Zhuhai Science & Technology Industry Group under Zhuhai municipal state capital, with participation from top securities-house funds CICC Capital and GTJA Haitong Creative Investment, as well as industry capital including Shangshi Capital of listed company Joyou Medical, Simu Capital and Mingjia Capital.

Existing investors were also active: Junshan Capital added a large amount, while Duowei Capital, Huaye Tiancheng, Lingang Sci-Tech, Jianfa Xingxin and Sanqi Interactive Entertainment over-subscribed. Fangjing says proceeds will go mainly to R&D of self-developed chips and systems, volume production, software ecosystem building and senior talent recruitment.

Founded in early 2023, Fangjing Technology develops separated-architecture intelligent computing systems and adheres to a "self-developed chip, self-developed system, self-developed software stack" integrated route. It closed three rounds in the past six months: Pre-A, Pre-A+ and A1. CEO Liang Jun said: "The next generation of competition in intelligent computing will be systemic innovation at the architecture level. From day one we chose a first-principles technology path, using a distributed AI computing architecture that decouples 'context-aware' and 'context-free' to address problems such as stale adaption to model iteration, high communication overhead and high latency." He said that at a key juncture where domestic computing power is moving from "usable" to "defining," Fangjing would push product delivery and ecosystem building and actively contribute to China's intelligent-infrastructure construction.

3. Crealight Locks In ~10 Million DSP Chips for 2027 to Grab AI Optical-Interconnect Bottleneck

Demand for AI optical-interconnect chips keeps climbing. Crealight, which focuses on AI silicon-photonics interconnects, announced on August 3 that it had finalized its 2027 chip procurement with an upstream core-chip supplier and, as of the announcement date, secured firm orders for roughly 10 million DSP (digital signal processing) chips for delivery that year.

AI data centers are seeing explosive demand for high-bandwidth optical-interconnect products such as 800G and 1.6T, and the DSP chip, as the "computing brain," directly determines the production capacity ceiling and delivery cadence of those products. Constrained by the global allocation of advanced-process capacity and high technical barriers, high-end DSP chips have remained in tight supply and emerged as a core industrial bottleneck. By locking in multi-million-unit DSP supply in advance, Crealight is essentially making a "locking-in" play on upstream capacity — converting supply-chain uncertainty into business certainty and laying the foundation for scaled delivery of its high-speed optical interconnect and silicon-photonics products in 2027.

Notably, the move also underscores a broader trend: as AI compute demand spreads from GPUs to more upstream segments — memory, optics, packaging — high-growth, high-certainty opportunities in the AI-infrastructure chain keep multiplying, and the "choke points" between supply-chain layers are becoming the focus of both capital and industry positioning.
