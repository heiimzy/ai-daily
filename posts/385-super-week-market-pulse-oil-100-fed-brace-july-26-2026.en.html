⚡ Super Week Market Pulse: Brent Tops $100 Then Retreats, Treasury Yields Near 2007 Highs, KOSPI Falls 4th Straight Week

Introduction: The Most Important Week of 2026 Arrives

Global financial markets are bracing for the highest-density week of 2026. Between July 27-31, the Federal Reserve's July FOMC rate decision, the Bank of England and Bank of Japan policy meetings, US Q2 GDP and June core PCE inflation data, and earnings reports from Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon all converge within a span of 72 hours — all against the backdrop of a simmering Middle East conflict and Brent crude oil fresh off a $100+ swing.

As of Sunday, July 26, markets have closed last week's trading and are preparing for a week where every single event could redefine the core logic of asset pricing. This article provides a comprehensive seven-dimensional market pulse check — oil, US equities, bonds, Fed policy, Asian markets, gold, and currencies — not a simple calendar listing.

1. Oil: Triple Supply Chain Disruption, Brent Hits $100 Then Retreats to $97

Last week was the most volatile for crude oil in 2026. Brent crude broke above $100/bbl intraday Thursday, touching $102 — the highest since May 26. WTI surged to $92.19. But on Friday, President Trump ordered a pause on Iran strikes amid Pakistan/China-brokered peace efforts, sending Brent plunging ~4% to $96.78 — the largest single-day drop since late June. For the full week, Brent still gained ~10% and WTI ~9%.

A Rare Triple Supply Shock

The oil market now faces simultaneous disruption across three critical shipping corridors:

  • Strait of Hormuz: Only 6 ships passed through on Thursday, with traffic dropping to one-tenth of pre-war levels, per Kpler data. Iran's IRGC declared the strait "fully closed" and mined the waterway. Roughly 20% of global daily oil — over 14 million barrels — normally transits here.
  • Red Sea/Bab al-Mandeb: Houthi rebels struck two Saudi Aramco tankers with missiles and drones, opening a second front and blocking Saudi Arabia's alternative route around Hormuz.
  • Black Sea/Kazakhstan: The Russia-Ukraine conflict halted the CPC pipeline, slashing Kazakhstan's Tengiz output by ~80% and removing critical export capacity.

Goldman Sachs warns that sustained Hormuz disruption could push Brent above $120/bbl. IEA data shows member countries have already released ~75% of the 400 million barrels of emergency reserves announced in March, with the buffer rapidly dwindling.

2. US Equities: Mag7 Loses $797B in Single Day, Nasdaq Falls for Second Week

All three major US indices finished in the red last week: S&P 500 fell 0.61% to 7,411.98, Nasdaq dropped 2.13% to 24,975.82, and the Dow declined 0.38% to 51,947.25. But the real shock came Thursday — Alphabet and Tesla earnings triggered an AI capex panic that wiped ~$797 billion off the Magnificent Seven's market cap in a single day, the steepest drop since April 2025.

AI Capex Becomes Market's Biggest Anxiety

Alphabet's Q2 cloud revenue grew 82% YoY, but the company raised its full-year capex guidance by 8% to $195-205 billion, pushing free cash flow negative — shares plunged ~8% for the week. Tesla was hit harder: Q2 Non-GAAP EPS missed estimates, operating margin fell to just 1.4%, and the stock crashed nearly 20% in a single week.

Goldman Sachs' trading desk reported overall fund flows skewed 12.6% toward selling, with long-only funds showing a 21% sell bias. The S&P 500 has fallen below its 50-day moving average, the Nasdaq is testing its June 9 low, and market makers are in negative gamma territory with CTA trigger levels closely watched.

3. Treasuries: 10-Year Yield Surges to 4.66%, 30-Year Approaches 2007 Highs

The bond market is undergoing a sustained and brutal repricing. The 10-year Treasury yield broke above 4.70% intraweek to hit 18-month highs, settling near 4.66%. The 30-year bond yield is threatening 5.20% — levels not seen since 2007.

Three forces drive the yield surge: oil prices above $100 reigniting inflation expectations, Trump's new 60-nation tariffs taking effect, and the unprecedented policy path uncertainty under Chair Warsh's no-forward-guidance regime. The 2-year yield has climbed to 4.37%, and the entire curve shift is transmitting higher financing costs to corporates and consumers.

YTD AI-related debt issuance has reached $489 billion, up 50% from all of last year, with 60% coming from non-hyperscaler companies. Their CDS spreads have surged to all-time highs — the race between AI infrastructure returns and rising funding costs is tightening by the day.

4. Federal Reserve: First Major Test of the Post-Forward-Guidance Era

The July 28-29 FOMC meeting is Chair Kevin Warsh's second rate decision since taking office. Markets are pricing a ~38% probability of a 25bp hike, up sharply from 12% a week ago. Yet Morgan Stanley, Barclays, Goldman Sachs, and virtually every major institution expects rates to remain unchanged at 3.50%-3.75%.

The Warsh Effect: Uncertainty Becomes the New Normal

Warsh has completely abandoned the forward guidance approach of his predecessor Powell. The June statement was substantially shortened, and his Congressional testimony offered virtually no clues on the rate path. CME data shows fed funds futures trading volume ahead of this meeting is 50% higher than the July 2025 decision. "Without forward guidance, we'll see probability distributions of 20%, 30%, 40% much more frequently," said Agha Mirza, CME's Global Head of Rates.

Division inside the FOMC is intensifying. Dallas Fed's Logan and Cleveland Fed's Hammack have both publicly stated the Fed has waited too long on inflation. The June dot plot showed 9 of 18 participants expect at least one hike this year. This meeting could see 2-4 dissenting votes.

The hold camp is anchored by June CPI data — headline CPI fell 0.4% MoM, the first negative monthly print since 2020. New York Fed's Williams says there are "signs inflation has peaked." A Bloomberg survey of 76 economists shows all respondents expect rates to remain unchanged.

5. Korea's KOSPI: Fourth Straight Weekly Decline, Sidecar Triggered 10 Days Straight

The KOSPI closed at 6,690.62, down 1.91% for the week and marking a fourth consecutive weekly loss. July's cumulative decline has reached 21.07%. In an unprecedented signal, the sidecar mechanism — which temporarily halts program trading — was triggered on both the KOSPI and KOSDAQ for 10 consecutive trading sessions, a Korea Exchange record.

Single-stock leveraged ETFs tracking Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix have amplified the decline. The 14 leveraged products linked to the two heavyweights have fallen 34.93% to 46.83% since listing. Korea's Financial Services Commission has accelerated planned regulatory tightening to August 19.

This week, SK Hynix (Wednesday) and Samsung Electronics (Thursday) report Q2 results. Analysts expect AI memory demand to support earnings, but oil prices and interest rate pressure remain twin headwinds. Brokerages forecast a 6,700-7,600 range for the KOSPI this week.

6. Gold: Five Weeks Holding $4,000 Support, When Will the Inflection Come?

Gold closed the week at $4,067.60/oz, up 1.37%. Despite multiple headwinds — surging oil prices pushing inflation expectations higher, Treasury yields climbing, and rising Fed hawkishness — gold has held the key $4,000 support level for five consecutive weeks. Analysts see this resilience as evidence that gold is transitioning from a traditional inflation hedge to a deeper geopolitical risk pricing instrument.

FXTM's Otunuga warns that if this week's PCE runs hot, September rate hike expectations could intensify, making $4,000 a clear downside target. Commerzbank's Nguyen argues that if the Middle East conflict triggers a full-scale energy crisis, central banks would ultimately be forced to cut rates — a scenario where gold would benefit significantly.

7. Dollar and FX: Yen at 40-Year Low, Carry Trade Stability in Question

The dollar index posted its best weekly performance in over a month but remains below 101.87. With Warsh's policy path unclear, investors are inclined to hold dollar longs heading into the FOMC meeting. EUR/USD could retest 1.13, while USD/JPY is approaching 163-164 — the yen's weakest level in 40 years.

The Bank of Japan meets July 30-31 and is expected to hold rates at 1.0%. But Natixis strategist Dayeon Hong warns that with USD/JPY far above 163, the BOJ's risk of sounding unexpectedly hawkish is rising. Any yen strength could force liquidation of yen-funded carry trades, triggering liquidity stress across global markets — a tail risk that even gold investors should watch.

Conclusion: Three Mountains of Pressure — How Should Investors Navigate?

The summer of 2026 is proving anything but calm for global investors. Surging oil prices, ballooning AI capital expenditures eroding return expectations, and resurgent tariffs are simultaneously testing the three pillars supporting the current bull market. Goldman Sachs' trading desk reports "almost no buy orders," and Barclays has downgraded risk assets to neutral.

Every single data point, central bank communication, and earnings report this week could be the decisive variable for the next market direction. Markets are transitioning from an environment driven by liquidity and expectations to one driven by data outcomes and geopolitical reality. For investors, the only certainty may be uncertainty itself — and that is precisely the new normal of the Warsh era.
