⚡ July 30 World Affairs: Iran Fires Ballistic Missiles at US Bases in Jordan, Trump Vows to 'Beat the S*** Out of' Iran, US-Saudi Joint Strikes Kill 20 in Iraq, Brent Crude Surges Toward $90

1. Iran Launches Ballistic Missile 'Surprise Attack' on US Forces in Jordan

At 5:45 PM ET (21:45 GMT) on Tuesday, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) launched multiple ballistic missiles from Iranian territory at US military installations in Jordan, targeting the Muwaffaq Salti Air Base (MSAB) and a CENTCOM command center. The attack marked the first major Iranian strike on US forces since President Trump suspended bombing operations last Friday, shattering several days of relative calm.

US Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed the attack within two hours, describing it as an "attempted surprise attack on US forces based in the Middle East." CENTCOM stated that all incoming Iranian missiles were "successfully intercepted" and that "US forces remain vigilant and at a high state of readiness." Jordan's military separately confirmed its air defenses had intercepted and destroyed five Iranian missiles. No casualties or damage have been reported.

The IRGC dubbed the operation "Nasr 2" (Victory 2), claiming it struck the airbase and CENTCOM headquarters in Jordan in response to what it called "the aggressive acts of the American child-killing army." In a statement carried by state-run IRNA news agency, the IRGC warned: "As long as threats against the Islamic Republic of Iran continue and illegal and vicious actions by American forces against our interests continue, the resistance will continue."

The attack came just hours after Trump met with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House, a timing experts say was deliberate. Jason Campbell, a senior fellow at the Middle East Institute, told Al Jazeera that Tehran is "trying to retake the initiative and momentum with these latest attacks." The strike also landed amid growing concerns over US interceptor stockpile depletion — NBC News reported earlier that US commanders had been allowing some Iranian attacks through to conserve munitions. This same base saw three US service members killed on July 17 in the deadliest single attack on American forces since March.

2. Trump Unleashes Expletive-Laden Threat: 'We'll Beat the F***ing S*** Out of Them'

In the aftermath of Iran's missile attack, President Donald Trump gave an incendiary interview to Fox News chief foreign correspondent Trey Yingst, threatening the most aggressive response yet. According to Yingst, Trump promised to "beat the f***ing s*** out of" Iran.

"We'll be hitting them hard," Trump told Fox News. "They're going to get a beating." When asked about diplomatic prospects, he added: "We're going to let them keep talking — but if we can't make a deal, I'll go back and finish the job."

The remarks represent some of Trump's most bellicose language since the US and Israel launched war on Iran on February 28. Trump has oscillated between diplomacy and escalation throughout the conflict — in April he famously declared that "a whole civilization will die tonight" before announcing a ceasefire hours later. He has previously threatened to strike Iran's Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site as well as civilian infrastructure including bridges and power plants, moves that international law experts say could constitute war crimes.

The president had expressed optimism about negotiations as recently as Monday, telling reporters aboard Air Force One that there was a "chance we can make a deal." But Iran's missile attack made clear that Tehran will not simply wait for Washington's diplomatic timeline. Dr. Andreas Krieg, a senior lecturer at King's College London, said Iran likely launched the attack to "shape the negotiations through force, not because it had abandoned diplomacy." He noted the timing — coinciding with Trump's Netanyahu meeting — sent a deliberate signal that "any new American or Israeli campaign would face an immediate response."

The administration is also under mounting domestic pressure. The five-month war is deeply unpopular in the US, with four US troops killed in recent weeks and the Pentagon asking Congress for additional funding. Even within Trump's MAGA base, rising fuel prices caused by the closure of the Strait of Hormuz have fueled growing discontent.

3. US-Saudi Jets Strike 7 Iraqi Provinces: 20 Killed, 32 Wounded — First Saudi Public Combat Role

Hours after intercepting Iran's missile salvo, CENTCOM announced joint precision strikes with the Saudi Arabian Armed Forces against Iran-aligned groups in eastern Iraq, marking the first time Saudi Arabia has publicly participated in combat operations alongside the US since the war began.

In a formal statement, CENTCOM said: "U.S. and Saudi fighter aircraft struck multiple terrorist logistics and weapons sites across eastern Iraq in a strong response to over 30 IRGC-directed aerial drone attacks in the last 72 hours." The statement described the drone attacks as "unwarranted" and warned: "The IRGC and its terrorist proxies must cease these attacks to avoid further US military response."

Iraq's Popular Mobilisation Forces (PMF) — a state-integrated umbrella organization of mostly Shiite, Iran-backed militias — confirmed that US-Saudi airstrikes hit its headquarters across seven provinces: Baghdad, Wasit, Nineveh, Basra, Kirkuk, Karbala and Diyala. At least 20 fighters were killed and 32 wounded, with the deadliest strikes reported in Nineveh and Diyala. Six Iranian military advisers were also killed in the attacks, two Iraqi militia officials told the Associated Press.

Saudi Defense Ministry spokesman Maj Gen Turki al-Maliki confirmed the strikes, stating the kingdom was exercising its "right to self-defense" after Iranian-backed militias launched drones from Iraqi territory at Saudi oil facilities in the Eastern and Riyadh regions on Monday and Tuesday. "The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia reiterates that it does not seek escalation but will respond decisively to any aggression directed against it," al-Maliki said.

The involvement of Saudi Arabia fundamentally alters the conflict's strategic calculus. Riyadh now faces an unprecedented three-front threat: direct Iranian missile attacks, drone strikes from Iraq-based pro-Iranian militias, and a Houthi blockade in the Red Sea. The Iraqi presidency condemned the strikes as "an unacceptable attack and a blatant violation of Iraq's sovereignty." Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi called an emergency National Security Council meeting. Iran-backed Iraqi faction Harakat al-Nujaba warned that US and Saudi forces would pay a "heavy price."

Iran's Foreign Ministry accused Washington and Riyadh of attempting to "expand the war" across the region, warning of "dangerous consequences." In a separate development, Israel accused Hezbollah of launching an explosive drone at an Israeli engineering vehicle inside Lebanese territory, calling it "a blatant violation of the ceasefire" — adding yet another potential flashpoint to the already volatile region.

4. Houthi Blockade Intensifies: Abqaiq Damaged, Jazan Refinery Shut Down, Tankers Turned Back

Iran's Houthi proxies in Yemen are executing an increasingly effective maritime blockade of Saudi Arabia in the Red Sea, expanding the conflict beyond the Strait of Hormuz to the Bab el-Mandeb strait — potentially choking off two of the world's most critical energy arteries simultaneously.

Abqaiq facility damaged: Planet Labs satellite imagery from Monday, analyzed by the Associated Press, revealed damage to Saudi Aramco's Abqaiq oil processing facility in the kingdom's Eastern Province. The facility — the world's largest crude stabilization plant — has a processing capacity of approximately 7 million barrels per day. A Houthi attack on Abqaiq in September 2019 disrupted 5.7 million barrels per day of production and sent global oil prices spiking.

Jazan refinery shut down: A regional official told the AP that an oil refinery in Jazan, on Saudi Arabia's Red Sea coast, has been temporarily shut down due to "relatively significant" damage from a Houthi attack on Saturday. The 400,000 barrel-per-day facility initially operated at reduced rates before fully shutting on Monday. Texas-based IIR Energy consultancy estimates repairs will take until at least August 15.

Tanker turned back: The Houthis claimed to have fired missiles at the Saudi oil tanker NCC Ghazal, forcing it to turn back. Last week, the group announced a comprehensive blockade of Saudi shipping. The UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) confirmed a tanker reported hearing explosions in the southern Red Sea, though crew and vessel were safe.

Saudi air defenses have been intercepting drones targeting petroleum facilities for a second consecutive day. The Houthis are also considering imposing fees on commercial ships transiting the Red Sea, according to sources cited by Reuters — essentially copying Iran's Hormuz playbook. Analysts warn that simultaneous closure of both the Strait of Hormuz and the Bab el-Mandeb strait would constitute an unprecedented energy supply crisis, potentially pushing global oil inventories to dangerously low levels within weeks.

5. Oil Markets Explode: Brent Jumps 7% Toward $90, Global Assets in Flux

The cascade of geopolitical shocks sent energy markets into overdrive on Wednesday. Brent crude futures surged more than 7%, breaching the $90 per barrel mark before settling near $88.50 — completely reversing the price plunge that followed Trump's weekend suspension of bombing. US WTI crude rose over 4%, climbing back above $82 per barrel.

This marks the second time since May that Brent has approached $90 after briefly spiking above $100 last week. The extraordinary volatility reflects a market that has become hypersensitive to every shift in the US-Iran conflict cycle. Each "cease-fire" and "re-escalation" triggers violent price swings in both directions.

The Strait of Hormuz — through which approximately 20% of the world's traded oil normally flows — has been effectively closed for months, with daily vessel transits plummeting from roughly 100 pre-war to single digits. Now the Bab el-Mandeb strait, another critical chokepoint, is also under threat. The International Energy Agency (IEA) has warned that global oil inventories could reach critical levels within weeks if both waterways remain obstructed. US Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) levels have already fallen to their lowest since the 1980s after months of releases.

Safe-haven gold edged higher while the US dollar initially rose on the news before giving back gains. Asian and European equity markets mostly declined. South Korea's KOSPI index fell another 3%, extending a two-day rout that has erased over $2 trillion in market value.
