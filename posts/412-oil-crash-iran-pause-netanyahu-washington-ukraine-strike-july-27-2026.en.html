⚡ July 27 International Roundup: Oil Crashes 8% Below $88 as US-Iran Truce Holds, Iran Says "Not Seeking Talks," Netanyahu Departs for Washington in Secrecy, Ukraine Strikes Deep into Russia

Monday, July 27 — The global geopolitical landscape shifted rapidly today. The US-Iran ceasefire held for a third consecutive day, sending oil prices crashing more than 8% with Brent crude falling below $88 a barrel for the first time in a week. But diplomatic hopes were tempered by Iran's foreign ministry, which stated emphatically at a Monday press conference that it is "not currently negotiating with the United States" and still maintains full control over the Strait of Hormuz. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu departed for Washington under unprecedented secrecy for a Tuesday meeting with President Trump. Meanwhile, Ukraine launched a massive long-range drone attack on Russian territory, crippling oil facilities at Rostov port and demonstrating strike capability at distances exceeding 1,300 kilometers.

I. US-Iran Truce Holds for Third Day: Oil Plunges 8%, But Iran Refuses Talks

The United States and Iran have now gone three consecutive nights without military engagement — the longest pause since the conflict reignited two weeks ago. The rare ceasefire window triggered a violent reaction in global crude markets.

Oil price collapse:

  • Brent crude plunged more than 8% in Asian trading Monday, briefly dipping below $86 before settling around $88 — a one-week low and a 9% single-day drop
  • WTI crude fell over 7% to trade near $82.80
  • Last week, Brent had briefly surged above $100 for the first time since May as the conflict escalated
  • Analysts say the market is pricing out the "risk premium" but any renewed hostilities could trigger another spike

However, market optimism was dampened by Iran's Monday press conference. Foreign Ministry Spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei stated:

"Mediators may convey messages from the American side to us concerning regional developments, but at the present time we have no negotiations with the American side. We will never allow America to determine the timing of war and peace. We will continue to defend ourselves for as long as our interests and considerations require." — Esmaeil Baghaei, Iran Foreign Ministry Spokesman, July 27

Baghaei insisted the Strait of Hormuz remains "closed" and that Iran maintains full control over the vital waterway. He confirmed that technical talks with Oman had made progress but denied any connection to US negotiations.

Why the US paused strikes:

  • Targets exhausted — Axios reported that CENTCOM Commander Admiral Brad Cooper told Trump the bombing campaign had reached its limits, with the target list largely depleted
  • Ammunition running low — NBC reported US forces have begun selectively letting some Iranian missiles through to conserve interceptor stockpiles
  • Domestic pressure — With midterm elections 100 days away and US gas prices above $4/gallon, only 29% of Americans approve of Trump's handling of the conflict (Washington Post/Ipsos poll)
  • Casualties mounting — Pentagon data shows 18 US service members killed and 624 wounded in the Iran war

II. Netanyahu Departs for Washington Under Unprecedented Secrecy

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu departed secretly from Nevatim Airbase Monday afternoon for Washington, where he is scheduled to meet President Trump at the White House on Tuesday at 6:00 PM. The level of operational secrecy around this trip is unprecedented.

Key details:

  • Extreme secrecy — The Prime Minister's Office withheld departure time, landing schedule, and takeoff location. Flight trackers showed the "Wing of Zion" aircraft leaving Nevatim Airbase in the Negev at approximately 1:00 PM Israel time (10:00 UTC), bypassing Ben Gurion Airport
  • Nuclear program at top of agenda — "Iran will be first and foremost on our agenda," Netanyahu said before departure. He plans to present Trump with updated intelligence on Iran's efforts to rebuild its nuclear program and ballistic missile systems, including deep underground facilities
  • Eighth meeting — This will be Netanyahu's eighth face-to-face meeting with Trump since the president began his second term, more than any other foreign leader
  • Graham funeral — Netanyahu will also attend the memorial service for the late Senator Lindsey Graham, whom he called "one of Israel's greatest friends of all time"

Analysts note the visit comes at a delicate juncture: the US-Iran truce is fragile, the ICC arrest warrant threat looms, and Israel's October elections are approaching. Netanyahu needs to demonstrate influence in Washington, while Trump wants to project control over Middle East developments.

III. Ukraine Launches Massive Deep Strikes: Rostov Port and Russian Oil Facilities Hit

Ukraine's defense forces conducted a massive long-range drone attack on multiple targets deep inside Russian territory overnight July 26-27, as part of President Zelenskyy's "40-day pressure operation."

Targets struck:

  • Rostov Sea Trade Port — Ukrainian drones hit the port's fourth cargo district, sparking a 400-square-meter fire. Rostov Oblast Governor Yury Slyusar confirmed 2 killed and 5 injured. The port handles 15-18 million tons of cargo annually and is a critical Black Sea logistics hub
  • Udmurtia state fuel reserve — Located 1,300 km from Ukraine's border, this Rosrezerv facility (known as "Gorizont") stores strategic emergency fuel reserves. The regional governor called it the "largest drone attack" on the region in recent months
  • Yaroslavl Oblast oil facilities — Northeast of Moscow, further demonstrating Ukraine's expanding strike radius
  • Taganrog — The city near the Ukrainian border was also hit in the overnight attack

President Zelenskyy confirmed the strikes on social media: "Last night, our long-range sanctions were effective in the Rostov region. An export terminal approximately 250 kilometers from the front line was hit. Deep strikes also targeted oil facilities in the Yaroslavl region and the Udmurt Republic, which is 1,300 kilometers from Ukraine's state border. We are implementing our long-range sanctions plan and reducing Russia's ability to finance the war."

Russia's Defense Ministry claimed it intercepted 276 drones overnight but did not disclose how many reached their targets.

IV. Iran Warns Ukraine: Caspian Strike "Will Not Go Unanswered"

The diplomatic confrontation between Iran and Ukraine escalated sharply Monday, with a senior Iranian lawmaker issuing a direct threat of retaliation.

Latest developments:

  • Ebrahim Azizi, head of parliament's National Security and Foreign Policy Commission, posted on X: "Any attack on Iran always comes with a cost, and that remains true today. Ukraine, too, may soon come to understand that Iran does not let actions go unanswered. The list of those who have miscalculated continues to increase!"
  • Foreign Minister Araghchi said Saturday's Ukrainian strike on an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea "cannot go unanswered," calling it a "blatant UN Charter violation carried out at Israel's behest"
  • Russia's Lavrov offered condolences for the killed Iranian sailor and stressed the need to "put an end to such adventures by the Kyiv regime"
  • Zelenskyy's response — The Ukrainian president fired back, saying Iran "already attacked Ukraine" by supplying Shahed drones to Russia, and pledged to continue striking military cargo routes between Russia and Iran

Analysts say this marks the first direct military linkage between the Russia-Ukraine war and the US-Iran conflict. The Caspian Sea route has become a vital logistics lifeline for Iran after the US imposed a naval blockade on its southern ports.

V. Red Sea Front Worsens: Houthi Blockade Disrupts Saudi Oil Exports

While the Persian Gulf front has temporarily calmed, the Red Sea-Bab al-Mandeb corridor continues to deteriorate. Iran-backed Houthi rebels have imposed a maritime blockade on Saudi Arabia, threatening the world's second-most-critical energy chokepoint.

Saudi exports forced to reroute — Kpler data shows Saudi Arabia has completely halted crude exports via the Bab al-Mandeb strait since the Houthi blockade took effect. Shipments to Asian buyers via the Suez Canal more than doubled to 1.06 million barrels per day in the week after the embargo.

The Houthis claimed to have shot down a Saudi drone over northwestern Yemen over the weekend, following attacks on Saudi Aramco facilities in Jizan and Yanbu. Saudi air defenses intercepted multiple drones launched from Iraqi territory, believed to be operated by Iran-backed Iraqi militias. The Saudi-led coalition conducted retaliatory airstrikes on Houthi missile and drone positions inside Yemen.

Washington think tanks describe the Trump administration as facing a "three-front crisis": the Persian Gulf-Hormuz main battlefield with ammunition and strategic dilemmas, the Red Sea-Saudi escort mission, and domestic political pressure from high gasoline prices and rising casualties. The next 48 hours — Netanyahu's White House visit, indirect US-Iran contacts via Oman, and whether oil prices can stabilize — will determine the conflict's next trajectory.
