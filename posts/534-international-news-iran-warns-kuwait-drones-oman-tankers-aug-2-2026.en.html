⚡ Aug 2 International News: Iran FM Warns of 'Decisive Response' in Calls with Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi, Kuwait Downs Iranian Drones, Tankers Attacked off Oman

This weekend, the Middle East is simmering with tension. In the early hours of August 2, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi placed a flurry of calls to senior officials in Turkey, Pakistan and Saudi Arabia, sending Washington one message: any "adventurous action" will be met with a "decisive and proportionate response". Meanwhile, between the Persian Gulf and the Red Sea, Kuwait intercepted Iranian drones, tankers came under fire off Oman, and multiple US embassies issued departure warnings - the prelude to an even larger storm is drawing closer.

Iran's FM Makes Rounds of Calls: Warning of a 'Decisive, Proportionate Response'

According to Araghchi's Telegram account and Iran's Foreign Ministry, the minister spoke on Saturday (August 1) with Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan, Pakistan's army chief General Asim Munir and Saudi Foreign Minister Prince Faisal bin Farhan. He told the Pakistani and Turkish officials that Iran would respond decisively to any "aggression"; in his conversation with the Saudi foreign minister, he made clear that any assault by the US or Israel - or the involvement of regional nations in such operations - would meet a "proportionate response" from Tehran.

The diplomatic blitz was a direct answer to Washington's war threats: Trump said at a live-streamed Camp David cabinet meeting on Friday that "we will be hitting them very hard," predicting Tehran would eventually say "we just can't take it anymore"; The Wall Street Journal, citing US officials, reported that Trump has ordered the military to prepare a fresh strike on Iran that could begin as early as this weekend - though it could be called off if there is progress on the diplomatic front. Trump also posted on Truth Social that Iran is experiencing "massive inflation" and that he is "destroying the Iranian currency."

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stressed in his calls that any "aggression" by the US or Israel, or participation by regional nations, would meet a "decisive and proportionate response" from Tehran.

US Embassies in Iraq, Jordan and UAE Urge Departure; Gulf on 'High Alert'

US embassies in Iraq, Jordan and the United Arab Emirates on Saturday warned American citizens in the region to consider departing immediately, or be prepared to leave should fighting escalate, while also preparing for flight cancellations. A senior Gulf government official and a foreign diplomat based in the Gulf told media that the region is on "high alert" in anticipation of an "increase" in military action in the coming days, adding that there will be "significant disruptions."

Officials said Gulf states have been briefed on potential US-Israeli attacks on Iran's energy infrastructure; if such an attack occurs, Iran is expected to retaliate by targeting energy sites in the Gulf. Al Jazeera's live blog noted that Trump spent the weekend at his Bedminster estate in New Jersey, with no unusual activity at US airbases - no "tangible indication" of imminent action, yet the threat remains hanging over the region.

Kuwait Downs Iranian Drones; Debris Damages Government Building and Homes

Kuwait's army reported that it intercepted and destroyed hostile drones launched by Iran against several critical sites. Falling debris damaged a government building in northern Kuwait and civilian property on Bubiyan Island; no injuries were reported.

It is the latest example of the conflict spilling beyond Iran's borders. After the US completed a "heavy wave" of strikes against Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps targets last week, Iran retaliated against US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain; now Iranian drones are targeting Kuwait's critical facilities, underscoring how the Gulf states are being drawn to the frontline.

Two Tanker Incidents off Oman: UKMTO Confirms Engine Room Hit by Unknown Projectile

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), backed by the British Navy, reported on Saturday that a tanker was hit by an "unknown projectile which has caused damage to the engine room" about 11 nautical miles northeast of Oman; the tanker is not under command and the regional Coast Guard has been informed, with no reported casualties or environmental impact. Another tanker in the waters off Oman reported "seeing a large splash and explosion in close proximity to the vessel."

The incidents come as the US naval blockade of Iran tightens. Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, warned that "the continuation of the naval blockade and warmongering by the US regime will not only lock the Strait of Hormuz tighter, but will also shut down other straits and chokepoints - the global economy, energy markets and American voters will pay the price". Since the war began on February 28, the waterway - through which about 20 percent of the world's oil and LNG once passed - has remained closed for more than five months.

Ripples Spread: Sulaimaniyah Hit by Drone Attack, US Sends More Refueling Aircraft, Iran Says Response 'Will Not Be Limited to the Gulf'

The conflict's ripples are reaching ever further. In the early hours of August 2, a drone attack targeted the Peshmerga's 70th Brigade headquarters in Sulaimaniyah, in Iraq's Kurdish region - the attack was intercepted, but the wreckage of an interceptor target caught fire after falling near the headquarters; Lebanese outlet Al Mayadeen reported the attack was carried out by Iran-backed groups in Iraq, while Iran's semi-official Fars News Agency described the site as the headquarters of "anti-Iran terrorists."

Meanwhile, Israeli media outlet Ynet, citing security officials, reported that the US military is expected to deploy about 10 additional aerial refueling aircraft to Israel in the coming days, most to be parked at Ben Gurion Airport in Tel Aviv, joining more than 30 already stationed there; the Israeli military has raised its defensive readiness, assessing that Iran could retaliate against Israel even if Israeli forces do not participate in any US strikes on Iranian infrastructure.

Iran's rhetoric is hardening too: Ebrahim Rezaei, an Iranian lawmaker and member of the National Security and Foreign Policy Commission, said Tehran's response to any enemy attack "will not be limited to the Persian Gulf region" and will target "all enemy bases and the origin of the attacks from anywhere"; "If a strike is made on Iran, we will return the entire region and its facilities to the Stone Age". The next round of escalation in the Middle East may be only a weekend away.
