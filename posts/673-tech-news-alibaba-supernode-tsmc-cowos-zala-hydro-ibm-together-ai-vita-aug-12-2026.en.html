⚡ Aug 12 Tech News: Alibaba Cloud's 2T-Parameter Supernode Goes Live, TSMC CoWoS Hits 99% Yield, World's Largest Impulse Hydro Rotor Lifted, IBM's $240M AI Compute Deal, Vita Life-Science Journal Debuts

The morning of Aug 12 brought a dense batch of general-tech headlines from China and beyond: Alibaba Cloud's Lingjun Zhenwu M890 supernode instance officially went live, becoming the first compute form in China to run large models above 2 trillion parameters; TSMC's 5.5x-reticle CoWoS advanced packaging is achieving up to 99% yield, further cementing the supply base for AI accelerators; the world's largest impulse hydro turbine unit completed its rotor lift at the Zala hydropower station in Tibet; IBM signed a multi-year $240 million deal with AI cloud platform Together AI to deploy an NVIDIA HGX B300 inference cluster; and China-led life-science journal series Vita published its first print issue. Here is a closer look at each story, with all source publish times individually verified.

1. Alibaba Cloud's Lingjun Zhenwu M890 Supernode: First Compute Form in China to Run 2T-Parameter Models

According to IT Home (published Aug 12, 03:27 UTC), Alibaba Cloud announced on Wednesday that its Lingjun Zhenwu M890 supernode instance is now officially live, initially available in Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia. The company says enterprise customers can provision a 64-GPU high-speed interconnect compute unit directly in the cloud, capable of supporting inference for MoE large models with up to 10 trillion parameters. The M890 supernode is also the first supernode-class compute in China to successfully run a model above 2 trillion parameters — Kimi K3 and Qwen3.8-Max are both already being served through the instance. On the technical side, the M890 supports FP8/FP4 low-precision compute, and its ICN Switch 1.0 chip scales the Scale-up interconnect from 16 GPUs to 64 GPUs at 800GB/s between cards; in training scenarios such as autonomous driving and embodied intelligence, it delivers up to 3x the performance of the previous-gen Zhenwu 810E. The launch region, Ulanqab, is one of Alibaba Cloud's five super data centers, with about 90% green electricity, and the data center's overall modularization rate reaches 90%, compatible with mainstream heterogeneous chips and supporting at least three chip generations. This year, Alibaba Cloud plans to more than double global production capacity of modular data centers, with supernode instances rolling out to other regions.

2. TSMC's Advanced Packaging Breakthrough: 5.5x-Reticle CoWoS Reaches 99% Yield

According to IT Home (published Aug 12, 03:48 UTC), He Jun, TSMC's deputy general manager of advanced packaging technology and services, said at the OCP APAC Summit that the company's mass-produced 5.5x-reticle CoWoS advanced packaging technology can reach 99% yield on certain products. As a 2.5D heterogeneous integration scheme, 5.5x-reticle CoWoS has been validated across multiple AI customer products, with yields stable above 98%. He Jun also previewed that TSMC plans to introduce a 15x-reticle ultra-large-area CoWoS in 2029, paving the way for next-generation ultra-large compute chips. In his speech he noted that validation of advanced SoCs is accelerating from the device level to the system level — the same chip can show edge damage in some systems that device-level analysis cannot predict. Tight supply across the semiconductor chain is reinforcing the trend: customers are diversifying ABF substrate suppliers to secure supply, and substrates from different vendors differ in material properties; without targeted optimization, system-level yield can deteriorate sharply. Advanced packaging, in other words, is shifting from a "manufacturing capability" contest to a "system-level co-optimization" contest.

3. World's Largest Impulse Hydro Unit Completes Rotor Lift in Tibet

According to CCTV News (carried by IT Home, published Aug 12, 03:10 UTC), on Aug 11, the world's largest impulse hydro turbine unit completed its rotor lift at the Datang Zala hydropower station, marking China's entry into the world-leading tier for ultra-high-head, high-capacity impulse hydropower equipment and boosting the construction of the southeastern Tibet clean-energy base. The Zala station sits on the Yuqu River at the border of Zuogong County (Chamdo) and Chayu County (Nyingchi) in Tibet, and is a key project of the national "Tibet power transmission" program, housing two 500MW impulse units — the largest single-unit capacity of their kind globally. It is also China's first (set) major technical equipment in the energy sector, combining high altitude, high head and large capacity. The rotor just lifted is the "power heart" of the generating unit's energy conversion, weighing about 840 tonnes, and its successful installation puts the project into its final sprint before commissioning. Once fully operational, the station is expected to generate nearly 4 billion kWh of clean electricity a year, saving about 1.3 million tonnes of standard coal and cutting CO2 emissions by 3.42 million tonnes.

4. IBM Lands $240M AI Compute Deal: HGX B300 Cluster for Together AI

According to IT Home (published Aug 12, 02:38 UTC), IBM announced on Aug 11 (US time) a multi-year, $240 million (about RMB 1.622 billion) agreement with AI-native cloud platform Together AI, under which IBM will deploy an NVIDIA HGX B300 cluster on IBM Cloud to power Together AI's open-source model inference services, with the system expected to enter service in Q1 2027. IBM says this is the first large-scale, inference-dedicated cluster on IBM Cloud built with NVIDIA HGX B300 systems and Spectrum-X Ethernet technology. Together AI CEO Vipul Ved Prakash said enterprises want state-of-the-art model performance without the high cost of closed models, and that "this cluster allows us to deliver production-grade inference to more companies faster — an important step in making open-source AI the enterprise default." Notably, Together AI recently closed an $800 million Series C at an $8.3 billion valuation, with monthly token processing capacity of 400T.

5. China-Led Life-Science Journal Vita Publishes Its First Print Issue

According to CCTV News (carried by IT Home, published Aug 12, 04:00 UTC), the first print issue of the China-led life-science journal series Vita was officially released on Wednesday. "Vita" comes from Latin and means "life." The series comprises a flagship journal, Vita, plus sub-journals, built around the "Life Science Open Alliance" — co-founded in Hong Kong on Aug 12, 2025 by 15 leading universities and research institutions from the mainland, Hong Kong and Macao, now including 39 institutions. The alliance treats high-level science journals and an open international research-data platform as key deliverables. The Vita series adopts a "flagship + sub-journals" model, benchmarking the three top international journals — Cell, Nature and Science — and their sub-journals, with the goal of building a first-class, independent Chinese journal series in life sciences. It will also pioneer a "journal evaluation by papers" system, assessing journal quality by evaluating the academic level of the original research it publishes, moving away from over-reliance on citation metrics such as SCI and steering journals back to scholarly substance.

6. Big Picture: An Infrastructure Race from Compute to Scholarly Journals

These five stories trace four threads running through China's hard-tech landscape right now: first, compute infrastructure is racing toward localization and cloudification — Alibaba Cloud's supernode runs 2T-parameter models while IBM deploys HGX B300 at scale for open-source inference, showing "who can compute faster and cheaper" is the new foundation of AI competition; second, advanced packaging is becoming the ceiling for chips — TSMC pushing CoWoS from 5.5x toward 15x reticles confirms that the compute race is shifting from pure process nodes to packaging and system-level co-design; third, energy and heavy equipment are upgrading in parallel — the world's largest impulse unit's rotor lift showcases national breakthroughs in green power and mega-scale engineering; fourth, scholarly voice is being built autonomously — Vita benchmarking the top international journals and proposing "journal evaluation by papers" reflects systematic soft-power building in the research ecosystem.
