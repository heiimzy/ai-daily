⚡ Aug 7 Tech Briefing: SIA Says Q2 Global Chip Sales Up 35%, Anthropic to Build In-House Silicon, LG Plans Three Tiers of AI Chips, SpaceX Eyes Gigasat Factory for AI Satellites

Over the past hour or two, the semiconductor and AI infrastructure world has been buzzing with major developments: the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA) reported global chip sales of $403.3 billion in Q2 2026, up 35.1% quarter-over-quarter, with the full-year market on track to top $1.5 trillion; Anthropic confirmed it is building a custom silicon team to co-design chips with its models and reduce reliance on Nvidia; LG is planning three tiers of AI chips for consumer appliances and home robots; and SpaceX intends to build a Gigasat factory to mass-produce orbital AI data centers. Here is this round's general-tech roundup.

1. SIA: Q2 Global Chip Sales Reach $403.3B, Up 35.1% Quarter-on-Quarter

According to data released by the Semiconductor Industry Association (SIA), global semiconductor sales reached $403.3 billion in Q2 2026, up 35.1% quarter-on-quarter. Looking at June alone, global sales were $134.5 billion, up 123.6% from June 2025 and 9.7% month-on-month. The monthly figures are compiled by the World Semiconductor Trade Statistics (WSTS) organization and represent a three-month moving average.

SIA President and CEO John Neuffer said: "Global chip sales are expected to exceed $1.5 trillion in 2026, with Q2 sales substantially outpacing Q1. Strong sales across the Americas, the Asia Pacific region, and China continue to drive market growth." Regionally, year-over-year June sales rose by Americas (160.9%), Asia Pacific/All Other (124.4%), China (112.8%), Europe (75.2%), and Japan (39%); month-on-month gains were seen in China (10.4%), Asia Pacific/All Other (9.8%), the Americas (9.6%), Japan (8.6%), and Europe (7.7%). The numbers show that the compute-infrastructure boom fueled by generative AI continues to supercharge the semiconductor upcycle.

2. Anthropic Confirms In-House Silicon Team: Co-Designing Chips with Models

Anthropic has confirmed it is building a "custom silicon team" to design chips on which to run its models, reducing its dependence on Nvidia. The company's careers page now lists a "senior silicon engineer" and a "technical program manager, silicon"; a spokesperson confirmed the plans to Business Insider and TechCrunch, while stressing the firm will still take a "multi-chip approach" and use hardware from other suppliers alongside its own designs.

This validates earlier reporting by The Information that Anthropic was considering working with Samsung as a manufacturing partner. Anthropic is not alone: OpenAI recently unveiled a custom inference chip, "Jalapeño," developed with Broadcom, while Google and Meta have long designed their own AI silicon, and Mistral is reportedly exploring the same path. Analysts cite two clear motives: reducing strategic dependence on Nvidia in an ultra-competitive compute market, and the performance and cost advantages of co-designing chips and models together. Anthropic says its teams will develop new hardware and models side by side, leaving room for developers to run smaller, open-weight models on their own or edge hardware.

3. LG Plans Three Tiers of AI Chips, From Appliances to Home Robots

According to Digitimes citing South Korean media, LG is planning three tiers of AI chips spanning appliances, phones, and home robots. The company reportedly aims to embed AI at different performance levels across its product line — mid-range appliances getting basic machine-learning accelerators for on-device voice and image recognition, while high-end and robotic products would receive more powerful dedicated silicon for on-device large-model inference and real-time interaction.

Separately, LG's Exaone large-language models are reportedly performing strongly in AI benchmark comparisons, with The Korea Herald noting the family beating some rivals on certain metrics. LG's combination of chips plus its own models — a "software-hardware co-optimization" — mirrors the end-side AI race among Samsung, Apple, and other consumer giants: on-device AI has become the core differentiation battlefield for consumer electronics. Whoever can run large models locally at lower power and latency will own the next generation of terminal experience. Analysts expect multiple appliances and robots with custom AI chips to enter mass production in 2026.

4. SpaceX to Build Gigasat Factory: Mass-Producing AI Satellites and Orbital Data

According to KVUE, SpaceX plans a new Gigasat factory in Bastrop County, Texas, to manufacture AI satellites and space-grade data centers at scale. The facility spans roughly 11 million square feet across more than 1,000 acres, targeting an equivalent of 1GW (gigawatt) of orbital AI compute capacity per year by late 2027, producing units such as AI satellites powered by SpaceX's in-house D3 inference chip.

The plan pushes AI data centers into orbit, leveraging the integration advantages of networks like Starlink to escape the land, power, and cooling constraints of terrestrial data centers. Industry analysts frame orbital AI data centers as a major milestone for "space-based compute": space has no power grid, and solar energy can power around-the-clock operation. SpaceX has previously filed frequency applications with the FCC, signaling its "AI to the orbit" strategy is moving from concept toward engineering. The move gives SpaceX a second high-value growth engine beyond launch itself — turning compute into an in-orbit commodity.
