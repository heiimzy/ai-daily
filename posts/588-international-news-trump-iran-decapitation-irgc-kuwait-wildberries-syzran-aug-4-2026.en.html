⚡ August 4 International Update: Trump Calls Talks Iran's 'Last Chance Before Decapitation,' IRGC Drones Hit US Base in Kuwait, Ukraine Strikes Wildberries Warehouses and Syzran Refinery

International affairs escalated on two fronts by the evening of Tuesday (August 4): the familiar US–Iran cycle of "talk then strike" — Trump again warned at the White House that Tehran faced a "last chance before decapitation," even as Iran flatly denied any direct talks with Washington — and the Russia–Ukraine war, where Kyiv launched a mass overnight drone raid on Russia's rear, hitting e-commerce logistics hubs and an oil refinery from the Moscow outskirts to the Samara region. Here is the latest international roundup.

1. Trump calls talks Iran's 'last chance before decapitation'; Tehran denies direct negotiations with the US

According to The Hindu's live West Asia war blog (updated 16:11 Beijing time, August 4) and AFP, US President Donald Trump said at the White House on August 3–4 that negotiations with Iran were "ongoing" and that Tehran faced a "last chance before decapitation" to sign a good document and avoid a military escalation. Trump said he had agreed to continue pursuing talks at the request of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates, Qatar and other countries.

Trump said the talks would proceed in two phases: Phase one is reopening the Strait of Hormuz, which he said could be "completely open by tomorrow"; phase two is the full denuclearization of Iran, which "would take a little while." However, Iranian foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei flatly denied direct talks with Washington: "To avoid any misunderstanding, it is important to clarify what the current negotiations are about and who they are with. We are not negotiating with the United States at this time. Our negotiations are with Oman and are focused on reaching an understanding on a route to ensure the safe passage of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz." Trump responded on his Truth Social platform by calling Iran's leadership "unbelievably duplicitous," saying they "beg" for meetings yet publicly deny any discussions.

2. Military friction persists: IRGC drones strike a US base in Kuwait; a cargo vessel hit by an 'unknown projectile' off Oman

Behind the diplomatic deadlock, military friction continued. According to The Jerusalem Post (cited by The Hindu's live blog, 10:44 India time, August 4), Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) attacked a US military base in Kuwait using at least three drones, with heavy blasts also reported in Iraq's Basra province, which borders both Kuwait and Iran; Iranian state broadcaster IRIB, citing regional outlets, reported a major blaze in Kuwait this morning. The US–Iran war, which began with US–Israeli strikes on February 28, has now run for more than five months, and such tit-for-tat retaliatory attacks have picked up notably in recent weeks.

Meanwhile, maritime risk persists. According to AFP (06:58 UTC, August 4), citing the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO), a cargo vessel was struck by an "unknown projectile" in the Strait of Hormuz off the coast of Oman, about 20 nautical miles (37 kilometres) northeast of Al Khasab, Oman; the vessel reported being hit and authorities were investigating, without giving specifics on damage or casualties. On markets, Brent crude rebounded 0.7% to $84.39 a barrel on August 4 after tumbling 7% the previous session to a three-week low, while US WTI rose 0.7% to $80.95, as traders weighed the impact of uncertain US–Iran diplomacy on energy supply.

3. Kyiv's overnight raid on Russia's rear: Wildberries hubs near Moscow and St. Petersburg hit, Syzran refinery ablaze

According to The Kyiv Independent (updated 12:48 Kyiv time, August 4, i.e. 17:48 Beijing time), citing Russian Telegram channels, Ukraine's military launched a wide-ranging drone attack on Russia's rear overnight on August 4, targeting logistics and oil infrastructure. Videos posted by local residents showed thick black smoke rising from a Wildberries e-commerce logistics hub in Chekhov, Moscow Oblast — about 65 kilometres (40 miles) south of the Kremlin — with Ukrainian drones reportedly seen flying over the city ahead of the strike.

Moscow Oblast Governor Andrey Vorobyov reported that several drone strikes hit the Novosyolki industrial zone, killing five people and injuring six. Later, a Wildberries warehouse in Krasny Bor, Leningrad Oblast — about 33 kilometres (20 miles) southeast of St. Petersburg — was also confirmed struck, with Leningrad Governor Alexander Drozdenko and Wildberries' press service both confirming damage. In addition, a large fire broke out at the Syzran Oil Refinery in Samara Oblast, roughly 800 kilometres behind the front line, a facility Kyiv's forces have repeatedly targeted with long-range drones. Citing Data Insight analyst Sergei Semko, Russian Forbes reported that the recent attacks could cost Wildberries marketplace sellers as much as 215–280 billion rubles ($2.7–3.5 billion). Wildberries — often dubbed the "Russian Amazon" — and its founder Tatiana Kim, Russia's wealthiest woman, have been sanctioned over the full-scale invasion, and the company's banking arm was added to the EU sanctions list in July.

4. ISW: Ukraine pushes back Russian forces in the Kharkiv sector, fighting rages in Kupiansk

According to RBC-Ukraine (10:50 Kyiv time, August 4, i.e. 15:50 Beijing time), citing a report by the US Institute for the Study of War (ISW), Russian forces continued their offensive in the Kupiansk direction on August 2–3, but Ukrainian forces launched counterattacks in the area. Volodymyr Dehtyaryov, spokesman for the Khartiia 2nd Corps of Ukraine's National Guard, said Ukrainian forces are conducting two simultaneous operations in the Kharkiv region: a defensive operation north and east of Kharkiv, and an offensive operation in the Kupiansk direction, aimed at pushing Russian forces away from the state border and maintaining full fire control over Kupiansk and the ground routes Russia uses to bring in reinforcements.

Although Russian military bloggers claimed Russian forces had advanced and seized positions in and around Kupiansk, ISW said there is currently no evidence confirming Russian advances in this sector. Dehtyaryov added that Russian forces lack manpower in this area, so "there is no need to fear a breakthrough of Ukraine's defenses," though the Russians are actively using glide bombs (KABs) because Ukraine lacks sufficient capability to destroy the aircraft carrying out such strikes. Overall, the Kharkiv sector remains a grinding back-and-forth along the Oskil River.

From the US–Iran diplomatic deadlock of "talk and strike," to the persistent maritime friction in the Strait of Hormuz, to the mass drone exchanges over Russia's rear logistics and energy infrastructure — August 4's international situation remains charged with tension. In the coming hours, the direction of the two-phase US–Iran talks, the progress of Iran–Oman consultations on a new route, and the military moves of both sides around Kharkiv are all worth following closely.
