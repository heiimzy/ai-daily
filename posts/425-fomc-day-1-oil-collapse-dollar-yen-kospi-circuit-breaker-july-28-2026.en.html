⚡ July 28 Financial Roundup: FOMC Day 1 — Rate Hike Odds Jump to 36%, Oil Collapses Below $87, KOSPI Triggers Circuit Breaker

📊 FOMC Day 1: Rate Hike Probability Surges to 36% as Markets Brace for Decision

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) began its two-day policy meeting on Tuesday, July 28 — the first interest rate decision chaired by Fed Chair Kevin Warsh. While the consensus expectation is for rates to remain unchanged at 3.50%-3.75%, CME FedWatch data shows the implied probability of a 25-basis-point hike has surged from just 10% two weeks ago to 36%, reflecting growing inflation concerns fueled by geopolitical uncertainty.

Goldman Sachs chief economist David Mericle expects the FOMC to hold rates steady but anticipates the policy statement will signal greater concern about upside inflation risks. At least one Committee member is expected to formally vote for a hike. The April 2026 meeting witnessed the widest dissent since 1992 with an 8-4 split, as four members opposed the decision to hold.

Since the July meeting does not include a Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) or dot plot, all attention will focus on the statement language and Chair Warsh's post-decision press conference on Wednesday at 2:30 PM ET. Markets will parse every word for signals about September. Fed funds futures currently price in a 68-70% probability of a hike by September.

"The key to this meeting isn't the rate decision itself — a hold is nearly fully priced — but the statement language and Warsh's tone in the press conference." — Brian Therien, Senior Analyst at Edward Jones

🛢️ Oil Extends Collapse: Brent Breaks Below $87 as US-Iran Truce Holds

Crude oil prices continued their dramatic slide on Tuesday. Brent crude futures for September delivery fell more than 2% to $86.52 per barrel, while WTI crude dropped 2.3% to $80.71. This follows Monday's brutal session in which Brent crashed 8.7% — its biggest single-day decline in over three months — and WTI tumbled 7.5%.

The selloff is driven by easing geopolitical risk premium as the US and Iran maintain a pause in hostilities for a third consecutive day. President Donald Trump stated that Washington and Tehran are engaged in "good talks" aimed at ending the conflict. According to Axios, Trump paused further strikes on Iran to allow diplomatic efforts to proceed, though Iran has denied agreeing to a formal 10-day ceasefire.

On the supply side, Kazakhstan's main crude export terminal — the Caspian Pipeline Consortium (CPC) — resumed loading operations after being disrupted by Ukrainian drone attacks, adding to expectations of improving global supply. Meanwhile, Iranian and Omani officials are negotiating a deal to restart commercial shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, a waterway that carries roughly one-fifth of the world's daily oil supply.

However, risks remain elevated. The Commonwealth Bank of Australia warned that fundamental disagreements over Strait of Hormuz navigation rights "could see hostilities reignite." Macquarie Group cautioned that if diplomatic efforts fully succeed, the oil market could swing into a surplus of approximately 2 million barrels per day in Q4 2026.

🇰🇷 Black Tuesday in Seoul: KOSPI Plunges 8%, Triggers 8th Circuit Breaker of 2026

Asian markets suffered a "Black Tuesday" on July 28, with South Korea's KOSPI index plunging more than 8% to trigger a Level 1 circuit breaker — the 8th such event in 2026 and the 3rd in July alone. Trading was halted for 20 minutes at 10:13 AM KST with the index at 6,213.51, down 542.24 points (-8.02%).

The carnage was concentrated in technology and semiconductor shares:

  • Samsung Electronics: plunged ~9%
  • SK Hynix: crashed ~11%, falling below its US IPO price
  • Japan's Nikkei 225: tumbled 4.44% to 62,046
  • Taiwan's TAIEX: fell 4.07% to 41,856
  • Kioxia (Japan): collapsed 18%, Tokyo Electron fell nearly 10%

Three catalysts converged to trigger the selloff: First, Wall Street's semiconductor rout continued overnight, with the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) now down 21% from its June 22 record high. Second, growing concern over AI investment returns — amplified by reports that Nvidia is discussing a $250 billion financing guarantee for OpenAI — sent Nvidia shares down 5%. Third, China's reported breakthrough in domestic DUV lithography production raised fears of intensified competition and potential chip oversupply.

China's markets also felt the pain on Tuesday. The Shanghai Composite fell 1.09%, the Shenzhen Component dropped 3.28%, and the ChiNext board plunged 4.77%, reversing Monday's gains driven by CXMT's blockbuster IPO debut.

💵 Dollar Hits One-Month High, Yen Flirts with 40-Year Lows

In currency markets, the US Dollar Index (DXY) held firm near 101.55, hovering around a one-month high. Despite falling oil prices easing some inflation fears, expectations of a potential Fed rate hike this week or in September continue to underpin dollar strength.

Key currency pairs:

  • EUR/USD: slipped to 1.1366, near monthly lows
  • USD/JPY: rose to 163.82, with the yen plumbing 40-year depths
  • GBP/USD: edged lower to 1.3284

The Bank of Japan (BOJ) concludes its policy meeting on Friday and is widely expected to keep rates unchanged. Verbal intervention from Japanese authorities has so far done little to stem the yen's decline. ANZ Head of FX Research Mahjabeen Zaman warned that an unexpected Fed rate hike would further strengthen the dollar, particularly against low-yielding currencies like the yen and Swiss franc.

"If the Fed actually hikes this week — a risk that is not yet fully priced — the dollar will strengthen sharply, and USD/JPY could test 165." — Mahjabeen Zaman, Head of FX Research, ANZ

This week is one of the most consequential of 2026 for financial markets, with a dense cluster of macro events:

  • July 28-29: FOMC rate decision (Wednesday 2:00 PM ET, press conference 2:30 PM ET)

Markets will need to process signals on growth, inflation, monetary policy, and corporate AI spending all at once. Volatility is expected to remain elevated through the week.
