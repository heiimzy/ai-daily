⚡ Aug 12 Finance News: Hang Seng Tech Index May Expand to 50 Constituents, Spot Gold Tops $4,390, WTI Back Above $84, CNY Fixing at 6.7882, Japan 2-Yr Yield at 1.64%

August 12 morning brought a burst of headlines as Asia-Pacific markets opened: Hang Seng Indexes Company is consulting on expanding the Hang Seng Tech Index from 30 to 50 constituents; spot gold climbed above $4,390 an ounce, up 0.50% on the day, with NY gold futures breaking past $4,450; WTI crude futures returned above $84 a barrel while China's fuel oil futures surged 4%; the PBOC set the CNY central parity at 6.7882 and Hang Seng futures opened slightly lower; Japan's 2-year government bond yield rose to 1.640%. Details below, with every source's publication time verified.

1. Hang Seng Tech Index Set to Expand from 30 to 50 Constituents

According to Jin10 (published 09:03, Aug 12), Hang Seng Indexes Company is consulting the market on amending the Hang Seng Tech Index, proposing to expand its constituents from 30 to 50 stocks. The Hang Seng Tech Index is the flagship benchmark tracking Hong Kong-listed technology companies, and has maintained a 30-constituent roster since its launch in 2020. If implemented, this would be the largest constituent overhaul in the index's history. A bigger basket means more eligible tech names could be included and capture passive fund flows, potentially reshaping valuations and liquidity across Hong Kong's tech sector. The proposal is still in the market consultation phase, with the final plan pending the company's announcement of consultation results.

2. Spot Gold Tops $4,390/oz, NY Futures Break $4,450

According to Jin10 market flashes (09:12, Aug 12), spot gold climbed above $4,390 an ounce, up 0.50% on the day, while NY gold futures crossed $4,450 an ounce, also up 0.50%. Earlier order-book reports flagged that spot gold was consolidating with dense sell orders above, while spot silver was testing the $65 level. Precious metals extended their strength through the overnight session and Asian morning, reflecting sustained demand for safe-haven assets amid geopolitical tensions and inflation expectations. Traders should watch the tug-of-war around key round numbers, with volatility likely to pick up once European and U.S. sessions begin.

3. WTI Back Above $84 a Barrel, Fuel Oil Leads China Commodities

According to Jin10 market flashes (09:08, Aug 12), WTI crude futures climbed above $84 a barrel, up 0.93% on the day, while on China's domestic futures market the fuel oil main contract surged 4.00% to 3,785 yuan per ton. Domestic futures opened mixed on Wednesday morning: fuel oil rose over 3% and low-sulphur fuel oil over 2% to lead gains, with coking coal, polysilicon, asphalt, red dates and coke up more than 1%; on the downside, palladium, platinum, bottle-grade PET and pulp fell over 1%, with eggs down nearly 1%. The broad strength in energy is tied to continued attention on the Strait of Hormuz — U.S. President Donald Trump said early in the morning that "we fully control the Strait of Hormuz," keeping a geopolitical premium under oil prices.

4. CNY Central Parity at 6.7882, Hong Kong Futures Open Slightly Lower

According to Sina Finance 7x24 (published 09:15, Aug 12), the PBOC set the CNY central parity rate at 6.7882 per U.S. dollar. The central parity, the daily benchmark rate published by the central bank, extended its recent trading range at 6.7882. On index futures, Hang Seng Index futures opened the day session down 0.32% at 25,546.68, a discount of 106.14 points; FTSE China A50 index futures opened down 0.15%; Taiwan's TAIEX, meanwhile, opened higher, gaining 194.55 points or 0.43% to 45,315.27. Overall, Asia-Pacific index futures opened mixed, with markets awaiting more economic data and policy signals.

5. Japan's 2-Year JGB Yield Rises to 1.640%

According to Jin10 (published 09:01, Aug 12), Japan's 2-year government bond yield rose 3.0 basis points to 1.640%. A move higher in the short end of the JGB curve usually signals shifting market expectations about the Bank of Japan's policy-normalization path. Against the backdrop of diverging monetary policy among major central banks, the trajectory of JGB yields and its spillover to the yen and Japanese equities is worth watching closely.

This hour's five financial updates sketch three themes across the Asia-Pacific morning session: first, safe-haven assets remain in demand — spot gold above $4,390 and NY futures past $4,450 show record-high bullion still drawing geopolitical and inflation-driven bids; second, the geopolitical premium in energy — WTI back above $84 and domestic fuel oil up 4% make the Strait of Hormuz the core variable for commodities; third, structural change in market benchmarks — the proposed expansion of the Hang Seng Tech Index from 30 to 50 constituents, if confirmed, would reshape passive fund flows into Hong Kong tech and is a medium-term signal worth more attention than a single day's move.
