⚡ August 5 Finance Update: S&P 500 and Dow Both Hit Record Highs, Caterpillar Posts First $20B Quarter, US June Job Openings Fall to 7.359M, Oil Slips to Three-Week Low

US equities extended Monday's surge in Tuesday's (Aug 4 ET) afternoon session, with the S&P 500 and the Dow Jones both touching record highs: the S&P rose 1.5% to top its June 2 closing record for the first time, the Dow climbed a further 893 points (1.7%) and the Nasdaq jumped 2.1%. Earnings season added another heavyweight: Caterpillar posted record second-quarter revenue above $20 billion for the first time and raised its full-year guidance, US June JOLTS job openings eased to 7.359 million, below forecasts, and Brent crude slipped as much as 5.6% to $79.12 on hopes of reopening the Strait of Hormuz, touching a three-week low. Here is this round's finance roundup.

1. US Stocks Hit Record Highs Intraday: S&P 500 Tops Its June Record, Dow +893, Nasdaq +2.1%

According to Reuters and the Associated Press (US morning/noon, Aug 4), US stocks extended their rally Tuesday, lifted by strong AI-linked earnings and easing Middle East tensions. As of noon Eastern time, the S&P 500 rose about 1.5%, topping its June 2 closing record of 7,620.90 for the first time; the Dow Jones gained 893 points, or about 1.7%, adding to its record close the day before; and the Nasdaq Composite rose about 2.1%.

Reuters cited early-session data showing that, buoyed by strong outlooks from Caterpillar and Palantir, the S&P 500 and Dow both hit fresh records — at 9:35 a.m. ET, the Dow rose 647.09 points (1.22%) to 53,825.50, the Nasdaq gained 304.36 points (1.16%) to 26,213.57, and the S&P 500 added 44.06 points (0.58%) to 7,644.56, the index's first record since June. The AP noted that even amid worries over high inflation, the Iran conflict and a possible AI bubble, the core driver of the rally is soaring corporate earnings — Palantir jumped more than 17% after hours, capping a clearly "profit-led" earnings season. SpaceX is set to release its first quarterly report since its IPO after the close, with AMD also reporting after the bell Tuesday.

2. Caterpillar Posts First $20B Quarter: Record Revenue on Data-Center Demand, Guidance Raised

According to Caterpillar's official results and Quartz (Aug 4), the industrial-equipment giant Caterpillar (CAT) reported second-quarter results: sales and revenues reached $20.5 billion, up 24% year over year — the company's first quarter ever above $20 billion; adjusted profit per share came in at $8.17, up from $4.72 a year earlier and well ahead of the $6.20 analysts expected.

By segment, the Construction Industries unit was the primary growth driver, with sales up 35% to $8.3 billion, including a 50% surge in North American construction demand. The Power & Energy segment added 17% revenue growth to $8.2 billion, with power-generation sales — largely tied to data-center projects — climbing 29%, making AI data-center buildout the standout tailwind. Operating profit rose 50% to $4.3 billion, with operating margin expanding to 20.9% from 17.3%; the company booked $9.4 billion in new orders, lifting its cumulative backlog to a record $72.1 billion.

Chairman and CEO Joe Creed said it was "the first time in company history that we have generated over $20 billion in sales and revenues in a single quarter," with strong order rates and a growing backlog reflecting broadening momentum across all three primary segments. The company raised its full-year revenue growth outlook to the mid-to-high teens and narrowed its tariff-cost estimate to roughly $2.2 billion. Shares rose about 9% in premarket trading. The report — showing AI data-center demand flowing through to real-economy equipment orders — is widely read as a strong rebuttal to concerns that AI spending is a bubble.

3. US June JOLTS Job Openings Fall to 7.359M: Firmer Hiring Points to a Steady Labor Market

According to the US Bureau of Labor Statistics and Reuters (released 10:00 a.m. ET, Aug 4), US job openings fell markedly in June, dragged by a sharp decline in the healthcare and social-assistance sector: openings decreased by 178,000 to 7.359 million by the last business day of June, below the 7.400 million forecast in a Reuters poll; the job-openings rate eased to 4.4% from 4.5% in May, with healthcare and social-assistance openings down 147,000 as the main drag.

The other side of the report, however, was more encouraging: hiring increased by 96,000 to 5.348 million in June, with the hires rate rising to 3.4% from 3.3%; layoffs and discharges changed little at 1.766 million, with the rate steady at 1.1%. Reuters noted that while the openings measure of labor demand eased, the rise in hiring and low layoffs suggest the labor market remains stable rather than cooling sharply. With markets focused on the Fed's rate path, this employment data will feed into investor bets on whether the central bank hikes at its September meeting (currently around a 64.5% probability per CME FedWatch).

4. Oil Slips to Three-Week Low: Hormuz Reopening Hopes Build, Brent Down as Much as 5.6%

According to the AP, BBC and Reuters (Aug 4), oil prices fell sharply Tuesday as hopes mounted that the US and Iran could reach a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz: intraday, international benchmark Brent crude fell about 5.6%, touching $79.12 a barrel, while US West Texas Intermediate (WTI) dropped more than 5% to around $76 — both hitting their lowest levels since July 13, a three-week low.

Driving the drop were positive diplomatic signals. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC he saw "a chance we may have a deal today or tomorrow to open the strait and move towards a more normalized position", while Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Bessent both said negotiations had progressed, with shipments potentially resuming as early as this week. The strait carries about one-fifth of global oil and LNG supplies, and its disruption had been a central driver of oil volatility since the conflict began. That said, Reuters quoted traders cautioning that Washington and Tehran still offer conflicting accounts of the talks, and vessel attacks continue, so the market remains wary of whether a deal will actually materialize — oil is likely to stay volatile in the near term. Goldman Sachs sees Brent ranging between $80 and $90 per barrel until the Middle East situation becomes clearer.
