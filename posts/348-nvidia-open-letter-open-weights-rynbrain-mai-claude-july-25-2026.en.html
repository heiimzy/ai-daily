NVIDIA, Microsoft, Meta and 25 Tech Companies Sign Open Letter: "Don't Prematurely Restrict Open-Weight AI"

In the early hours of July 25, 25 U.S. technology companies including NVIDIA, Microsoft, and Meta co-signed an open letter to policymakers, warning against premature restrictions on open-weight AI models. NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang first promoted the letter on social media, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella reposted it, and Elon Musk expressed his "full support" on X.

The signatories emphasized that open-weight models help strengthen competition and ensure that the benefits of AI technology are "broadly shared, not concentrated in the hands of a few." The letter refutes the "open equals unsafe" argument, noting that closed-source models are "not inherently secure" — they can also be hacked, abused, or fail in ways invisible to the outside world.

Notably, OpenAI and Anthropic — both valued near $1 trillion and preparing for IPOs — did not sign. However, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman publicly stated Friday that he "welcomes" the letter, saying he hopes America wins in both open-source and proprietary AI. OpenAI President Greg Brockman said at a Thursday media briefing that "AI and the use of AI, fundamentally, are things that need to be democratized."

Alibaba Damo Academy Open-Sources RynnBrain: Robots Gain Spatiotemporal Memory for the First Time

Alibaba's Damo Academy released RynnBrain, an embodied intelligence brain foundation model, in the early hours of July 25, open-sourcing all 7 models in the series including a 30B MoE variant. RynnBrain gives robots spatiotemporal memory and spatial reasoning abilities for the first time, marking a significant leap in AI intelligence.

RynnBrain set new SOTA records across all 16 embodied intelligence benchmarks, surpassing industry leaders including Google's Gemini Robotics ER1.5. The core breakthrough lies in two innovations: spatiotemporal memory enables robots to locate objects and target areas within their complete historical memory and even predict motion trajectories, granting them global spatiotemporal backtracking capabilities.

Spatial physics reasoning uses an interleaved text-and-spatial-localization strategy, ensuring reasoning is firmly grounded in the physical environment and dramatically reducing hallucination. For example, a robot running RynnBrain that is interrupted mid-task to handle an urgent B task can remember where it was in task A and resume seamlessly afterward — something nearly impossible in previous robot models.

Microsoft Launches MAI-Image-2.5-Pro and MAI-Voice-2-Flash, Cutting GPU Costs by 89% vs OpenAI

Microsoft AI announced public preview of MAI-Image-2.5-Pro and MAI-Voice-2-Flash on July 23, marking a major milestone where Microsoft's in-house models graduate from research labs to full production deployment across Bing, PowerPoint, OneDrive, Dynamics 365, and GitHub Copilot.

MAI-Image-2.5-Pro is Microsoft's highest-fidelity image model, targeting hero imagery, detailed editing, and pixel-precise in-image text rendering — long a notorious weak spot for image generators. Pricing is set at $5 per 1M text input tokens, $8 per 1M image input tokens, and $106 per 1M image output tokens.

MAI-Voice-2-Flash targets high-volume voice workloads, running 2x faster than MAI-Voice-2 at 32% lower cost ($15 per 1M characters). In Dynamics 365 Contact Center (used by T-Mobile and EasyJet), Microsoft reports GPU cost reductions of up to 89% versus OpenAI models. CEO Satya Nadella posted on X that Microsoft is "beginning to route traffic across our first-party surfaces to MAI," signaling a systematic reduction in reliance on OpenAI.

Anthropic Upgrades Claude Voice Mode with Opus, Sonnet, Haiku and App Integrations

Anthropic rolled out a major Claude Voice Mode upgrade on July 24, expanding from Haiku-only to supporting Opus, Sonnet, and Haiku models. Voice conversations now automatically use the last model selected in text chat, creating a seamless cross-modal experience.

More significantly, Claude Voice Mode now integrates deeply with Gmail, Google Calendar, Slack, Canva, and Notion. Users can schedule meetings, draft emails, create documents, and update calendar events entirely by voice. Free users get Haiku plus one connected app, while paid subscribers unlock Opus and Sonnet with full app connectivity.

Anthropic's strategic bet contrasts with OpenAI's approach: while OpenAI focuses on making ChatGPT's voice sound more natural, Anthropic is betting that deeper workplace integrations and more capable reasoning models will make Claude a true productivity assistant rather than just a conversational chatbot.
