⚡ Aug 6 Tech Briefing: Microsoft Makes GPT-5.6 Sol the Default Copilot Model, Google's $15B India Data Center Faces Water & Wildlife Challenges, Shanghai Issues Auto Marketing Compliance Charter, Virus Center Warns on Fake Software

In the past hour or two, four stories dominated the tech world: Microsoft issued an internal memo asking developers to make OpenAI's flagship GPT-5.6 Sol the default model in GitHub Copilot; Google's $15 billion "largest-ever" India data center campus entered full construction, only to face a court challenge over water stress and wildlife concerns; Shanghai regulators jointly released an auto-industry marketing and online-information compliance charter targeting paid unlocks, below-cost selling and smear campaigns; and China's National Computer Virus Emergency Response Center flagged July's four major virus trends, with fake software, activation tools and tutoring apps as the main vectors. Here is this round's tech briefing.

1. Microsoft internal memo: GPT-5.6 Sol becomes the default Copilot model

According to CNBC, which reported on Aug 5, Microsoft has asked developers working on AI coding projects to make OpenAI's flagship model their first choice. Jay Parikh, Executive Vice President of Microsoft's CoreAI engineering team, made the request in an internal memo sent to employees this week, aiming to improve the efficiency of AI resource usage: "Moving more internal workloads to OpenAI models lets our token spend go further."

Parikh's CoreAI organization spans GitHub, Visual Studio and Visual Studio Code. He asked employees to select OpenAI's flagship GPT-5.6 Sol as the default when using GitHub Copilot, and to keep using it in the vast majority of cases. Notably, Microsoft has built its own coding models and offers cloud customers access to more than 11,000 models including Anthropic's — yet it still wants employees to leverage the intellectual property gained from its early investment in OpenAI. A Microsoft spokesperson said the company periodically updates default model settings in internal tools to balance performance and resource efficiency, and engineers can always switch to other models. Earlier disclosures showed Microsoft earned $24.1 billion (about RMB 162.97 billion) from OpenAI in the fiscal year ended June 2026, with the bulk of its AI business revenue coming from the long-time partner.

2. Google's $15B India data center: water pressure and a sanctuary just 860 meters away

According to Reuters, Google's $15 billion (about RMB 101.43 billion) data center campus in Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh, has entered full construction — and environmental opposition is growing. The "largest-ever" Google investment in India may soon face its first major test: the project is alleged to add strain on local water supplies, and the construction site sits near a wildlife sanctuary home to leopards and pangolins.

Andhra Pradesh government data shows Visakhapatnam can draw 410 million liters of water per day from reservoirs and rivers, but the city needs 480 million liters daily; with a population of about 2.5 million, water rationing is already routine — and data centers consume vast amounts of water for server cooling, plus enormous amounts of electricity. On Aug 2, the Andhra Pradesh High Court ordered the state government to respond to allegations by activist group Jal Biradari, which argues the project will burden nearby reservoirs and tighten local water supplies. The public-interest litigation also worries that vibration and noise from construction could reach the Kambalakonda wildlife sanctuary just 860 meters away. Google responded that the project will comply with all applicable laws, adopt advanced air-cooling technology and protect important local water resources, and promised noise-reduction measures to be a "quiet, unobtrusive neighbor." The project is being developed with Adani Group, owned by Indian billionaire Gautam Adani, and could create up to 188,000 jobs.

3. Shanghai issues auto marketing compliance charter: paid unlocks, below-cost selling and smear campaigns targeted

According to the "Shanghai Cyberspace" official account today (Aug 6), Shanghai's Cyberspace Administration, together with the municipal economy and informatization commission, commerce commission, market regulator and public security cyber unit, launched a special campaign to regulate online information dissemination in the auto industry, targeting four problem areas: maliciously talking down the market, distorting policy interpretation, improper marketing traffic and inciting irrational consumer complaints. The campaign found the industry lacked self-regulatory guidance tailored to its characteristics, with uneven compliance awareness among automakers and dealers.

To address this, the authorities guided the Shanghai Automobile Sales Industry Association and the Shanghai Auto Parts Industry Association to draft the Shanghai Auto-Industry Marketing Behavior and Online Information Dissemination Compliance Charter. The charter focuses on the sharpest risks in online auto marketing: on pricing, it requires clear price tags and fully transparent promotional terms, and regulates "paid unlocks" — features with a free period must disclose the free period and future fees, with a reminder before the free period ends; below-cost selling is banned, meaning no sales below cost aimed at squeezing rivals or monopolizing the market. On online information, promotional content must be truthful — no fabricated prices, malicious market-bashing, disparaging competitors or inciting irrational complaints; AI livestreams and generative content must be labeled as such, and fake "internal discounts" are barred; organizing "black PR" or "paid trolls" to smear or maliciously compare is strictly prohibited. On sales and service, consumers' freedom of choice must be protected, all after-sales policies disclosed, and complaints must be acknowledged within three working days. The charter took effect on the day of release.

4. Virus center warns: fake mainstream software, activation tools, official documents and summer tutoring apps are top vectors

China's National Computer Virus Emergency Response Center and the National Engineering Laboratory for Computer Virus Prevention, using the national collaborative virus analysis platform, detected a series of malware programs and summarized July 2026's four major virus trends:

  • Fake mainstream software: imitations span popular instant-messaging, system utilities, office, browser, shopping, gaming and AI software;
  • Fake "activation tools": counterfeit pirated-OS activation tools, clearly targeting users who just installed or reinstalled their systems;
  • Fake official documents: the "Yinhu" (Silver Fox) trojan family continues forging documents around sensitive HR topics like "layoffs" and "disciplinary violations" to trick users;
  • Fake summer tutoring apps: multiple mobile and PC malware variants masquerade as summer training and education software, spreading specifically to parents and students.

The advisory's sample list includes 100 entries such as AliyunWrap.dll, HEU_KMS activation tools, a file disguised as a "layoff list and compensation plan," deepseek.exe, Pinduoduo.exe and EducationBao.apk. The center reminds users to search for details and upload suspicious files via the National Collaborative Virus Analysis Platform (https://virus.cverc.org.cn), and recommends that network administrators strengthen defenses based on the listed file signatures.
