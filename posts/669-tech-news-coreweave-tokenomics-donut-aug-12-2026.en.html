⚡ Aug 12 Tech News: CoreWeave More Than Doubles Revenue and Jumps 14%, BBC Asks Why Pricing AI Is So Hard, Argonne's DONUT Reads X-Ray Data in Real Time, Northwestern Finds AI Grants More Likely to Win

Early Wednesday brought a dense wave of AI and research stories: GPU-cloud leader CoreWeave posted a Q2 in which revenue more than doubled year over year, sending its stock up 14% after hours on a backlog that now exceeds $100 billion; BBC published a deep dive titled "Tokenomics: Why making AI pay is tricky", zeroing in on how unpredictable token costs are leaving companies stumped; Argonne National Laboratory unveiled a machine-learning tool called DONUT that analyzes X-ray data in real time at the Advanced Photon Source, sharply accelerating materials science; and a Northwestern Kellogg study found AI-assisted research proposals are more likely to secure NIH funding, yet may also narrow research ideas. Details below; all source timestamps have been verified.

1. CoreWeave Revenue Doubles in Q2, Stock Rises 14% as Backlog Tops $100B

According to CNBC (published Aug 11, 22:51 UTC / Aug 12, 06:51 Beijing time), AI cloud infrastructure provider CoreWeave reported second-quarter revenue up 112% year over year, beating Wall Street estimates and lifting its stock 14% in extended trading. The company posted an adjusted loss of $1.03 per share (vs. the $1.20 loss expected) and a net loss of $626 million (up from $290 million a year earlier). Revenue backlog stood at $104 billion, a figure that excludes over $25 billion in new commitments from the third quarter, and it reached 1.5 gigawatts of active power. For guidance, management called for $3.4 billion to $3.6 billion in Q3 revenue (158% growth at the midpoint), raised full-year revenue to $12.4 billion to $13.2 billion, and guided to over 1.85 GW of active power by year end with $35 billion to $39 billion in annual capital expenditures. The quarter included a new business relationship with Anthropic and about $6 billion in new commitments from quantitative trading firm Jump Trading. CEO Mike Intrator said pricing and margins for Nvidia's Blackwell and Vera Rubin SKUs are "setting new highs," and the company is passing component price increases on to customers. Despite the growth, the 8-year-old company is still unprofitable, carrying $35 billion in debt, and watching regulators tighten—New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law in July establishing a moratorium on new large-scale data centers.

2. BBC: Why Is Pricing AI So Hard? A "Tokenomics" Deep Dive

According to BBC (published Aug 11, 23:03 UTC / Aug 12, 07:03 Beijing time), while big model labs have poured hundreds of billions of dollars into AI, setting a sustainable price for AI services and agents has proved surprisingly difficult. The article explains that familiar cost models break down: when you prompt ChatGPT or Claude, your query is broken into "tokens" (word chunks) the model processes, and the outcome is never fully predictable—the same prompt does not always yield the same answer, and different models answer differently. Agentic systems, where multiple AI agents collaborate to make decisions, amplify token usage and unpredictability further. Goldman Sachs analysis cited in the article forecasts that token consumption will grow 24-fold between 2026 and 2030, to 120 quadrillion tokens a month. Will Venters, associate professor at the London School of Economics, notes companies can be caught out by unpredictable token costs: "It's a non-deterministic output, so it's a non-deterministic value." smartR AI founder Oliver King-Smith says smaller firms can use flat-fee personal accounts to "fly under the radar," but that this won't last—once big platforms face shareholder pressure to show profit, "they will start clamping down." The piece advises companies to choose models carefully, write more precise prompts, and factor token costs into product pricing. Bottom line: AI money is easy to spend but hard to account for.

3. Argonne's DONUT Tool Analyzes X-Ray Data in Real Time

According to Phys.org (published Aug 11, 23:20 UTC / Aug 12, 07:20 Beijing time), researchers at the US Department of Energy's Argonne National Laboratory and the Advanced Photon Source (APS) have developed a machine-learning tool called DONUT that is transforming how X-ray experiments run. At a synchrotron light source, traditional X-ray diffraction and related experiments often require hours of offline processing before scientists can make sense of the large datasets. DONUT moves analysis into the experiment itself, delivering real-time insights: researchers can glimpse structural and chemical information while the measurement is still running, adjust parameters on the fly, and raise data quality—turning "learn only after the run finishes" into "see clearly as you go." The tool targets a core pain point of big-science facilities: data is produced far faster than it can be analyzed by hand. For materials, chemistry, and energy research, real-time data analysis promises to significantly shorten research cycles.

4. Northwestern: AI-Assisted Grant Proposals Are More Likely to Win—But May Narrow Ideas

According to Phys.org (published Aug 11, 23:20 UTC / Aug 12, 07:20 Beijing time), a new study from Northwestern University's Kellogg School of Management found that research proposals showing stronger signs of AI-assisted writing were four percentage points more likely to receive funding from the National Institutes of Health (NIH). After comparing AI-writing signals across a large set of proposals, the researchers found AI-assistance correlates with a higher probability of funding—clearer, more polished writing tends to fare better in review. But the efficiency comes with a caution: AI-assisted writing may inadvertently narrow and homogenize research ideas, making proposals more formulaic and less likely to advance breakthrough, non-mainstream questions. The authors caution that in a review culture that rewards polished text, AI is both a tool that boosts win rates and a force that could make the scientific ideas ecosystem more conservative. The study offers a thought-provoking data point on the growing practice of drafting grant proposals with large language models.

5. The Big Picture: Compute, Cost, and Science Align

These four stories sketch three converging threads in AI today: a capital frenzy in compute infrastructure—CoreWeave doubling revenue with a $100B backlog and nearly $40B in annual capex shows the AI chip and data-center arms race is still accelerating, even before the company turns a profit and despite heavy debt; a "chaotic period" for business models and cost-accounting—the BBC's Tokenomics report reveals a reality where 24x token-growth forecasts coexist with big labs still exploring how to price their services, leaving the question of how AI will be metered and priced as stably as electricity unresolved; and AI's double-edged effect on research—Argonne's DONUT makes data readable in real time while Northwestern finds AI makes grant applications easier to win, together confirming that AI is accelerating the entire pipeline from experimental efficiency to scholarly output, yet raising new questions about homogeneity and interpretability.
