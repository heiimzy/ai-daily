⚡ July 27 Financial Roundup: US-Iran 60-Day Ceasefire Framework Revealed, Super Week Ultimate Showdown Begins — Durable Goods Data Sets the Stage

1. US-Iran Ceasefire Framework Disclosed: 60-Day Truce, Lifting of Naval Blockade

Global financial markets experienced a major geopolitical turning point on July 27. Iran's semi-official "Balance" news agency, affiliated with the judiciary, disclosed the preliminary informal document of the US-Iran Memorandum of Understanding framework, committing the US to withdraw military forces from around Iran and lift the naval blockade.

Iranian National Security Council member Boroujerdi confirmed on July 27 that under the draft preliminary agreement, the US would commit to a 60-day comprehensive ceasefire on all fronts in the first phase, particularly within Lebanon. The agreement also includes releasing a significant portion of Iran's frozen assets and ending the maritime blockade. Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) naval official Mohammad Akbarzadeh stated that the possibility of war restarting with the US is "low," though Iran's armed forces remain "fully loaded and prepared."

Meanwhile, Tabriz International Airport — Iran's third busiest international airport — reopened on July 27 after being damaged during the recent conflict, now operating 9 international routes. Iran's Civil Aviation Organization also announced that 10 airports nationwide would extend operating hours to 24/7 service.

Immediate Global Market Reaction

Following the news, European stock indices rallied across the board:

  • Euro Stoxx 50: +0.85%
  • France CAC 40: +0.87%
  • Germany DAX 30: +0.41%
  • US Futures: Nasdaq futures +0.68%, Dow futures +0.39%, S&P 500 futures +0.35%

Brent crude, which briefly surged above $100/barrel, retreated to around $96.78 as markets reassessed supply-side risk premiums. Analysts noted that if the ceasefire holds and Strait of Hormuz shipping resumes, global oil supply chain pressures would ease significantly.

2. Super Week Ultimate Showdown: FOMC + Big Tech Earnings + GDP + PCE

This week brings the most critical convergence of market events in 2026: the Federal Reserve's FOMC rate decision (July 28-29), Q2 earnings from four mega-cap tech companies (Microsoft, Meta, Apple, Amazon), US Q2 GDP advance, and June core PCE inflation data — all within 72 hours.

Fed Decision: 34% Probability of a Hike, Market Eyes "Hawkish Hold"

Markets currently price a 66% probability of no rate change and 34% probability of a 25-basis-point hike. While June CPI came in below expectations, the Middle East conflict-driven energy price surge has reignited inflation concerns. New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh faces a dilemma — does energy-driven inflation justify a rate increase?

According to CME FedWatch, September rate hike probability has surged to approximately 80%, with at least one hike priced in by year-end. Bank of America analysts note that if Thursday's PCE data shows core inflation still stubbornly above 3%, it would open the door for September action.

Big Tech Earnings: AI Capex Anxiety Takes Center Stage

Alphabet and Tesla's earnings last week triggered widespread AI capex concerns:

  • Alphabet: First-ever negative free cash flow, 2026 CapEx raised to $195-205 billion, stock down over 7% for the week
  • Tesla: Q2 profit miss, negative free cash flow, CapEx doubled YoY, stock crashed 14.5%

This week's reports from Microsoft (Wed after close), Meta (Wed), Apple (Thu), and Amazon (Thu) will test whether AI investments are translating into revenue. Wall Street fears that if all four report surging CapEx with weak revenue guidance, tech stocks could face another wave of selling.

The Magnificent Seven collectively lost approximately $797 billion in market cap last week. The Nasdaq fell 2.90% for the week, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 17% in July — its worst monthly decline this year. Capital continues rotating from tech growth into energy, financials, and materials.

4. Asian Markets: KOSPI Rebounds, Samsung Surpasses Berkshire

This comes after the KOSPI suffered four consecutive weeks of decline, with July volatility reaching 6.23% — exceeding levels seen during the 2008 Global Financial Crisis. Last week, the KOSPI and KOSDAQ markets triggered a combined 7 sidecar circuit breakers.

SK hynix reports earnings on July 29 and Samsung Electronics on July 30, with markets focused on HBM memory demand and AI chip order outlook. While Korea's market leverage unwinding continues, foreign investors have begun showing bargain-hunting interest.

Japan's Nikkei 225 also rose over 1% in early Monday trading, with Asia-Pacific sentiment broadly improving.
