⚡ Aug 1 Tech Roundup: Siri AI May Get a Paywall, Samsung Warns Memory Shortage to Last Until 2028, GM Builds Its Own In-Car AI, Australia's Teen Social Media Ban Shows Limited Impact

AI Assistants: Tim Cook Hints Siri AI Could Come With a Paywall

Apple CEO Tim Cook hinted during Thursday's earnings call that the long-awaited Siri AI upgrade could come with some paywalled limits for power users — and it may have been his final earnings call as Apple's chief executive. Cook conceded the plans are not set in stone, but said he envisions users being able to buy more compute for Siri AI through the company's existing iCloud+ subscriptions, which already offer extended cloud storage.

"We do believe there will be people that want to use [Siri AI] a lot, and so we will have some kind of upgrade possibilities on iCloud+, where people can buy up the stack on iCloud+, and we'll see how the pickup for that is," Cook said. "But we could not be more excited about where [Siri AI] is."

The model mirrors how most other AI providers like Anthropic and OpenAI operate: a limited free version for consumers, with paid upgrades for heavier usage. The revamped Siri AI is available in the iOS 27 beta and is planned to roll out more broadly this fall. Notably, as longtime Senior VP of Hardware Engineering John Ternus steps into Cook's shoes, Apple is at a critical juncture: it has fallen behind in building an advanced AI assistant, even licensing a custom Google Gemini model to augment Siri, and the Siri AI overhaul was delayed so badly that Apple paid $250 million to settle a class action lawsuit over how it marketed the iPhone 16's AI capabilities.

Chips: Samsung Warns "RAMaggedon" Memory Shortage to Intensify in 2027, Last Until 2028

Samsung said on its Q2 earnings call that the AI-driven shortage of RAM chips is not only expected to persist into next year, but will likely intensify in 2027, with tight supply conditions lasting until at least 2028. Samsung manufactures and supplies roughly a third of the world's memory chips, making its guidance a bellwether for the industry.

The company revealed that frontier AI labs, desperate to secure access to memory infrastructure, have been "sharing their medium- to long-term demand forecasts" directly with the Korean giant to lock in future supply. The high demand lets Samsung prioritize customers willing to sign long-term contracts, and this multi-year visibility allows it to install equipment and ramp up production without worrying that demand will dry up — helping it avoid the memory industry's historical boom-and-bust cycles.

The shortage has pushed up chip prices, a double-edged sword for Samsung: sales at its semiconductor unit hit an all-time high in Q2, but profitability in its smartphone and TV divisions shrank as higher-priced chips drove up component costs. Samsung has started passing those costs on to consumers by raising prices on Galaxy phones and tablets, and demand for those devices has since dropped. The crunch, informally dubbed "RAMaggedon," is rippling across the industry: Apple raised prices on MacBooks, Macs and iPads last month and warned revenue growth will slow to between 9% and 11% year-over-year, down from a recent 16% quarterly pace, while Nvidia is expected to raise consumer graphics card prices by 20% to 30%, likely pushing up gaming devices, desktops, consoles and laptops.

Automotive: GM Plans Its Own Deeply Integrated In-Vehicle AI Assistant

General Motors plans to launch its own in-vehicle AI system later this year that's better tailored for its customers than the Gemini AI assistant it currently offers with Google, a GM executive told CNBC. Anna Santos, GM's director of product management for voice and AI/machine learning, said the new assistant will combine conversational AI with GM vehicle knowledge and OnStar intelligence to deliver capabilities beyond what a general-purpose assistant can do.

GM last year announced its Gemini AI bot would launch this year in millions of 2022 model-year vehicles and newer, followed by a GM AI assist, without detailing the technology. Santos said the new assistant, whose name she declined to disclose, will better "understand the vehicle, the drive and our customers' needs, and make everyday ownership simpler." GM is working with an unnamed large language model provider on predictive maintenance, vehicle telemetry and other auto-focused features — including commands like a "kids setting" that tailors music, seats, heating/cooling and door lock controls for children.

"This is the beginning of a broader AI journey for us," Santos said. "There's a limit to what an AI that's just sort of sitting at the top level of the vehicle can do." The Gemini assistant currently supports natural conversation without memorized commands, "live sessions" where the bot chats or plays games like trivia and 20 Questions, and control of some vehicle features such as temperature and radio.

Internet: Australia's First Report on the Under-16 Social Media Ban Shows Limited Impact

Australia's eSafety Commissioner has published its first official tracking report on the country's social media ban for under-16s, and the data aligns with earlier studies: the ban has had limited impact so far, largely because teens have found ways around the restrictions — not hard when some platforms only require a self-declaration of a user's age.

The regulator is running a two-year study following more than 4,100 children and families; the first data was collected in March, three months after the rules took effect. The report found the share of under-16s holding an account on an age-restricted platform dropped from 52.4% to 42.1%, with Reddit steady at 3.6%, Threads rising from 1.5% to 2% and Kick from 0.2% to 0.6%; about a third said an account had been shut down or deactivated because of the ban. Yet even without accounts, 81.5% of children still accessed a restricted service — down just 4.4 points from before the law — since most restricted platforms (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, X, YouTube and others) let people browse content without logging in.

The report also found kids shifting to platforms not yet covered: Pinterest usage rose from 16.6% to 21.8%, BeReal from 0.2% to 2.2%, and Mastodon from zero to 0.7%. Compliance gaps were stark: half of the children said a platform had never asked them to verify their age, 37% said the age listed on their account was over 16, 18.2% said the platform got their age wrong, about 11% said parents helped them get around restrictions, and 7.4% used a VPN. The ban also pushed more kids toward messaging services (52.3%, up from 40.5%) and video games (29%, up from 26.4%), while there was "no statistically significant change" in AI chatbot use. "Complex reform of this nature is unwinding 20 years of social media entrenchment and will therefore take time," eSafety Commissioner Julie Inman Grant wrote. "The impact of the law will not be measured in weeks or months but over generations."
