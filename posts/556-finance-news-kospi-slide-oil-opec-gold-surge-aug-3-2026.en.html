⚡ Aug 3 Finance Update: KOSPI Slides ~5% Giving Back Record Rebound, Oil Plunges 6%+ as OPEC+ Adds 188K bpd, Gold Gaps Up Nearly $40

Asian markets opened Monday (Aug 3) to a rare tale of two tapes. On one side, confirmation that President Trump will hold talks with Iran today, plus an OPEC+ decision to keep raising output, sent crude tumbling more than 6%, pushed spot gold up nearly $40 at the open, broke the US dollar index below 100, and lifted US futures and cryptocurrencies. On the other side, South Korea's KOSPI gave back Friday's record rebound, sliding nearly 5% as foreign investors flipped from record buying to net selling in the very same chip shares that had powered the surge - a reminder that Asia's tech sentiment repair remains fragile.

Korea Gives Back the Epic Rebound: Foreigners Flip to Selling, KOSPI Slides ~5%

Sina Finance reported at 08:14 Beijing time that Korean stocks opened lower and kept sliding, with the KOSPI's decline widening to nearly 5% at 6,282.81 points, as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix both fell more than 5%. Aju Press's opening snapshot was starker: the KOSPI lost 322.7 points, or 4.9%, to 6,272.8; Samsung Electronics fell 7.8% to 242,000 won and SK hynix dropped 7.5% to 1,590,000 won, dragging the large-cap KOSPI 200 down 5.3%, while the KOSDAQ slipped just 0.35% to 718.18. Foreign investors sold a net 795.6 billion won (about $555.1 million) on the main board - a mirror image of Friday's record 7.22 trillion won buying spree, unwinding the same positions in the same names.

The context is Friday's extraordinary session: on July 31 the KOSPI soared 17.91% to close at 6,595.45, its steepest one-day gain ever, and SK hynix hit its daily upper limit for the first time in roughly 17 years. Over July as a whole, however, the KOSPI fell 22.2% and the KOSDAQ 21.4% - the worst monthly drop since October 2008 - after the index had more than doubled in a year, with Samsung and SK hynix alone accounting for about 60% of the KOSPI's total value. Citing Goldman Sachs data, ABC News reported that more than 1.2 million Korean retail investors who borrowed to trade have received margin calls in the past fortnight, with around 360,000 accounts forcefully liquidated. Tokyo slipped in sympathy: the Nikkei 225 fell 1,016.2 points, or 1.6%, to 63,345.9, surrendering part of Friday's advance. Notably, Morgan Stanley on the same day upgraded Korean equities to Overweight with a 12-month target of 9,000, calling the post-liquidation entry point attractive and favoring industrials, defense and finance.

Crude Opens Sharply Lower: Seven OPEC+ Countries Add 188K bpd for September

De-escalation hit oil directly. Sing Tao Finance reported at 06:03 Beijing time that WTI futures opened down 6.22% at $79.401 a barrel; according to Xinhua-cited trading data, at 19:00 EDT on Aug 2 the NYMEX September WTI contract bottomed at $74.78 (-6.96%) and October Brent at $81.55 (-7.26%). By 06:40 Beijing time, WTI was at $80.82 (-4.55%) and Brent at $83.91 (-6.89%).

Supply is adding pressure on the other side. Sina Finance reported at 07:16 that on Aug 2 local time, seven key OPEC+ producers - Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman - held an online meeting and decided to raise output by 188,000 barrels per day for September 2026 to support market stability, saying the move would also let them accelerate compensatory cuts; they reaffirmed full compliance with the Declaration of Cooperation and pledged to keep meeting monthly, with the next gathering set for Sept 6. This marks the sixth consecutive monthly increase since the group restarted hikes in April. Analysts caution the short-term impact is limited while Strait of Hormuz shipping remains blocked, and that the real effect will only come once exports normalize. Iran's foreign ministry spokesman Baghaei reiterated that the strait will not return to its pre-conflict status, and that Tehran and Oman have agreed on an entirely new shipping lane - neither the existing northern nor southern route - defined by mutual agreement.

Gold Gaps Up Nearly $40 Toward $4,100 as the Dollar Breaks Below 100

With safe-haven demand and rate expectations moving in tandem, gold was Monday's clear winner. China Gold Network (中金在线) reported at 07:43 Beijing time that spot gold gapped up at the Asian open, touching $4,082.28 an ounce - nearly $40 above Friday's close - before consolidating around $4,070, last at about $4,068 (+0.64%); silver rose 1.5% to $58.44. Over July, gold gained roughly 1%, its first monthly advance in five months. The logic is straightforward: the oil slide is cooling inflation fears, and markets have cut the implied probability of a September Fed hike to about 65% from above 80% a week ago. The US dollar index dipped 0.24% at the open to 99.52, a fresh 1.5-month low - after Thursday's 2.4% plunge, the biggest one-day drop since January 2023, and a 1.6% weekly loss, the largest since late January. Kitco's latest survey shows Wall Street split on the week ahead: 29% of analysts bullish versus 35% bearish, while 47% of retail investors are bullish.

Outlook: US-Iran Talks Land Today, US Futures Rally

The first trading day of the week is all about a second pass at pricing the geopolitical de-escalation. Per CCTV, Trump said on Aug 2 that an agreement on the Strait of Hormuz already exists and a denuclearization deal will follow, and the US will hold talks with Iran on Aug 3; Iran's foreign minister Araghchi said the same day that negotiations with Oman on jointly managing the strait are in their final stage, close to completion. Israel, meanwhile, was reportedly kept in the dark for hours - senior officials including Prime Minister Netanyahu learned of the cancelled strike from Trump's Truth Social posts, per Israeli media cited by Xinhua. Sentiment is recovering at the margin: at 06:40 Beijing time, Nasdaq 100 futures were up 0.78%, S&P 500 futures up 0.44% and Dow futures up 0.4%; cryptocurrencies rebounded broadly, with bitcoin up 0.98%, ether up 1.93% and SOL, XRP, Dogecoin and BNB all gaining more than 2%. But the Korean rout is a sharp reminder that while a geopolitical risk premium can be squeezed out overnight, the tail risk of deleveraging is far from over.
