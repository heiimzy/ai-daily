⚡ July 29 Financial Super Day: Fed FOMC Decision Day — Maximum Uncertainty in Two Years; KOSPI Triggers Historic Back-to-Back Circuit Breakers; Asian Chip Rout Deepens; Brent Crude Falls to Three-Month Low Below $84

1. Fed FOMC Decision Day: 24-38% Hike Probability, Chair Warsh Faces Defining Moment

July 29 marks the most consequential Federal Reserve meeting since September 2024. The FOMC will announce its interest rate decision at 2:00 PM ET, with markets pricing a 24-38% probability of a 25-basis-point hike — the widest policy uncertainty divergence in two years.

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh presides over his second FOMC meeting amid extraordinary uncertainty. Warsh has publicly stated he has "no tolerance" for persistently elevated inflation and has vowed to make it "a thing of the past." Yet June's CPI data showed inflation cooling to 3.5% (from 4.2% in May), marking the first monthly decline since January and strengthening the case for a hold.

According to CME FedWatch, as of July 29 morning, the probability of the Fed holding rates at 3.50%-3.75% stands at 68-72%, while a 25-basis-point hike to 3.75%-4.00% carries 28-32% odds — up sharply from just 12% one week ago. On Kalshi, 76% of participants expect a hold. Interest rate swaps imply roughly 40% probability of a hike, an unusually high level of pre-decision uncertainty.

The wildcard is energy markets. Brent crude briefly surpassed $100 per barrel earlier this month, though it has since retreated to below $84 as US-Iran ceasefire talks progress. Citadel Securities' Head of Macro Strategy Frank Flight has publicly argued for a surprise hike, stating it would "emphatically end the forward guidance era" and cement the Fed's independence and anti-inflation credibility.

The FOMC itself appears deeply divided. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack have both signaled support for a rate hike. At least one dissenting vote is expected. Goldman Sachs' Chief US Economist David Mericle believes "the majority of voting members are unlikely to push for a rate hike this week, but at least one member will cast a dissenting vote."

All 104 economists surveyed by Reuters expect no change, with 78 forecasting rates to remain unchanged through December. However, Warsh has made clear he will not provide forward guidance, and his post-meeting press conference may offer minimal insight into the committee's thinking.

2. South Korea's Historic Stock Market Collapse: KOSPI Circuit Breaker Two Days Running

The most dramatic Asian market story comes from South Korea. On July 29, the KOSPI index opened 1.09% higher at 6,089.11 on bargain hunting, then dramatically reversed course, collapsing 672.52 points (-11.16%) to close at 5,351.14. The KOSDAQ index plunged 10.01% to 635.21.

This triggered a cascade of historic records:

  • First-ever consecutive dual-market circuit breakers — For the first time in Korea Exchange history, both KOSPI (triggered at 12:32 PM) and KOSDAQ (12:19 PM) were halted by circuit breakers for a second consecutive day
  • 9th KOSPI circuit breaker in 2026 — Surpassing even the 2020 pandemic-era frequency
  • 4th KOSDAQ circuit breaker this year — The index crashed 10.01%
  • Samsung Electronics -11.32% — Shares fell to the 220,000 won range, erasing tens of billions in market value
  • SK Hynix -16.58% — Despite reporting record Q2 earnings just yesterday (revenue 79.3T won, operating profit 60.5T won), the stock was hammered by panic selling

South Korean financial authorities have convened emergency industry meetings. President Lee Jae-myung ordered the Financial Services Commission to rapidly implement supplementary measures, including raising the basic deposit requirement for single-stock leveraged products to 30 million won and considering individual investment caps. Industry estimates suggest these measures could reduce related accounts from 100,000 to approximately 10,000, cutting daily trading volume by roughly 60%.

Foreign investors net sold 472.5 billion won during the session, while individual investors dumped 1.68 trillion won. Institutions bought the dip with net purchases of 2.15 trillion won. However, market confidence remains fragile as investors grapple with dual pressures: AI capex bubble concerns and intensifying semiconductor competition from China.

3. Asian Chip Rout Spreads: Taiwan's TAIEX Suffers 3rd-Largest Drop on Record

Korea's collapse is not an isolated event — a full-blown Asian semiconductor rout swept across the entire East Asian region.

Taiwan's TAIEX plunged 2,030.83 points (-4.65%) on July 29, marking its third-largest single-day point decline on record, closing at 41,603.36. TSMC fell 2.98% despite raising its sales outlook and confirming strong demand. A Bank of America global fund manager survey revealed that 82% of fund managers now view the long semiconductor trade as overcrowded, while 45% cite AI as a potential bubble risk — the biggest tail risk identified by investors.

Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 3.95% to approximately 62,262, led by semiconductor equipment stocks. Kioxia plunged 18%, Tokyo Electron fell over 7%.

The Asian semiconductor collapse is driven by multiple converging pressures: China's breakthroughs in DUV lithography self-sufficiency; CXMT's 471% surge on its STAR Market debut triggering global memory oversupply concerns; and growing "AI capex fatigue" as companies like Uber report burning through their full-year AI budget in just four months.

Analysts note that the current selloff is driven more by technical factors — crowded trade unwinding and leveraged product liquidation — than by fundamental deterioration. But the market's extreme concentration in AI-related names (Samsung and SK Hynix account for 48.46% of KOSPI market cap) means any correction in these bellwethers triggers outsized index moves.

4. Oil Continues Slide: Brent Below $84 as Geopolitical Premium Fades

While Asian equity markets convulse, oil markets are moving in the opposite direction — lower — as Middle East geopolitical tensions ease. Brent crude futures fell 5% to approximately $83.80 per barrel, while WTI crude dropped 4% to around $79, both hitting three-month lows.

The oil price decline is driven by the ongoing US-Iran de-escalation. The US and Iran have now observed multiple consecutive days without military strikes. Iran and Oman are conducting technical negotiations on Strait of Hormuz management, with Gulf states and the international community pushing for a joint management framework. The geopolitical risk premium embedded in oil prices has rapidly unwound — crude has fallen nearly $17 from its early-July peak above $100.

The oil price decline has multiple ripple effects across financial markets:

  • Inflation expectations cooling — Lower oil directly reduces near-term inflation expectations, taking pressure off the Fed to hike
  • Treasury yields retreating — The 10-year yield has fallen from 4.7% to 4.60%, easing global financing cost pressures
  • Airlines rallying — European and US airline stocks rose on lower fuel cost expectations
  • Consumer confidence room to improve — July US consumer confidence fell to 90.8 (below 92.4 expected), but sustained oil declines could support an August rebound

However, analysts warn geopolitical risk is far from eliminated. Iran's IRGC Navy continues to intercept commercial vessels in the Strait of Hormuz, and Houthi Red Sea attacks persist. Should peace talks collapse, oil prices could spike again.
