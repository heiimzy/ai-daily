⚡ July 27 Financial Morning Pulse: Oil Plunges 5% as US-Iran Halt Strikes, Super Week Showdown — FOMC + Big Tech Earnings, Global Markets Rebound

Monday (July 27) Asian morning markets opened with a thunderclap: the United States and Iran both paused military operations after 13 consecutive nights of airstrikes and retaliation, triggering a massive cross-asset rotation. International crude crashed over 5% while US stock futures, gold, silver, and bitcoin surged. But beneath the risk-asset party, a deeper fear is taking center stage: inflation — not geopolitics — is now the markets' biggest worry.

1. US-Iran Pause Trigger: Oil Crashes, Risk Assets Rally

President Trump ordered a halt to US airstrikes on Iran on Friday (July 24), breaking the pattern of 13 consecutive nights of bombardment, Axios reported. US Ambassador to the UN Mike Waltz confirmed the decision was aimed at creating diplomatic space. Iran signaled it would also cease military operations as long as the US halt holds.

The market reaction was immediate and dramatic:

  • Oil prices plunged: WTI crude futures crashed over 5% to $83.76/barrel, while Brent crude dropped over 5% to $86.67/barrel. Brent had surged to a $102 high the prior week before closing Friday near $97. The two-day truce window has already sent oil sharply lower.
  • US stock futures surged: As of 6:40 a.m. Beijing time, Nasdaq 100 futures gained 1.24%, S&P 500 futures rose 0.66%, and Dow futures added 0.47%. Last week's tech rout erased nearly $800 billion from the Magnificent Seven, making Monday's pre-market bounce a welcome reprieve.
  • Precious metals rallied: Spot gold climbed nearly 1% to $4,091.72/oz, while silver surged over 2% to $59.83/oz. Gold has now held the $4,000 support level for five consecutive weeks.
  • Cryptocurrencies jumped: Bitcoin and Dogecoin rose over 1%, Ethereum surged over 3%, and SOL and HYPE climbed over 2% as rate expectations stabilized and risk appetite improved.

Iran remains skeptical of US intentions. An Iranian source told Reuters the decision appeared "more tactical than strategic," adding that Tehran's outlook is "more doubt than optimism." Iran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei reiterated in a letter to Hezbollah that support for the Lebanese group remains strategic policy, and demanded a "complete and unconditional end to Israeli military operations in Lebanon" as a precondition for any MOU with the US.

2. Super Week Showdown: FOMC + Big Tech Earnings + GDP + PCE

This week (July 27-31) is being called the most important Super Week of 2026, with five macro events set to reshape global asset pricing:

Fed FOMC Rate Decision (July 28-29)

The Federal Open Market Committee begins its two-day meeting Wednesday, with the rate decision due Thursday. This is Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's second meeting. Swap markets now price a 37% probability of a 25bp rate hike at this meeting, up from just 12% a week ago. Traders see nearly 80% odds of at least one hike by September. The surge in crude oil prices — up nearly 40% this month — has transmitted significant inflationary pressure, forcing a rapid repricing of the Fed's policy path.

Big Tech Earnings Deluge

Microsoft (Wednesday after close), Meta (Wednesday), Apple (Thursday), and Amazon (Thursday) will report Q2 results. This is the critical test for the AI capex narrative. After Alphabet posted its first-ever negative free cash flow and Tesla missed earnings estimates, the Magnificent Seven lost $797 billion in a single session. Investors need proof that AI's massive infrastructure spending is translating into revenue growth.

Wall Street expects: Microsoft Azure growth at ~33% (AI contributing ~12pp); Meta ad revenue growth of 15-18% with Reality Labs losses widening to ~$5B; Apple services revenue up ~12% amid DRAM price pressure on iPhone; AWS growth at ~20% with capex guidance as the key variable.

US Q2 GDP Advance (Thursday)

Expectations call for a 1.8% annualized growth rate, down from 2.3% in Q1. A weaker-than-expected print would amplify the "stagflation" narrative — slowing growth paired with stubborn inflation — putting the Fed in an increasingly difficult position.

June Core PCE (Thursday)

The Fed's preferred inflation gauge is expected to hold near 2.7-2.8% year-over-year. An upside surprise would push September rate hike probabilities above 90%.

3. Asia-Pacific Market Open: KOSPI Rebounds, Naver Soars, A股 Mixed

  • KOSPI: Opened 1.73% higher at 6,806, staging a sharp rebound from Friday's 5.72% plunge — the largest single-day drop of the year. Two forces drove the recovery: easing Middle East tensions lowering oil prices, and the San Francisco AI Summit where Korean companies signed over $950 billion in cooperation frameworks with Nvidia and other tech giants. Naver surged 11% after disclosing Nvidia's participation in a 1.48 trillion won ($1.1B) capital increase.
  • Nikkei 225: Rose ~1.5% in early trade, recovering from last week's 4%+ rout driven by chip stock losses and yen weakness.
  • China A-shares: Opened modestly higher but AI hardware stocks continued to slide. Foxconn Industrial Internet hit the daily limit down to a two-month low as AI capex anxiety spread from Wall Street to Chinese tech names. Wind power, defense, and shipbuilding sectors outperformed.
  • Hong Kong: Hang Seng opened 0.4% lower, with tech stocks mixed. AI application and defense names attracted buying interest.

4. Inflation Fear Replaces Geopolitics as Markets' Core Concern

The Australian Financial Review noted Monday that while the "TACO" (Trump Always Chickens Out) meme has gone viral on Wall Street, inflation — not geopolitics — is what truly scares markets.

The Trump Trade Index, created by Ned Davis Research and tracking ETFs that should benefit from homebuilding, defense, and manufacturing reshoring policies, has slumped 16% since May, erasing all its year-to-date gains. The index handily beat the S&P 500 at the start of 2026, but the combination of tariffs (10-12.5% on 60 economies effective July 24), AI capex anxiety, and rising rate expectations has shattered the trade.

Bond Markets: The 10-year US Treasury yield closed Friday at 4.69%, down 2bp for the week but still near 18-month highs. The 30-year yield has held above 5% for 14 consecutive sessions — the longest streak since 2007. The 2-year yield rose to 4.34%, reflecting aggressive short-end rate hike pricing.

Commodities: Brent crude has retreated rapidly from last week's $102 high to ~$87, though month-to-date gains still exceed 20%. Goldman Sachs maintains its bullish view, noting Strait of Hormuz traffic has collapsed 60% and supply recovery will take time even if diplomacy progresses. LME copper trades at $14,065/t, in elevated territory.

FX: The dollar index (DXY) stands at 101.44. USD/JPY hit 163.85 — a 40-year low for the yen. The Bank of Japan also meets this week, with markets watching for further tightening signals. Offshore CNH trades at 6.77, relatively stable.

5. Week Ahead Strategy: Defensive Rotation Still in Play

  • Energy & Materials: ATB Financial and Tradingkey both recommend maintaining energy and commodity exposure as geopolitical hedges. The energy sector led the S&P 500 last week, with Lockheed Martin surging over 10% to all-time highs.
  • Mid & Small Caps: Capital is rotating out of Mag 7 into mid- and small-cap names. The S&P MidCap 400 posted a 0.23% gain last week even as large-cap benchmarks fell.
  • Goldman Sachs raised data center forecast: Global data center capacity raised to 217GW by 2030 (from 168GW), requiring ~$6 trillion in capex. Top picks include FirstEnergy, Duke Energy, Digital Realty, Talen Energy.
  • Daishin Securities on KOSPI: The 6,000 range is a zone where "upside potential exceeds downside risk," recommending maintaining core positions through the FOMC and earnings volatility.

The central market question this week: can the oil price pullback offset AI capex anxiety and rising inflation risks? If the FOMC delivers a hawkish hold and the Big Tech earnings underwhelm, US stocks could face a deeper correction. But if the tech titans demonstrate that the AI investment payoff is arriving, markets could stage a powerful relief rally.
