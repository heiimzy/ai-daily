⚡ July 25 Financial Roundup: KOSPI Suffers Worst Day of 2026, China's CXMT Readies $8.6B Mega IPO, Oil Retreats on Peace Talk Hopes as European Stocks Rally

🇰🇷 KOSPI Crashes 5.72% in Worst Rout of 2026: Samsung Tumbles 7.59%, SK Hynix Sinks 8.34% as Foreign Investors Flee

South Korean stocks suffered their most brutal session of 2026 on Thursday, July 24, as the benchmark KOSPI Composite Index collapsed 406.27 points, or 5.72%, closing at 6,690.62. The index touched an intraday low of 6,650.41 and triggered a five-minute suspension of program selling in early trade as the selloff accelerated.

Foreign and institutional investors dumped a combined net 5.2 trillion won (approximately $3.55 billion) worth of shares, while retail investors absorbed 5.18 trillion won in a classic "buying the dip" pattern. Losers overwhelmed gainers by 582 to 301, with turnover surging to 30.9 trillion won, the highest in months.

Semiconductor heavyweights led the carnage:

  • Samsung Electronics plunged 7.59% to 249,500 won — its steepest single-day drop in nearly two years
  • SK Hynix cratered 8.34% to 1,759,000 won
  • Hyundai Motor slid 7.18%
  • KB Financial Group lost 2.72%
  • HD Hyundai Heavy Industries fell 2.51%

Biotech stocks provided the rare bright spot. Samsung Biologics surged 10.08% on strong earnings, and Celltrion advanced 3.14%. The Korean won edged marginally weaker to 1,466.6 against the US dollar, while the three-year Treasury yield rose 4.2 basis points to 3.959% and the five-year yield added 5 basis points to 4.214%.

Analysts attributed the rout to three compounding factors: President Trump's renewed threat of a "massive attack" on Iran, deepening fears over Strait of Hormuz supply disruptions; a 4.25% plunge in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index that triggered a global chip stock selloff; and growing anxiety that the semiconductor supercycle may have peaked — a concern that hits Korea's export-driven economy particularly hard. The Korea Development Institute (KDI) warned in a report released the same day that second-half export growth could face headwinds from moderating chip demand.

🇨🇳 China Stocks Slide 1.6% as CXMT's Record $8.6B IPO Beckons — Second-Largest in A-Share History

Chinese mainland stocks also felt the weight of the global tech selloff on Friday. The Shanghai Composite Index fell 1.6% to close at 3,814.20, paring its weekly gain to 1.3%. The Shenzhen Component Index dropped 2.5%, and the tech-heavy ChiNext board lost 2.6%.

Tech shares were the biggest drag. Zhongji Innolight, the world's largest producer of optical transceivers used in AI data centers, fell 2.4%; chipmaker Cambricon lost 1.9%; and Biwin Storage Technology plunged 5.6%. Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index also slipped nearly 1% on Friday, though it still managed a 1.6% weekly gain.

All eyes were on Monday's blockbuster IPO of ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), China's largest DRAM manufacturer, which will debut on Shanghai's STAR Market on July 27. The $8.6 billion (62 billion yuan) offering is the second-largest IPO in mainland Chinese history, trailing only Semiconductor Manufacturing International Corporation's (SMIC) 53.2 billion yuan IPO in 2020. Market speculation suggests CXMT could surpass Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC) to become the most valuable company on the mainland once trading begins.

Analysts noted a potential "suction effect" — some investors sold positions on Friday to free up cash for CXMT subscription participation, exacerbating the day's selling pressure.

Elsewhere in Asia, Japan's Nikkei 225 bucked the regional trend, rising 0.73% to close at 64,611.15 and posting a modest weekly gain. Taiwan's TAIEX also managed a positive week.

🛢️ Brent Retreats From Above $100 as China Pushes Pakistan-Mediated US-Iran Peace Talks

Oil prices pulled back sharply on Friday but still posted nearly 10% gains for the week. Brent crude settled at $96.78 per barrel, down $3.91 or 3.88%, while WTI crude finished at $89.31, down $2.88 or 3.12%.

The retreat was driven by diplomatic developments — a Reuters exclusive report revealed that China has been pushing Pakistan to explore ways to restart stalled peace negotiations between the United States and Iran. "China is dissatisfied, judging that Iran's attacks on Gulf states and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz damage its interests," a Pakistani government official told Reuters. The news triggered profit-taking after five consecutive sessions of gains.

Despite Friday's pullback, Brent still gained approximately 9.85% for the week, and WTI added 9.21%, marking the fourth consecutive weekly advance. On Thursday, Brent had briefly breached $100 for the first time since May, touching an intraday high of $102, after Houthi forces attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea while Iran effectively sealed the Strait of Hormuz.

Kpler ship-tracking data showed daily tanker transits through the Strait of Hormuz averaged just three over the past three days, a fraction of normal levels. The CPC Black Sea export terminal in Kazakhstan remained shut after suspected Ukrainian drone attacks, adding further supply-side uncertainty. JPMorgan analysts warned that each additional month of supply disruption adds approximately $7-8 per barrel to Brent, with three months of disruption potentially pushing monthly average prices to $114.

🏛️ US 10-Year Yield Eases to 4.67%, September Fed Rate Hike Odds Near 80%

US Treasury yields edged lower on Friday, with the benchmark 10-year note yielding 4.67%, pausing after a four-session rally that had pushed it to 4.71% on Thursday — the highest since January 2025. The 30-year bond yield stood at 5.16%, still near 19-year highs.

Soaring oil prices are reshaping the rate outlook. According to the CME FedWatch Tool, markets are now pricing approximately a 35% probability of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the July 28-29 FOMC meeting, while odds for a September hike have surged to nearly 80% — up sharply from just 12% a week ago.

Friday's flash S&P Global US PMI data painted a complex picture: the services sector PMI accelerated to 56.2 (an 8-month high), while manufacturing PMI slowed to 49.5 (the lowest since March). Input price inflation rose to its highest since January 2025. This "hot services, cold manufacturing, sticky inflation" combination is being described by economists as a "mildly stagflationary" scenario — precisely the Federal Reserve's worst nightmare.

Coinbase Institutional's latest "Charting Crypto Q3 2026" report described the Fed's stance as "hawkish and mildly stagflationary," warning that higher rates and a stronger dollar will constrain risk asset liquidity.

🇪🇺 European Stocks Rally Against the Grain: Stoxx 600 Gains 0.82%, DAX Returns Above 25,000

European equities were a rare bright spot on Friday, rallying broadly despite the global risk-off mood. The pan-European Stoxx 600 index rose 0.82% to close at 644.51 points.

All major bourses finished in the green: the UK's FTSE 100 gained 0.91% to 10,736.23; Germany's DAX 40 climbed 1.36% to 25,099.00, reclaiming the psychologically important 25,000 level; France's CAC 40 added 0.88% to 8,372.28; Italy's FTSE MIB 30 rose 0.95% to 51,802.23; and Spain's IBEX 35 advanced 1.65% to 19,585.40.

The European Central Bank held its key interest rate at 2.25% at Thursday's meeting, as widely expected. President Christine Lagarde signaled a potential September rate hike, with markets now pricing a 73% probability. Eurozone July composite PMI came in at 51.0, an 11-month high, but the headline masked deep internal divergence — Germany's economy is nearly stagnating, while France remains in contraction territory.

₿ Bitcoin Hovers Near $64,000 as ETF Outflows Pressure Crypto Markets

Crypto markets extended their pullback on Friday. Bitcoin was trading near $64,000, down approximately 1.65% over 24 hours at $63,877. Ethereum slipped 1.48% to $1,854. Total global crypto market capitalization edged down 1.24% to $2.19 trillion.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $240 million in net outflows on July 24, led by BlackRock's IBIT at $212 million. Ethereum spot ETFs also saw their five-day inflow streak end with $70.6 million in net outflows. In the derivatives market, $87 million in Bitcoin long positions were liquidated on July 24, contributing to $312 million in total crypto leveraged position liquidations.

The mining sector added to the bearish narrative — Poolin Technologies, a major Bitcoin mining pool, filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection on July 24, highlighting the operational and financial stress facing miners amid rising costs and regulatory scrutiny. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index fell to 35, back in "fear" territory.

Analysts noted that Bitcoin's $63,700-64,000 support zone remains intact for now, though a break below could open the door to $62,000. Standard Chartered reiterated its year-end $100,000 price target, while Coinbase Institutional warned that regulatory uncertainty and hawkish Fed policy could continue to weigh on risk assets in the near term.
