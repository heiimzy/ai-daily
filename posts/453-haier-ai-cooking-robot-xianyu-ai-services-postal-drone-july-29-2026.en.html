⚡ July 29 Tech Roundup: Haier Launches Fully Autonomous AI Cooking Robot, Xianyu AI Services Surge 157%, China Post Pushes AI Drones, Amazon Redesigns Alexa to Cut Costs

1. Haier Unveils World's First Fully Autonomous AI Cooking Robot CR3

On the afternoon of July 29, Haier Smart Home signed a strategic partnership agreement with Xionglu Robotics and jointly launched the CR3 — the world's first fully autonomous AI cooking robot for home use, marking Haier's entry into the home cooking robot market.

The CR3, developed over one year, leverages Xionglu Robotics' core advantages in precision temperature control, full-chain cooking data modeling, and a vast library of standardized recipes to deliver completely unattended intelligent cooking. Users simply place ingredients into the robot, which autonomously handles the entire cooking process — from ignition and temperature control to stirring, seasoning, and serving.

As a key component of Haier's home robot ecosystem, the CR3 deeply integrates with Haier's smart home platform, bridging the gap between living room and kitchen to create an integrated future living space. Haier previously released cleaning, companionship, and household robots at AWE in March, and the cooking robot marks another step toward turning the vision of a "chore-free home" into reality.

Technical Breakthroughs in AI Cooking Robotics

Xionglu Robotics' core technologies include:

  • Precision temperature control — AI vision-based ingredient recognition with dynamic heat adjustment, achieving temperature curves comparable to professional chefs
  • Full-chain cooking data modeling — Trained on tens of thousands of hours of cooking data, covering stir-frying, braising, steaming, pan-frying, and deep-frying
  • Extensive standardized recipe library — Over 3,000 built-in standardized recipes with OTA cloud updates and user-customizable options via mobile app
  • Multi-sensor fusion — Integrated temperature sensors, infrared detection, and weight sensors for real-time cooking monitoring

Analysts project strong market potential for AI cooking robots. China's home cooking robot market reached approximately 8 billion RMB in 2025 and is expected to exceed 30 billion RMB by 2028. Haier's entry will accelerate the industry transition from semi-automated assisted cooking to fully autonomous cooking.

2. Xianyu AI Services Explode: Nearly 10 Million Orders, Up 157% YoY

On July 29, Alibaba-owned online marketplace Xianyu released its H1 2026 AI services transaction data, revealing a consumer AI adoption revolution in progress.

Key findings include:

  • 9.816 million AI service orders in H1 2026, up 157% year-over-year
  • Nearly 5 million unique buyers purchased AI services on Xianyu in H1, up 98% YoY
  • That's an average of over 54,000 AI service transactions per day

3. China's State Post Bureau Unveils AI + Logistics Blueprint

On July 29, China's State Post Bureau held a press conference for its "15th Five-Year Plan" for the postal industry, explicitly prioritizing "AI + Postal Express" as a focus area for the next five years, with low-altitude delivery logistics and smart postal infrastructure set to accelerate.

The plan outlines several concrete measures: expanding drone delivery for high-timeliness and high-value needs as well as for mountainous, island, and plateau regions; encouraging the construction of drone landing platforms at delivery stations and village service points; and promoting large-scale deployment of autonomous delivery vehicles on urban streets.

AI-powered logistics are already gaining traction: SF Express, JD Logistics, and ZTO are operating regular drone delivery routes in select cities; Cainiao's autonomous vehicles provide last-mile delivery services at over 200 universities and 100 communities nationwide; and China Post has piloted drone delivery in mountainous areas of Zhejiang and Sichuan, reducing a 3-hour mountain transport route to just 20 minutes.

China's low-altitude logistics market was valued at approximately 12 billion RMB in 2025 and is projected to exceed 80 billion RMB by 2028, driven by maturing technologies in AI visual recognition, path planning, and autonomous obstacle avoidance.

4. Amazon Redesigns Alexa to Reduce Anthropic Claude Dependence, Cut Costs

Internal Amazon documents obtained by Business Insider reveal that the company has redesigned its Alexa voice assistant to reduce reliance on Anthropic's Claude models, in a sweeping effort to lower AI operational costs.

The documents show a three-pronged cost-reduction strategy:

  • Shifting to in-house models — Routing more Alexa requests to Amazon's own AI models, reserving Claude only for complex reasoning scenarios
  • Reducing unnecessary inference calls — Implementing caching to avoid redundant computation, migrating Alexa "Experts" modules from Claude Sonnet to Amazon's models
  • Improving GPU utilization — Optimizing compute scheduling with a target of quadrupling tasks per compute unit

Internal projections showed that without intervention, Alexa+'s AI inference costs could balloon to approximately $1.7 billion by 2026 — nearly triple the previous year's spend. The numbers forced Amazon to take urgent action.

The episode highlights a shifting focus in the AI industry: as frontier model capabilities mature, the competitive battleground is moving from "building smarter AI" to "running AI more economically." From Amazon to small AI startups, controlling inference costs has become a central challenge for all players.

Industry analysts note that AI is transitioning from an "arms race" phase to a "commercial deployment" phase. Companies that cannot effectively control inference costs — even with the most advanced models — will struggle to achieve commercial success in consumer markets. Amazon's Alexa cost-control case is set to become a reference template for the entire industry.

5. The Bigger Picture: AI Moves from Labs into Every Home

These four stories from July 29 collectively point to a clear trend: AI is moving from research laboratories into homes and daily life, transforming from a technical concept into everyday consumption.

Haier's AI cooking robot represents the deep integration of AI with physical consumer electronics, making AI tangible and visible. Xianyu's nearly 10 million AI service orders demonstrate how AI tools have penetrated ordinary people's work, creation, and entertainment. The State Post Bureau's policy push signals that AI infrastructure is rapidly scaling. And Amazon's AI cost-control case foreshadows the next major battleground in AI commercialization: cost efficiency.

In 2026, AI is no longer the exclusive domain of a few technical elites. It is becoming infrastructure as essential as electricity and water, seamlessly integrated into everyone's daily life.
