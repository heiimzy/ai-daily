⚡ July 29 Financial Markets Live: Trump Threatens to "Hit Iran Hard" — Oil Surges 6% to $89.61; Iran Formally Rejects Oman's Hormuz Split Proposal; KOSPI Crashes Another 6% as SK Hynix Sinks 20% on Earnings Miss; FTSE 100 Hits All-Time High; FOMC Decision Hour Approaches

1. Oil Surges 6% as Trump Vows to "Hit Iran Hard"

International crude oil prices surged more than 6% in Asian and early European trading on Wednesday, after President Donald Trump told Fox News he would "hit Iran hard" in retaliation for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps' ballistic missile attack on U.S. forces stationed in the Middle East.

Trump stated Iran "is going to get a beating" and declared: "We'll be hitting them hard."

By 11:00 GMT, Brent crude futures had jumped 6.6% to $89.61 per barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) futures advanced 6.4% to $84.31. The U.S. Central Command confirmed that IRGC forces launched "multiple ballistic missiles in an attempted surprise attack on U.S. forces based in the Middle East," adding that all missiles were successfully intercepted.

The rally reverses three consecutive days of declines — Brent briefly touched $102/barrel last week before crashing to around $82 as a brief U.S.-Iran truce took hold. Oil has swung more than $20 in just seven days, with market sentiment oscillating wildly with every geopolitical headline.

2. Iran Formally Rejects Oman's 50-50 Hormuz Split — Strait Remains Closed

Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi told state broadcaster IRIB on Tuesday that Tehran has rejected Oman's proposal to divide the Strait of Hormuz shipping lanes 50-50 between the two countries.

Under the Omani proposal — backed by Gulf states — shipping lanes would be split north-south and managed jointly by Iran and Oman, with a "voluntary fee" mechanism for navigational services. Iran countered with a more aggressive demand:

  • The inbound lane (into the Persian Gulf): must be entirely controlled by Iran
  • The outbound lane: Iran demands partial control
  • If Oman does not accept, the strait remains closed, and Iran is "prepared for the resumption of war"

Gharibabadi stressed: "Our proposal is that one shipping lane through the strait must be entirely under Iran's control, with part of the other lane also under Iranian control. This is our definitive position, and Iran will accept no other formula."

The Wall Street Journal reported that mediating countries were enraged by Iran's rejection. A U.S. official said "the strait is an international waterway, and Iran cannot control or restrict passage through it," calling Iran's demands "outrageous and overreaching." Analysts note that handing Iran control of the Strait of Hormuz and allowing it to charge tolls is a "red line" Washington cannot accept.

UBS analyst Giovanni Staunovo said: "Renewed military strikes in the Middle East and Iranian officials reiterating that they want to control shipping activity through the Strait of Hormuz... are lifting oil prices again." DBS Bank's Suvro Sarkar expects "Brent oil prices will continue to whipsaw in the $80-$100 per barrel range."

3. KOSPI Crashes Another 6%: SK Hynix Plunges 20% — Halved from Peak in One Month

South Korea's KOSPI index suffered another brutal session on Wednesday, closing down 6.0% at 5,663.24, extending Tuesday's near-11% collapse. The two-day rout has wiped nearly 17% off the benchmark, which is now down approximately 42% from its June peak.

SK Hynix was the day's biggest drag, crashing nearly 20% after reporting record Q2 earnings that still fell short of the market's inflated expectations:

  • Revenue: 79.32 trillion won (+257% YoY), below the 84T won consensus
  • Operating profit: 60.54T won (+557% YoY), below the 64.68T won estimate
  • Net profit: 93.92T won (+1,242.5% YoY), well above expectations
  • Operating margin: 76.3% — a record high, surpassing Nvidia (65.6%) and TSMC (60.3%)

While SK Hynix has begun mass HBM4 shipments and delivered HBM4E samples, delays in some advanced product shipments limited DRAM price gains. The market's message is clear: after a two-year AI rally, investors are no longer rewarding AI exposure by default — they demand proof of earnings conversion, margin resilience, and demand durability. SK Hynix has now lost over 50% of its value since hitting an all-time high just one month ago.

Samsung Electronics fell another ~12% as it awaits its own earnings. Japanese chip stocks also suffered: Kioxia plunged 13%, Tokyo Electron fell 12%, and the Nikkei 225 closed down 1.5% at 61,434.

4. FTSE 100 Hits All-Time High — Energy Stocks Defy the Tech Rout

In stark contrast to the global tech bloodbath, London's FTSE 100 index hit an all-time high of 10,951.06 points on Wednesday, surpassing the previous record of 10,934.94 set in late February. By afternoon trading, the index had eased to around 10,893, still up 0.2% on the day.

The FTSE 100's outperformance stems from two factors: soaring energy giants and minimal tech exposure. Shell and BP surged alongside oil prices, while the index's low weighting in technology shielded it from the global AI-driven selloff. Strong earnings from companies like Coca-Cola (which beat Q2 estimates and raised full-year guidance) also supported the index.

Other European markets lagged: France's CAC 40 fell 0.5%, Germany's DAX edged up 0.2%, and Italy's FTSE MIB declined. The pan-European Stoxx 600 index slipped 0.34% as most sectors retreated.

5. FOMC Decision Countdown: The Most Uncertain Fed Meeting in Years

The Federal Open Market Committee will announce its interest rate decision at 2:00 PM ET (18:00 GMT) Wednesday, followed by Chairman Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET. This is Warsh's second FOMC meeting as Chair and the first real test of his deliberate removal of forward guidance.

Market expectations:

  • CME FedWatch: ~70% probability of hold, ~30% probability of a 25bp hike
  • Polymarket: 26% probability of a hike
  • Economist consensus (FactSet): unanimous for hold at 3.50%-3.75%

Beneath the consensus surface lies historic uncertainty. The probability of a hike has swung from 10.7% on July 15 to 46.5% intraweek as oil briefly topped $100, then back to 26-34%. At least two FOMC members — Cleveland Fed President Hammack and Dallas Fed President Logan — are expected to dissent in favor of an immediate hike.

Warsh's deliberate abandonment of the Fed's traditional forward guidance has fundamentally changed how markets interpret the FOMC. Under Powell, the Fed pre-negotiated meeting outcomes through speeches and signals. Under Warsh, uncertainty itself does the tightening work — when investors can't predict the outcome, risk premia rise and financial conditions automatically tighten.

The case for holding: June CPI came in softer than expected, payroll growth moderated to just 57,000, geopolitical uncertainty is extreme, and Warsh has only just begun his tenure. The case for hiking: oil has rebounded sharply, tariff risks persist as an inflationary headwind, financial conditions are not notably tight, and Warsh may want to establish early that "uncertainty is now part of the reaction function."

After the FOMC decision, Microsoft and Meta report earnings after the bell. Their combined 2026 AI capital budgets exceed $300 billion, and investors will scrutinize whether AI infrastructure spending is translating into revenue growth.

6. Global Markets Roundup

Asia-Pacific: Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index bucked the trend, closing up 2.0% at 25,808. Shanghai Composite edged up 0.4% to 3,828. Japan's Nikkei fell 1.5%, and Taiwan's TAIEX dropped nearly 4%.

FX: The U.S. dollar was broadly flat. EUR/USD traded at 1.1388, GBP/USD at 1.3292. The yen strengthened slightly to 163.66 per dollar as markets assessed the BoJ's rate path.

Commodities: Gold traded around $4,100/oz, supported by safe-haven demand. Copper edged up 0.86%.

Bonds: The 10-year U.S. Treasury yield edged up to 4.614%, with the 2-year at 4.291% and the 30-year flat at 5.1%. Markets are pricing a 76% probability of a September hike.

Outlook: The next 48 hours may be the most information-dense period of 2026 — FOMC decision + Warsh press conference tonight, Microsoft/Meta earnings after the close, Apple/Amazon earnings on Thursday, and U.S. Q2 GDP and PCE data on Friday. Any surprise in any of these events could trigger outsized market moves in a compressed time window.
