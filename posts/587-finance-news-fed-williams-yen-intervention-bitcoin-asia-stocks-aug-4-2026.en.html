⚡ Aug 4 Finance Update: Fed's Williams Flags Potential Rate Hikes, US-Japan Yen Intervention Rattles Carry Trades, Bitcoin Nears $64K, A-Shares Rotate Into AI

After a powerful Wall Street rally that sent the Dow to a record closing high on Monday, global markets on Tuesday (August 4) entered a phase of reading the tea leaves — balancing geopolitical de-escalation against monetary-policy uncertainty. Fed officials sent fresh signals on the inflation outlook, a rare US-Japan joint intervention around the yen put over $1 trillion of carry trades on edge, Asian equities firmed in early trade while Chinese A-shares underwent a violent style rotation, and bitcoin seesawed between risk-off and risk-on. Here is this round's finance roundup.

1. Fed's Williams: Inflation Should Ease in H2, But the Fed Will Hike If It Doesn't

In a Reuters interview (Aug 3), New York Fed President John Williams said he remains optimistic that inflation is on track to ease gradually toward the 2% target, but warned the central bank "would absolutely not hesitate" to respond with rate hikes if price pressures fail to cool. Williams expects inflation to come down in the second half of this year and fall further next year, citing the Fed's preferred gauge — June PCE slipping 0.1% month-on-month — as evidence that prices are moving in the right direction. He reiterated that the current policy stance is "well positioned."

Last week the FOMC left the federal funds target range unchanged at 3.50%-3.75% — the fifth consecutive hold of 2026 — but three regional presidents (Cleveland's Hammack, Minneapolis' Kashkari and Dallas' Logan) dissented in favor of a 25-basis-point hike, the first time since 2016 that three members voted against the majority. Futures traders have already priced in a decent chance of a hike by year-end, and new Chair Kevin Warsh has abandoned "forward guidance," adding to uncertainty around the policy path. Most analysts see September as the key window for whether the Fed can keep holding.

2. US-Japan Yen Intervention: Bessent Sold Euros to Buy Yen and Pressured the Fed to Expand FIMA

According to Sina Finance / Wall Street CN (Aug 4), to stop the yen from sliding to near 164 — its weakest level in about 40 years (since 1986) — the US and Japan intervened jointly last Friday. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent took part in an unusual way, selling euros and buying yen rather than selling dollars, sidestepping the political sensitivity of pushing the greenback down, and pulling the yen back to around 157 by Monday afternoon. Japanese Finance Minister Satsuki Katayama confirmed that Tokyo "won't hesitate" to intervene again with Washington.

Even more striking, Bessent publicly called on the Fed to expand the "Foreign and International Monetary Authorities" (FIMA) repo facility, so Japan could raise dollars for future intervention without selling US Treasuries. Fed watchers called a Treasury secretary openly pressing the Fed on its own tooling "extremely unusual"; former senior Treasury official Mark Sobel said it was unprecedented in his tenure. Yet the facility's $60 billion daily per-counterparty cap is only slightly above the size of Japan's single-day intervention last week, and the tool has sat largely idle, raising questions about its usefulness for sustained large-scale intervention.

Analysts broadly warn that without a genuine shift in Bank of Japan monetary policy, the intervention is unlikely to hold. More alarming, HSBC estimates the total yen carry trade — borrowing cheap yen to buy higher-yielding assets such as US tech stocks — exceeds $1 trillion; a sharp yen rally triggering a systemic unwind would hit global risk assets. History is recent: the yen's sudden surge in 2024 forced investors to cover carry positions and roiled global markets. Markets have so far stayed calm, but this tail risk remains squarely on the table.

3. Asian Stocks Firm in Early Tuesday Trade, Brent Steadies Near $84.65; A-Shares Rotate Into AI, Out of Banks

According to Al Jazeera (data as of 07:00 GMT), after Monday's rally in US indices, Asian equities firmed in early Tuesday trading — South Korea's KOSPI rebounded 1.6% (following a week of violent swings driven by AI memory-chip demand), Japan's Nikkei 225 edged up 0.3%, while only Hong Kong's Hang Seng dipped 0.6%. On commodities, Brent crude steadied after Monday's roughly 5% slump, standing at $84.65 per barrel as of 07:05 GMT, up more than 1% from Monday's close. Markets are still weighing the outlook for Strait of Hormuz transit talks — the strait carried roughly a fifth of global oil supply before the conflict.

On the mainland, according to FX168 (Aug 4), A-shares staged a dramatic "fire and ice" style rotation: tech and growth sectors led by AI compute, semiconductors and components surged, lifting the ChiNext index more than 4.2% in early trade, with communications-equipment and semiconductor sectors seeing main capital net inflows of RMB 13.16 billion and RMB 33.48 billion respectively, and optical modules, CPO and AI servers triggering limit-ups; meanwhile high-dividend defensive names such as banks and coal were hammered, with the big four banks down more than 3% each. Capital is flowing from low-beta bank stocks into high-odds AI tech names, driven by the overseas spillover of Tuesday's Wall Street tech surge and the earnings confirmation of North American cloud vendors' upwardly revised capex guidance.

4. Bitcoin Nears $64,000: Reclaims $63K in Asian Hours, Strategy Sells Another 1,638 BTC

According to CoinDesk (published Aug 4, 05:21; updated 05:53), bitcoin rebounded toward $64,000 in early Asian trading, up nearly 2% on the day and reclaiming the $63,000 level it lost on Monday, after dipping to about $62,250 intraday before a steady bid carried it back above roughly $64,100. Ether hovered near $1,865, marginally higher but still the only major large token down on the week; XRP traded near $1.08 (up 2% on the week), while BNB led majors with a weekly gain of nearly 5%.

Separately, bitcoin treasury firm Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) disclosed it sold 1,638 bitcoin for about $105 million between July 27 and Aug 2 — its third sale of 2026 — at an average price of $63,957, well below its $75,419 average cost basis, with proceeds going to preferred dividends and share buybacks. Meanwhile, the "Coldcard sweep" tied to a wallet-firmware exploit continues: the fourth wave of transfers moved roughly 449 bitcoin from more than 700 addresses this week. Despite these headwinds, bitcoin has shown resilience, and the market is watching whether it can hold above $63,000 ahead of Friday's US non-farm payrolls report for directional guidance.
