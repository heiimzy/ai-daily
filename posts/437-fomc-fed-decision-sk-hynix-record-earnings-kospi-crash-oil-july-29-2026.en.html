⚡ FOMC Decision Day: The Ultimate Showdown for Global Markets

1. Fed Decision Today: 34% Hike Probability — Biggest Policy Divide Since 2024

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) delivers its rate decision at 2:00 PM ET today (July 29), followed by Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30 PM ET. The federal funds rate currently sits at 3.50%–3.75%, where it has remained for four consecutive meetings.

According to CME FedWatch, markets assign roughly a 34%–38% probability of a 25-basis-point hike — the widest policy divergence since September 2024. Citadel Securities has publicly positioned for a surprise hike, arguing it would demonstrate the Fed's independence. However, 104 of 104 economists surveyed by FactSet expect a hold. Polymarket traders price 26% odds of a July move, rising to 43% by September.

The backdrop is unusually complex. Brent crude touched $100 last week on Middle East tensions before crashing back below $84. June CPI cooled to 3.5%, but core PCE remains elevated near 3.3%. Chair Warsh told Congress he has "zero tolerance" for high inflation but refuses to provide forward guidance — amplifying uncertainty around any single data point.

Allianz Global Investors expects 50bp of total rate hikes by year-end. Danske Bank flags December and March 2027 as the most likely window. Kalshi traders price a 68% probability of at least one hike in 2026.

2. SK Hynix Q2 Earnings: Record 64 Trillion Won Operating Profit Expected

South Korean memory chip giant SK Hynix (000660.KS / SKHY.NASDAQ) reports Q2 2026 results today at 9:00 AM KST. Consensus from 14 brokerages compiled by Yonhap Infomax projects revenue of 84.1 trillion won (~$57.6B) and operating profit of 64.1 trillion won (~$43.7B) — a staggering ~600% year-over-year surge and 70% quarter-over-quarter growth.

The headline metric is operating margin. Analysts expect 75%–77%, up from Q1's 72% and surpassing TSMC (60.3%) for the third consecutive quarter. This means SK Hynix converts roughly 75 out of every 100 won of revenue into operating profit — an extraordinary figure for a manufacturing company.

The core driver remains high-bandwidth memory (HBM) dominance. SK Hynix holds 58%–62% of the global HBM market as Nvidia's primary HBM3E/HBM4 supplier. HBM4 is expected to begin volume production in Q3 2026. Enterprise SSD demand from North American hyperscalers provides a second growth engine.

If results meet expectations, SK Hynix's first-half operating profit will exceed 100 trillion won — more than double its full-year 2025 profit of 47.2 trillion won. Combined with Samsung Electronics' projected Q2 operating profit of 89.4 trillion won, the two Korean chip giants would together post over 150 trillion won in quarterly operating profit.

Markets will focus on management guidance: HBM4 qualification progress, 2027 demand visibility, long-term agreement (LTA) renewals, and capex plans. SK Hynix ADRs have corrected ~30% from their peak, making the earnings call a crucial inflection point for the stock.

3. KOSPI Crashes 10.84%: Worst Monthly Decline Since 2008 Financial Crisis

South Korea's KOSPI index collapsed 732.09 points (-10.84%) on July 28 to close at 6,023.66, triggering the year's 8th circuit breaker (20-minute marketwide halt). Samsung Electronics plunged 13.39% to 220,000 won; SK Hynix crashed 14.65% to 1.55 million won. Foreign investors net sold 1.0653 trillion won ($724M) in a single session.

July's 28.9% monthly decline already exceeds the 23.1% record set during the 2008 Global Financial Crisis and approaches Black Monday territory (27.2% in October 1987). From its June all-time high of 9,385.59, the KOSPI has lost approximately 34% in just 26 trading sessions.

Three forces drove the meltdown:

  • China chip competition: Chinese firm Aishengna began mass production of immersion DUV lithography tools. CXMT's 466% surge on its Shanghai debut raised fears of global DRAM oversupply.
  • AI circular financing fears: Nvidia reportedly involved in $750B in circular financing deals. Its 5-year CDS premium surged from 0.35% to 0.82%.
  • Leveraged ETF clampdown: Korea's FSC is imposing deposit requirements on single-stock ETFs starting July 31, following 18 such products listed since late May.

Today's SK Hynix earnings are the pivotal catalyst. A beat with strong guidance could trigger a relief rally; cautious commentary risks pushing the KOSPI below the 6,000 psychological level.

4. Brent Crude Below $84: Geopolitical Premium Evaporates

Oil prices continued their dramatic slide. Brent crude closed at $83.63/barrel on July 28, down 5.36% on the day and roughly 18% from the July 24 peak of $102. WTI fell to $78.54. This marks Brent's sharpest quarterly decline since April 2020.

The collapse is driven by rapid de-escalation in US-Iran tensions. Trump suspended airstrikes over the weekend, and diplomatic channels reopened. Iran's Foreign Minister held talks with Saudi and Omani counterparts on Strait of Hormuz security. Oman has proposed a joint management mechanism. Qatar declared full maritime navigation resumption.

However, supply risks persist. Saudi Arabia intercepted drones targeting oil facilities. Houthi rebels claimed attacks on Saudi's East-West pipeline. Analysts warn that a sustained break below $80 could trigger an emergency OPEC+ meeting on production cuts.

5. Super Week Showdown: Meta and Microsoft Earnings Tonight

This week is the most consequential earnings period of 2026. Following the Fed decision, Meta Platforms and Microsoft report after the US close today. Apple and Amazon follow on July 30.

The central question: Are AI capex investments generating returns? Microsoft is expected to report revenue of $87.67B (+14.7% YoY) with a $37B annualized AI revenue run rate, but Q4 capex alone exceeds $40B — part of a $190B full-year forecast. Meta's Q2 revenue is estimated at ~$60B, with 2026 capex raised to $125–$145B.

Bank of America's Chief Investment Strategist Marta Norton warns "the market is going to punish" Magnificent Seven companies reporting this week, as AI spending compresses free cash flow and investors shift from "AI euphoria" to "ROI verification."

S&P 500 bullish sentiment has collapsed from 44.9% to 29.6% in a single week — the weakest since September 2025. A hawkish Fed surprise combined with disappointing Meta/MSFT guidance could trigger compounding selling pressure in what analysts describe as the single most consequential trading session of 2026.
