China Rolls Out Quantum Tech Across Its Power Grid to Banish Blackouts

After a successful trial at a substation, China plans to expand quantum technologies — including precision sensors and computer simulations — across its national power grid to help prevent blackouts and improve reliability (South China Morning Post, by Dannie Peng in Beijing, published 3:00pm, Aug 1, 2026).

During the 18-month trial in Hefei, Anhui province, quantum technology was used to rapidly detect and diagnose equipment defects and faults in the power system, according to a report published Wednesday by People's Daily. Quantum tech harnesses the physics of individual atoms, which exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured — a phenomenon known as "superposition" — enabling novel ways to measure, compute and communicate data. Though quantum applications are only beginning to transition from research labs to real engineering, this trial proved the technology works on a live grid.

"The operation has proved that quantum technology is not a 'showpiece,' but a 'hard support' for guaranteeing the power supply and promoting development." — Tian Teng, researcher, Anhui Electric Power Company

As early as 2023, State Grid Shandong used quantum-encrypted communication to safeguard its network. What stands out in the Hefei pilot is quantum sensing and simulation's ability to spot hidden faults far faster than manual inspection and conventional sensors. With extreme weather more frequent and electricity demand surging globally, positioning quantum tech as a national "blackout prevention" route marks a meaningful step in taking quantum out of the lab and into critical infrastructure.

OpenAI Prepares Astra: A New Model Series Built for Multi-Agent Collaboration

OpenAI is set to launch a new model series named "Astra," designed to enhance multi-agent task collaboration — enabling multiple agents to work together on complex, long-horizon problems in areas such as mathematics and project management (The News International, published 11:47am Pakistan time, Aug 1, 2026).

According to reports, OpenAI founder Sam Altman presented Astra to policymakers and regulators in Washington this week. The series is positioned as a new class of models following OpenAI's Sol, Terra and Luna releases, with the core pitch being agents that "divide work and collaborate over the long term" rather than single-model, single-turn responses. Astra is already in testing; whether it belongs to the GPT-6 lineage remains unclear.

  • Astra could become one of the first model families subject to the Trump administration's new CFT pre-release clearance framework, which would require AI models to be submitted to the federal government before public release;
  • OpenAI also plans to release a report showcasing how its AI can solve complex mathematical problems;
  • The multi-agent route is seen as a watershed in moving large models from "chat tools" toward autonomously executing complex tasks.

If CFT pre-clearance takes hold, US AI labs would face federal gatekeeping on release timing for the first time — with potential fallout for innovation speed and investor sentiment. Whether Astra extends OpenAI's dominance is the question markets are watching closely.

Barron's: Chinese AI Models Are Upending Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic

Barron's published an analysis arguing that Chinese AI models (from Huawei, Alibaba, Moonshot AI and others) are leveraging cost advantages to "upend" the market positions of Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic (Barron's, published ~2:45pm Beijing time / Aug 1).

The core thesis: Chinese models train and infer at significantly lower cost than US peers, and their open-source ecosystems are thriving, intensifying a global AI inference price war — OpenAI has already cut GPT-5.6 Luna prices by 80% in response to Chinese-model pressure. Analysts argue low-cost Chinese models are reshaping three fronts:

  • For Nvidia, if Chinese labs lean less on high-end GPUs or shift toward domestic compute, its demand curve and pricing power face pressure;
  • For closed-source giants like OpenAI and Anthropic, the free accessibility of Chinese open models compresses subscription pricing headroom;
  • For US cloud providers, competing Chinese model APIs squeeze margins on the "selling compute" business model.
When the technology gap shrinks to months, cost becomes the first variable that decides market share.

Despite compute constraints, China's AI industry has pivoted to a "low cost, high performance" route this year, with Kimi, DeepSeek and Qwen models spreading quickly among developers worldwide. Barron's take aligns with Bloomberg's earlier exclusive on Moonshot's Kimi K3 training on an Alibaba-provided Nvidia cluster: US-China AI competition has shifted from "benchmark battles" to "cost and ecosystem battles."

UBS Forecasts Samsung Overtaking SK Hynix in HBM Next Year on HBM4 Push

UBS released a fresh forecast: Samsung Electronics' HBM (high-bandwidth memory) market share will hit 41% in 2027 versus SK Hynix's 39%, with the HBM4 ramp and customer expansion pulling Samsung's overtake of the long-time leader forward (as covered by Korean Economic Daily and republished by bloomingbit, ~3:20pm KST, Aug 1).

UBS analysts say the reversal comes "earlier than expected" — the market previously assumed Samsung would not catch up until after 2028, but HBM4 mass-production timing and qualification for Nvidia's next-generation GPUs (Rubin and the later Feynman) have moved the window sharply forward. SK Hynix currently holds roughly half the HBM market and is Nvidia's main HBM3E supplier; Samsung is countering with heavier investment in next-gen packaging like hybrid bonding, plus custom HBM that integrates logic dies directly into the stack via 3D system-in-package designs.

  • UBS 2027 HBM share forecast: Samsung 41%, SK Hynix 39%, Micron ~20%;
  • Samsung plans to install 50 hybrid-bonding machines from Netherlands' BE Semiconductor starting late this year, with mass production in 2029-2030;
  • Nvidia's Feynman AI GPU, expected after Rubin Ultra, will rely on 3D stacking and custom HBM — aligning with Samsung's timeline.

AI memory is shifting from a commodity to contracted infrastructure; whoever locks in custom orders from big customers like Nvidia controls HBM pricing power for the next three years. The Samsung-SK Hynix "memory throne" battle will directly shape the cost of global AI compute.

Dalio Warns a 'Textbook' AI Asset Bubble Is Brewing as the 80-Year Supercycle Nears Its End

Bridgewater founder Ray Dalio warned that a "textbook" AI asset bubble is brewing, and that combined with internal debt crises and external geopolitical conflict, humanity is in the twilight of an 80-year "big cycle" (republished by Sina Finance, published 2:16pm Beijing time, Aug 1).

In an episode of The Diary Of A CEO podcast aired July 30, host Steven Bartlett pressed Dalio — the macro investor who famously profited 9.5% during the 2008 crisis — through a 90-minute conversation on AI mania, asset allocation and geopolitics:

  • He agreed with Jeremy Grantham's call that this is the biggest investment bubble in US history — every new technology brings mania, and AI is like electrification in 1929 or the internet in 2000, but markets "don't pay attention to price";
  • Leverage inflating AI valuations makes a reckoning inevitable; at the tail of the 80-year cycle, ballooning debt, record inequality and intensifying geopolitical conflict are "resonating";
  • On allocation, he called cash "the worst long-term investment" and said he prefers gold bars over bitcoin as a safe haven;
  • AI's mass replacement of human cognition and labor will further tear the wealth gap.
"When everyone is crazy about a revolutionary technology, ask yourself: what price are you paying for it?" — Ray Dalio

Dalio's warning lands amid volatile AI stocks this week: Nvidia's market cap is oscillating around the $5 trillion mark, OpenAI and Anthropic valuations keep climbing, while macro debt and geopolitical risks churn beneath the surface. For investors, the gap between the "AI narrative" and "AI valuations" is shaping up as one of the biggest risks of the second half.
