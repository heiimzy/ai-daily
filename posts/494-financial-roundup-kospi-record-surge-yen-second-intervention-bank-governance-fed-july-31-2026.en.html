⚡ July 31 Global Finance: KOSPI Soars Record 18%, Suspected Second Yen Intervention, China's 22-Point Bank Governance Overhaul, Fed Holds 9-3

South Korea: KOSPI Closes Up 18% in Record Single-Day Surge

South Korea's KOSPI index surged 18% at Friday's close (17.9% per Yonhap), reversing a three-day slide and posting the biggest single-day gain in its history. Korean media reported the benchmark jumped roughly 1,000 points, with SK Hynix soaring nearly 30% to hit the daily price limit as the main driver. The Wall Street Journal's Chinese edition noted that authorities' efforts to calm market swings had been thwarted.

According to LSEG data, five of the KOSPI's ten biggest daily gains on record have occurred since March, while three of the past decade's four largest daily losses came within the last five weeks — a striking measure of the market's volatility. "The gamification of the stock market has led to sharp fluctuations, driven more by news flow and fads than by fundamentals," said Peter S. Kim, global strategist at KB Financial Group. The market's rapid boom-and-bust has earned it the nickname "Squid Game market." BNP Paribas analyst William Bratton noted that Korea sits at the center of the debate over the sustainability of the AI-driven tech supercycle.

Yen: Suspected Second Round of Intervention, USD/JPY Plunges ~150 Pips

At 17:08 Beijing time, flash reports showed the yen strengthening across the board on suspected second intervention by Japanese authorities: USD/JPY plunged about 150 pips, EUR/JPY about 130 pips, GBP/JPY about 200 pips, and CAD/JPY and AUD/JPY about 100 pips each. Earlier at 16:58, USD/JPY had already dropped sharply to touch the 159 level, down 0.31% on the day. This marks the second official-intervention signal after this morning's report of a joint US-Japan intervention that sent the yen up 3.3%.

Meanwhile, Bank of Japan Governor Kazuo Ueda delivered a hawkish press conference after the rate decision: the BOJ could accelerate rate hikes if it judges financial conditions too loose; the risk of returning to deflation has declined; the yen's exchange rate has a bigger impact on prices than before; and AI-related demand is very strong. Separately, the onshore yuan closed at 6.7476 per dollar at 16:30, up 88 pips from the previous session.

China: Four Regulators Unveil 22 Measures to Overhaul Financial Institution Governance

The National Financial Regulatory Administration, the People's Bank of China, the China Securities Regulatory Commission and the Ministry of Finance jointly issued the "Implementation Opinions on Improving the Governance of Financial Institutions," containing 22 measures. The document sets a 2029 target: basically forming a financial institution governance mechanism with clear authority boundaries, compatible incentives and constraints, strict risk management, and standardized efficient operations, with markedly stronger internal stability and risk-resistance of the financial system.

Key points include: tightening shareholder admission standards and building a "firewall" between industrial capital and financial capital, barring highly leveraged firms or those with serious misconduct from becoming major shareholders; prohibiting financial institutions from transferring benefits to shareholders and their related parties, with mechanisms to claw back improper shareholder gains and pursue post-hoc liability; penetrating supervision of equity and related-party transactions to prevent "sick" personnel from moving between institutions; early intervention in institutions with major governance defects and risks; performance assessments that balance returns and risk with long-cycle evaluation to curb short-term over-incentives; encouraging liability insurance for independent directors; and strengthening the connection between administrative enforcement and criminal justice to crack down on financial crime.

Federal Reserve: 9-3 Vote to Hold Rates, Hike Debate Moves to September

The Federal Reserve voted 9-3 to hold interest rates unchanged at its latest meeting. Chinese financial media reported that the three dissents signal building pressure to hike, while others asked why markets fell even though the Fed did not raise rates. Market reports indicate that record positions built to hedge against a hike were quickly unwound after the decision, adding to short-term volatility.

Asset manager Fidelity believes the Fed is more likely to delay the start of its hiking cycle until December, provided inflation and labor market data stay firm; however, economic data and geopolitical developments over the next two months could revive inflation risks, so a September hike cannot be ruled out. Fidelity added that the decision highlights the "Warsh era" policy style — with no clear policy framework or forward guidance, the Fed will rely more on real-time economic data and financial conditions.

Market Wrap: A-Shares Rebound to Close July, US Futures Diverge, Gold Pulls Back

Chinese A-shares rebounded to close out July: the Shanghai Composite rose 0.72% to 3,832.26, the Shenzhen Component gained 2.21% to 13,578.93, and the ChiNext added 3.06% to 3,343.96, with nearly 4,700 stocks advancing and turnover across Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing reaching 2.56 trillion yuan, up over 200 billion from the prior day. Despite the rebound, the ChiNext and STAR boards are still down more than 20% for the month. In Hong Kong, the Hang Seng edged up 0.1% to 25,884.43 and the Hang Seng Tech Index rose 0.53%; Hang Seng Indexes Company launched a new "Hang Seng Stock Connect AI Infrastructure Index" today.

US premarket trading was mixed: Apple fell 7% after its Q4 revenue guidance missed estimates, while Amazon jumped 11% on better-than-expected Q2 cloud revenue. Semiconductor names rallied — TSMC up over 4%, ARM up 4.8%, AMD up 4%, ASML up 3.2% — with memory stocks strong as SK Hynix rose over 6.5% premarket and Micron gained 3.35%. In commodities and crypto, spot gold fell more than 1% to $4,062.35 an ounce, while bitcoin held steady near $64,000.
