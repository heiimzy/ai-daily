⚡ July 25 International Politics Roundup: Trump Confirms Iran Talks as Tehran Gets "More Serious", US Intel Reveals Khamenei Pursues Nuclear Warhead, Senate Blocks War Powers Curb, UK Pulls Diplomats Qatar Halts Flights, China Water Cannons Philippine Vessels

1. Trump Confirms US-Iran Talks: "They're Getting More Serious"

President Donald Trump told reporters in the Oval Office on Friday that diplomatic talks between the US and Iran are "happening as we speak," and that Tehran is becoming "more and more serious" in negotiations. It marks the first time Trump has acknowledged active dialogue channels since hostilities reignited on July 8.

Asked whether he had made a decision on a "massive strike," Trump answered clearly: "No, I haven't. Look, we're talking to them right now. I think they're getting more and more serious as the days go by, for maybe the obvious reason." He simultaneously emphasized that US forces remain "locked and loaded and ready to go," but said "while we're talking, we'll see what comes of it."

Trump said Iran faces a choice between reaching a deal or facing a "much higher level" of strikes. He revealed that Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance are involved in the talks but added that he is "not in a hurry" to end the war and reiterated that Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon as a red line.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi responded by telling Iranian media that the Islamic Republic is "neither intimidated by threats nor will we yield to pressure," noting ongoing discussions with China and Russia.

2. Exclusive: US Intel Says New Supreme Leader Pursues Nuclear Warhead

The New York Times reported Friday evening, citing officials briefed on intelligence assessments, that US intelligence agencies believe Iran's new Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei is "far more interested" in acquiring nuclear weapons than his father was. Unlike the late Ayatollah Ali Khamenei, who issued a fatwa against nuclear weapons in the 1990s, his son is assessed to favor a miniaturized thermonuclear warhead capable of being mounted on a long-range missile.

The assessment is based on pre-war public statements and intercepted communications. Mojtaba was wounded in the February 28 Israeli strike on his father's compound and has not appeared publicly or spoken since. The report said he viewed the "bomb-in-a-box" concept evaluated late in the Biden administration as having limited value, instead seeking a deliverable warhead.

Meanwhile, the Wall Street Journal reported that Iran has moved centrifuges to the fortified underground complex known as "Pickaxe Mountain" — a deeper facility chosen after Iran assessed the effectiveness of US bunker-busters on the Fordow nuclear site. US intelligence warned that Iran has transferred ground forces to the site over fears of an Israeli commando raid.

"Mojtaba does not share his father's hesitation and seeks to obtain nuclear weapons" — US intelligence assessment via The New York Times

The White House convened senior officials Friday evening to discuss escalating the military campaign. The intelligence finding that the supreme leader is open to obtaining a nuclear bomb could provide additional justification for escalation.

3. Congress Divided: House Passes War Powers Resolution, Senate Blocks It

The US Congress showed deepening divisions over the Iran war on Friday. The House of Representatives voted 214-208 to pass a war powers resolution directing President Trump to cease unauthorized military hostilities against Iran. Four Republicans crossed party lines to support the measure: Reps. Tom Barrett (MI), Brian Fitzpatrick (PA), Warren Davidson (OH), and Thomas Massie (KY).

Just 87 minutes later, the Senate voted 49-47 to block an equivalent resolution sponsored by Sen. Chris Van Hollen (D-MD). The sole Republican crossing over was Sen. Susan Collins (R-ME). Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA) was the only Democrat to oppose it. Sens. Lisa Murkowski (R-AK) and Rand Paul (R-KY), who previously supported similar measures, did not vote.

The Senate procedural vote means the resolution cannot reach Trump's desk, rendering the House vote a political statement rather than an operational constraint. Van Hollen argued on the Senate floor: "President Trump said we won the war back in March. If we won the war in March, how is it that American service members are still being killed and gas prices are going through the roof again?"

4. Regional Fallout: UK Withdraws Diplomats, Qatar Suspends Flights

As the US-Iran conflict intensifies, the United Kingdom announced it had temporarily withdrawn all remaining diplomatic staff from Iran. The Foreign Office said operations would continue remotely, advising against all travel to Iran and urging British nationals already there to "carefully consider the risks."

Qatar Airways announced temporary flight suspensions to Bahrain, Kuwait, and Erbil (capital of Iraq's Kurdistan region). Flights to Bahrain and Erbil are suspended through July 25, and services to Kuwait remain suspended until July 31, reflecting the deteriorating security environment across the Gulf region.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres told the Security Council that the Middle East was "teetering on the edge of the unimaginable," warning that "the situation is getting out of control — one crisis feeds another, one escalation triggers the next." UN human rights chief Volker Türk called for urgent action to assist approximately 6,000 seafarers stranded on about 400 vessels around the Strait of Hormuz.

5. 13th Consecutive Night of Strikes as Iran Retaliates Against Gulf Bases

The US military completed its 13th consecutive night of strikes against Iran, with CENTCOM stating the attacks targeted military command centers, drone storage facilities, communication networks, coastal surveillance sites, and maritime capabilities. Iranian state media reported explosions in Isfahan province (home to a major air base and nuclear sites) and on Qeshm Island, a key base for drone boats in the Strait of Hormuz.

In retaliation, Iran's Revolutionary Guard claimed to have struck US bases in Kuwait (Udairi — ammunition sheds set ablaze), Bahrain (Isa Air Base — Fifth Fleet watchtower), and Jordan (Al-Azraq Air Base — aircraft hangars). Both Bahrain and Jordan confirmed intercepting aerial attacks from Iran. The IRGC warned civilians in Gulf countries to "immediately move away from a 500-meter radius of areas where American soldiers are hiding," signaling further strikes.

In northern Iraq, US-led coalition forces shot down five explosive-laden drones near Irbil airport, which temporarily closed. US embassies across the Middle East began alerting American citizens that departure options could become more limited.

6. South China Sea: China Water Cannons Philippine Government Vessels

China Coast Guard vessels again deployed water cannons against Philippine government ships at Scarborough (Panatag) Shoal on Friday. The Philippine Coast Guard reported that CCG vessel 3107 fired water cannons at BRP Cape San Agustin at 6:26 a.m., followed by another CCG vessel targeting BFAR's BRP Datu Paduhinog at 7:35 a.m. — coming within approximately seven meters and risking collision.

Three direct water cannon hits were recorded from 8:00 a.m., with up to 15 Chinese CCG and maritime militia vessels confronting 10 Philippine ships. The PCG condemned China's "illegal, coercive, aggressive, and deceptive actions."

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio confirmed that a US Coast Guard vessel was present during the July 20 Ayungin Shoal baton attack and helped rescue injured Philippine Navy personnel. "We're already here, we're already present, we have a role and we're not going to abandon our allies," Rubio said, reaffirming the Mutual Defense Treaty commitment.

7. ICC Prosecutor Karim Khan Removed in Historic Vote

The International Criminal Court's member states voted 82-0 to remove Prosecutor Karim Khan following an investigation into serious sexual misconduct allegations, marking the first time a sitting ICC prosecutor has been ousted in the court's history.

The decision was taken during a special meeting of the ICC's 125 member states at the UN headquarters in New York. The removal follows findings by the court's oversight body that Khan committed serious misconduct related to allegations made by a woman who worked under him, involving "coercive and non-consensual sexual behavior." Khan has consistently denied the allegations.

Khan, a prominent British barrister elected as ICC prosecutor in 2021 for a nine-year term, was responsible for leading investigations into war crimes, crimes against humanity, and genocide. The unprecedented decision resolves one of the most significant internal crises the Hague-based court has faced in recent years.
