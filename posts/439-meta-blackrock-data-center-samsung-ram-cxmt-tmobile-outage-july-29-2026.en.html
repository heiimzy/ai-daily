⚡ July 29 Tech Roundup: Meta & BlackRock Form $14B Venture for 1 GW AI Data Center, Samsung Rushes RAM Lines to Block Apple from Buying Chinese CXMT, T-Mobile Outage Fully Resolved

Meta and BlackRock Announce $14 Billion Joint Venture for 1 GW AI Data Center in El Paso, Texas

On July 28, Meta Platforms and BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, announced a joint venture to develop and operate a massive AI data center campus in El Paso, Texas. The facility is designed to deliver 1 gigawatt of compute capacity with total development costs estimated at approximately $14 billion, and is expected to come online in 2028.

Under the agreement, funds managed by BlackRock — together with Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP) and HPS Investment Partners, both part of BlackRock — will hold an 80% interest in the venture, while Meta retains the remaining 20% ownership. Meta will contribute land and construction-in-progress assets valued at approximately $2.3 billion, while BlackRock will make a cash contribution of roughly $4.9 billion. A $12.5 billion debt financing will support a portion of the investment.

"Building the infrastructure for superintelligence is key to making sure the benefits of this technology are distributed to everyone," said Mark Zuckerberg, Meta founder and CEO. "Our partnership with Larry and the team at BlackRock allows us to move faster and at greater scale."

Meta will serve as construction manager and property manager for the campus, and will be the initial sole tenant upon completion. The lease has a four-year initial term with four extension options, potentially spanning up to 20 years. This is not Meta's first off-balance-sheet financing structure — its Hyperion data center in Louisiana used a similar model where Blue Owl held 80% and Meta held 20%, which set a record with $27 billion in private debt.

Larry Fink, Chairman and CEO of BlackRock, commented: "Companies around the world are looking for long-term strategic partners to help develop their most important projects, and BlackRock is built to meet that need. This transaction highlights the strength and scale of our combined capabilities with GIP and HPS."

The campus is already under construction in El Paso, Texas. Meta has separately guided to spending approximately $600 billion on US infrastructure through 2028. BlackRock previously acquired data center operator Aligned Data Centers for $40 billion, and Meta recently agreed to lease a large Aligned project in Shippingport, Pennsylvania.

RAMageddon Intensifies: Samsung Rushes New DRAM Lines to Prevent Apple from Turning to China's CXMT

The global memory crisis — dubbed "RAMageddon" — continues to escalate. Samsung Electronics is reportedly rushing to establish new DRAM production lines, in part to prevent one of its largest customers, Apple, from turning to alternative suppliers — especially China's newly listed memory maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT).

On July 27, CXMT made its debut on Shanghai's STAR Market, surging 465.82% on the first day to reach a market capitalization of 3.28 trillion yuan ($484 billion), making it Asia's largest IPO of the year. Market sources indicate that Apple CEO Tim Cook has reportedly sought permission from the U.S. government to purchase DRAM from CXMT amid the intensifying memory shortage — a move that has put Samsung on high alert.

The global DRAM market is experiencing unprecedented supply tightness. Prices for 1 GB of DRAM have surged from roughly $2.80 to $12 — an increase of over 300%. Framework Laptop has even raised its starting price by $800 due to the memory cost spike. CXMT's blockbuster IPO and potential Apple orders are further reshaping the global memory landscape.

Samsung's aggressive capacity expansion signals that the competitive dynamics of the memory chip industry are being shaken by the rise of Chinese players. While CXMT currently focuses on mature-node DRAM products, its massive fundraising from the IPO provides ample ammunition for future technology upgrades. Analysts note that if CXMT secures orders from top-tier customers like Apple, it would mark a pivotal transition from a "replacement supplier" to a "mainstream vendor" in the global memory chip industry.

T-Mobile Fully Restores Service After Major Multi-Day Outage

U.S. mobile carrier T-Mobile announced at 12:10 PM ET on Tuesday, July 28, that it had "fully restored service for all customers" following a major network disruption that began on Monday and lasted over 24 hours. The outage affected T-Mobile users across the United States and also impacted some customers on mobile virtual network operators (MVNOs) that rely on T-Mobile's infrastructure.

T-Mobile thanked customers for their patience but did not disclose the root cause of the outage. This is one of the most widespread service disruptions T-Mobile has experienced in recent years. Industry analysts point out that as U.S. telecom networks grow increasingly complex — with 5G and AI workloads placing greater demands on infrastructure — the risk of large-scale outages is rising.

The incident has renewed focus on the resilience of critical U.S. communications infrastructure. T-Mobile, one of the three major U.S. carriers with over 120 million subscribers, plays a vital role in daily communications, business operations, and emergency services.

Industry Perspective: The AI Infrastructure Boom and Supply Chain Transformation

These three stories, taken together, reveal the dominant forces reshaping the global tech industry. Meta and BlackRock's $14 billion data center joint venture signals that tech giants are moving from traditional "build-and-own" models toward more flexible, asset-light financing structures — freeing up capital to focus on core AI research and development.

Meanwhile, the RAMageddon-driven memory supply chain turmoil underscores that the AI arms race requires not just GPUs, but massive quantities of memory chips. The rise of Chinese memory manufacturer CXMT and potential U.S. customer interest is rewriting the geopolitical landscape of global memory chips. And the T-Mobile outage serves as a reminder that as the industry races to build AI infrastructure, the resilience and reliability of foundational communications networks cannot be overlooked.
