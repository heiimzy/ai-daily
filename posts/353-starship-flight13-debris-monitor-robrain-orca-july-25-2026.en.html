⚡ July 25 Tech Roundup: SpaceX Starship Flight 13 Successfully Deploys 20 Starlink V3 Satellites, China Launches First Commercial Space Debris Monitoring Satellite, BAAI Releases RoboBrain Orca World Model

Between July 24 and the early hours of July 25, three major tech stories broke: SpaceX's Starship successfully completed its first post-IPO test flight, deploying 20 next-generation Starlink V3 satellites in orbit; China launched its first commercial space debris monitoring satellite "Gande-1 01"; and the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI) unveiled RoboBrain Orca, a multimodal world model capable of predicting the evolution of complete world states for the first time.

1. SpaceX Starship Flight 13 Success: First Operational Starlink V3 Deployment

At 5:51 PM CDT on July 24 (06:51 CST July 25), SpaceX's giant Starship rocket successfully launched from Starbase in South Texas, marking the company's first major test flight since its Nasdaq IPO. The Super Heavy Booster 20 (B20) ignited all 33 Raptor 3 engines, propelling Ship 40 (S40) toward space.

Launch background: Flight 13 was originally scheduled for July 16 but was automatically aborted at T-0 when four engines failed to ignite. SpaceX replaced two suspect Raptor engines and implemented hardware and software modifications to the propulsion system. A subsequent July 23 attempt was postponed 24 hours due to weather before Friday's successful launch.

Key Mission Milestones

  • Booster recovery: Super Heavy executed a successful boostback burn and splashed down in the Gulf of Mexico approximately 6 minutes after liftoff. This marked a significant improvement over Flight 12, where the booster experienced control issues and engine failures during return
  • Satellite deployment: Starship successfully released 20 Starlink V3 satellites from its PEZ dispenser-style payload bay door — the first deployment of production V3 satellites in space. The satellites extended solar arrays and antennas and attempted to connect with the global Starlink constellation via high-capacity laser links
  • Thermal protection imaging: Six of the 20 Starlink V3 satellites carried high-resolution cameras to capture real-time imagery of Starship's heat shield tiles during atmospheric reentry, providing critical engineering data
  • Reentry and disposal: The Starlink satellites reentered Earth's atmosphere approximately 20 minutes after deployment, burning up as planned. Starship's upper stage targeted a controlled splashdown in the Indian Ocean approximately 65 minutes after launch

Strategic significance: This was the second flight of the Starship V3 configuration and the first to carry operational Starlink V3 satellites. Each V3 satellite weighs approximately 2,000 kg — roughly six times the mass of original Starlink V1 units — and is too large for Falcon 9's 5.2-meter payload fairing. Starship's 9-meter-wide payload bay is the only SpaceX vehicle capable of economically deploying V3 satellites. The success validates critical steps toward SpaceX's goal of full and rapid Starship reusability, which Elon Musk predicts could be achieved by the end of 2026.

NASA has a ~$4 billion contract with SpaceX to use Starship for returning astronauts to the Moon as early as 2028 under the Artemis program. Flight 13's success is a key stepping stone toward orbital operations and the complex in-orbit propellant transfer required for lunar missions.

"The Super Heavy booster executed another perfect recovery landing — absolutely stunning to watch," said SpaceX engineer Nathan Komissariat. This was also Starship's first flight since SpaceX became a publicly traded company (NASDAQ: SPCX). The stock closed 3% lower on Friday, with a ~25% decline over the past month as investors weigh the risks of SpaceX's iterative "test-fail-fix" development model.

2. China Launches First Commercial Space Debris Monitoring Satellite

On July 24, the Kinetica 1 (Lijian-1) Y15 carrier rocket successfully launched from the Dongfeng Commercial Aerospace Innovation Experimental Zone, delivering five satellites into their designated orbits. Among them, "Gande-1 01" is China's first commercial space debris monitoring satellite and the pioneering satellite of the Gande constellation.

Context: With global space activities intensifying, the number of orbital debris fragments has surged, posing a serious threat to in-orbit assets and crew safety. According to ESA estimates, over 40,000 trackable debris fragments are currently in orbit, with collision risks in Low Earth Orbit rising exponentially. The successful launch of Gande-1 01 marks a major step forward for China in space-based situational awareness and space traffic management.

Technical Specifications

  • Dual-mode detection: Equipped with wide-field survey cameras and high-resolution precision cameras for full-domain detection of low, medium, and high orbital space objects, enabling centimeter-level debris cataloging with dynamic updates
  • Onboard AI computing: Features star extraction and correlation positioning algorithms with autonomous mission planning for routine patrol, emergency response, and rapid tracking
  • Flexible maneuverability: Equipped with multiple new propulsion systems for "space walking" capability, supporting multi-orbit maneuvers and close-proximity observation

Gande constellation plan: The full Gande constellation will consist of 120 satellites, expected to be fully deployed by 2030. Once complete, it will integrate with ground-based detection and pervasive sensing systems for full high/medium/low orbit coverage, generating over 200,000 valid detection arcs per day with autonomous space object cataloging capability. The constellation aims to achieve "detect upon appearance, sense upon maneuver, and alert upon anomaly" — dramatically improving China's commercial space situational awareness capabilities.

3. BAAI Unveils RoboBrain Orca: World's First Global State Evolution World Model

On the morning of July 25 (Beijing time), the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI) officially released "Wujie·RoboBrain Orca" (悟界·RoboBrain Orca), a groundbreaking multimodal world model that breaks through the limitations of existing large models — which typically predict only text, images, or actions in single modalities — to achieve holistic prediction of world state evolution for the first time.

Technical breakthrough: If ChatGPT taught AI to "talk," RoboBrain Orca teaches AI to "understand the logic of how the world works." Its core innovation lies in constructing a unified latent representation space for the world, enabling the model to process videos, images, text instructions, event descriptions, and other multimodal inputs to both predict how a current scene will evolve and simulate multiple possible futures under different conditions.

Dual-Path Training System

  • Unsupervised learning path: Trained on 125,000 hours of real-world video footage, replicating how human infants accumulate common sense through observation — autonomously perceiving object falls, movement, scene evolution, and other physical laws without labels
  • Supervised learning path: Trained on 160 million event-annotated data points for targeted learning of state transition logic and event temporal relationships, completing the paradigm shift from "single-output prediction" to "global state reasoning"

Multimodal Decoding Capabilities

  • Text decoding: Excels at dynamic changes and state-transition logical reasoning
  • Image decoding: Precisely reconstructs real-world scene interaction predictions
  • Robot action decoding: Significantly improves downstream robot complex task generalization without requiring pre-learned action labels

Industry context: In the first half of 2026, global investment in the "world model" track surged past 40 billion USD-equivalent (400 billion RMB), with 46 deals in China and over 10 companies reaching valuations exceeding 10 billion RMB. When AI can understand the causal laws of the physical world, robots evolve from pre-programmed puppets to autonomous decision-making agents capable of adapting to their environment. This breakthrough represents a critical step toward true artificial general intelligence.
