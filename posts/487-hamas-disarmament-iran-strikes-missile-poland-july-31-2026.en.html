⚡ July 31 World Roundup: Hamas Agrees to Disarm, US Launches Heavy Strikes on Iran, Russian Missile Falls in Poland

Hamas Agrees to Fully Disarm as Trump Announces Gaza Phase-Two Deal

On July 31, US President Donald Trump announced that the Board of Peace, his mediation body, had reached an agreement on the "complete disarmament" of Hamas and other armed groups in Gaza. A senior Hamas official told the BBC that the group has agreed to the plan and that an official statement will be released soon. Israel has yet to comment publicly.

According to the four-page roadmap obtained by the BBC, the plan proceeds in carefully structured phases, linking Hamas's disarmament to a gradual Israeli military withdrawal and the deployment of a new Palestinian transitional authority backed by an international stabilization force. US officials said the plan was deliberately built on "zero trust," with verification mechanisms — neither side moves to the next phase until the other has fulfilled its commitments. Smaller armed groups would be disarmed first, before Hamas begins dismantling what US officials described as its state-like military infrastructure of tunnels, weapons depots and production facilities, a process they acknowledged would take considerably longer.

"This is a major milestone in the implementation of the Trump 20-Point Plan," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "As disarmament is completed, Israeli forces will withdraw, and the International Stabilization Force will work with a new Palestinian police force to take responsibility for Gaza being safe for its residents and its neighbors." He thanked mediators Egypt, Qatar and Turkey. The Times of Israel, citing an unnamed source, reported that the agreement had been signed between Hamas negotiators and the board and mediating countries — but said an "Israeli office" had issued a statement that the disarmament proposal did not meet Jerusalem's demands. Egyptian state media said Cairo would host a mediator meeting on the second phase of the ceasefire plan.

US Completes 'Heavy Wave' of Strikes on Iran; Iran Retaliates Against Kuwait and Jordan

After Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) launched ballistic missiles at a US base in Jordan and ships in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, the US Central Command (CENTCOM) said Thursday it had completed "a heavy wave" of strikes against Iran. The strikes began at 8:00 p.m. ET Wednesday (8:00 a.m. Beijing time Thursday) and lasted about an hour, hitting "dozens" of IRGC targets, including military command centers, missile and drone facilities, coastal surveillance and defense sites, and maritime capabilities. Trump had vowed from the White House: "We're going to be hitting them very hard because it's our turn to hit them. They know it's coming. They asked us not to do it."

Iranian state media reported that a missile struck a residential area on Qeshm Island, killing a couple and their two-year-old child; two other children were wounded and hospitalized, and a third was pulled from the rubble, with more people still believed trapped. Explosions were also reported in Kazerun on the outskirts of Fars province, on Kish Island, in Bushehr province (home to Iran's only nuclear power plant), in Ahvaz in Khuzestan province (at least seven locations), and in the port city of Bandar Abbas.

Iran retaliated on Thursday morning: Jordan's armed forces intercepted five missiles launched from Iran for the second consecutive day, and Kuwait's Defense Ministry said one worker was killed in an Iranian attack on a building belonging to a Chinese company in the country's north. The IRGC said "the aggressor will be punished today." Egypt also confirmed that a fire damaging two ships at the Damietta port on Wednesday was caused by a drone attack.

Russian Kh-101 Cruise Missile Crashes in Poland, NATO F-16s Scramble

Meanwhile, Russia launched a massive overnight missile and drone barrage against Ukraine early Thursday, firing more than 70 missiles — a significant number of them ballistic — and over 280 attack drones, of which more than 260 were intercepted. The attack hit at least 10 regions, killing at least 10 civilians and injuring more than 50, with more than 56,000 people sheltering overnight in Kyiv's subway stations. In the Kryvyi Rih area of Dnipropetrovsk region, a ballistic missile flattened an ordinary home, killing two parents and three of their children.

During the onslaught, a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile entered the airspace of NATO member Poland and crashed near the village of Tarnawa-Kolonia in Lublin province, leaving a crater about 10 meters in diameter. Poland's military said radar detected the airspace violation around 3:40 a.m. local time and scrambled an F-16 fighter jet, which quickly lost track of the target. NATO activated two fighter jets, an attack helicopter and two other aircraft, along with ground defenses.

"Everything indicates" it was a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said at the site, calling it "a very serious incident" — while stressing there was no reason to believe Poland was the target. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called it "yet another reckless act by Russia and a dangerous consequence of its war against Ukraine." Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the violation of NATO airspace was a clear demonstration that strengthening Ukraine's air defenses would also guarantee wider European security.

Saudi Arabia Forms 14-Nation Maritime Defense Alliance as Red Sea Tensions Mount

With the US-Iran conflict spilling into the Red Sea and Yemen's Houthis declaring a maritime blockade on Saudi Arabia on July 20 while attacking Saudi-linked vessels, Saudi Arabia's Defense Ministry announced Thursday the creation of a Multinational Maritime Defense Alliance with 13 other countries to safeguard freedom of navigation, international trade and energy supply routes through the Bab al-Mandeb Strait, the Red Sea and the Gulf of Aden.

The Riyadh meeting was attended by military chiefs of staff and representatives from 43 of the 51 invited countries, plus a European Union delegation. The 14 countries issuing the joint statement were Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Jordan, Egypt, Pakistan, Djibouti, Somalia, Bangladesh, Yemen and the Comoros. Notably, the United Arab Emirates and Oman did not sign up. Saudi Arabia was designated as the alliance's founding and leading state and permanent headquarters, hosting the Joint Operations Coordination Center and related bodies.

Analysts noted the alliance charter has yet to be finalized, with countries expressing support but stopping short of formally committing, and it remains unclear how the coalition will coordinate with the EU's existing Aspides naval protection mission in the Red Sea. Burcu Ozcelik, senior research fellow at the UK's Royal United Services Institute (RUSI), warned the real danger is the conflict settling into "a prolonged period of sustained confrontation, where regular military exchanges become the norm," alongside the threat of Houthi escalation in the Bab al-Mandeb.
