⚡ Super Week Opens with a Bang: Oil Crashes 7% on US-Iran Truce, Durable Goods Miss, CXMT Soars 466% in Record Debut

1. Global Market Panorama: Second Day of US-Iran Pause Fuels Broad Risk Rally

Monday, July 27, marked the opening of 2026's most consequential "Super Week" for global financial markets. Risk assets rallied broadly after the United States and Iran paused military strikes for a second consecutive night, creating a diplomatic opening after 13 days of continuous bombardment. Late Friday, President Trump ordered an abrupt halt to US airstrikes after CENTCOM commander General Cooper advised the Pentagon and White House that "targets were mostly exhausted." Iran's military confirmed Sunday it had also suspended retaliatory operations.

The dramatic reversal triggered a massive repricing across asset classes. Brent crude futures plunged as much as 7.4% to below $89 a barrel, collapsing from last week's $100+ peak. WTI crude fell ~7% to the $83 handle. The oil crash cooled inflation fears, driving bond yields lower — the 10-year US Treasury yield dropped 5 basis points to 4.63%, while the German 10-year Bund yield fell to 3.13%.

US equity futures surged: Nasdaq 100 futures +1.4%, S&P 500 futures +0.9%, Dow futures +571 points (+1.1%). European markets rallied across the board: Germany's DAX +1.36% to 25,099, France's CAC 40 +0.7%, UK's FTSE 100 +0.9%, pan-European STOXX 600 +0.9%. Asian markets also closed higher: Nikkei 225 +0.5%, KOSPI +1%, Australia ASX 200 +1.4%, Shanghai Composite +1.15% to 3,858, Hang Seng +1%.

The US Dollar Index (DXY) weakened ~0.2% to 101.40. EUR/USD reclaimed the 1.14 handle, GBP/USD held near 1.3330. Gold climbed over 1% to above $4,100/oz as safe-haven demand rotated. Bitcoin rose 0.9% above $65,000, while Ethereum surged 4%.

2. US Durable Goods Orders Miss Badly: +0.3% vs +1.6% Expected

The US Census Bureau reported Monday that June durable goods orders rose just 0.3% ($1.1B) to $334.8 billion, sharply below the 1.6% consensus estimate and following a 4.0% decline in May (revised from -4.5%). Excluding transportation, orders increased 0.6%, while excluding defense, orders rose 0.3%.

Computers and electronic products led the increase, up 3.1% (+$0.9B) to $31.1B — up in nine of the last ten months. However, the broad-based weakness in capital goods orders signals cautious business investment sentiment heading into the FOMC decision on Wednesday.

Market reaction was muted, with the Dollar Index hovering near 101.40. Evercore ISI's head of central bank strategy Krishna Guha noted: "We think the Fed will probably not hike. But we cannot take the probability too low given Warsh's refusal to set out his strategy." Markets currently price a 33-37% probability of a July rate hike, with September odds around 80%.

3. CXMT Shatters Records: +466% in STAR Market Debut, $205B Trading Volume

Chinese DRAM giant CXMT Corp (688825.SH) made a historic debut on Shanghai's STAR Market Monday, opening at 49.50 yuan per share — 471.6% above its IPO price of 8.66 yuan — and closing at 49.01 yuan for a gain of 465.82%. The company's market capitalization reached approximately 3.28 trillion yuan ($485 billion), surpassing Industrial and Commercial Bank of China to become the most valuable listed company on mainland China's A-share market.

CXMT's single-day trading volume exceeded 140 billion yuan ($20.5 billion), shattering the record for the highest daily turnover of any individual A-share stock. Turnover rate exceeded 66%. The IPO raised approximately 57.9 billion yuan, and with the full exercise of the greenshoe option could reach 66.6 billion yuan — surpassing SMIC's 2020 record of 53.2 billion to become the largest IPO in STAR Market history.

The explosive debut was fueled by CXMT's stunning financial performance. In Q1 2026, the company reported revenue of 50.8 billion yuan (+719% YoY) and net profit of 24.76 billion yuan. For H1 2026, CXMT expects revenue of 110-120 billion yuan (+612-677% YoY) and net profit of 50-57 billion yuan (+2,244-2,544% YoY). According to Omdia data, CXMT is now China's largest and the world's fourth-largest DRAM manufacturer with a 7.67% global market share.

CXMT's blockbuster listing lifted the broader Chinese market. The Shanghai Composite closed +1.15%, Shenzhen Component +2.72%, and ChiNext +3.16%. Nearly 5,200 stocks advanced with over 100 hitting the daily limit up. Brain-computer interface, battery, and component sectors led the gains.

4. European Data Surprises to the Upside: German IFO Beats, ECB Hawks Remain Vocal

European economic data provided additional positive catalysts. Germany's July IFO Business Climate index came in at 86.6, beating the 86.0 consensus, with the Expectations component surging to 86.7 (vs 84.7 expected) — a strong signal that business sentiment is improving. However, the Current Assessment component slightly missed. Germany's composite PMI recovered to 51.2, ending three consecutive months of contraction, while France's composite PMI rose to 49.6 — the highest since February, though still in contraction territory.

ECB Governing Council member Kazimir (Slovakia) stated Monday that a September rate hike was likely necessary even if the economic outlook improves. Markets now price a ~90% probability of a September ECB hike. ECB Chief Economist Philip Lane emphasized that Europe's economy is primarily supported by domestic demand, and that EU-US trade, while important, is not the dominant issue.

Eurozone July manufacturing PMI beat expectations at 52, with the composite PMI at 51.9 returning to expansion. However, European bond yields declined alongside the oil drop, with the German 2-year yield falling to 2.77% and the 10-year to 3.13%.

5. Singapore MAS Tightens Unexpectedly; China Industrial Profits +15.1%

Asian macro news was equally eventful. The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) unexpectedly tightened monetary policy for the second consecutive meeting, increasing the slope of the Singapore dollar's nominal effective exchange rate (NEER) policy band while keeping the width and center level unchanged. The MAS noted that core inflation is projected to rise from July and remain elevated, only moderating discernibly from mid-2027. The Singapore dollar strengthened on the announcement.

China's National Bureau of Statistics reported that industrial profits for January-June rose 15.1% year-on-year. New economy sectors showed exceptional growth: fiber optic manufacturing profits surged 410.4%, additive equipment manufacturing rose 55.4%, and semiconductor device manufacturing gained 18.7%. June single-month industrial profit growth stood at 15.1%, moderating from May's 21.1% but still maintaining double-digit expansion.

Japan's June core CPI rose 1.6% year-on-year — the first acceleration in three months but still below the 2% policy target. The Bank of Russia cut its key rate by 25 basis points to 14%, the tenth consecutive reduction since June 2025, bringing cumulative easing to 700 basis points.

6. Super Week Watchlist: FOMC Showdown + Big Tech Earnings Marathon

The 2026 Super Week is just getting started. The next 72 hours will feature a relentless cascade of macro and corporate catalysts:

  • Wednesday (July 29): FOMC rate decision (2:00 PM ET) + Microsoft and Meta Q2 earnings (after close); SK Hynix reports as well
  • Thursday (July 30): US Q2 GDP advance estimate, Bank of England rate decision, Apple and Amazon Q2 earnings
  • Friday (July 31): Bank of Japan rate decision, Eurozone July CPI preliminary, US June core PCE inflation data

Markets currently price a 33-37% probability of a Fed hike on Wednesday, though most economists expect the committee to hold steady. The AI capex anxiety remains the dominant narrative for tech stocks — Alphabet reported its first-ever negative free cash flow last week, triggering a 7% selloff. The Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon reports this week will determine whether the AI investment thesis holds.

"The market has been punishing AI capex guidance all July even when the underlying numbers beat," said Billy Leung, investment strategist at Global X Management. "The read-through from these three on spending trajectory and monetisation will do more to set direction than anything in today's session."

Overall, Monday's market action paints a clear picture of a "ceasefire trade" — risk assets rallying, energy plunging, bonds and rate-sensitive sectors gaining. However, geopolitical tail risks remain extremely elevated: the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, Iran denies direct negotiations with the US, Houthi attacks on Saudi tankers continue, and the underlying drivers of the conflict are unresolved. Whether this rally sustains depends on whether the pause translates into a verifiable peace framework — and on how the Fed navigates its most difficult policy decision in years.
