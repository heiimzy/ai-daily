⚡ Aug 2 World News: Trump Cancels Iran Strikes, Says 'Perimeters of a Deal' Agreed; Saudi Crown Prince Urges Restraint; Israel Kills 8 in Gaza

The Middle East took a dramatic turn on the afternoon of August 2: US President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social that he has cancelled a new round of military strikes on Iran at the request of Tehran and other Middle Eastern countries, saying "the perimeters of a deal" have been agreed - hours after US media reported that Washington and Israel were planning "one of the harshest bombing campaigns to date" against Iran's energy infrastructure, possibly lasting all weekend. Meanwhile, Israeli strikes killed at least eight Palestinians across Gaza on Sunday morning, with Trump's 15-point roadmap still unresolved, and a drone attack hit a Peshmerga headquarters in Sulaimaniyah in Iraq's Kurdish region.

Trump: Strike Cancelled, Subject to a Deal 'Rapidly' Reached

"The U.S.A. is locked and loaded and ready to go against the Islamic Republic of Iran, at levels of Military Terror, Strength, and Power not seen since World War II," Trump wrote on Truth Social. "Despite this, we have just been asked by Iran, and other Middle Eastern Countries, to hold off any attack in that the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to." He said the deal would include the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat", adding that "The Country of Israel joins me in this commitment" and urging: "Get to work, everybody, and get it DONE."

Just hours earlier, multiple US media outlets reported that the US and Israel had been planning a joint campaign of strikes on Iranian power plants, refineries and other energy infrastructure - with cutting off Tehran's electricity among the options discussed. The State Department had issued broad security alerts for Americans across the Middle East, urging preparations for flight cancellations, temporary airspace closures and travel disruptions. The Associated Press noted the war, which began on February 28, has now run more than five months: a June ceasefire memorandum collapsed within weeks, and fighting resumed on July 8.

Saudi Crown Prince Called Trump, Worried About Energy Targets

According to Axios and the Associated Press, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman spoke with Trump before the announcement and "expressed concern and asked for clarity about the plan of action". A person briefed on the call told AP the Saudis worried that if the US targeted Iran's energy infrastructure, Tehran could retaliate against the energy infrastructure of the kingdom and other Gulf states - so the crown prince urged Trump to de-escalate and refrain from launching the strikes. A White House official confirmed the two leaders spoke Saturday but offered no detail.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told Fox News that the military campaign has made Iran "more willing... to do a deal on denuclearisation, to do a deal on the straits" - "because we are now approaching them from a position of strength, not from a position of weakness." He claimed the strikes had destroyed Iran's navy, air force, missile defences and weapons-production facilities, while conceding "they still have missiles, and they still have drones, and they can still do damage." Ross Harrison of the Middle East Institute cautioned that unless Washington and Tehran reach a clear agreement on reopening the Strait, "this announcement is only going to be another pause."

Israel Kills 8 in Gaza as 15-Point Roadmap Stalls

Two days after Trump's Board of Peace published a 15-point roadmap for implementing last year's Gaza ceasefire agreement, Israeli forces killed at least eight Palestinians in strikes across the Strip on Sunday morning, Gaza's civil defence said: a man and a woman were hit in an Israeli helicopter strike on an apartment south of Deir el-Balah, with separate strikes killing three people in Khan Younis and one in Gaza City's Old City. On Saturday, Israeli strikes also destroyed a medical supply warehouse in central Gaza, leaving a crater 20 metres (66ft) wide and 10 metres deep. Al Jazeera reported that more than 58,000 children in Gaza have now lost one or both parents to the war.

The roadmap's fate hinges on the same core standoff: Hamas says it will hand over its weapons for storage only after Israel halts military operations and withdraws its forces in line with last year's agreement, while an Israeli official told Reuters there would be no withdrawal unless Hamas undergoes "genuine disarmament". Far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir has called the plan unacceptable, while former senior Fatah official Mohammed Dahlan said Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner told him he was working with Israel to halt the attacks on Gaza.

Drone Attack in Iraq's Sulaimaniyah; US Sends More Refuelling Aircraft

A large fire broke out in the Iraqi city of Sulaimaniyah in the northern Kurdish region after a drone attack: the strike targeted the Peshmerga's 70th Brigade headquarters but was intercepted, with "the wreckage of one interceptor target" catching fire near the site, Shafaq News reported, and videos published by Rudaw showed a large blaze. No group has claimed responsibility - Lebanese outlet Al Mayadeen reported the attack was carried out by Iran-backed groups in Iraq, while Iran's semi-official Fars news agency described the site as the headquarters of "anti-Iran terrorists". Iran has carried out several strikes against Kurdish opposition fighters in Iraq since the war began on February 28.

Separately, Israeli news outlet Ynet reported, citing security officials, that the US military is expected to deploy about 10 additional aerial refuelling aircraft to Israel in the coming days, most to be parked at Ben Gurion Airport in Tel Aviv - joining more than 30 US refuelling planes already stationed there, a signal that Washington intends to keep its strike capability over Iran ready.
