⚡ July 26 Financial Weekly: Fed Rate Hike Odds Surge 4x in One Week, AI Capex Shock Wipes $797B from Mag 7, Chip Stocks Plunge 17% in July

The trading week ending July 24, 2026, delivered a rare "triple shock" to global financial markets. Fed rate hike expectations quadrupled from 10.7% to 38% in just one week. AI capital expenditure anxiety wiped $797 billion in market value from the Magnificent Seven in a single session. Chip stocks suffered their worst monthly decline of the year at -17%. Meanwhile, hedge fund short interest in the S&P 500 surged to its highest level since 2010. Here is a comprehensive breakdown of the week's key financial developments.

1. FOMC July Meeting Preview: Rate Hike Odds Triple in One Week as Warsh Abandons Forward Guidance

The Federal Reserve will announce its latest rate decision on Wednesday, July 29. According to CME FedWatch data as of July 24, market-implied probability of a 25-basis-point hike at the July meeting surged from 10.7% one week ago to 34-38%. September hike odds crossed 82% — meaning markets have effectively priced in a September rate increase as the base case.

Three catalysts drove this dramatic shift. First, the escalating US-Iran military conflict has reduced Strait of Hormuz transits by 66% to just 13 vessels per day, sending Brent crude above $102/barrel and directly fueling inflation expectations. Second, "Trumpflation" has entered a new phase — Core PCE stickiness suggests inflation pressure has broadened beyond energy into the wider economy. Third, AI data center build-out is creating hardware shortages and concentrated pricing power that continue to push headline prices higher.

New Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has completely abandoned the forward guidance practice of his predecessor Jerome Powell. The June FOMC statement was significantly streamlined, and Warsh declined to reveal rate path intentions at both the ECB Forum and Congressional hearings in early July. Barclays US economist Pooja Sriram noted that Warsh has emphasized the necessity of restoring price stability, suggesting monetary policy may not yet be restrictive enough.

Despite this hawkish tilt, most major institutions still expect the Fed to hold steady in July. June CPI posted an unexpected 0.4% month-over-month decline — the first negative monthly reading since 2020 — and PPI also came in below expectations, providing data support for a "wait and see" approach. Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan both expect rates to remain at 3.50-3.75% in July, with a September hike as the next likely move. Bank of America goes further, predicting Warsh could hike 25bp each in September, October, and December — a cumulative 75bp of tightening for 2026.

"While a July rate hike remains highly unlikely, the September FOMC meeting could become the first meaningful test of whether the recent improvement in inflation proves durable," said Gregory Daco, Chief Economist at EY-Parthenon.

2. AI Capex Nuclear Bomb: Alphabet's $205B CapEx, Tesla's Negative FCF Trigger $797B Mag 7 Wipeout

The week's most shocking market event was the AI capital expenditure panic triggered by Alphabet and Tesla earnings. Alphabet reported Q2 revenue of $119.8B — above the $116.9B consensus — with Google Cloud surging 82% to $24.8B. But these strong figures were overwhelmed by one number: Alphabet raised its full-year 2026 capital expenditure guidance to $195-205B, up $15B from the prior range. Quarterly capex hit nearly $45B, pushing free cash flow to negative $5.9B. Shares plunged 6.9%.

Tesla's situation was even more acute. Revenue rose 26% to $28.2B, but adjusted EPS of $0.33 missed the $0.51 consensus by a wide margin. Quarterly capex climbed to $5.8B, pushing FCF to negative $1.1B. Elon Musk maintained plans to invest over $25B annually in AI, robotaxis, and batteries. Shares cratered 14.5% in a single session.

The market-wide damage was staggering: the Magnificent Seven collectively lost roughly $797 billion in market capitalization on Thursday alone. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index plunged 4.25% on Thursday and is now down 17% for July — its worst monthly performance of 2026.

The macro backdrop compounds the pain. The 10-year Treasury yield reached 4.71% this week — an 18-month high. The US Dollar Index climbed to 101.4, its strongest weekly advance in five weeks. Higher discount rates directly compress the present value of long-duration growth equities, and AI stocks are among the longest-duration assets in the market.

Total AI-related investment has surpassed $1.6 trillion as of mid-2026 by some estimates. Returns remain elusive. Alphabet and Tesla's earnings sent a clear signal: the market no longer accepts "spending on AI is always right" on faith.

3. Intel Beats Q2 Estimates But Stock Falls: CapEx Hike to $20B Scares Investors

Intel delivered what appeared to be a stellar Q2 earnings report after Thursday's close: revenue of $16.1B, up 25% YoY, crushing the $14.4B consensus; non-GAAP EPS of $0.42, double the ~$0.21 estimate; Data Center and AI revenue surging 59%. Q3 revenue guidance of $15.8-16.8B also exceeded the $15.1B analyst consensus.

Yet Intel shares fell approximately 2.3% on Friday to close at $100.23. The reason: Intel raised its 2026 capital expenditure plan from roughly $18B to over $20B. In the wake of the Alphabet and Tesla "capex punishment" template, any additional spending commitment is now treated as a sell signal by the market.

Analysts are calling this the "capex penalty" — even quality earnings are being punished if they contain upward capex revisions. This sets an extremely high bar for the four Mag 7 companies reporting next week: Microsoft (July 29), Meta, Apple (July 30), and Amazon (July 30).

4. Hedge Fund Shorts Hit Highest Since 2010: S&P 500 Short Interest Reaches 3.7%

According to fresh data from S3 Partners, short interest across the S&P 500 has climbed to 3.7% of the index's free float — the highest since the firm began tracking the metric in 2010. This compares to roughly 2% a year ago and approaches the 3.8% level recorded just before the 2008 financial crisis.

Hedge funds are not merely hedging; they have built outright directional bearish positions. This aligns perfectly with growing Wall Street anxiety over the AI bubble. Alphabet's negative FCF, Tesla's cash burn, chip stocks down 17% — all provide ammunition for short sellers.

Friday offered a modest relief rally. Reports that Pakistan, backed by China, is brokering renewed US-Iran negotiations sent Brent crude back to $96.78 (down ~5% from Thursday's $102 high). The 10-year Treasury yield retreated from 4.71%, and the S&P 500 finished flat. But this is not a trend reversal — oil is still up ~40% for July, and the US-Iran conflict shows no sign of genuine de-escalation.

5. Next Week's Blockbuster Calendar: Fed Decision, 4 Mag 7 Earnings, US Q2 GDP

The coming week (July 28-31) is arguably the most consequential macro trading week of 2026. Key events:

  • Tuesday (July 28): US Consumer Confidence, Coca-Cola, Visa earnings
  • Wednesday (July 29): FOMC rate decision + Warsh press conference, Microsoft, Meta earnings
  • Thursday (July 30): US Q2 GDP advance, PCE price index, Apple, Amazon earnings
  • Friday (July 31): Chicago PMI, China PMI, Bank of Japan rate decision

Four of the seven Mag 7 companies report within 72 hours of each other. Every single report will face the same core question: is the capex producing visible revenue growth? If Microsoft and Meta can demonstrate a credible capex-to-revenue conversion path, the AI narrative could be reborn. If the answers are ambiguous, the market may accelerate its rotation out of technology.

The convergence of Fed policy and Big Tech earnings means volatility will surge. The VIX closed Friday at 18.58 — down slightly from Thursday's 18.70 but still elevated. Options markets are pricing one of the highest expected move windows of the year.
