⚡ July 28 Geopolitical Update: Trump Meets Netanyahu at White House as Oman Proposes Joint Hormuz Management, Ukraine Strikes Moscow with 390+ Drones

1. Trump-Netanyahu White House Summit: First Face-to-Face Since Iran War

President Donald Trump met Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House on Tuesday (July 28) in their first face-to-face meeting since the joint U.S.-Israeli strikes on Iran in late February. The talks, held against the backdrop of nearly five months of conflict between Washington and Tehran, covered the trajectory of the Iran war, Strait of Hormuz security, the Lebanon situation, and expansion of the Abraham Accords.

Ahead of the meeting, Trump told reporters there was "a little difference" between him and Netanyahu on Iran but their positions were "pretty close." However, the tone of this summit differs markedly from previous encounters. Trump has in recent weeks publicly criticized Netanyahu's aggressive stance on Lebanon, reportedly using profane language to describe the Israeli leader in a phone call. White House officials indicated Trump was frustrated that Netanyahu had "undermined diplomatic efforts" during multiple ceasefire negotiations and warned that continued Israeli escalation could jeopardize peace talks with Iran.

Netanyahu, speaking before departing Israel, said: "In these complex times, you need to act with both great determination and great wisdom. We will discuss all the issues on the agenda, first and foremost Iran. Naturally, our goal is to safeguard our security and also to expand the circle of peace around us."

Analysts note that the meeting carries significant political weight for Netanyahu ahead of Israel's October 27 elections, where he trails in opinion polls. Demonstrating the strength of the U.S.-Israel alliance through a White House visit is seen as a crucial campaign asset.

2. Oman Presents Joint Regional Mechanism for Strait of Hormuz

Oman has presented a proposal to Iran for a joint regional mechanism to manage the Strait of Hormuz with voluntary fees, a Gulf source told Reuters on Tuesday. The proposal has received regional backing from multiple Gulf states.

The proposed model is based on the Strait of Malacca system, where voluntary contributions fund navigation safety, environmental protection, and search and rescue operations. Oman has suggested establishing three maritime lanes through the strait: one through Iranian territorial waters, an international corridor, and one through Omani territorial waters. If accepted, Iran would take responsibility for mine clearance, though technical negotiations have yet to finalize details.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi held separate phone calls Monday evening with his Saudi and Omani counterparts—Faisal bin Farhan and Badr Al-Busaidi—regarding Hormuz security. According to an Iranian Foreign Ministry statement, the calls "emphasized the need to strengthen cooperation and advance joint diplomatic efforts to establish stability in the region and eliminate the insecurity imposed on the Strait of Hormuz by the aggressive actions of the United States."

Trump, meanwhile, told a campaign rally in Michigan that the U.S. was engaged in "very friendly negotiations" with Iran, while warning that military operations would resume if talks fail to deliver. Iran has denied holding any direct talks with the U.S. but acknowledged receiving messages through intermediaries.

3. U.S.-Iran Pause Holds for Third Day as Diplomacy Intensifies

The pause in U.S.-Iran hostilities entered its third day on Tuesday, marking the longest period of calm since the June ceasefire collapsed. Both sides have refrained from launching new strikes since Friday, with Qatar and Pakistan leading mediation efforts to restart ceasefire negotiations.

However, regional violence persists. Saudi Arabia reported intercepting multiple drones launched from Iraq by Iran-backed militias targeting oil facilities in the Eastern Province and Riyadh. Yemen's Houthi rebels also claimed responsibility for attacks on Saudi oil infrastructure. Iraq has launched an investigation into the drone attacks launched from its territory against Saudi Arabia.

Jordan's military reported downing a drone that entered its airspace at dawn. The persistence of low-level hostilities through proxies highlights the fragility of the current ceasefire.

The U.S. Central Command said Monday it has forced 17 commercial vessels to change course, disabled two ships, and boarded two others since reinstating the naval blockade on Iran. Iran's Armed Forces warned that the blockade would be considered "an expansion of war in the region" and pledged to respond.

4. Ukraine Launches Massive 390+ Drone Attack on Moscow

Ukraine launched an unprecedented large-scale drone attack on Moscow overnight Monday-Tuesday, with Russian authorities reporting over 390 UAVs heading toward the capital. Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin claimed Russian air defenses intercepted 81 drones approaching the city during the 10-hour assault from 8:30 p.m. Monday to 6:30 a.m. Tuesday.

Moscow Region Governor Andrei Vorobyov confirmed a drone struck a residential apartment building in the town of Chekhov, south of Moscow. The attack also sparked a fire at one of Russia's largest tire recycling facilities and set ablaze a third-party logistics warehouse in Koledino. Russia's aviation authority Rosaviatsia imposed temporary restrictions at Vnukovo, Domodedovo, and Zhukovsky airports, with Domodedovo lifting restrictions after 6 a.m. Moscow time.

Meanwhile, a Ukrainian drone strike on an electrical substation in the occupied Crimean city of Feodosia left much of the city without power. Ukraine's military claimed to have struck 14 energy facilities in Crimea over three days.

The strikes come amid a sharp expansion of Ukraine's long-range campaign against targets deep inside Russia, including energy and logistics infrastructure. Ukraine has increasingly struck Russian oil refineries, depots, and logistics hubs tied to Moscow's war effort, creating a national fuel shortage.

5. Iran Reveals War Damage: Bushehr Airport Destroyed, 230M cm Gas Capacity Lost

Iranian government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani disclosed significant war damage figures in her weekly press conference on Tuesday—the first time Iran has provided detailed quantification of losses from the U.S. bombing campaign.

Mohajerani said Bushehr Airport in southern Iran has been rendered completely inoperable following U.S. strikes, with a newly purchased passenger plane destroyed by a direct missile hit. Iran has lost approximately 230 million cubic meters of gas production capacity due to the war, she added.

The government spokesperson also reported that 12 bridges and two tunnels were targeted in recent U.S. strikes, causing "very extensive" damage to both public infrastructure and private property. Iran had previously requested assessments of damage caused by U.S. and Israeli attacks since the war began on February 28.

Oil markets responded positively to the pause in hostilities, with WTI crude falling below $80 per barrel and Brent crude trading at $87.82, down 0.6%. However, Iranian officials stressed that the Strait of Hormuz remains closed, and the foreign ministry insisted no direct negotiations with the U.S. are taking place.

6. Zelensky Arrives in Washington for Talks with Trump

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky arrived in Washington on Tuesday for meetings with President Trump and to attend the funeral of the late Sen. Lindsey Graham. Sources told Reuters that Ukrainian and American officials discussed an "air ceasefire" proposal during the visit, which would be presented to Russia as part of a new peace initiative.

A major Russia sanctions bill is also expected to be presented to the Senate. The legislation would impose 100% tariffs on goods from the five largest importers of Russian oil and gas, including China and India—one of the most severe sanctions packages ever proposed against Moscow.

Zelensky has also accused Russia of conducting satellite reconnaissance on Gulf states and U.S. military facilities since early July, providing imagery to Iran for targeting purposes. Trump said he would ask President Putin directly about the allegations, further highlighting the deepening military coordination between Russia and Iran.
