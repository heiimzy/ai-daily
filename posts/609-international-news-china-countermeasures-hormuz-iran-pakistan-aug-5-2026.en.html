⚡ Aug 5 International Update: China Escalates Countermeasures (Drone Export Curbs + 6 US Entities Sanctioned), Iran Says Hormuz Deal Won't Reopen Strait Immediately, Pakistan Reveals Role as US-Iran Channel

Over the past hour or two, the China-US economic and geopolitical rivalry and Middle East tensions delivered a fresh wave of major developments: China moved on multiple fronts — tightening dual-use drone export controls to the US, placing six US entities on its countermeasure list, and opening a national security probe into imported printers and copiers; Iran's state TV, citing insiders, poured cold water on expectations of a quick Hormuz reopening, saying a possible Iran-Oman vessel-passage agreement would not mean the strait reopens immediately; and Pakistan publicly confirmed it is one of the communication channels between Washington and Tehran. Here is this round's international roundup.

1. China Escalates Countermeasures: Drone Export Curbs Tightened, 6 US Entities Sanctioned

On Aug 5 (16:02 Beijing time), China's commerce ministry (MOFCOM) issued a notice under the Export Control Law and the Regulations on the Export Control of Dual-Use Items, deciding to strengthen export controls on drone-related dual-use items to the United States: exports to the US of drones and their key components and related technologies listed on the Dual-Use Items Export Control List will be subject to strict case-by-case review, with licensing facilitation measures no longer applicable, effective immediately upon publication. It is the latest round of countermeasures in response to recent US restrictions on Chinese entities.

Almost simultaneously (16:02), MOFCOM announced countermeasures against six US entities including Applied DNA Sciences. MOFCOM said the US sanctions on Chinese companies under the pretext of "forced labor" seriously violate international law and basic norms of international relations and infringe on China's sovereignty, security and development interests; the six entities, including Applied DNA Sciences, assisted and supported US unlawful sanctions related to Xinjiang, which is egregious in nature. Under Articles 3, 4, 6 and related provisions of China's Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law, the six entities were placed on the countermeasure list, with organizations and individuals in China prohibited from transacting or cooperating with them.

At 16:04, the decision on countermeasures against US Compliance Testing LLC — which assisted and supported the US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in its negative measures toward China — was published and took effect the same day, approved through the national coordination mechanism against foreign sanctions. The National Certification and Accreditation Administration also announced that it would suspend, effective immediately, factory follow-up inspections conducted by US certification bodies on behalf of CCC (China Compulsory Certification) designated agencies.

At 16:08, Beijing summarized the full package: strengthened drone and key-component/technology export controls to the US; suspension of CCC factory follow-up inspections by US certifiers; placement of US Compliance Testing LLC on the countermeasure list; a foreign-trade national security investigation into imported printers and copiers; and six US entities added to the countermeasure list. Beijing stressed that the countermeasures are "overall restrained" and that China cherishes the hard-won stability of China-US economic and trade relations, demanding Washington immediately revoke its measures and stop its wrongdoings; should the US insist on introducing new restrictive measures against China, China will respond with further countermeasures.

2. MOFCOM Opens National Security Probe into Imported Printers & Copiers; Industry Chamber Backs It

At 16:03, citing Articles 41 and 42 of the Foreign Trade Law, MOFCOM decided to launch, effective Aug 5, 2026, an investigation into matters involving national security interests in foreign trade. Preliminary information suggests imported printers and copiers equipped with foreign system software may affect national security interests in foreign trade, meeting the conditions described in Article 41 of the Foreign Trade Law.

At 18:06, the China Chamber of Commerce for Import & Export of Machinery & Electronic Products (CCCME) issued a statement firmly supporting MOFCOM's lawful launch of the investigation. The chamber noted that printers and copiers, as key terminals for information input and output, are foundational equipment for social and economic operations, and that ensuring this industry meets national security interests is important; it also said the recent negative measures by the US FCC and the Department of Homeland Security — including multiple restrictive market-access actions of an extremely egregious nature — plus Washington's practice of overstretching the national security concept, even putting conventional goods like lumber into Section 232 investigations and abusing tariffs, are firmly opposed. Going forward, CCCME will actively organize relevant companies to cooperate with MOFCOM's investigation.

3. Iran: Hormuz Agreement Would Not Reopen the Strait Immediately — Reopening Depends on the US

At 17:31 Beijing time, Iran's state TV, citing informed sources, said a possible agreement between Iran and Oman on vessel-passage arrangements in the Strait of Hormuz does not mean the strait will immediately resume opening. The sources said the talks only involve coordination between the two sides on passage order and have nothing to do with the US; if the US continues what Iran calls "violations," the strait will not reopen even if an Iran-Oman agreement is reached, and reopening ultimately depends on the US changing its behavior and correcting its "violations".

Reports earlier suggested the US, Iran and Oman were "close to" a temporary agreement on reopening the Strait of Hormuz, with Washington hoping to announce it on Aug 5. However, messages from Iranian and Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps sources on Aug 5 said the main reason the agreement has been elusive is US intervention and President Trump's threats — as long as the US keeps interfering and issuing military threats against Iran, no agreement can be reached. Market optimism surrounding "Hormuz peace talks" may need to be re-priced.

4. Pakistan Says It Is a US-Iran Communication Channel; the Two Sides "Remain in Contact"

At 17:26 Beijing time, Pakistan's Ambassador to Russia, Tirmizi, said in an interview that the United States and Iran currently remain in contact over the conflict, and Pakistan is one of the communication channels between the two countries. Tirmizi said Pakistan believes in diplomacy and has open communication channels; he also stressed that the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding signed separately by the US and Iran in June this year attests to Pakistan's effective mediating role between the two sides.
