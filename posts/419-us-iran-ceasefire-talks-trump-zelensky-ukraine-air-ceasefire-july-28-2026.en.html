⚡ July 28 Geopolitical Update: US-Iran Pause Extends to Third Night as Diplomacy Gains Traction; Trump and Zelensky Meet in Washington for Air Ceasefire Talks; Russia Launches 331 Drones and Missiles at Ukraine Overnight

1. US-Iran: Third Consecutive Night of Calm as Diplomatic Window Opens

The United States and Iran have gone three consecutive nights without exchanging fire — the longest stretch of calm since the Middle East crisis spiraled in early July. US Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz told Fox News on Sunday that President Trump is "giving talks some space" and "giving it a little bit of room."

Speaking to NBC News, Waltz added: "I wouldn't go that far at all. The president is keeping all options on the table." This indicates that while military operations have paused, the option of force remains very much alive.

Iran has responded in kind. Iranian army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia confirmed Sunday that Tehran has halted "retaliatory" attacks on US military assets in the region. This follows nearly two weeks of intense tit-for-tat strikes — US forces struck over 13,000 targets across Iran, while Iranian ballistic missiles and drones targeted US bases in Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, and Iraq.

"Our strategy has essentially been retaliatory. We have also halted our retaliatory operations." — Iranian army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia

Despite the quiet on the ground, Trump posted AI-generated images on Truth Social depicting US warplanes bombing Iranian ships and Kharg Island, signaling that military options remain on standby.

Diplomatic Progress: Oman-Brokered Talks Near Agreement

According to CCTV reporting, US and Iranian negotiators have reached a framework for a 60-day memorandum of understanding (MoU) that would extend the ceasefire and launch negotiations on Iran's nuclear program. The agreement still requires final approval from President Trump. US officials say the terms were largely finalized by July 26.

Key provisions of the draft MoU include: unrestricted passage through the Strait of Hormuz with no tolls or interference; Iran clearing all naval mines from the strait within 30 days; and a gradual lifting of the US naval blockade. Iran would also commit to not developing nuclear weapons, with a 60-day negotiation period to address the disposition of Iran's enriched uranium stockpile.

Speaking to Axios, Trump confirmed that the US is engaged in "very deep talks" with Iran, but warned that if diplomacy fails, "we go back to the same thing" — powerful military action. He declined to set a specific deadline, saying talks will "either get done quickly, or they won't get done at all."

2. Trump-Zelensky Washington Summit: Air Ceasefire Proposal Takes Center Stage

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky arrives in Washington today for a face-to-face meeting with President Trump — their first bilateral meeting since 2025. The core agenda: a US-Ukraine joint proposal for an aerial ceasefire to be presented to Russia.

According to Reuters, citing a Ukrainian source, Kyiv and Washington have been discussing a proposal for a mutual halt to long-range aerial attacks as part of a new round of peace efforts. The proposal is narrower than a full ceasefire — ground combat could continue while both sides stop drone, missile, and air attacks. This arrangement could reduce strikes on cities and infrastructure without requiring either side to accept the current front line as a permanent border.

Humanitarian Toll: Civilian Casualties Up 37%

United Nations monitors verified 1,396 civilian deaths and 7,978 injuries in Ukraine during the first half of 2026 — a 37% increase from the same period in 2025. The UN assessment tied much of that increase to expanding missile and drone attacks.

Missile and drone launches leave radar tracks, satellite evidence, wreckage and identifiable impact sites, making violations easier to document than ground clashes. Russia and Ukraine briefly halted attacks on energy infrastructure in January, though differences over duration and scope quickly emerged.

Kremlin Dismissive, Putin Shows No Signs of Compromise

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov dismissed reports of an air ceasefire proposal on Sunday, calling them "newspaper reports and nothing more." He said Moscow would only assess concrete proposals once received.

Bloomberg, citing sources close to the Kremlin, reported that Putin has no plans to resume talks until Russian forces capture the remainder of Donbas. However, one US official said Washington believes Russia may be more receptive to an air ceasefire than it was months ago, as Ukrainian deep strikes inflict mounting economic pressure.

On July 23, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio met Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov in Manila, saying afterwards that any breakthrough would require "new ideas" after previous proposals failed.

3. Russia Launches Massive Overnight Aerial Assault on Ukraine

On the night of July 27-28, Russia launched a massive aerial assault on Ukraine, deploying 331 aerial weapons: 324 Shahed-type attack and decoy drones, 4 Kh-101 cruise missiles, and 3 Kh-47M2 Kinzhal aeroballistic missiles.

The Ukrainian Air Force reported intercepting 311 of the incoming weapons by 09:30 local time, including 309 drones and 2 cruise missiles. The primary target direction was Starokostiantyniv in Khmelnytskyi Oblast.

In Kyiv's Darnytskyi district, a 25-story residential building was struck. The blast wave shattered windows and damaged balcony glazing from the 3rd to 11th floors in one entrance. According to Kyiv City Military Administration head Tymur Tkachenko, eight people were injured, with four requiring hospitalization. Among the injured was a 2-year-old girl with shrapnel wounds to her foot and a 3-year-old child with shrapnel injuries.

In Dnipropetrovsk Oblast, drone and artillery attacks injured a 15-year-old girl and a 49-year-old woman. The attacks damaged an educational institution, an apartment building, three private houses, and utility buildings.

Chernihiv Supermarket Strike Kills Two

The previous day, a Russian daytime drone strike hit a supermarket in Chernihiv, killing two people — including a child — and wounding 25. Ukraine condemned the attack as a deliberate strike on civilians.

4. Jordan Intercepts Drones, West Bank Violence Escalates

Amid heightened regional tensions, Jordan's armed forces intercepted and shot down two drones on Monday morning. The military said air defense units neutralized the unmanned aircraft before they could cause harm, with no casualties or material damage reported. The origin of the drones was not disclosed.

Meanwhile, violence in the occupied West Bank continues to escalate. Israeli settlers attacked multiple Palestinian villages overnight Saturday, torching two mosques, a home, and numerous vehicles, and leaving graffiti reading "Benayahu's Revenge." The attacks followed a deadly clash near the village of Tal that left four Palestinians and two Israelis dead.

Former Israeli Chief of Staff Moshe Ya'alon accused far-right ministers Bezalel Smotrich and Itamar Ben Gvir of "encouraging, funding, and arming Jewish pogromists" to dispossess Palestinian communities. Over 550 retired Israeli generals and intelligence officials published an open letter urging the US to pressure Israel to crack down on settler violence.

5. Outlook: Three Fronts at a Crossroads

Global geopolitics stands at a rare crossroads, with the US-Iran negotiations, US-Ukraine coordination, and the Russia-Ukraine war all advancing simultaneously. Key developments to watch:

  • US-Iran track: The diplomatic window is open, but Trump insists it's "either a great deal or back to the battlefield." The next 48 hours are critical — whether Trump approves the 60-day MoU will determine whether the Middle East moves toward de-escalation or all-out escalation.
  • Russia-Ukraine track: The air ceasefire faces strong Russian resistance, but Ukraine's expanding long-range strike capabilities may force Moscow to recalculate. Zelensky's Washington meetings today will be crucial.
  • Regional spillover: Deepening Iran-Russia military cooperation (following Ukraine's strike on an Iranian vessel in the Caspian Sea) and continued Houthi threats in the Red Sea make the two conflict theaters increasingly difficult to separate.

As Secretary Rubio stated, the end of this conflict is "a matter of weeks, not months." But the interplay of red lines on all sides could shatter the fragile equilibrium at any moment.
