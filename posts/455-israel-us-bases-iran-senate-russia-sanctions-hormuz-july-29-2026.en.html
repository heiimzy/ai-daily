⚡ July 29 World Affairs Quadruple: Israel Confirms US Warplanes Used Its Bases to Strike Iran; Senate 86-12 Advances Russia Sanctions Bill; Iran Rejects Hormuz 50-50 Split, Threatens European Warships; IRGC Intercepts 3 Tankers

1. Israel Confirms US Warplanes Used Israeli Bases to Strike Iran

Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz publicly acknowledged for the first time Tuesday that U.S. fighter jets have been operating from Israeli military bases to conduct airstrikes against Iran — a revelation that exposes Israel's logistical role in the expanding U.S.-Iran conflict.

"The Iranians know that American planes are taking off from here to carry out strikes," Katz told Israel's right-leaning Channel 14. He issued a stark warning to Tehran: "We announced in the clearest terms that if Israel is fired upon, we will attack with all our might. We are prepared to strike Iran."

Although Israel has not directly participated in the recent phase of hostilities between the United States and Iran — which flared earlier this month after the collapse of an April ceasefire — Katz's remarks confirm Israel's behind-the-scenes role. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich separately described the current situation as "the best for us," adding that Israel has no interest in joining the fighting directly as long as the U.S. carries the burden.

Katz also revealed that Israel had strongly pushed to strike Iranian energy targets but was blocked by Washington. "We strongly want to attack Iranian energy targets, but the U.S. does not agree to that at this stage," he said, citing concerns that further escalation could trigger Iranian retaliation against neighboring states and a global oil crisis.

Over the past two days, Israel intercepted at least three drones near the Jordanian border. The disclosure raises concerns that Israel is being drawn deeper into the conflict.

2. U.S. Senate Overwhelmingly Advances Russia Sanctions Bill 86-12, Zelenskyy Watches from Gallery

The U.S. Senate voted 86-12 on Tuesday to advance the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026 — a sweeping sanctions package named after the late South Carolina senator. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy attended the vote from the Senate gallery after meeting with senators behind closed doors.

The legislation authorizes the president to impose tariffs of up to 200% on the world's largest purchasers of Russian oil and natural gas — with China and India at the top of the list — while expanding sanctions against Russian financial institutions, political elites, oligarchs, and the "shadow fleet" used to evade existing restrictions. It also extends Iran sanctions through 2031.

"Today in Washington, we had a big meeting with representatives of both parties — more than 60 senators were present," Zelenskyy said after the vote. "It was an honor to be present when the votes were counted. This is the first step towards implementing Lindsey's plans and certainly a step towards peace."

The bill must still clear final Senate passage and House consideration after the August recess to become law, but Tuesday's vote demonstrated rare bipartisan consensus on economic pressure against Moscow. Earlier in the day, Zelenskyy also held a closed-door meeting with President Trump at the White House, discussing Patriot missile production licensing and diplomatic efforts to end the Ukraine war.

3. Iran Formally Rejects Oman's 50-50 Hormuz Proposal, Threatens European Warships

Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi appeared on state television Tuesday to formally reject Oman's proposal for a 50-50 shared shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz, presenting a counter-demand: Iran must have full control of the inbound lane and partial control of the outbound lane.

"If Oman accepts this proposal, we will move to the next stage. If not, the strait will remain closed and we are ready for war's resumption," Gharibabadi declared. He stressed that the Strait of Hormuz will "never return to its pre-war state," calling control over the waterway a "new defense capability" integral to national security.

In perhaps the most provocative statement, Gharibabadi warned: "Any European naval vessel that approaches the Strait of Hormuz would be a legitimate target for us." The warning came amid reports that some European countries may consider joining U.S. efforts to reopen the strategic waterway.

Gharibabadi also denied that Iran has sought negotiations with the United States, claiming "it was Washington who sent messages pleading for an end to the conflict." Separately, Iran threatened to bar any country or company receiving compensation from frozen Iranian assets from using the Strait of Hormuz — a direct response to Trump's social media threat to use those funds to pay for damages to ships attacked in the waterway.

4. IRGC Intercepts and Boards 3 Oil Tankers in Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) Navy intercepted three foreign oil tankers in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday, firing warning shots and boarding the vessels after accusing them of "ignoring safety warnings and deviating from designated routes."

The IRGC Navy Command said in a statement that the three "violating oil tankers continued to move on an unsafe and illegal route despite our warnings and were hit and stopped." It did not identify the vessels, disclose their ownership, or specify the extent of damage.

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baqaei asserted Iran's legal jurisdiction over the area, warning that any unauthorized foreign vessels face similar action. The U.S. Fifth Fleet, based in Bahrain, has raised its alert level and deployed monitoring vessels.

Shipping traffic through the Strait of Hormuz — which normally carries about 20% of the world's oil — has collapsed to just 11 vessels on July 26, compared to a pre-war daily average of around 100, according to MarineTraffic data. Oil prices edged higher following the incident, with Brent crude rising 1.13% to $83/barrel and WTI jumping 4.11% to $83/barrel.

5. U.S.-Saudi Joint Strikes Target Iran-Backed Militias in Iraq

U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM) announced Tuesday that American and Saudi fighter aircraft struck "multiple terrorist logistics and weapons sites across eastern Iraq" in response to more than 30 IRGC-directed drone attacks over the previous 72 hours. The Popular Mobilization Forces (PMF) in Iraq confirmed at least 10 members killed in the strikes, with the hardest-hit province being Nineveh. The PMF condemned the operation as a "dangerous escalation and violation of Iraqi sovereignty."
