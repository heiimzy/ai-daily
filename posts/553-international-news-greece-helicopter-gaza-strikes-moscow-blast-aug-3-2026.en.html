⚡ Aug 3 World Update: Firefighting Helicopters Collide in Greece Killing 2, Gaza Strikes Kill at Least 13 After Hamas Disarmament Deal, Moscow Restaurant Blast Branded a Terrorist Act

Three stories are dominating the international agenda as Monday, August 3, gets underway: two firefighting helicopters collided mid-air west of Athens, Greece, killing two crew members and leaving two survivors; Israeli airstrikes hit three parts of Gaza for a second straight night just days after Hamas agreed to disarm, killing at least 13 Palestinians in the deadliest day in weeks; and a homemade bomb exploded outside an upscale Italian restaurant in central Moscow, killing three and wounding at least 21, with the city's mayor branding it a "brutal terrorist act".

Greece: Firefighting Helicopters Collide West of Athens, Danish Pilot and Greek Liaison Killed

According to the BBC, Al Jazeera and Greek media, two leased Bell firefighting helicopters collided mid-air late Sunday night near Psatha, about 65 km (42 miles) west of Athens, while battling a large wildfire on the Attica-Boeotia border, including the major blaze at Porto Germeno. Footage of the incident shows one helicopter's main rotor striking the other's, which bursts into flames and crashes into a ravine, while the second aircraft made an emergency landing in an area of low vegetation.

The Greek Fire Service confirmed the two fatalities were a Danish pilot and a Greek fire service liaison officer; the survivors - a British pilot and a Greek fire service coordinator - escaped with minor injuries and were taken to the 251 Air Force General Hospital in Athens. Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis confirmed the deaths on social media, saying the loss of a Greek coordinator and a Danish pilot while they were operating in the major fire at Porto Germeno "fills us all with grief." By protocol, all Bell helicopters across the country have been grounded pending review, and an investigation into the cause of the collision is underway.

"The loss of a Greek coordinator and a Danish pilot while they were operating in the major fire at Porto Germeno fills us all with grief." - Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis

The wildfire is among the worst Greece has faced this summer: more than 100 homes have been destroyed and over 10,000 hectares burned, with more than 500 firefighters struggling for 72 hours to contain multiple fronts. The flames have threatened the coastal town of Megara (population about 30,000), while a fresh blaze on the southern Ionian island of Kefalonia forced overnight evacuations. Greek daily Ekathimerini reported the fire may have been sparked by conductors on a private network transmitting electricity generated by wind turbines; two Greek citizens have been arrested on suspicion of negligence and a third suspect remains at large. With heatwaves intensifying across Europe this summer, the mid-air collision underscores the extreme risks of aerial firefighting.

Gaza: Israeli Strikes Kill at Least 13 for a Second Night After Hamas Agrees to Disarm

According to Reuters, NBC News and the BBC, after President Trump announced on Thursday that Hamas and other armed groups had agreed to "complete disarmament," Israeli aircraft struck Gaza for a second successive night, hitting Gaza City in the north, Deir al-Balah in the center and Khan Younis in the south from dawn on Sunday, killing at least 13 Palestinians - the biggest daily death toll in weeks (casualty figures ranged from 8 to 18 depending on the outlet, with Palestinian and Israeli officials still verifying).

Palestinian health officials and the Civil Defence Authority said the dead included: a couple and their child in an apartment in Gaza City, two people in a tent near the office of the Egyptian representative mission, one person near Jabalia in northern Gaza, a father, mother and their nine-year-old son in a house in Khan Younis, and at least four people in Deir al-Balah; a separate attack in Mawasi near Khan Younis wounded five people and set tents ablaze. An IDF spokesperson said the strikes were targeting "military operatives" and that two Hamas militants had been killed.

The key backdrop: Trump's Board of Peace - the U.S.-led body overseeing the Gaza ceasefire - published a 15-point roadmap on Friday setting out the final steps of the deal, under which a Palestinian governing committee (NCAG) would decommission and store Hamas's heavy weapons, tunnels and production sites, with an international stabilization force separating the sides. But Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen, a member of Prime Minister Netanyahu's security cabinet, told Army Radio on Sunday that there was no deal to halt attacks on Gaza and that he was "very skeptical" Hamas would disarm, noting Israel already controls 70% of the enclave and may need to take full control if Hamas does not hand over its weapons. Hamas says it will surrender weapons only after Israel stops its attacks and withdraws troops, and a senior Hamas official accused Israel of trying to impose a de facto situation through intensified strikes. Gaza health authorities say at least 1,230 Palestinians have been killed in Israeli strikes since the ceasefire was reached in October.

Moscow: Blast Outside Upscale Restaurant Kills 3, Wounds 21 - Mayor Brands It a "Terrorist Act"

According to the Associated Press, The Guardian and NBC News, shortly before 8 pm local time on Saturday evening, an explosion ripped through the area outside the Balzi Rossi Italian restaurant, located in one of Moscow's seven Stalin-era skyscrapers on Kudrinskaya Square. Police said a woman attempted to carry a homemade explosive device into the restaurant and was stopped by a security guard at the entrance, after which the device detonated; the woman, the guard and a customer were killed on the spot, and at least 21 people were injured. Russia's Kommersant daily, citing its own sources, said the bomb had been intended to maim and kill guests dining on the restaurant's summer terrace.

Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin called the attack a "brutal terrorist act" on Sunday, saying the victims were receiving care in the city's hospitals, but did not name any suspected perpetrator; Russia's National Anti-Terrorism Committee said a homemade explosive device was responsible. Russian outlet REN TV, citing a source, reported that two hospitalized victims later died of their injuries, which would raise the death toll to five. No group has claimed responsibility, and speculation on social media has linked the attack to the war in Ukraine. It is the latest in a series of explosions targeting civilian venues in Moscow, drawing renewed attention to Russia's domestic security situation.
