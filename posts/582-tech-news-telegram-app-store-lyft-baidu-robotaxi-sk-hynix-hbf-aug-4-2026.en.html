⚡ August 4 Tech Roundup: Telegram Pulled from Global App Store Then Restored, Lyft and Baidu Team Up for European Robotaxis, SK hynix Debuts HBF Standard at FMS 2026

Around midday on August 4, three big stories hit the global tech scene in quick succession: Telegram, the messaging app used by hundreds of millions, went through a dramatic "removed worldwide – restored" episode within a few hours; US ride-hailing giant Lyft and China's Baidu officially announced a partnership to deploy driverless robotaxis across Europe starting 2026; and at the opening of the FMS 2026 storage conference in Silicon Valley, SK hynix and Sandisk unveiled the industry's first High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) standard while publicly showing a 10th-generation 375-layer 4D NAND wafer. Here is this round's tech news roundup.

1. Telegram Pulled from the Global App Store, Restored About Two Hours Later

According to Reuters (republished by cryptopanic at 02:13 UTC / 10:13 Beijing time), Turkey's Anadolu Agency (August 4) and Russia's TASS (02:42 GMT, August 4), starting in the early hours of August 4, iPhone users in multiple regions found that Telegram had "vanished" from Apple App Store search results and charts: the official store page returned a 404 "The page you're looking for can't be found" error, making new downloads impossible. Data from independent monitor Apple Censorship showed the app unavailable across all 175 tested regional storefronts, including Russia, the US, Turkey and the Philippines, ruling out a localized block and pointing to a platform-wide removal.

About two hours later came the reversal. At roughly 02:37 UTC (10:37 Beijing time), Telegram confirmed to Anadolu and Reuters that "Telegram has been restored on the App Store and should soon be available again for all users"; by 03:25 UTC (11:25 Beijing time), multiple outlets confirmed the app's store page was back to a normal state, though direct search results were still occasionally delayed. As of this writing, neither Apple nor Telegram founder Pavel Durov has issued an official statement explaining the removal — speculation ranges from content moderation (App Review Guideline 1.1 "Objectionable Content" and 5.0 "Legal"), to a technical glitch or platform-level enforcement, but nothing has been confirmed.

In terms of impact, existing installations kept working normally for messaging, but new users could not download the app and iOS updates were frozen; Telegram's Android, web and desktop versions were unaffected. For context: in February 2018 Apple removed Telegram and Telegram X over illicit content in public channels, and the apps were restored within 24 hours after moderation fixes; in April 2024 Apple also pulled Telegram, WhatsApp, Signal and Threads from the Chinese App Store. This global episode also rattled Telegram-linked tokens and on-chain assets, which saw short-term price dips. Analysts advise iOS users not to uninstall the app until the listing is fully stable, and to avoid unofficial "restoration packages" that could be phishing traps.

2. Lyft and Baidu Team Up to Deploy Robotaxis in Europe from 2026

According to Cailian Press (August 4) and Reuters, US ride-hailing giant Lyft and China's Baidu announced a partnership to deploy driverless robotaxis in Europe starting 2026, beginning operations in the UK and Germany. Under the agreement, Lyft will handle platform operations including customer service and fleet dispatch, while Baidu supplies the autonomous vehicles and core technology — its sixth-generation mass-produced autonomous vehicle, the RT6, will join the Lyft platform once regulatory approvals are in place, with the fleet expected to grow to thousands of vehicles over the next few years. Following the news, both Lyft and Baidu rose more than 3% in US pre-market trading.

The deal marks Lyft's first autonomous ride-hailing push outside North America. Just last week, Lyft completed its acquisition of European ride-hailing app FreeNow, instantly gaining an operating network spanning nine European countries and more than 180 cities. On the demand side, the UK government has pulled forward its robotaxi commercialization timeline, targeting paid passenger service by spring 2026, with Uber and UK-based autonomous driving firm Wayve also waiting for the regulatory green light. For Baidu, this marks the first entry of its autonomous ride-hailing business into Europe: its Apollo Go service currently operates more than 1,000 robotaxis across 15 cities globally, having completed over 11 million rides; last month Baidu also struck a deal with Uber to deploy autonomous vehicles on its platform in markets outside the US and mainland China, focusing initially on Asia and the Middle East.

Analysts see the Lyft-Baidu "platform plus technology" combination as mirroring the multi-pronged strategies of Uber with Waymo, WeRide and Pony.ai. As regulatory frameworks in Europe and the US become clearer, 2026 could prove a watershed year for cross-border robotaxi scale-up — and for Chinese autonomous driving technology expanding overseas at a faster pace.

3. FMS 2026 Opens: SK hynix and Sandisk Unveil the First HBF Standard, 375-Layer 4D NAND Debuts

According to PR Newswire (August 3, Korea time), ChainCatcher (August 4, 08:28) and Yahoo News Taiwan (published August 4, 10:29), the FMS 2026 (Future of Memory and Storage) conference runs August 4–6 at the Santa Clara Convention Center in California. On opening day, SK hynix announced that it and Sandisk had jointly unveiled the industry's first High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) standard specification — roughly six months after the two companies formally launched the consortium in February. Published to the industry through the Open Compute Project (OCP), the spec covers capacities up to 512GB (in 8-high and 16-high NAND die stacks), three bandwidth grades (approximately 0.4TB/s to 3.0TB/s), and uses the UCIe interconnect for flexible integration with heterogeneous processors such as GPUs and CPUs. Google and Tenstorrent have joined the HBF consortium.

HBF is positioned as a brand-new storage tier between HBM and SSDs: it uses NAND for large-capacity scaling while delivering HBM-like high-speed data transfer, directly targeting the "bandwidth and capacity both needed" bottleneck of the AI inference era. At the booth, SK hynix also publicly showed, for the first time, its 10th-generation (V10) 375-layer 4D NAND wafer and products currently under development, delivering 2.5 times better performance per watt than the previous generation and designed for power-hungry AI data center environments; the company plans to begin mass production of high-performance, high-capacity enterprise SSDs (eSSDs) based on this technology early next year. In the opening keynote, SK hynix Executive VP and Head of Solution Development Kim Chun-sung proposed a "Tiered Memory" architecture to handle the data challenges of the agentic AI era, chaining different memory and storage types into one optimized unified system.

From a platform scare that was "removed worldwide, then restored," to Chinese and US robotaxi fleets squaring off in Europe, to storage makers carving out a "new memory tier" for the AI data flood — platform governance, autonomous driving and memory architecture all advanced on the same day. This week also brings AMD earnings (Tuesday after the close), SpaceX's first earnings report since listing (Tuesday after the close) and US non-farm payrolls (Friday) — all worth watching.
