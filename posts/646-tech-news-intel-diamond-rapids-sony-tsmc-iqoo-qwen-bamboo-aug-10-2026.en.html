⚡ Aug 10 General Tech Update: Intel's 256-Core Diamond Rapids Xeon Leaked, Sony & TSMC to Pour ¥1 Trillion into Kumamoto Image Sensors, iQOO Neo11 Ultimate Launches Aug 18, Qwen Open Platform Debuts, Bamboo Packaging Goes Mainstream

August 10 morning brought a burst of general-tech news: Intel is reportedly still planning a 256-core version of its next-gen Xeon, "Diamond Rapids"; Sony and TSMC plan to invest about ¥1 trillion in a Kumamoto joint venture to mass-produce next-gen image sensors for Apple's iPhone by 2029; iQOO Neo11 Ultimate is confirmed for an Aug 18 launch as the "industry's only 2K + 9100mAh performance flagship"; Alibaba's Qwen Open Platform went live with partners from logistics, real estate, finance, and more; and bamboo-based degradable express packaging entered pilot testing at major courier brands, targeting 1 billion units a year within five years. Details below; every source's publish time has been verified.

1. Intel Reportedly Still Planning a 256-Core "Diamond Rapids" Xeon

According to ITHome (11:09 AM Beijing time, Aug 10), ahead of COMPUTEX 2026 Intel said it plans to launch a next-gen Xeon, "Diamond Rapids," in 2027 with "50% more cores." Since its direct predecessor "Granite Rapids" tops out at 128 CPU cores, the industry widely read that as Diamond Rapids maxing out at 192 cores.

But X leaker @jaykihn0 has a different view: he says Intel will still ship a 256-core version of Diamond Rapids, paired with the 650W high-power "Johnson City" motherboard platform, while the regular up-to-192-core version uses a 500W-rated Johnson City. The same leaker previously said Intel had canceled the denser 512-core and 384-core "Diamond Rapids HD" variants. If the leak holds, a 256-core Xeon would give Intel a key weapon against AMD EPYC in the AI and high-density compute race (source: ITHome).

2. Sony & TSMC Plan a ¥1 Trillion Kumamoto JV for Next-Gen Image Sensors by 2029

Per ITHome, citing Nikkei (10:22 AM Beijing time, Aug 10), Sony Group and TSMC plan to start mass production of next-generation image-sensor semiconductors in Kumamoto Prefecture, southwestern Japan, as early as 2029. Production would be handled by a joint venture in which Sony holds about 60% and TSMC about 40%, with total investment estimated at roughly ¥1 trillion (about RMB 42.7 billion).

The two companies reached a basic agreement on R&D and production cooperation for next-gen image sensors in May and are expected to finalize an investment plan soon, establishing the JV by the end of fiscal 2026 (by March 2027). The JV is expected to supply high-performance camera sensors for Apple's iPhone; longer term, both firms want to push sensor performance so AI systems can recognize objects more accurately, targeting the emerging "Physical AI" space — AI controlling robots and vehicles. Production would sit inside a Sony Semiconductor Solutions image-sensor plant in Koshi, Kumamoto, with talks ongoing with Japan's METI over subsidies. A ¥1 trillion outlay equals about four years of Sony's semiconductor capital expenditure and approaches the $8.6 billion total investment in TSMC's first Kumamoto fab (sources: ITHome, Nikkei).

3. iQOO Neo11 Ultimate: Three Colors Revealed, Launching Aug 18

Per ITHome (11:15 AM Beijing time, Aug 10), the iQOO Neo11 Ultimate's three colors — Chengfeng, Jiying Black, and Chiguang White — have been officially revealed, with the phone launching and going on sale at 19:00 on Aug 18. iQOO bills it as the "industry's only 2K + 9100mAh performance flagship," featuring a top-tier domestic 2K display and the largest battery ever in a Neo device, with a focus on eye comfort, durability, and network stability.

ITHome's hands-on gallery shows the "Chiguang White" edition continuing the floating-porthole camera design with large rounded corners, finished with a new 3D flowing-light effect that shifts and shimmers at different angles. For reference, the iQOO Neo11 launched last October with a 6.82-inch 2K 144Hz display and Snapdragon 8 Elite, starting at RMB 2,599. The Ultimate edition will debut alongside the Z11S as the Neo lineup's next-gen performance flagship (source: ITHome).

4. Alibaba's Qwen Open Platform Goes Live: Rent, Ship, and Bank via Chat

Per ITHome (10:16 AM Beijing time, Aug 10), the Qwen Open Platform launched today, opening service integration across phones, PCs, and AI glasses to ecosystem partners and developers, with the first batch of partners spanning more than a dozen industries including logistics, real estate, local life services, finance, and automotive. Third parties can create AI agents that live as standalone chat spaces inside the Qwen app, delivering the full journey from consultation and recommendation to fulfillment.

Onboarded partners include SF Express, Tiandao, Lenovo Lexiang, Ziroom, Yumai Fund, Hello Rent-a-Car, Caiyun Weather, Kuaidi100, Shansong, Variflight, Midea, and Dudu Bus, with Alibaba ecosystem products like Cainiao, Quark Drive, and Quark Scan joining as well. Users simply @ a service in Qwen or tap the dot badge in the top-right corner. For example, the SF Express agent remembers your shipping addresses and can directly handle "send a document to my client in Shanghai," while Yumai Fund's agent filters and analyzes funds based on your investment needs and can diagnose an authorized portfolio for overlaps and concentration. The platform supports low-friction onboarding via standardized protocols, one-click authorization and end-to-end testing, and provides account, AI payment, and order infrastructure — with plans for proactive services like a personalized "rental calendar" (source: ITHome).

5. Bamboo-Based Degradable Express Packaging: Pilots Underway, 1 Billion Units a Year Targeted

Per ITHome, citing CCTV (10:53 AM Beijing time, Aug 10), bamboo has traditionally been made into furniture and tableware, but researchers now want to turn bamboo fiber into a new green material: in the lab, bamboo powder is ground, modified, and blended with fully biodegradable plastics to create bamboo-based degradable materials. In June 2026, the China Express Association issued a "Replace Plastic with Bamboo, Go Green" initiative proposing that annual usage of bamboo express packaging climb to 200M, 300M, 450M, 680M, and 1 billion units over five years — steady 50% year-over-year growth.

According to Tao Chunyan, deputy director of the Sanming Post Office in Fujian, major courier brands have already piloted bamboo-based packaging in Sanming with strong feedback, and collaboration is advancing. Liu Yuxiang of SF Express Sanming noted the current pain point is price — bamboo packaging bags cost about 10% more than traditional ones — but believes economies of scale will narrow the gap (sources: ITHome, CCTV).
