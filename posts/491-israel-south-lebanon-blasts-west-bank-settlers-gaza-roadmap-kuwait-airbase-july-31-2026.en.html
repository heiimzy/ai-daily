⚡ July 31 World Roundup: Israel Pounds Southern Lebanon Overnight, Settlers Storm West Bank Town, New Gaza Roadmap Details, Iran Claims Strike on Kuwait Airbase

Israel Launches Massive Overnight Strikes on Southern Lebanon, Destroying Hezbollah Tunnels

Hours after Hamas agreed to the "historic" disarmament deal, Israeli forces launched a major overnight assault on southern Lebanon in the early hours of July 31. According to Lebanon's National News Agency (NNA), a series of massive explosions rang out after midnight across the southern areas of Yohmor al-Chqif, Arnoun and Kfar Tebnit, with shockwaves felt as far away as el-Kharroub and Khalde on the outskirts of Beirut.

In the Marjayoun district, violent explosions near Beaufort Castle shattered windows and damaged homes in the nearby town of Qlayaa. Israeli forces also combed the outskirts of al-Mansouri and Biyout al-Sayyad in the Tyre District with heavy machine-gun fire while launching artillery shells at al-Mansouri, and tank movements were reported from the Baraachit hill toward Aainata in the Bint Jbeil District.

CNN, citing Israeli officials, reported that Israel destroyed tunnels in southern Lebanon with tons of explosives, claiming the underground infrastructure was central to a plan by Iran-backed Hezbollah to invade communities in northern Israel. The operation comes as the fragile framework governing the Israel-Hezbollah front shows fresh signs of strain, raising fears that tensions could flare again.

West Bank: Armed Settlers Storm Tal Under Israeli Protection, 7 Arrested in Nablus

The West Bank saw a parallel escalation. Dozens of Israeli settlers stormed the town of Tal, south of Nablus, in the early hours of July 31 under the protection of Israeli forces, according to Al Jazeera correspondents on the ground, with footage showing armed settlers among the group. Last week, settlers launched a deadly raid on the town that left four Palestinians and two Israelis dead, and the area has remained on edge ever since.

Israeli forces began raiding Tal on Thursday, seizing four homes and converting them into military barracks, sources told Al Jazeera. On Friday morning, the Israeli army arrested seven Palestinians from the Nablus governorate amid raids in several neighborhoods of Nablus city as well as the towns of Tal and Salem — at least three of them from Tal, where settlers have been carrying out what Palestinian media described as retaliatory attacks after two Israelis were killed there last Friday, with the military providing protection rather than intervention.

New Gaza Roadmap Details: National Committee to Audit Finances, Settle $400M in Debts Over Three Years

As Hamas confirmed its acceptance of the disarmament roadmap, implementation details are beginning to emerge. A senior Hamas source told Al Jazeera on July 31 that the agreement includes protections for employees who lose their jobs or retire during the transitional period, guaranteeing them the benefits they are entitled to under Palestinian law.

The source added that the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG) will carry out a full audit of the territory's financial and administrative status, evaluate legitimate debts to suppliers and contractors, and seek to address liabilities of up to $400 million over three years. Under the roadmap, the NCAG will be the sole entity authorized to hold, store and control weapons in Gaza, with heavy weapons, tunnels and military production sites dismantled and stored under its supervision alongside an international monitoring mechanism. The International Stabilization Force (ISF) will work with a newly formed Palestinian police force to take responsibility for security as Israeli troops withdraw incrementally in step with verification progress.

Egypt, the US, Qatar and Turkey — the mediating countries — are expected to hold a summit in Cairo to discuss implementation of the second phase of the ceasefire plan. The Board of Peace said the conditions for the roadmap's formal entry into force will be announced within two weeks.

Iran Claims Drone Strike on Kuwait's Ahmed Al-Jaber Airbase; US Has Not Confirmed

The US-Iran conflict's front lines also continue to spread. The Iranian army said on July 31 that its forces had targeted strategic US military facilities at Kuwait's Ahmed Al-Jaber airbase in a drone attack, describing the operation as retaliation for a US strike on a residential home on Qeshm Island. In a statement carried by the Tasnim news agency, the army said the attack hit hangars, satellite communication systems and equipment warehouses, adding that the base serves as a vital hub for US aerial surveillance and operational support in the region.

As of writing, neither US Central Command (CENTCOM) nor Kuwaiti authorities had confirmed the strike. CNN's live coverage early Friday noted the attack on the Kuwait base was reported by state media, with Kuwait's army yet to confirm. The claim follows a day in which Kuwait's Defense Ministry denounced an Iranian attack on a building belonging to a Chinese company in the north that killed one worker. Previous Iranian claims — including the destruction of US F-35 jets and a tanker breaking the US naval blockade — have each been rejected by CENTCOM.

Analysts note that behind the short-lived optimism of the Hamas disarmament deal, multiple Middle East fronts are burning simultaneously — southern Lebanon, the West Bank and the Gulf all escalated within hours of each other, underscoring that the five-month regional conflict is far from over.
