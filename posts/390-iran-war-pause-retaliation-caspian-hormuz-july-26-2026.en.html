⚡ July 26 International Roundup: Iran Formally Halts Retaliatory Strikes, Trump Shelves Escalation Over Missile Shortages, Ukraine Strikes Iranian Ship in Caspian Opening New Front

Iran Announces Halt to Retaliatory Attacks

Iran's military officially announced Sunday (July 26) that it has suspended retaliatory attacks against US allies across the Middle East. Army spokesman Mohammad Akraminia stated: "These attacks continued until two nights ago, but over the past two nights the Americans have stopped their attacks. Since our strategy has essentially been retaliatory, we have also halted our retaliatory operations."

The declaration marks the first simultaneous de-escalation by both sides after 13 consecutive nights of intense US airstrikes on Iran and daily Iranian retaliatory drone and missile attacks on US bases in Jordan, Kuwait, Bahrain, and the UAE. Akraminia warned, however, that if the US restrikes, the war will "expand further geographically," particularly amid concerns that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's planned visit to Washington next week could push the US toward renewed escalation.

Trump Shelves Major Escalation: Patriot Interceptor Shortage Takes Center Stage

The New York Times reported Saturday that President Donald Trump has set aside, at least for now, plans to sharply escalate the US military assault against Iran. Two people briefed on the discussion said Trump made the decision after a closed-door meeting Friday with top advisers and Cabinet members. The core concern: the Pentagon's rapidly shrinking inventory of Patriot anti-missile interceptors — over 1,200 have been used in the nearly five-month war at more than $4 million each, leaving stocks at alarmingly low levels.

CNN further reported that Vice President JD Vance and Joint Chiefs Chairman Gen. Dan Caine jointly raised munitions stockpile concerns during the White House meeting. A Defense Department source said operations are "on a hold" with no timeline for approving new strike plans. While the Pentagon has rushed additional forces, Patriot systems, and supplies to the Middle East, officials widely agree that resuming major combat would dangerously deplete already strained air defense munitions.

However, the US naval blockade against Iran remains in full effect. CENTCOM said late Saturday that as of July 25, it has redirected 12 commercial vessels trying to run the blockade, disabled 2 that didn't comply, and boarded 2 for inspections.

Ukraine Strikes Iranian Vessel in Caspian Sea: Two Wars Entwine

Iran's Foreign Ministry summoned Ukraine's chargé d'affaires in Tehran on Sunday to protest a Ukrainian strike on an Iranian commercial vessel in the Caspian Sea. The attack killed one sailor and injured several others, according to Iran's Islamic Republic News Agency, which described the act as "hostile and criminal" in violation of UN Charter principles.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy confirmed on X that Kyiv's forces carried out "long-range strikes in the Caspian Sea — including vessels used in military cargo shipments involving Iran, as well as a warship." Ukraine's Security Service (SBU) further confirmed the target was a merchant vessel shuttling military cargo between Iran and Russia, aiming to disrupt Iran's supply of drones and weapons to Moscow.

Zelenskyy also alleged that Russia is sharing satellite intelligence with Tehran to help identify and strike US military facilities in the Middle East. If confirmed, this would represent an unprecedented level of military-intelligence cooperation between Moscow and Tehran. Analysts note that Ukraine's Caspian operation is increasingly intertwining the US-Iran war with the Russia-Ukraine conflict, further internationalizing both wars.

Red Sea Crisis Deepens: Houthis Strike Saudi Aramco Facilities

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi rebels announced Saturday (July 25) that they launched ballistic missiles and drones at two Saudi Aramco facilities along the Red Sea coast. The first wave targeted the Aramco refinery in Jizan, where NASA satellite imagery and verified local video showed massive fires and plumes of smoke rising from the facility — which has a processing capacity of 400,000 barrels per day. The second wave struck facilities in Yanbu, where two missiles aimed at oil installations were intercepted by Greek-operated Patriot batteries.

In response, the Saudi-led coalition conducted airstrikes on Houthi military targets in Hodeidah province — the first publicly acknowledged strikes since the 2022 ceasefire. Coalition spokesman Brig. Gen. Turki al-Maliki said the strikes targeted "legitimate military targets used by the Houthis to threaten commercial vessels in the Red Sea." On Sunday, the Houthis claimed to have shot down a Turkish-made armed reconnaissance drone belonging to Saudi Arabia and reiterated that their naval blockade of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait remains in effect.

The Red Sea has become Saudi Arabia's critical alternative export route. Since Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, Riyadh has been forced to redirect oil exports through Yanbu via an east-west pipeline. Analysts warn that if the Houthis succeed in blocking the Bab el-Mandeb, both of the world's major energy chokepoints could be simultaneously disrupted, potentially sending oil prices even higher.

Iran-Oman Hormuz Talks Show Progress

Despite the stalemate in high-level US-Iran negotiations, Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei confirmed Sunday that Tehran and Muscat held multiple rounds of technical talks on Strait of Hormuz management over the weekend. The Omani delegation left Tehran Saturday afternoon, but Baghaei said progress was made and consultations will continue. A regional official involved in mediation efforts revealed that both Washington and Tehran are interested in returning to the interim ceasefire, with the core compromise being: Iran manages strait transit with fewer restrictions on vessels.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps Navy earlier Sunday claimed it has "established complete authority over the Strait of Hormuz," forcing six vessels to anchor after warnings in the past 24 hours. Meanwhile, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi stated there is "no military solution" to the Saudi-Houthi conflict and offered Tehran's assistance in facilitating dialogue between the parties.
