⚡ Aug 2 International Update: Ukraine Launches Massive Drone Raid Deep Into Russia, Armenia's Government Resigns, Zimbabwe's 'Shutdown Day' Passes Quietly, Israeli Minister Calls for New Gaza Settlements

Sunday, August 2, brought a wave of developments across the globe: on the Russia-Ukraine front, Ukrainian drones launched a massive overnight raid on Russia's Volga River region, with Moscow reporting 635 drones shot down in a single night; in the Caucasus, Armenia's president accepted the government's resignation under the constitution as Pashinyan prepares to form a new cabinet; in southern Africa, Zimbabwe's opposition-called "national shutdown" passed quietly under a heavy police presence; and in the Middle East, an Israeli minister called for new settlements in Gaza while Iran responded defiantly to threats.

Ukraine's Massive Drone Raid on Russia's Volga Region: Samara Warehouse Hit, 2 Dead in Engels

According to Reuters and The Moscow Times, in the early hours of August 2, Ukrainian drones staged a large-scale attack on Russia's Volga River region. Samara region Governor Vyacheslav Fedorishchev confirmed that a Wildberries logistics warehouse in Novosemeykino, about 800 km from the front line, was struck and caught fire - Wildberries is Russia's largest online retailer, often described as "Russia's answer to Amazon." Saratov region Governor Roman Busargin reported that two people were killed when a drone hit a residential building in the city of Engels, with civilian infrastructure damaged in both Engels and Saratov.

Russia's Defense Ministry said air defenses intercepted and destroyed 635 drones overnight across 16 regions, including Belgorod, Bryansk, Voronezh, the Moscow region and Crimea; Moscow Mayor Sergey Sobyanin said six drones were downed on the approaches to the capital. Ukraine has been systematically striking Wildberries facilities since July 18 - around 18 attacks in two weeks have destroyed roughly 38% of the retailer's major warehousing capacity, pushing prices for some goods up 10-30%, and the Kremlin has acknowledged it may have to financially prop up the company.

Armenia: President Accepts Government's Resignation, Pashinyan to Form New Cabinet

According to the Armenpress news agency, Armenian President Vahagn Khachaturyan signed a decree on August 2 formally accepting the government's resignation. The move followed constitutional procedure on the day of the inaugural session of the newly elected 9th National Assembly: under Article 130 of the constitution, the government submits its resignation on the first day the new parliament begins its work. Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan had signed the letter of resignation addressed to the president, and the decree was published on the president's official website.

Under the procedure, the president will appoint the candidate nominated by the parliamentary majority as prime minister. Pashinyan's Civil Contract party retains a majority in the new parliament, so he is expected to be re-appointed, with the new cabinet to be formed within 15 days. Pashinyan had announced after the June elections that the government would resign on August 2, making this a procedural change of government rather than a political crisis.

Zimbabwe: 'National Shutdown Day' Passes Quietly Amid Constitutional Dispute

According to Al Jazeera, Harare's streets were unusually quiet on August 2. Opposition groups and civil society activists had called for a nationwide shutdown to protest constitutional amendments signed into law in July - changes that move the next presidential election from 2028 to 2030, extend presidential and parliamentary terms from five to seven years, and would replace direct presidential elections with a system in which parliament elects the president. Critics say the amendments could allow President Emmerson Mnangagwa to remain in office until 2030.

Police were deployed across the city in force: they blocked access to a popular park and parked water cannon trucks in an open space used for previous demonstrations. By mid-morning, the central business district was slower than usual but no mass protests materialized, and the city gradually returned to its normal rhythm. The opposition People's Coalition has challenged the amendments before the Constitutional Court, and the political dispute over the constitutional changes continues.

Middle East: Israeli Minister Calls for New Gaza Settlements, Iran Responds Defiantly

According to Al Jazeera's live coverage, Israel's far-right Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called on August 2 for the government to approve the construction of three new settlements along the northern fence of the Gaza Strip, saying the Settlements Administration under his leadership was "prepared to establish 3 settlements in the northern sector of the Gaza Strip" and calling on Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu to "give the green light." Israel evacuated all its settlements in Gaza in its 2005 unilateral disengagement plan; Smotrich's call is widely seen as a challenge to the peace process around the Trump Peace Board's 15-point roadmap.

Meanwhile, Iran's acting Defense Minister Majid Ibn al-Reza said on August 2 that Tehran views every threat from its adversaries as "real and credible", "even if made as part of psychological warfare... We will neither be caught off guard nor remain passive." Gaza's Health Ministry put the total death toll from the war since October 2023 at 73,356, with at least 1,230 Palestinians killed since the ceasefire took effect.
