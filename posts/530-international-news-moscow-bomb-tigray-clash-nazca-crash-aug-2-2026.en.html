⚡ Aug 2 World News: Bombing at Upscale Moscow Restaurant Kills 3 and Wounds 21, Fighting Resumes in Ethiopia's Tigray, 13 Die in Peru Nazca Lines Tourist Plane Crash

Over the past 24 hours, several international news threads heated up simultaneously across Europe, Africa and South America: an explosion at an upscale restaurant in central Moscow was confirmed by Russia's National Anti-Terrorism Committee as a homemade-bomb attack that killed three people and wounded 21; fresh armed clashes erupted in western Tigray, Ethiopia, in what analysts call the most serious military escalation since the 2022 peace agreement; and a tourist plane flying over Peru's Nazca Lines crashed, killing all 13 people on board.

Moscow Restaurant Bombing: 3 Killed, 21 Wounded, Confirmed as a Homemade Bomb Attack

At around 8:10 p.m. local time on Saturday, August 1 (1:10 a.m. Beijing time on August 2), an explosion ripped through the upscale Italian restaurant Balzi Rossi on the ground floor of a Stalin-era skyscraper on Kudrinskaya Square in central Moscow, killing at least three people and wounding 21. Russia's National Anti-Terrorism Committee, quoted by the Tass state news agency, confirmed that the blast was caused by a homemade improvised explosive device (IED): an unidentified woman attempted to carry the device into the restaurant and was stopped by a security guard, at which point the device detonated near the entrance, killing the woman, the guard and a restaurant customer. The wounded suffered injuries of varying severity.

The blast happened during the restaurant's summer terrace hours; the venue's website showed it had been closed on Saturday for a private event. Armed police quickly sealed off the scene and the Moscow Investigative Committee opened a probe. Notably, Russian state television initially speculated that a gas canister may have exploded, but the National Anti-Terrorism Committee later ruled that out and confirmed it was a bombing - one of the rare confirmed terrorist-style explosions in central Moscow in recent years. There is intense interest in whether the attack is linked to the wave of strikes inside Russian territory amid the war in Ukraine, but no group has claimed responsibility so far.

Russia's National Anti-Terrorism Committee (via Tass): "A homemade explosive device blew up at a restaurant on Kudrinskaya Square in Moscow, killing three people." CNN cited the committee saying the dead included the suspected bomber, a security guard and a customer.

Fighting Resumes in Ethiopia's Tigray: Federal Government and TPLF Trade Blame, Peace Deal Faces Its Sternest Test

The Horn of Africa is ablaze once again. Al Jazeera reported that fighting broke out at dawn on August 1 in western Tigray, in the direction of Sheraro (Shererina), near the border with Sudan, involving heavy artillery and still ongoing at the time of reporting. The Tigray regional government, led by the Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF), said from the regional capital Mekelle that federal forces had launched the assault - an "open war" - alleging that the federal government's long-running blockade, siege and drone campaign against the region had "escalated into full-scale war." It called on Tigrayan forces, civil society and religious institutions to prepare for a struggle of "national survival" under the slogan "No to National Destruction."

On the federal side, minister Abraham Belay, who led Tigray's transitional government during the war, confirmed the clashes but accused forces loyal to TPLF chairman Debretsion Gebremichael of starting them. Kjetil Tronvoll, an Ethiopia specialist at Oslo New University College, warned this is "the most serious incident" since the 2022 Pretoria peace deal - a "clear military escalation and the most serious confrontation in northern Ethiopia for some time," which could mark either the start of a wider military campaign or a coercive show of force aimed at deterring the Tigrayan side.

In context, the clash stems from the November 2022 Pretoria Agreement that ended a two-year war killing hundreds of thousands (an African Union estimate put the toll at roughly 600,000), yet core disputes over disarmament and the TPLF's legal status were never truly resolved. Relations have deteriorated for months: in June the United States imposed visa restrictions on TPLF "hardliners" accused of blocking peace, and by mid-July the TPLF had declared the Pretoria deal "effectively dead," even as US Ambassador Ervin Massinga traveled to Mekelle to try to preserve it. Regional analyst Faisal Roble cautioned: "Without a powerful international intervention, we could be heading to a much larger war."

Analyst Kjetil Tronvoll, quoted by Al Jazeera: "This is a clear military escalation and the most serious confrontation in northern Ethiopia for some time - either the start of a wider military campaign or a coercive show of force."

Tourist Plane Crash Near Peru's Nazca Lines Kills All 13 on Board

In South America, a small sightseeing plane flying over the Nazca Lines in southern Peru crashed at around 12:55 p.m. local time on August 1, killing all 13 people on board. "We have information that 11 passengers and two crew members have died," Police Major Jorge Andrade told reporters at the crash site. The aircraft, part of the fleet of Peruvian sightseeing company Aerodiana, had taken off from Pisco Airport in the Ica department; most of those aboard were tourists heading to view the UNESCO-listed Nazca Lines geoglyphs. The crash site was quickly engulfed in flames, preventing any rescue attempts, and no one survived. The cause of the crash remains under investigation.

The Nazca Lines are a series of colossal geoglyphs carved into the desert floor of southern Peru between 500 BC and AD 500, one of South America's most famous archaeological and tourist landmarks. The disaster is among Peru's deadliest sightseeing-aviation accidents in recent years and has thrown the spotlight back on the safety of the Pisco-to-Nazca sightseeing route.
