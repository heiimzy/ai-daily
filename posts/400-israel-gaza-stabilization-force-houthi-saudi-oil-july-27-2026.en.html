⚡ July 27 International Politics Roundup: Israel Approves International Stabilization Force for Gaza — Netanyahu Heads to Washington Today; Houthi Ballistic Missiles Hammer Saudi Aramco as Oil Surges Past $100; Kremlin Flatly Rejects 'Freeze' Proposal

1. Historic Shift: Israel's Security Cabinet Approves International Stabilization Force for Gaza

In a landmark decision on Sunday (July 26), Israel's political-security cabinet narrowly approved the deployment of the International Stabilization Force (ISF) — a core component of President Donald Trump's 20-point Gaza Peace Plan — into the Gaza Strip. This marks the first time since the October 7, 2023 conflict that Israel has authorized a multinational force to enter Gaza territory.

According to Israel Hayom and KAN public broadcaster reports, the cabinet's approval clears the legal path for the ISF to establish a headquarters near the southern Gaza city of Rafah. The initial contingent of approximately 200 personnel will come from Morocco, Uganda, Albania, and Kazakhstan. Israeli Foreign Ministry officials confirmed that each foreign contingent's entry requires separate approval from the Prime Minister, Defense Minister, and Foreign Minister, with Israel retaining veto power over the ISF's composition.

Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar stated during the vote: "This is an international plan; it may not succeed, but we should not be the ones to undermine it." National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir cast the sole dissenting vote, arguing that Hamas has not fulfilled its commitment to disarm and demilitarize Gaza, and Israel should not proceed with reconstruction.

IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir ordered the military on Sunday morning to prepare for the entry of foreign forces from Morocco and Kosovo. The force will primarily conduct policing and public order missions in areas outside full IDF operational control. Some personnel will be stationed at bases inside Israel near the Gaza border communities, while others will be housed in designated compounds inside the Strip.

Netanyahu Departs for Washington Today: Trump Meeting to Shape War's Trajectory

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is scheduled to depart for Washington on Monday (July 27) and meet President Trump at the White House on Tuesday (July 28). This will be the first face-to-face meeting between the two leaders since February 2026. Key agenda items include:

  • Iran's nuclear program — Netanyahu told Fox News on Sunday that Iran's nuclear weapons program "must end, with or without an agreement." He warned that if Iran attacks Israel again, "it will make a terrible mistake."
  • Gaza stabilization follow-through — Israel aims to demonstrate cooperation with the Trump administration's initiative to secure continued diplomatic backing. The pilot phase, if successful, is expected to expand to 500 Moroccan troops in the Rafah refugee camp.
  • US-Israel military coordination — During the fragile pause in US-Iran strikes, Israel seeks assurances that its security interests will not be sacrificed in any Washington-Tehran negotiations.

Trump's 20-point Gaza plan, unveiled in January 2025 and endorsed by UN Security Council Resolution 2803 in November 2025, establishes four governing bodies: the Board of Peace (BoP), the Gaza Executive Council, the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG, a technocratic government), and the International Stabilization Force (ISF). Al Jazeera reported that most Western countries have declined to join the Board of Peace.

2. Red Sea Escalation: Houthi Ballistic Missiles Strike Saudi Aramco — Brent Crude Breaches $100

While the US and Iran observe a third consecutive day without direct military strikes, fighting on the Red Sea front has escalated dramatically. Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi forces launched a massive ballistic missile and drone assault on two strategically critical Saudi Aramco facilities on the Red Sea coast on July 25-26.

Jizan Refinery Hit: NASA Satellite Confirms Large Fire

Houthi military spokesman Brigadier General Yahya Saree announced the group targeted "sensitive Aramco-affiliated facilities" in Jizan and Yanbu with "dozens of ballistic missiles and drones." NASA's FIRMS satellite fire detection system registered an abnormal thermal anomaly at the Jizan oil facility at 01:17 UTC on July 26, independently confirming the attack's impact.

The Jizan refinery, with a processing capacity of approximately 400,000 barrels per day (about 4% of Aramco's total global liquids production), houses the world's largest integrated gasification combined-cycle plant. Asian crude traders reported preliminary indications of physical damage at the site. Witnesses reported multiple explosions near Jizan, forcing commercial flights to divert or enter holding patterns.

Yanbu Air Defense: Greek-Operated Patriot System Intercepts in First Combat Engagement

Over Yanbu — Saudi Arabia's primary Red Sea export terminal — two Houthi ballistic missiles were intercepted by a Patriot air defense system. Critically, the system was operated not by US or Saudi forces but by Greek military personnel deployed under a bilateral agreement with Riyadh. Greek security officials confirmed that their Patriot battery intercepted two ballistic missiles and one drone over Yanbu.

Yanbu's strategic importance has soared in 2026. Since Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz, Saudi Arabia has rerouted crude via the 1,201-kilometer East-West Pipeline (Petroline) to the Red Sea. Pipeline throughput hit a record 7 million barrels per day in March. Kpler shipping data shows Yanbu handled approximately 92% of Saudi Arabia's seaborne crude exports in June 2026, loading 4.7 million barrels per day in mid-July. This creates a structural vulnerability: every tanker departing Yanbu must transit south through the Houthi-controlled Bab al-Mandeb Strait.

UBS strategist Giovanni Staunovo noted Brent crude has surged roughly $28/barrel in just three weeks, as global oil markets face multi-region supply chain disruptions. Brent crossed the $100/barrel threshold on Friday, while US national retail gasoline prices averaged $4.09/gallon.

Analysts warn that if the Houthis execute a full blockade of the Bab al-Mandeb Strait — through which 12% of global trade and 25% of container traffic passes — both of the world's two most critical oil chokepoints (Hormuz and Bab al-Mandeb) would be simultaneously obstructed, potentially triggering an energy crisis exceeding the 1973 oil embargo.

3. Kremlin Flatly Rejects 'Freeze' Proposal: Russia Must Achieve All Objectives in Ukraine

Kazakh President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev, during a meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin on July 26, proposed "freezing" the Russia-Ukraine conflict and restarting negotiations under a modified "Istanbul 2.0" framework. The Kremlin's response was unambiguous — impossible.

Putin's spokesman Dmitry Peskov told Russian media: "We will not agree to a freeze of the war until Russia achieves all of its set objectives." Peskov emphasized that achieving these goals remains the main condition for ending hostilities, and Tokayev's proposal can only be discussed in relation to this aim.

Peskov reiterated conditions laid out by Russia's Foreign Ministry two years ago: "They are clear and consistent. Therefore, all of this could end as early as today, by the end of the day, if the Kyiv regime makes the appropriate decisions."

Tokayev had told Putin bluntly that he does not understand Russia's reasons for the war against Ukraine. The Kazakh leader proposed freezing hostilities on modified terms from the earlier Istanbul talks. Moscow's categorical rejection signals that despite pressure on multiple fronts, Putin remains committed to full control over Ukraine's four eastern oblasts and Crimea, accepting no "freeze" compromises.

Meanwhile, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk demanded Poland have a direct seat at any Russia-Ukraine peace talks, warning Warsaw will not recognize agreements made without its participation. British intelligence has warned the Kremlin may mount a direct provocation against NATO in the "coming months."

Markets' pricing of "multi-front war premium" may rise significantly in the weeks ahead.
