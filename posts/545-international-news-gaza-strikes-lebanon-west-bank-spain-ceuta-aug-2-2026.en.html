⚡ Aug 2 International Update: Israeli Strike Kills 2 in Gaza Tent Camp as Daily Toll Hits 14, Phosphorus Shells Ignite South Lebanon, Israeli Minister Says Israel Should Take 'Full Control' of Gaza, Ceuta Crisis Leaves 67 Dead

Sunday, August 2, brought simultaneous escalation across the Middle East and Europe: in the Gaza Strip, Israeli strikes continued with the daily death toll rising to 14, the latest drone attack hitting a tent camp for displaced people in the Rimal neighborhood; Israeli forces fired phosphorus shells that ignited forests in southern Lebanon; demolition notices were issued in the occupied West Bank; an Israeli minister publicly argued for "full control" of Gaza; and across Europe, the political fallout of Spain's Ceuta migrant crisis swept through the EU - at least 67 people died attempting the crossing, Italy suspended its Schengen agreement with Spain, and EU interior ministers are set to hold an emergency meeting.

Gaza: Drone Strike on Rimal Tent Camp Raises Daily Toll to 14

According to Al Jazeera's live blog, an Israeli drone strike hit a tent sheltering displaced people in the Rimal neighborhood, west of Gaza City, on the afternoon of August 2, killing two Palestinians - one of them a child - and wounding eight others. Health officials said at least 14 people have been killed in Gaza so far today. Over the past 48 hours, Israeli warplanes have struck multiple targets, most of them residential buildings, apartments and civilian gatherings, flying at low altitude without issuing prior warnings; Israel says the sites housed members of Hamas or other armed groups.

The strikes come as Trump's "Board of Peace" published a 15-point roadmap on Friday for implementing the second phase of the Gaza ceasefire agreement - under which Hamas agreed to disarm and Israel would withdraw gradually. Since the October 2025 ceasefire took effect, more than 1,222 Palestinians have been killed and over 4,000 injured in Israeli operations, according to local health authorities, fueling widespread skepticism about the gap between "paper deals" and reality on the ground.

"Gaza's death toll for the day has risen to 14 - an Israeli drone strike hit a tent housing displaced people in the Rimal neighborhood, killing two Palestinians including a child and wounding eight." - Al Jazeera live blog, 14:02 GMT, August 2

Southern Lebanon: Israeli Phosphorus Shells Ignite Forests

According to Lebanon's National News Agency, the Israeli army fired phosphorus shells at Ali al-Taher Heights in southern Lebanon on August 2, igniting fires in the area's forests. The area sits on the outskirts of the Nabatieh al-Fawqa municipality. The shelling follows Israel's demolition last Friday (July 31) of a tunnel beneath the 12th-century Beaufort Castle - a move Lebanese President Joseph Aoun protested as sending a "negative signal" on the eve of truce talks - as tensions along the southern border keep escalating.

West Bank: Demolition Notices for Seven Homes in Nahalin

According to the Wafa news agency, Israeli forces raided the West Bank town of Nahalin, west of Bethlehem, early on August 2 and issued demolition notices for seven homes. Mayor Naeem Fanoun confirmed the verbal notices were delivered during a raid on the town this morning. Israeli troops and settlers have recently intensified raids and attacks across the occupied West Bank, with Palestinians killed, injured or arrested, homes set on fire and agricultural land destroyed.

Israeli Energy Minister Cohen: Israel Should Take 'Full Control' of Gaza

According to Al Jazeera, Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen said on August 2 that Israel, which controls 70 percent of Gaza, should take "full control" of the enclave. Cohen also said there was no deal to halt attacks on Gaza. He is the latest senior Israeli leader to voice resistance to a US-brokered deal that would see Hamas disarm - far-right National Security Minister Itamar Ben-Gvir had earlier dismissed the draft agreement as "not acceptable," while Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has yet to comment publicly.

Spain's Ceuta Migrant Crisis: At Least 67 Dead, EU Political Storm

According to the BBC, tens of thousands of migrants swam into the Spanish exclave of Ceuta on Thursday (July 30). Most have now returned to Morocco, escorted by Spanish soldiers and police, but the extraordinary surge has left at least 67 people dead - their bodies pulled from the sea where they floated alongside abandoned rubber rings and flippers.

The crisis has shaken Europe politically: Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez accused some EU countries of "attacking" Spain instead of showing support; Italy's hard-right Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni quickly posted images of migrants racing through Ceuta's streets mocking "the Sanchez model," then announced she was suspending Italy's Schengen agreement with Spain, citing a security threat - a move that has left Madrid fuming. Meloni also called for an urgent meeting of EU interior ministers on Tuesday, a request signed by almost two dozen EU heads of state calling for the elimination of all policies that could act as "pull factors" to Europe.

Investigations point to viral social media posts promising an easy path to Europe - accounts shouting "Spain is Open" and filming themselves jumping into the sea - as the trigger for the influx. "All their promises were false," the BBC reported. Spain is now extending its border fence out to sea with lines of orange buoys patrolled by ships to deter future swimmers, while EU interior ministers will discuss strengthening external borders next week.

"The surge of arrivals has left at least 67 people dead. The social media promises of 'Spain is Open' were all false - dozens of young people set out to sea chasing a vision they'd seen online, and are now being sent back to Morocco in coffins." - BBC, August 2
