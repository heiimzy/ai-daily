1. AT&S CEO Warns: AI Demand Pushes Advanced Packaging Into a New Bottleneck

On July 28, Nikkei Asia reported from Penang, Malaysia that Michael Mertin, CEO of Austrian advanced substrate supplier AT&S, issued a stark warning — the global artificial intelligence boom is creating a new bottleneck in semiconductor manufacturing as demand for advanced chip packaging services and substrates far outstrips supply.

Mertin told Nikkei Asia that AI chips have dramatically increased the complexity of vendor-supplier relations. "You have to cooperate, you have to think, you have to have a joint roadmap," he said. As AI chips grow larger with higher I/O densities, traditional packaging technologies can no longer meet performance requirements, driving demand for advanced packaging and premium substrates faster than anticipated.

AT&S is aggressively expanding capacity in Malaysia to address this trend. As one of the world's leading IC substrate suppliers, AT&S products go directly into CPU and GPU packaging. Against the backdrop of surging AI computing investment, global packaging capacity — from CoWoS (wafer-level packaging) to EMIB (embedded multi-die interconnect bridge) to various 2.5D and 3D packaging solutions — is escalating from "tight supply" to "severe shortage."

"AI chips have increased the complexity of vendor-supplier relations. You have to cooperate, you have to think, you have to have a joint roadmap." — AT&S CEO Michael Mertin to Nikkei Asia

This assessment aligns with broader industry trends. TSMC's CoWoS capacity is already booked through 2027, and Intel has been seeking external customers for its EMIB-T packaging technology. Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan revealed on the Q2 earnings call that Intel's EMIB-T packaging has even attracted interest from rival TSMC — because TSMC cannot produce enough CoWoS-L packaging to satisfy customer demand.

Mertin's comments come at a time when the global semiconductor industry is in an unprecedented investment boom. Nvidia, AMD, Intel, and major cloud providers are all competing for limited packaging capacity, and AI chips require far more advanced packaging than traditional chips. Industry data shows that a single AI accelerator requires 3-5 times more substrate area than a traditional CPU, further intensifying capacity pressure.

Market Impact

  • Substrate pricing: ABF substrate prices have risen over 30% since 2024, with AI-related order lead times extending beyond 20 weeks
  • Equipment makers benefit: Packaging equipment suppliers like Disco and Tokyo Electron continue to benefit from the expansion cycle
  • Competitive landscape: Taiwanese manufacturers Unimicron and Nanya PCB are accelerating capacity expansion to capture AI packaging substrate market share

2. China Achieves Mass Production of High-Purity Silicon-28, Breaking Key Quantum Chip Material Bottleneck

On the morning of July 28, China National Nuclear Corporation (CNNC) announced a major breakthrough in stable isotope enrichment and high-purity silicon preparation — the first successful domestic mass production of silicon-28 isotopes with purity exceeding 99.99%, with key product indicators reaching international advanced levels. This achievement will strongly support independent research in silicon-based quantum computing core materials and drive high-quality development in advanced semiconductor manufacturing, precision navigation, and metrology standards.

Silicon-28, a stable isotope of silicon, has zero nuclear spin, effectively reducing environmental noise interference in quantum computing. It is hailed as "the world's purest silicon" and is an indispensable core material for silicon-based quantum chips. The breakthrough was achieved by the Physical and Chemical Engineering Research Institute under China Atomic Energy Industry Co., Ltd., a CNNC subsidiary.

This technological milestone arrives in the final year of the "Three-Year Action Plan for High-Quality Development of Nuclear Technology Application Industry (2024-2026)," marking substantial progress in building an independent, controllable, and collaborative stable isotope industrial framework.

Expert Perspectives

  • Yu Dapeng, CAS Academician: This breakthrough solves the critical material shortage for silicon-based quantum computing, paving the way for large-scale qubit manipulation
  • Lei Zengguang, CAE Academician: The mass production of high-purity silicon-28 isotopes represents the culmination of years of research and holds milestone significance

The Physical and Chemical Engineering Research Institute has achieved production of 12 elements and 26 stable isotopes including molybdenum, tellurium, nickel, zinc, silicon, and ytterbium, gradually building a full-chain innovation system from basic research to industrial transformation. Future R&D will extend to nuclear energy, nuclear medicine, aerospace, quantum information, particle physics, and deep space exploration.

The mass production of silicon-28 carries profound implications for the global quantum computing landscape. Silicon-based qubits are considered one of the most promising technical pathways for large-scale quantum computing, with quantum computing companies in Australia, the Netherlands, and the US racing to develop silicon-based quantum processors. Domestic silicon-28 production ensures China will not be supply-constrained on this critical frontier.

3. BAAI Releases Wujie RoboBrain Orca: AI Achieves Global State Evolution Reasoning for the First Time

Also on the morning of July 28, the Beijing Academy of Artificial Intelligence (BAAI) unveiled a new multimodal representation world model — "Wujie RoboBrain Orca." This marks a paradigm shift from "single-output prediction" to "global state evolution reasoning," giving AI a capability akin to human "mental simulation" for the first time.

The core innovation of RoboBrain Orca lies in constructing a unified "world potential representation space." When processing multimodal information — video, images, language, text instructions, task intent — the model unifies vision, action, and other modalities into a single world representation space, enabling it to both project current scenarios forward and simulate multiple possible future outcomes under different conditions.

Technical Breakthroughs

  • Dual-path training: A complementary "conscious + unconscious" training system — unconscious learning leverages 125,000 hours of real video data and 160 million event annotations to autonomously learn object motion and spatial interaction; conscious learning uses text events and visual QA annotations to master state-transition logic reasoning
  • Unified multimodal representation: Integrating vision, language, action, and task intent into a single world representation space for true multimodal cognition
  • Multi-decoder output: Text decoding excels at dynamic changes and logical reasoning; image decoding accurately restores real-world interaction predictions; robot action decoding understands complex physical tasks without pre-learned action labels

"When AI begins to truly simulate how the world evolves in its 'mind,' it is no longer merely a tool that passively responds to input — it becomes an agent capable of actively reasoning, planning, and predicting. The distance to AGI will no longer be measured in 'years,' but in 'version iterations.'" — BAAI official

World models are considered one of the critical pathways to artificial general intelligence — if AI can simulate the world's operating principles internally, it can dramatically enhance robot generalization in complex tasks.

The training data scale, architectural innovation, and multimodal capabilities of RoboBrain Orca have reached internationally leading levels, providing a new foundation model option for embodied intelligence, autonomous driving, and robotics R&D.
