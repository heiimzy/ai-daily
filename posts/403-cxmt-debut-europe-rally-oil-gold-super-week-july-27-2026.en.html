⚡ July 27 Midday Markets: CXMT Soars 471% to Become China's Largest Stock, Europe Extends Rally on US-Iran Truce, Gold Breaks $4,100

Global financial markets navigated the opening day of a historic Super Week with dramatic cross-asset moves. In Asia, China's DRAM champion CXMT debuted on the STAR Market with a stunning 471% surge to become A-shares' most valuable company. European equities opened higher, extending the weekend ceasefire rally. Commodities saw divergent action — crude oil consolidated in the $91-92 range while gold decisively breached $4,100. A powerful repricing driven by de-escalating geopolitics is now underway across global markets.

1. History Made: CXMT Surges 471%, Becomes A-Share Market Cap Champion

CXMT (688825.SH), China's largest DRAM manufacturer, officially listed on the Shanghai STAR Market on Monday morning. The stock opened at RMB 49.50 per share, a 471.59% surge from its IPO price of RMB 8.66, giving it a market capitalization of RMB 3.31 trillion ($463 billion) — surpassing Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC) to become the most valuable company in A-shares.

Key IPO metrics:

  • Fundraising: RMB 66.6 billion ($9.3 billion) after full exercise of the over-allotment option — the largest IPO in STAR Market history and the third-largest ever in A-shares
  • Subscription mania: 9.43 million retail investors participated, with an online allotment rate of 0.4714% — a STAR Market record. Winning bidders earned over RMB 20,000 per lot (500 shares)
  • Institutional participation: 285 institutional investors across 10,907 accounts participated. Top quantitative funds including Jiukun, High-Flyer, Yanfu, and Century Frontier each received over RMB 130 million in allocations
  • Fund dominance: 100 fund management firms with 5,507 products received RMB 10.83 billion in allocations. E Fund led with RMB 1.46 billion
  • Financial performance: H1 2026E revenue of RMB 110-120 billion (+612-677% YoY), net profit of RMB 50-57 billion (+2,244-2,544% YoY)

Reports indicate CXMT signed a five-year DRAM supply agreement worth over $7 billion with ByteDance this month. The company is building two new fabs in Shanghai and Hefei, which will double capacity to over 600,000 wafers per month, targeting to surpass Micron's global market share by 2030.

The A-share semiconductor sector was boosted by CXMT's debut. The STAR Chip ETF (589190) recovered from an early dip to close up 0.73%. Semiconductor materials and equipment stocks led gains, with Chengdu Huavei and CSSC特气 rising double digits, and Zhongke Feice up over 8%.

2. Europe Opens Higher: Stoxx 600 Extends Ceasefire Rally

European equities opened broadly higher on Monday, extending the Asia-Pacific relief rally. Key levels as of midday European trading:

  • Pan-European Stoxx 600: Up ~0.5-0.6%, led by financials and energy
  • UK FTSE 100: Modest gains, supported by lower oil prices. AstraZeneca and Vodafone earnings in focus
  • Germany DAX: +0.3%, supported by improving manufacturing PMI readings
  • France CAC 40: +0.4%, luxury stocks stabilizing

Energy stocks showed mixed performance — BP and Shell edged higher as the "supply premium" narrative persisted despite Brent's retreat. Defense stocks continued their rally amid expectations of higher Western military spending.

Europe faces its own set of challenges this week: the Bank of England announces its rate decision Thursday (all 70 economists polled expect a hold at 3.75%), and Eurozone Q2 GDP preliminary data is due Friday. ECB President Lagarde has signaled a potential 25bp rate hike in September.

3. Crude Oil Consolidates: Brent at $91-92 Awaiting Catalyst

The weekend US-Iran ceasefire triggered a sharp repricing in oil markets. Brent crude, which settled Friday at $96.78, gapped lower at Monday's open, briefly touching $91 before recovering to the $91.50-92.50 range during European hours — a net decline of about 4-5%. WTI traded at $84-85.

Key factors capping oil prices:

  • Rapid war premium unwinding: After 13 consecutive nights of US airstrikes, both sides suspended military operations. US UN Ambassador Waltz confirmed Trump wants to "give diplomacy room," with Oman-facilitated Strait of Hormuz talks showing progress
  • Supply fundamentals remain tight: Hormuz transit volume is still ~60% below pre-war levels, with over 500 vessels backlogged. Houthi attacks on Saudi Aramco facilities in Jizan and Yanbu persist, keeping Red Sea risks elevated
  • Technical rebound risk: UBS analysts warn that if truce talks collapse, Brent could quickly re-test $100. Goldman Sachs maintains a bullish outlook, noting supply normalization will take weeks or months

Goldman's base case: if the Hormuz blockade continues through July, Brent averages $105-110 in August; if tensions fully de-escalate within two weeks, prices could fall to $85 by year-end.

4. Gold Breaks $4,100: Safe-Haven Demand + Weaker Dollar

Spot gold traded in the $4,090-4,110 range during European hours on Monday, up approximately 1% on the day and briefly touching an intraday high of $4,115 — marking gold's first decisive break above $4,100 since mid-July.

Two key drivers behind gold's rally:

  • Easing inflation fears: The oil price slide reduces imported inflation pressure, diminishing the urgency for Fed tightening. CME FedWatch shows July rate hike probability easing from 38% Friday to 36%
  • USD weakness: The dollar index slipped from 101.45 to 101.20, with EUR/USD rising above 1.1410 and GBP/USD at 1.3355. A broadly weaker dollar directly supports gold prices

Silver outperformed, rallying over 2% to $59.80/oz. The gold-silver ratio narrowed from 70x to 68.5x, reflecting a marginal improvement in risk appetite.

5. Super Week Progress: Durable Goods Data, FOMC Countdown

The first major economic data point of Super Week is Monday's US durable goods report for June (due 8:30 AM ET = 12:30 GMT):

  • Headline durable goods orders: Expected +1.6% month-on-month, recovering from -4.5% in May. A robust reading would signal that corporate capital spending is holding up despite geopolitical headwinds, providing support for tech sector sentiment
  • Core capital goods orders (non-defense ex-aircraft): The key proxy for business investment, expected +0.3% MoM
  • FOMC decision: The July 28-29 meeting kicks off Wednesday. Chair Warsh has abandoned forward guidance, amplifying market sensitivity to any signal. Swap markets price a ~36% chance of a hike — highly unusual uncertainty
  • Megacap earnings: Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday after close; Apple and Amazon Thursday. The AI capex ROI narrative faces its sternest test yet

6. Asia-Pacific Close: Divergent Performance, Capital Rotation

Asia-Pacific markets closed Monday with mixed results:

  • KOSPI: Closed up 0.68% at 6,736, well below the opening surge of 1.73%. Foreign investors trimmed chip stocks in late trade — Samsung +1.6%, SK Hynix +0.06%. Naver surged 12.29% on Nvidia's $1.1B investment news
  • Nikkei 225: Edged up 0.3%, dragged by chip equipment stocks. USD/JPY held near 163.6 as markets await the BoJ decision Friday
  • Shanghai Composite: +0.4%, Shenzhen Component +0.2%. CXMT's gravitational pull activated the broader semiconductor complex, but AI hardware names remained under pressure. Northbound flows recorded a net RMB 2.5 billion outflow
  • Hang Seng: -0.2%, tech stocks mixed. State-owned energy names outperformed

Week Ahead: Three Scenarios

JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon issued a fresh warning Monday — markets are underestimating the multitude of risks moving beneath the surface, including geopolitical conflict, persistent inflation, global fiscal deficits, and elevated asset prices. Against this backdrop, here are three scenarios for Super Week:

Scenario 1 (40% probability): Measured Rally — FOMC holds + megacap earnings beat + US-Iran talks progress = S&P 500 back above 7,500, Brent base at $90.

Scenario 2 (35% probability): Choppy Consolidation — FOMC hawks dissent + mixed earnings + slow Hormuz reopening = wide-range choppy trading, tech digests valuations, defense/energy outperforms.

Scenario 3 (25% probability): Risk Re-emergence — Iran talks collapse + oil back to $100 + megacaps cut Q3 guidance = broad risk-off, S&P tests 7,300, gold challenges $4,200.

Options markets imply an S&P 500 trading range of 7,360-7,650 this week. Whatever scenario unfolds, the last week of July will set the tone for H2 asset allocation. Investors are advised to reduce leverage and maintain cash flexibility until the key information windows close.
