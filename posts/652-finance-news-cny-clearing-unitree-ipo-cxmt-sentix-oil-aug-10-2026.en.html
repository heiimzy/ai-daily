⚡ Aug 10 Finance Update: CNY Gains 59 Pips to 6.7442, PBOC Names Deutsche Bank Frankfurt RMB Clearing Bank, Unitree IPO Subscription Opens, July IPO Supply at Year High, Oil Rises on Hormuz Uncertainty

Several major financial stories landed in quick succession on the evening of August 10: onshore CNY closed 59 pips higher at 6.7442, while the PBOC authorized Deutsche Bank as the Frankfurt RMB clearing bank, marking another step in RMB internationalization; humanoid-robot leader Unitree Robotics officially opened its IPO subscription today at a striking 219.23x P/E; July saw a year-high IPO supply, with CXMT's RMB 57.92 billion raise becoming the largest A-share IPO since the registration system reform; overseas, the Eurozone August Sentix investor confidence index beat expectations sharply, Japan's 30-year JGB yield climbed to 3.98%, and USD/JPY rose over 0.5%; in energy, Brent crude gained 0.81% to $84.23 while European gas jumped 5%. Details of each story follow, with all source publish times verified.

1. CNY Closes 59 Pips Higher at 6.7442; PBOC Names Deutsche Bank as Frankfurt RMB Clearing Bank

According to Wallstreetcn (published 16:30, Aug 10), the onshore yuan's official 16:30 close was 6.7442, up 59 pips from the previous session's official close and 14 pips above the overnight close, extending the currency's recent firm tone.

In a related development, the People's Bank of China (flash news at 17:07, Aug 10) announced that, in line with the Memorandum of Understanding between the PBOC and the Deutsche Bundesbank, it has authorized Deutsche Bank to serve as the Frankfurt RMB clearing bank. The move deepens Sino-German financial cooperation, improves offshore RMB clearing efficiency, and widens the currency's use cases in Europe — extending the recent policy push for RMB internationalization (source: Wallstreetcn).

2. Unitree Robotics Opens IPO Subscription: RMB 150.80/Share, Nearly RMB 6.1B Raised, 219x P/E

According to Sina Finance 7x24 (published 16:36, Aug 10), Unitree Robotics officially launched its online and offline IPO subscription today. The offer price is RMB 150.80 per share, implying a post-listing market cap of about RMB 60.99 billion, with total proceeds of nearly RMB 6.1 billion and an issue P/E of 219.23x — making it one of the most closely watched "must-subscribe" deals in the A-share robotics sector.

Notably, Unitree was once backed by DJI: in 2018, DJI took about 17% of the company for RMB 10.1286 million (implying a valuation of roughly RMB 60 million at the time), but eventually exited through a capital reduction. Public records show founder Wang Xingxing briefly worked at DJI before resigning to start his own company. The high valuation and sky-high P/E underscore the intense market debate over humanoid-robot industrialization expectations (source: Sina Finance).

3. Supply Peak: 21 IPOs Disclose Registration Approvals in July, a Year High; CXMT Becomes Largest A-Share IPO Since Registration Reform

According to Securities Times (published 15:21, Aug 10), the IPO market hit a year-high supply in July: 21 companies disclosed registration approvals, a single-month record for 2026, with the Beijing Stock Exchange contributing over half and the STAR Market approving four — keeping its relatively brisk registration pace.

On the issuance side, 20 IPO companies launched their offerings in July, at the high end of the year, with combined fundraising setting a new annual record. Among them, CXMT raised RMB 57.92 billion in actual proceeds, making it the largest A-share IPO since the registration system reform. The mega-listing of a memory-chip champion, alongside heavier STAR Market and BSE supply, could meaningfully shape secondary-market liquidity and new-share performance in H2 (source: Wallstreetcn/Securities Times).

4. Global Macro: Eurozone Sentix Beats Big, Japan 30Y JGB Yield Hits 3.98%, Yen Softens

According to Wallstreetcn (published 16:30, Aug 10), the Eurozone August Sentix investor confidence index printed 0.9, far above the -0.5 consensus and up from -3.1 in July — turning positive after a steady recovery and signaling that investor pessimism over the euro-area outlook is easing. Eurozone data may take center stage this week.

In rates and FX: Japan's 30-year JGB yield rose 5 bps to 3.98% (flash 15:14), approaching the 4% threshold as long-end pressure persists; USD/JPY climbed over 0.50% on the day to 158.57 (flash 16:35), with the yen staying weak. Meanwhile, South Korea's presidential office announced a new KRW 5 trillion fund focused on semiconductor materials, components, and equipment (flash 16:53) — Asian industrial capital flows are also worth watching (source: Wallstreetcn).

5. Energy: Hormuz Uncertainty Lifts Oil, Brent +0.81%, Dutch Gas Jumps 5%

According to CNBC (published 08:46 GMT, updated 09:12 GMT, Aug 10), oil prices were mixed on Monday as traders digested conflicting signals from the U.S. and Iran over a potential Strait of Hormuz deal. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran is not currently in direct talks with the U.S. to end the war and reopen the strait; Citi noted risks remain elevated outside Hormuz, with Yemen's Houthis continuing attacks and threats; and Westpac said "uncertainty remains high as we go into the sixth month of the Iran war." As of 4:36 a.m. ET, Brent crude futures for October delivery were up 0.81% at $84.23 a barrel.

European gas moved higher in tandem: per ICE data (flash 16:41), Dutch month-ahead wholesale gas prices rose 5% to EUR 58.34 per MWh, while UK near-month gas gained 5.1%. Geopolitical risk premiums are being repriced into energy markets, and the oil-gas co-movement may persist (source: CNBC).
