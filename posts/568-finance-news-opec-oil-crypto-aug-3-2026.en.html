⚡ Aug 3 Finance Update: OPEC+ Adds Another 188,000 bpd in September, Oil Tumbles as US-Iran Talks Resume, Bitcoin Slips Below $63K

Monday's global markets were re-priced along two threads — a ceasefire hope and a supply recovery. Trump called off planned strikes on Iran and said talks would resume, raising prospects for a full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz and sending oil sharply lower, with Brent sinking more than 7% at one point. Separately, OPEC+ agreed on Sunday to add another 188,000 barrels per day in September, completing the full rollback of its 2023 voluntary cuts. Crypto, meanwhile, moved independently lower, with Bitcoin slipping below $63,000 as a widening Coldcard hardware-wallet hack weighed on confidence — a sharp divergence from the broadly rising stock, oil and bond complex. Here is this roundup.

1. OPEC+ Adds Another 188,000 bpd in September: 2023 Voluntary Cuts Fully Rolled Back

On Sunday, August 2, the seven core OPEC+ producers — Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman — held a virtual meeting and decided to raise output by a further 188,000 barrels per day from September. That marks a sixth consecutive month of increases and completes the full rollback of the voluntary cuts (totaling about 1.65 million bpd) announced in April and November 2023. In an official statement, OPEC+ said the move honors its commitment to full conformity with the Declaration of Cooperation while giving overproducing countries a chance to accelerate compensation; the next Joint Ministerial Monitoring Committee (JMMC) meeting is scheduled for September 6.

An important nuance: because Gulf and Russian exports have been repeatedly disrupted by the Middle East war and Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian infrastructure, much of this year's successive hikes "largely stayed on paper," with little actual market impact. Russian output is currently around nine million barrels per day, below its target of 9.8 million bpd. Most analysts expect OPEC+ to pause output increases in the fourth quarter to gauge demand and the geopolitical outlook — the roughly two million bpd of cuts introduced in 2022 will remain in place until the end of the year.

On prices, July posted the largest monthly gain for oil since March (Brent up about 24% and WTI up about 21%), setting the stage for Monday's sharp pullback once the hike decision met renewed US-Iran diplomacy.

2. Oil Crumbles as US-Iran Talks Resume: Brent Falls Over 7% to $81.55

Trump said Sunday he had canceled a planned attack on Iran, adding that Tehran and its regional neighbors had reached the parameters of an agreement on the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat," with talks set to resume Monday. The news sent Brent crude futures down as much as 7.3% to $81.55 a barrel (October contract), before easing to the $82.4–83.4 range by midday, while US WTI crude fell more than 6% toward $79.45. After more than five months of Middle East war, Brent had settled as recently as Friday at $90.12.

The drop in oil quickly transmitted into inflation expectations and asset prices. The US 10-year Treasury yield fell 4–6 basis points to near 4.68%, while the 30-year yield eased about 4.5 basis points to 5.23%, stepping back from a 19-year high. Spot gold edged up about 0.3%–0.5% to the $4,060–4,070 an ounce area; and US equity futures rallied, with S&P 500 futures up about 0.5%–0.6%, Nasdaq futures up 0.6%–0.8% and Dow futures up more than 1% at one point. European equities were broadly higher: the pan-European Stoxx 600 opened about 0.35% higher and Germany's DAX hit an intraday record, up 1.3%.

For the Federal Reserve, the drop in energy prices eased the pressure from supply-shock inflation, cooling market expectations of a September hike and allowing safe-haven and risk assets to rise together — an uncommon coupling. Still, analysts caution that the outcome of US-Iran negotiations and the actual recovery of shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remain the key swing factors for oil's medium-term direction.

3. Bitcoin Slips Below $63K: Coldcard Hack Deepens, Spot ETF Outflows Return

In contrast to the broadly firmer macro backdrop, crypto markets weakened again on Monday. According to CoinDesk, Bitcoin slid to about $62,600 — down about 1.4% in 24 hours and breaking below the $63,000 threshold — while Ethereum fell nearly 2% to about $1,842 and XRP eased toward $1.06. The main drag is the Coldcard hardware-wallet attack, now in its fifth day: three waves of exploits targeting a firmware flaw have drained roughly 1,367 bitcoin (about $89 million) from some 4,585 addresses, with some tallies putting total losses above 1,800 bitcoin across more than 5,000 addresses — rattling confidence in the "self-custody" model.

Fund flows are also under pressure. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded roughly $61.5 million of net outflows in the week of July 27–31, ending three consecutive weeks of inflows; July 31 alone saw $265.4 million of outflows, led by BlackRock's IBIT at $122.7 million, Fidelity's FBTC at $54.8 million and Grayscale's GBTC at $52.6 million. On the policy front, the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act (CLARITY) did not appear on Monday's Senate agenda, removing a near-term catalyst. In the options market, call bets remain clustered around the $68,000 and $70,000 strikes, with traders waiting on the US-Iran talks and this week's US jobs data for direction.

The next key catalyst this week is the US July nonfarm payrolls report. If employment comes in strong and inflation remains sticky, the credibility questions around the Fed's decision to hold could push long-end Treasury yields higher again; if jobs data disappoint, the market's pricing of an "end to the hiking cycle" will be reinforced. The aftershocks of oil, the yen and AI earnings will continue to dominate global risk appetite in the coming sessions.
