Google's One-Day Experiment: Earth AI Image Feature Rolled Back After a Single Day

Google launched an AI image-generation feature in the web version of Google Earth on Thursday (July 30), powered by Nano Banana 2 — users could type a text prompt and superimpose convincingly realistic images such as "refugees" or "bomb craters" onto real satellite imagery. Just one day later, Google announced it was rolling the feature back (TechCrunch, 2026-07-31 19:47 UTC). Digital forensics researcher Henk van Ess of Digital Digging had deliberately generated images like "refugees near the Mexican border" and "a bomb crater near a hospital in Gaza," and in testing fooled Hive's AI detection tool with a video generated in Google Earth — he said "nothing was refused, nothing was softened, and nothing suggested" his prompts should be blocked.

In its latest statement, Google said: "We know that people uniquely trust Google Earth for a reliable view of the world. We've seen geospatial professionals using this feature for a range of useful purposes, however we've also seen people sharing screenshots of generated imagery that appear to violate our policies. So we're rolling back this feature in Google Earth while we work on implementing stronger guardrails." Google stressed that generated images never appeared in the main Google Earth experience and were watermarked as AI-generated. Yet van Ess pointed out that watermarked content can still spread through screen recordings, so the deepfake risk has not disappeared. The move is the latest emergency brake by an AI giant amid a broader "generative AI trust crisis" — Google Earth had earlier promised a dual defense of SynthID digital watermarks and harmful-topic blocking, which proved insufficient to quell the controversy.

NHTSA Opens Probe Into Nearly 1.2 Million Teslas Over Suspension Failure Reports

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) is investigating nearly 1.2 million Tesla vehicles after receiving complaints about a suspension failure that could cause "a loss of vehicle directional control" (The Verge, 2026-07-31 18:33 UTC). According to a filing from NHTSA's Office of Defects Investigation, the preliminary investigation covers the 2018-2020 Model 3 and 2021-2023 Model Y, with 156 complaints received about the issue, which "involves the detachment of the front lower lateral link." In most cases reviewed by the NHTSA, failure occurred with "no advance warning," though some complaints cited noises occurring prior to failure. The NHTSA has not recorded any crashes, injuries, or fatalities related to the potential issue so far.

This isn't the first time Tesla has faced suspension scrutiny. In 2021, Tesla recalled 2,791 Model Y and Model 3 vehicles over a potential suspension defect, and later recalled another batch of Model 3s after the NHTSA found that a "loose fastener could cause the lateral link to separate." The Model Y covered by this probe is Tesla's global best-seller, so an escalation to a recall would carry significant reach. Tesla has not yet commented publicly on the investigation.

Index Ventures Raises $2 Billion: $3.5 Billion War Chest to Keep Betting on AI

European venture firm Index Ventures announced Friday that it has raised $2 billion in fresh capital across three funds (TechCrunch, 2026-07-31 19:39 UTC). The 30-year-old firm raised $400 million for its new seed-focused fund and $900 million for its venture fund, and added $700 million to a $1.5 billion growth fund raised in 2024, bringing its total available investing capital to $3.5 billion. Two years ago, Index raised $2.3 billion across two funds, including $800 million toward a predecessor venture fund.

The haul comes fresh off Index's payout from the Wiz deal — Google's $32 billion acquisition of cloud security company Wiz, in which Index was a major early investor. While several top VCs have ballooned their fund sizes, Index has kept a comparatively restrained pace while continuing to stand out with sharp bets on AI, cybersecurity, and developer tools. With $3.5 billion in fresh ammunition, the market broadly expects Index to accelerate its pace in AI application layers and the security space.

Hyundai Neira Concept: A Seven-Seat Electric Minivan Aimed at Southeast Asian Families

Hyundai unveiled the Neira Concept, a seven-seat electric minivan, at the 2026 Gaikindo Indonesia International Auto Show (GIIAS), with local production in Indonesia planned to begin by the end of 2026 (Electrek, 2026-07-31 18:33 UTC). Hyundai said the electric people mover is "designed to address the space and practicality needs of Indonesian families without sacrificing sustainability." From exterior to interior, the Neira is nearly a replica of the Kia Carens EV electric MPV launched in India last year — Hyundai differentiated it with unique front and rear designs, headlights, and alloy wheels, while the interior features the same dual 12.3-inch instrument cluster and center infotainment screens plus a separate climate control panel.

Although Hyundai hasn't confirmed specs, the sibling model suggests two battery options: a 42 kWh pack with an MIDC range of up to 404 km (251 mi), and a 51.4 kWh pack offering up to 490 km (304 mi), with both recharging from 10% to 80% in about 39 minutes on a 100 kW DC fast charger. Hyundai also showcased the IONIQ 3 alongside the concept. Due to the Trump administration's import tariffs, the Indonesian-built minivan is unlikely to reach the US market — Hyundai continues to offer the seven-seat IONIQ 9, built at its Metaplant in Georgia, to American buyers.
