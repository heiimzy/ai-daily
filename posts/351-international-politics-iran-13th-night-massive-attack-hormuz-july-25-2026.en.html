⚡ July 25 International Politics Roundup: US Completes 13th Night of Strikes on Iran, IRGC Launches Drone Attacks on US Bases in Kuwait/Bahrain/Jordan, Trump Threatens 'Unprecedented Massive Attack'

US Completes 13th Consecutive Night of Strikes on Iran — Ahvaz Pounded with 35+ Blows

US Central Command (CENTCOM) confirmed Friday that American forces completed a 13th consecutive night of strikes against Iranian military targets. The operation began at 6:45 PM ET Thursday (6:45 AM Beijing time Friday), targeting Iranian military command centers, drone storage facilities, communication networks, coastal surveillance sites, and naval capabilities to "further diminish the threat Iran poses to civilian mariners and commercial vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz."

The southwestern Iranian city of Ahvaz bore the brunt of Friday's attacks. Social media footage showed massive explosions and large fires, with more than 35 strikes concentrated in Khuzestan Province within a single hour. Four people were killed and five injured in the Ahvaz strikes, according to state broadcaster IRIB.

Iranian media also reported explosions in Bandar Abbas (where a residential neighborhood was hit, injuring two), Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, Khorramabad near the Iraqi border, the strategic port city of Jask, Andimeshk, and Omidiyeh. Iranian air defense systems were activated over eastern Tehran.

Iran's Health Ministry reported that recent US strikes have now killed 55 people and injured 645, with Khuzestan Province recording the highest casualties, followed by Hormozgan and Sistan-Baluchestan provinces.

IRGC Launches Arash Drone Attacks on US Bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) announced retaliatory Arash suicide drone strikes against US military facilities across three Gulf states simultaneously — a significant escalation in the scope of the conflict.

  • Kuwait: Iran claimed strikes on Camp Arifjan, Camp Doha, and Camp Adairi (formerly Camp Buehring), targeting troop positions and equipment depots. Kuwait's military confirmed on X that its air defenses were "confronting hostile drone attacks launched from Iran," adding that any explosion sounds were from interceptions and urging the public to follow safety instructions.
  • Bahrain: The IRGC claimed significant damage to a US Fifth Fleet surveillance tower. The Bahrain Defence Force said it had "confronted, intercepted and destroyed a number of treacherous Iranian air attacks." The Interior Ministry sounded air raid sirens and urged citizens to "remain calm and head to the nearest safe place."
  • Jordan: Iran claimed strikes on Muwaffaq Salti (Al-Azraq) Air Base, targeting aircraft hangars, maintenance facilities, and barracks. Jordan's military said it intercepted seven missiles and six drones launched from Iran, with "no casualties or material damage."
  • Erbil, Iraq: Iran also claimed attacks on US barracks, a spy balloon, and Patriot air defense systems in Iraq's Kurdish region. At least seven explosions were heard around 9:30 AM local time Friday.

The IRGC then warned civilians across Gulf countries to "immediately move away from a radius of 500 meters from locations where US soldiers are hiding," signaling further attacks could be imminent.

Trump Threatens 'Massive Attack': 'Close to a Decision'

President Donald Trump told Axios that he is "seriously considering" restarting major combat operations against Iran on a scale surpassing anything during "Operation Epic Fury." Trump stated: "I am considering a massive attack. Bigger than ever before. I am close to making a decision. We are all set for it."

Trump added that Israel "would join in two minutes if I ask them to," but emphasized "we don't need anybody" to launch a new operation. He acknowledged there would be "consequences" for such a decision.

A senior administration official told the Wall Street Journal that Trump is growing increasingly frustrated that the conflict is dragging on "with no end in sight," is in "revenge mode," and believes Iran only understands force. The US military is reportedly surging special operations forces, weapons, and medics to the Middle East to broaden options.

However, hours later at a White House press briefing, Trump struck a dual tone, saying "we're talking to Iran" and that the Iranians are "getting very serious." He claimed "they want to make a deal, I just don't think they're ready yet" — maintaining Washington's familiar dual-track strategy of pressure and diplomacy.

Trump: Frozen Iranian Assets to Pay for Ship Damage

Trump announced on Truth Social that from now on, "Any and all damages done to Ships, Cargo, or anything related thereto, will be paid for by Iranian Money that the United States has in its possession, and controls." He acknowledged damages "may be very substantial but, nevertheless, this is the fair and equitable thing to do."

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi swiftly responded on X, warning that "no one's assets were safe once governments normalized such confiscation" and that "ensuing chaos will not be pretty or peaceful."

The escalation follows Houthi attacks on two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea's Bab el-Mandeb strait on Thursday, which briefly pushed Brent crude above $100 per barrel for the first time since May. Prices retreated to around $95 on Friday. The Houthis declared a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia, threatening a second major energy chokepoint alongside the near-closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

Saudi Strikes on Hodeidah as Red Sea Front Expands

Saudi warplanes carried out strikes on the Houthi-held port city of Hodeidah in western Yemen, according to Al Masirah TV. The strikes targeted telecommunications infrastructure and Kamaran Island. This follows the Houthi declaration of a naval blockade on Saudi Arabia, which has forced Riyadh to divert millions of barrels of daily oil production via pipeline to the Red Sea to bypass Iran's blockade of the Strait of Hormuz.

An Omani delegation arrived in Tehran to discuss a Strait of Hormuz management mechanism. Iranian FM Araghchi and his Omani counterpart discussed regional security and maritime navigation. Iran reiterated it will not accept a ceasefire "until its goals are achieved."

Pakistan Explores US-Iran Talks as China Pushes Diplomacy

Pakistan is exploring a path toward resuming stalled US-Iran talks following a China-led diplomatic push, three Pakistani sources told Reuters. Iran's Interior Minister Eskandar Momeni visited Islamabad for the second time in 10 days, meeting government leaders and the army chief. A halt in attacks on Saudi Arabia and other Gulf states would be a "pre-requisite for any talks to resume," a Pakistani official said.

China's foreign ministry stated it "supports the mediation efforts made by Pakistan and other parties" and will continue to "play an active role in restoring peace and tranquility in the Middle East Gulf region." China, as Iran's largest trading partner and primary crude oil buyer, is deeply concerned about the closure of the Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea shipping disruptions.

Meanwhile, Iran's FM Araghchi met Pakistan's Deputy PM Ishaq Dar on the sidelines of the SCO summit in Kyrgyzstan. Araghchi reiterated that Iran will "continue our movement until our goals are achieved" and stated "There is no mediator problem between us and America, but the problem is America's approaches."
