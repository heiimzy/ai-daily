⚡ Aug 5 Finance Update: Gold Breaks $4,200, Eurozone June PPI Slows to 4.6% YoY, Shanghai Composite +1.81% Back Above 3,800, Japan Approves Food Tax Cut

Over the past hour or two, global financial markets delivered a dense wave of data and price action: Europe published June PPI and final July PMI readings in a burst as inflation pressures keep easing; the precious-metals rally intensified with gold decisively clearing the $4,200/oz mark; Chinese and Hong Kong equities closed higher, with Shanghai back above 3,800 points; Japan approved a food consumption tax cut and pledged not to fill the revenue gap with new debt; and US pre-market trading saw fresh earnings-driven swings. Here is this round's finance roundup.

1. Eurozone June PPI Slows to 4.6% YoY; July PMI Finals Show Mixed Picture

According to Wallstreetcn's live feed (published 17:00 on Aug 5, 2026), eurozone June PPI came in at -0.3% MoM (in line, prior +0.2%) and +4.6% YoY (in line, prior 5.9%). The marked slowdown in producer prices points to further cooling upstream industrial inflation, giving the European Central Bank more room on policy. Final July PMIs landed around the same time: the eurozone composite PMI final was 52 (in line) with services at 51.7 (16:00); the UK composite PMI final was 52.2 and services 52.1, both revised slightly higher (16:30). Inside the bloc the picture is uneven — Italy's July services PMI jumped to 52.5, well above the 51 expected and 50.2 prior (15:45), a standout, while Germany's composite final was 51.3 with services at 49.8 (15:55) and France's composite final 49.4 with services 49.6 (15:50), both services sectors hovering around the breakeven line with softer momentum.

2. Gold Breaks $4,200 to Fresh Highs, Silver Jumps, Brent Back Above $80

Precious metals and energy extended their strength. According to live quotes (COMEX futures, 09:01 UTC on Aug 5, 2026), gold decisively cleared the $4,200/oz level, trading at $4,222.70, up 1.69% from the prior settle of $4,152.60 — delivering on the market's earlier "looking toward 4,200" expectation. Silver followed suit: silver futures traded at $61.74, up 2.49%, with the market eyeing the $64 level. In energy, per Wallstreetcn's live feed (15:14), Brent crude climbed back above $80 per barrel, up about 0.81% on the day, with WTI also moving higher. Geopolitics and haven demand remain the core driver behind gold's ascent, with uncertainty around the Strait of Hormuz continuing to underpin precious metals.

3. Shanghai Composite +1.81% Back Above 3,800; Hong Kong Closes Higher, CNY Firms

Asian markets closed on a positive note. According to live market data (A-share close at 15:00 on Aug 5, 2026), the Shanghai Composite rose 1.81% to close at 3,878.43, reclaiming the 3,800 mark. In Hong Kong, per Wallstreetcn (16:10), the Hang Seng Index closed up 0.24% and the Hang Seng Tech Index gained 0.97%, with MINIMAX up about 10%, CMOC Group up about 9% and Hua Hong Semiconductor up about 5%. On FX, onshore CNY fixed at 6.7503 at the official 16:30 close, appreciating 30 pips from the prior official close (Wallstreetcn, 16:30), keeping the yuan on a relatively firm footing.

4. Japan Approves Two-Year Food Tax Cut; Minister Pledges No New Debt to Fill Gap

Japan's fiscal and monetary picture saw new developments. According to Wallstreetcn's live feed (citing Kyodo, 15:14 on Aug 5), Japan's cabinet approved cutting the food consumption tax for two years — a significant step to ease living costs amid domestic inflation pressure. Shortly after (16:21, 16:31), Finance Minister Katsunobu Kato said the government will not rely on new debt issuance to fill the tax revenue gap, saying it will review spending and revenue items to raise funds, and noting that "US Treasury Secretary Bessent believes Japan's fiscal discipline has improved." On FX, USD/JPY traded at 157.83 (live quotes). Japan's balancing act between fiscal stimulus and fiscal discipline will keep influencing the yen and global bond sentiment.

5. US Pre-Market: SpaceX Slides ~7% After Earnings, JPMorgan Still Hikes Target to $240

Earnings season triggered sharp pre-market swings in US stocks. According to Wallstreetcn's live feed (16:00), SpaceX fell about 7% in pre-market trading — revenue surged 92% year over year, well above expectations, but capital expenditures jumped sharply, with investors wary of a longer cost-investment cycle; AMD also dropped more than 7% pre-market, with Q2 revenue and profit beating estimates but Q3 guidance falling short of aggressive investor expectations. (AMD's Q2 beat with cautious guidance was covered in our earlier report.) Even so, one major bank remains bullish on SpaceX: JPMorgan raised its SpaceX price target from $225 to $240 (Wallstreetcn, 16:22). On the supply chain, Foxconn (Hon Hai) posted July revenue of NT$946.51 billion — the first time it has topped NT$900 billion — up 54.2% year over year (15:31), and expects AI rack shipments to keep growing in Q3. Overall, AI capex remains the earnings-season growth driver, but market pricing over "when heavy investment turns into profits" is diverging.
