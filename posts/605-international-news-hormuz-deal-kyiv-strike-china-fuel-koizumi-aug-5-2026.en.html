⚡ August 5 World News: Hormuz Interim Deal Nears as Kyiv Strike Kills 14, China Eases Fuel Exports, Koizumi Nuclear Debate

Over the past few hours, major developments have landed on three fronts at once: the Middle East, Eastern Europe, and the Asia-Pacific. The US, Iran and Oman are reportedly close to a 60-day interim deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, with an announcement possible as early as Wednesday — President Trump says talks are going "very well" while warning Iran will be "hit very hard" if the strait stays closed, though Tehran blames continued US military threats for the delay. Russia struck the Kyiv region with ballistic missiles and drones after midnight, killing at least 14 people and wounding 22 in one of the deadliest attacks on the capital this year. Reuters reported that China has eased controls on fuel exports for a second straight month. And in Japan, Shinjiro Koizumi backed a nuke-free world after his call for a nuclear debate "without taboos" triggered backlash. Here is this round's world news roundup.

1. Strait of Hormuz: Iran and Oman near a 60-day interim deal, announcement possible Wednesday; Iran blames US 'interference'

According to Reuters (August 5, 2026, 05:11 GMT), President Donald Trump said the US and Iran are having "very good discussions," adding that the two sides held an "all-day negotiation" on Tuesday. Citing officials familiar with the matter, News9live (04:28 GMT) and The Statesman (04:58 GMT) reported that Iran has approved a 60-day interim agreement for the Strait of Hormuz, and the US, Iran and Oman are close to a deal, with an official announcement possible as early as Wednesday (August 5) aimed at restoring traffic through the world's most important oil artery. Trump himself (05:35 GMT) said a deal "could come as early as Wednesday," while warning Iran it will be "hit very hard" if the strait is not reopened soon.

However, Anadolu Agency (06:03 GMT), citing Iranian sources, reported that "US interference" is delaying the Iran-Oman agreement, and Roya News (05:28 GMT) also said Tehran blames continued US military threats for the delay. Earlier, BBC (updated 05:08 GMT) reported Trump telling Fox News that the Strait of Hormuz would reopen "very soon" or Iran would be "hit very hard"; Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent both said talks have progressed and shipments could potentially resume "later this week." On Tuesday, Brent crude fell nearly 5% to below $80 a barrel, and US West Texas Intermediate dropped more than 5% to $76, both at their lowest levels since July 13, with prices continuing lower in Asian trading Wednesday. US Central Command said the "southern route through the Strait of Hormuz remains free and open for all commercial vessels."

2. Russia strikes Kyiv with missiles and drones, killing at least 14 and wounding 22; Ukraine pleads for Patriots

According to BBC (updated August 5, 2026, 05:08 GMT, by Toby Mann), Russia attacked Ukraine with ballistic missiles and drones after midnight on Wednesday, killing at least 14 people in the Kyiv region and leaving at least 22 injured — one of the deadliest strikes across Kyiv this year. Kyiv's military administration head Timur Tkachenko said the "enemy is once again massively attacking the Kyiv region," with air raid alerts in place for more than an hour as waves of ballistic missiles hit the city and its outskirts, damaging residential buildings and warehouses. By district, nine people were killed in Boryspil, four in Bucha and one in Fastiv; Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said there were fears people could be trapped under rubble, and four of the injured were in serious condition.

Russia's defense ministry said it had struck logistics hubs and supply centers in Kyiv and the surrounding region involved in storing, delivering weapons and military supplies and producing and distributing drones, adding that it also hit three cargo vessels "transporting weapons and military equipment" near the Black Sea port of Odesa. Ukraine, for its part, again urged allies for more US-made Patriot interceptors to defend against ballistic attacks. Cross-strikes on civilian and logistics targets have escalated sharply: five people were killed and 10 wounded in the Moscow region on Tuesday morning in strikes on warehouses, and the day before Russian officials said seven were killed and 58 injured after a drone hit a busy beach in the southern Krasnodar region. Ukraine also voiced outrage over a video showing a man chased by a drone in Kherson, calling it a deliberate "hunt" and "safari" targeting civilians.

3. China eases controls on fuel exports for a second straight month, sources say

According to Reuters (August 5, 2026, 04:37 GMT, citing sources), China has eased controls on fuel exports for a second consecutive month. Against a backdrop of soft demand and squeezed refiner margins, Beijing is further increasing refined-product export quotas or relaxing related approvals, opening the door for refiners to ship more product abroad and relieve domestic oversupply. The report was picked up by outlets including The Economic Times (04:56 GMT). Analysts note that with global crude supply highly volatile amid Middle East tensions, China — one of the world's largest refined-fuel exporters — plays an outsized role in the supply-demand balance of Asia-Pacific and global product markets.

4. Japan politics: Shinjiro Koizumi backs nuke-free world after his call for a nuclear debate 'without taboos' triggers backlash

According to The Japan Times (August 5, 2026, 05:09 GMT) and The Asahi Shimbun (05:22 GMT), a heavyweight of Japan's ruling Liberal Democratic Party, Shinjiro Koizumi, has backed a nuke-free world after his call for a nuclear weapons policy debate "without taboos" stirred controversy. Koizumi had suggested Japan should discuss security issues including nuclear deterrence "without taboos," a remark read by some as testing the waters on "nuclear sharing" or even a nuclear-weapons path, drawing sharp backlash at home and abroad. He subsequently clarified his position, reaffirming support for the goal of a nuke-free world. The episode comes as debate intensifies in Japan over whether to deepen reliance on the US nuclear umbrella amid a worsening regional security environment; nippon.com (03:53 GMT) also covered the story under the headline "Japan's Koizumi Backs Nuke-Free World after Controversy."
