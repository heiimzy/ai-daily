⚡ July 30 Semiconductor Triple: Samsung Chip Profit Soars 250x, Shortage Through 2028; UMC Silicon Photonics Enters Mass Production; US CHIPS Act Awards $874M to 7 R&D Firms

1. Samsung Q2: Semiconductor Profit Surges 250-Fold to Record, Warns Chip Shortage Through 2028

On July 30, Samsung Electronics reported second-quarter 2026 results that shocked the semiconductor industry. The Device Solutions (DS) division posted an operating profit of 89.2 trillion won ($61.7 billion), surging over 250-fold year-over-year, with revenue reaching 127.5 trillion won ($92.1 billion) — both all-time records.

Consolidated quarterly revenue hit 171.5 trillion won ($119 billion), another all-time high, while operating profit reached 89.5 trillion won ($62 billion), up from 4.68 trillion won a year earlier. The quarterly profit surpassed Samsung's combined earnings over the past three years, confirming that the AI-driven memory super-cycle is fundamentally reshaping the semiconductor landscape.

But what captured the market's attention even more than the historic profit figures was Samsung's supply-side warning. Kim Jaejune, executive vice president of Samsung's memory business, told analysts on the earnings call: "The supply shortage in 2027 is expected to worsen compared to this year, and it is expected to continue in 2028."

He explained that building a new fab to producing wafers takes more than three and a half years, making near-term capacity expansion difficult. In response, Samsung has signed long-term supply agreements (LTAs) with the top five global data center firms and is nearing deals with five more large companies. These LTAs run at least five years, covering 60%-70% of Samsung's long-term capacity, and include upfront payments and floor pricing to hedge capital investment risk.

"Management's commentary on the conference call was better than expected, and it was one of the more reassuring calls we've heard in quite some time." — Ryu Young-ho, senior analyst at NH Investment & Securities

In HBM (High Bandwidth Memory), Samsung is accelerating its catch-up with SK Hynix. HBM4 revenue is expected to more than triple in the third quarter, with HBM4 accounting for over 60% of total HBM revenue in the second half. Samsung aims to bring its HBM market share in line with its overall DRAM market share. Samsung counts Nvidia and AMD among its HBM customers.

For foundry, Samsung confirmed its Taylor Fab 1 in Texas will begin operations this year, with Fab 2 groundbreaking by year-end (targeting mass production in 2030). The company is also evaluating additional capacity for 1.4nm production. 2nm order projects are expected to more than double this year, with major cloud service providers and AI/HPC customers already engaged in design.

However, surging memory prices had a side effect: Samsung's mobile division posted a 700 billion won ($485 million) operating loss — its first quarterly loss. Analyst Josh Gilbert noted: "The chips enriching one side of Samsung are now hurting the other, leaving the group more exposed than ever to memory pricing and the durability of hyperscaler demand." Samsung shares initially surged 8% before closing down 1.1%, reflecting market tension over massive capex (Q2 capital spending: 16.8 trillion won) versus cyclical risk.

2. UMC Q2 Beats Estimates: Silicon Photonics Wafers in Mass Production, Intel 12nm PDK by Year-End

United Microelectronics Corporation (UMC) also delivered strong Q2 2026 results on July 29-30. Revenue reached TWD 68.73 billion ($2.12 billion), up 12.6% quarter-over-quarter, with gross margin expanding to 32.5% and fab utilization climbing to 85% from 79%. Both metrics beat expectations, reflecting robust mature-node recovery driven by AI spillover demand.

Two developments stood out in UMC's earnings: mass production of silicon photonics wafers and concrete progress on the Intel 12nm collaboration.

Silicon Photonics Milestone: 12-Inch Production for Optical Interconnects

On July 14, UMC and Singapore-based chip designer SILITH Technology announced the first delivery of mass-produced silicon photonic integrated circuit (PIC) wafers from UMC's Singapore Fab 12i. The wafers use imec's iSiPP300 silicon photonics platform on a 12-inch SOI process, supporting SILITH's 1.6Tbps silicon photonics platform — capable of moving 1.6 trillion bits of data per second as modulated light.

SILITH's end customers include optical transceiver manufacturers Innolight and Coherent, both key optical suppliers to Nvidia and Google — placing UMC directly in the AI data center optical supply chain. UMC plans to open its 12-inch silicon photonics platform to general customers in 2027, with an open platform expected in 2028.

Silicon photonics is viewed as a critical breakthrough for AI data center interconnects. Co-packaged optics (CPO), which integrates optical engines directly alongside switch ASICs, can reduce interconnect power consumption by 60-70%. UMC's 12-inch production capability positions it as a key CPO supply chain player.

Intel 12nm: PDK Delivery by Late 2026, Volume Production in 2028

UMC CEO Jason Wang confirmed on the earnings call that process design kits (PDKs) for the Intel 12nm FinFET node will be delivered to customers by end of 2026, with first customer tape-outs expected in 2027 and volume production in 2028. The collaboration, announced in January 2024, has UMC running 12nm production at Intel's Ocotillo Technology Fabrication site in Chandler, Arizona (Fabs 12, 22, and 32) under a revenue-sharing model.

UMC also raised its 2026 capex to $2 billion, with approximately $5 billion in cumulative capex planned through 2027, 90% allocated to 12-inch wafer capacity for silicon photonics and advanced packaging. The board approved a cleanroom expansion at Singapore's P4 facility and a new advanced packaging fab at the Tainan 12A campus.

UMC shares are up 124% year-to-date, reflecting market recognition of its strategic transformation.

3. US CHIPS R&D Office Awards $874M to 7 Semiconductor Companies

On July 29, the US Department of Commerce's CHIPS Research and Development Office announced letters of intent (LOIs) with seven companies, providing up to $874 million in federal incentives for next-generation semiconductor R&D. This is one of the largest concentrated CHIPS R&D allocations, targeting silicon photonics, novel AI memory, advanced packaging, thermodynamic computing, and other frontier areas.

The seven awardees and their projects:

  • GlobalFoundries — Up to $300M: Accelerate domestic R&D of co-packaged optics (CPO), integrating photonics directly with AI processors for ultra-fast, energy-efficient data movement. The goal is to commercialize CPO 2-3 years earlier than current timelines.
  • Kepler — Up to $245M: Develop a new class of high-performance AI memory technology enabled by innovative 3D and ferroelectric technologies for next-gen AI training and inference workloads.
  • Multibeam Corporation — Up to $140M: Develop advanced packaging technology for stacking and interconnecting multiple chips with thousands of wires, enabling the advanced systems required for AI and other computing applications.
  • Extropic — Up to $75M: Develop thermodynamic sampling units (TSUs) that use natural thermal fluctuations to probabilistically solve complex problems in simulation, optimization, and AI at a fraction of conventional computing energy.
  • Thintronics — Up to $50M: Develop ultra-low-loss inter-layer dielectrics for next-gen semiconductor interconnects and advanced packaging in HPC, AI, and networking infrastructure.
  • OBSIDIA Semiconductors — Up to $34M: Deliver non-invasive counterfeit and malicious component identification systems for supply chain provenance and traceability in AI and advanced electronics.
  • Aeluma — Up to $30M: Develop large-diameter indium-phosphide-free substrate technology for photodetectors and lasers used in AI photonic interconnects.

Bill Frauenhofer, Executive Director for Semiconductor Innovation and Investment at the Department of Commerce, said: "These CHIPS R&D incentives will support breakthroughs in compute and communication infrastructures to power next-generation AI capabilities. Accelerating R&D for domestic photonics capabilities, novel memory architectures, advanced packaging, and substrates will provide American industries the extreme bandwidth and energy efficiency to scale complex AI workloads securely and rapidly."

The awards signal a dual-track US semiconductor strategy — combining manufacturing reshoring with R&D leadership. As the CHIPS Act's $52 billion total allocation continues to flow, the R&D track is receiving increasing emphasis.

Summary: AI Hardware Enters a 'Seller's Market' — Supply Constraints Define Industry Power

Bringing together the signals from Samsung, UMC, and the CHIPS Act, a clear industrial theme emerges: AI computing is experiencing an unprecedented seller's market. Samsung warns of memory shortages through 2028, TSMC's 2nm and CoWoS capacity is sold out through 2028, and UMC's mature-node utilization is approaching full capacity. From leading-edge to mature nodes, every semiconductor segment is tightening.

HBM4 revenue about to explode in Q3, silicon photonics transitioning from lab to 12-inch production lines, and the US accelerating domestic R&D through the CHIPS Act — three threads pointing to the same conclusion: AI hardware supply constraints have replaced model capability competition as the defining variable in how fast the AI industry can evolve. Those who control capacity control the conversation.
