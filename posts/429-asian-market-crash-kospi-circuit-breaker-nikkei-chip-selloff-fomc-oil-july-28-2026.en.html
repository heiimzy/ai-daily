⚡ July 28 Asian Market Meltdown: KOSPI Plunges 10.84%, Nikkei Crashes 4%, Chip Stocks Decimated, FOMC Day 1 Opens with 38% Rate Hike Odds, Oil Tumbles 8%

Asian financial markets suffered a "Black Tuesday" bloodbath on July 28, 2026. South Korea's KOSPI cratered 10.84% to close at 6,023.66, triggering its eighth circuit breaker of the year. Japan's Nikkei 225 plunged 4.11% to 62,262, now down 14.5% from its June record high. The U.S. Federal Reserve's FOMC two-day meeting opened with market-implied rate hike probability surging to 38%. International oil prices extended their rout, with Brent crude crashing 8.7% to $88.36. Technology and semiconductor stocks bore the brunt of the selloff across the region.

South Korea KOSPI: 10.84% Crash — 8th Circuit Breaker of 2026

The Korea Composite Stock Price Index experienced an unprecedented bloodbath on Tuesday. The index opened 5.26% lower at 6,400.27 and rapidly extended losses. At 9:06 a.m. KST, a sell-side sidecar was triggered on the KOSPI, suspending program sell orders for five minutes. But selling pressure continued unabated — at 10:13 a.m., the KOSPI breached the 8% decline threshold, triggering a Level 1 circuit breaker that halted all trading for 20 minutes.

After trading resumed, the selloff accelerated further. The KOSPI broke below the psychologically critical 6,000 level at 3:18 p.m., eventually closing at 6,023.66 — down a staggering 10.84%. This marks the eighth circuit breaker this year and the 14th in the index's history.

Blue-Chip Stocks Decimated

  • Samsung Electronics: Crashed 9.84%, erasing over $50 billion in market cap
  • SK Hynix: Plunged 11.67%, falling below its U.S. IPO price
  • Samsung Electro-Mechanics: Collapsed 15.47%, hitting a year low
  • SK Telecom: Nose-dived 16.04%, as AI infrastructure concerns hit telecoms
  • Naver: Fell 4%, fully unwinding the Nvidia investment boost

The Korea Exchange attributed the crash to three core factors: the overnight selloff in U.S. semiconductor stocks, intensifying fears over Chinese chip competition (following CXMT's 466% surge on its STAR Market debut, sparking oversupply panic), and persistent doubts about the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending.

Japan Nikkei: 4.11% Plunge — Two-Month Low, Chip Giants Evaporate Trillions

Japanese equities suffered a synchronized rout. The Nikkei 225 closed 4.11% lower at 62,262.92, with an intraday low of 61,923.60 — approximately 15% below the record high of 72,831.73 set on June 22. The broader TOPIX index fared better with a 2.8% decline, reflecting the Nikkei's price-weighted design that amplified losses in high-priced chip stocks.

Chip Supply Chain Under Siege

  • Kioxia Holdings: Crashed 18.11% to the daily limit, down over 57% from late-June highs
  • Tokyo Electron: Fell 10.89%, as equipment outlook comes into question
  • Advantest: Dropped 10.31%, as AI-testing demand expectations soften
  • SoftBank Group: Declined 5.16%, dragged by ARM holdings valuation

The yen traded near 163.74 against the dollar, but the traditional weak-yen boost for exporters was completely overwhelmed by the magnitude of chip stock losses. Toyota rose only 0.5% and Sony 0.6%, but double-digit percentage declines in heavyweight chip names crushed any positive signal from currency tailwinds.

"This is not a broad selloff, or the market is not on the risk-off sentiment." — Daisuke Hashizume, Senior Strategist at Daiwa Securities

The Nikkei's crash comes just ahead of earnings reports from Japan's three chip giants (Advantest, Tokyo Electron, Kioxia) on July 29-31, combined with the Fed rate decision (July 29) and the Bank of Japan policy statement (July 31). The next 72 hours represent a concentrated catalyst calendar that will determine market direction.

FOMC Day 1: Rate Hike Odds Surge to 38%, Historic Divide

The Federal Reserve's July 28-29 FOMC meeting officially opened on Tuesday. Market-implied rate hike probability has surged from 10% two weeks ago to 36-40%. While all 104 economists surveyed by Reuters expect rates to remain unchanged at 3.50%-3.75%, derivatives market pricing reflects a 38% probability of a hike.

Key Battle Lines

  • Citadel Securities is betting on a surprise hike. Its Head of Macro Strategy Frank Flight warns: "The market may once again be underestimating the extent of the hawkish shift at the Fed."
  • Goldman Sachs economist David Mericle expects a hold but anticipates at least 1-2 dissenting votes in favor of a hike, similar to April's historic 8-4 split — the widest dissent since 1992
  • June CPI fell to 3.5% (from 4.2% in May) with core CPI at 2.6%, but energy prices remain 15.7% higher year-over-year
  • President Trump is publicly pressuring the Fed to cut rates, claiming other countries have lower rates. Chair Kevin Warsh's aversion to forward guidance is adding to market uncertainty
  • September rate hike probability has already risen to 68-70%, with markets viewing September as the more consequential decision point

The Fed will announce its rate decision on Wednesday, July 29 at 2:00 p.m. ET (2:00 a.m. Beijing time on July 30), followed by Chair Warsh's press conference at 2:30 p.m. ET.

Oil Market: Brent Crashes 8.7% Below $88, Down Over 10% in Two Sessions

International crude prices suffered their worst single-day drop in three months on Monday. Brent crude plunged 8.7% to settle at $88.36/barrel, while WTI fell 7.5% to $82.61. Both benchmarks continued sliding in Asian trading on Tuesday, with Brent dipping below $88 and WTI near $82.

Crash Drivers

  • US-Iran Truce Window: Trump ordered a pause on airstrikes against Iran. An Omani mediation team has been stationed in Tehran for technical negotiations on reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Trump said the US and Iran are having "very good talks."
  • Supply Recovery: Kazakhstan's CPC crude export terminal resumed loading after Ukrainian drone strike disruptions were resolved
  • Supply Glut Risk: Macquarie warns that successful diplomacy could leave the global oil market with a ~2 million bpd surplus in Q4
  • But Risks Persist: Strait of Hormuz daily transit volumes remain far below pre-war levels — just 2.9 million bpd last week versus 5.9 million bpd previously. Trump explicitly warned that strikes would resume "very strongly" if negotiations fail
"Any strike on major Gulf export infrastructure could force a retest of $95-100 and beyond." — Anindya Banerjee, Head of Commodity Research at Kotak Securities

Wall Street Close: Dow Stands Alone, Apple Retakes Global No. 1

U.S. stocks closed mixed overnight. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 262.83 points (+0.51%) to 52,210.08, a one-week high. The S&P 500 was nearly flat at 7,413.18. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.18% to 24,932.08, extending its losing streak to four consecutive sessions.

  • Apple (AAPL): Rose 1.17% to $336.91, market cap approaching $5 trillion, reclaiming the title of world's most valuable company from Nvidia
  • Nvidia (NVDA): Crashed 4.99% to $196.51, as WSJ reported it is in talks to provide $250 billion in financing guarantees for an OpenAI data center project, reigniting "circular financing" controversy
  • Philadelphia Semi Index: Fell over 2%, now down 21% from its June 22 record high
  • SanDisk: Plunged 11%, Kioxia ADRs fell over 7%, as memory stocks sold off broadly
  • China ADRs: Bucked the trend, with the China ADR龙头 index rising 1.22%

Super Week is far from over: earnings reports from Microsoft, Meta, Apple, and Amazon are due this week, along with U.S. Q2 GDP and core PCE inflation data on Thursday. Markets are entering the highest-density information window of the quarter.
