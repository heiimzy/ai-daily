Alibaba Chairman Joe Tsai Announces Divorce: No Plan to Sell Alibaba Shares

Joe Tsai, the 62-year-old chairman of Alibaba Group and owner of the NBA's Brooklyn Nets and WNBA's New York Liberty, and his wife Clara Wu Tsai announced on August 1 the end of their nearly 30-year marriage. In a joint statement, spokespeople for the couple said ending the marriage was a mutual decision made with deep respect for one another. "Over the years, their relationship evolved into a partnership — running their businesses together and raising their children together. Although their differences have grown, their divorce is amicable and their relationship remains strong and professional," the statement said.

The statement confirmed that Tsai will continue as chairman of Brooklyn Sports & Entertainment and chairman of the Brooklyn Nets, while Wu Tsai remains vice chairman and chairman of the New York Liberty. The teams will continue to be owned and managed by the professional teams, with three adult children planned to participate in related businesses. Regarding Alibaba Group, the statement was clear: there is no plan to sell any Alibaba shares, and Tsai will continue to serve as Alibaba Group chairman with no change to his role.

According to Alibaba's annual report for the fiscal year ending March 2026, Tsai holds a total of 270 million Alibaba shares in various forms — including beneficial ownership, spousal interests and controlled-entity interests — representing approximately 1.43% of total issued shares, of which the designated spousal interest is 1.28 million shares, or 0.01%. Tsai joined Alibaba in 1999 as one of its founders, served as CFO until 2013 and executive vice chairman until September 2023, and now serves as chairman. He is also a director of Ant Group.

Four Regulators Issue Top-Level Governance Rules: 22 Measures, Firewalls and Lifelong Accountability

On July 31, the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA), the People's Bank of China, the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) and the Ministry of Finance jointly issued the Opinions on Improving Financial Institution Governance, targeting chronic problems such as disorderly intervention by major shareholders, equity chaos and misconduct by executives. The document puts forward 22 measures covering shareholder governance, internal governance and supervision, spanning the banking, securities and insurance sectors. The goal: by 2029, a governance mechanism with clear rights and responsibilities, compatible incentives and constraints, strict risk management and efficient operations.

A key breakthrough is the establishment of a tiered, differentiated regulatory system: institutions will be supervised according to asset size, risk profile and ownership structure, with look-through supervision of shareholder equity and related-party transactions based on substance over form. The rules build a "firewall" between industrial and financial capital, barring enterprises with excessive leverage, cross-industry operations, serious dishonesty or major violations from becoming major shareholders or actual controllers of financial institutions. They also prohibit false capital contributions, circular capital injections and capital flight, and establish systems to claw back improper shareholder gains and pursue post-event compensation. For executives and core staff, the rules close the "job-hopping loophole": those who violate rules at one institution can no longer escape accountability by moving to another, with lifelong accountability emphasized.

Xiangcai Securities and Guosheng Securities Probed by CSRC on Same Day

On the evening of July 31, Xiangcai Co., Ltd. (600095) and Guosheng Securities (002670) respectively announced they had received "case filing notices" from the CSRC. Both Xiangcai Securities, the wholly-owned subsidiary of Xiangcai, and Guosheng Securities are under investigation for suspected violations of real-name account rules under the Measures for the Administration of Securities Brokerage Business. Both companies said they will cooperate fully with the investigation and that operations remain normal.

Notably, Xiangcai Securities is in the middle of a share-swap absorption merger with Shanghai DZH (601519): Xiangcai shares at 7.51 yuan per share, DZH at 9.53 yuan, with each DZH share exchanging for 1.27 newly issued Xiangcai shares. The deal was suspended by the Shanghai Stock Exchange in March 2026 due to expired valuation data, then resumed in May after updates. Xiangcai Securities also faces a lawsuit involving approximately 233 million yuan in estimated liabilities, with the case remanded for retrial. Guosheng Securities is the only provincial-level securities firm in Jiangxi, with registered capital of 1.935 billion yuan and 166 branches across 27 provinces.

Jiaoda Angli, First Listed Health Supplement Company, Faces ST Over Financial Fraud

On the evening of July 31, Jiaoda Angli (600530, share price 4.99 yuan, market cap 3.867 billion yuan) disclosed receipt of an advance notice of administrative penalty from the CSRC Shanghai bureau: both its 2023 accounting error restatement announcement and 2024 annual report contained false records. The bureau plans to order rectification, issue a warning and impose a 4 million yuan fine. The stock will be subject to other risk warnings, with its ticker changing to ST Jiaoda, though it does not trigger mandatory delisting for major violations.

The violations center on Angli Small Loan, a 50%-owned associate, and Jiuding Pawnshop, a wholly-owned second-tier subsidiary: in 2015, Jiuding Pawnshop lent 47 million yuan to Xingpu Garments, with 23.5 million yuan in impairment provisions by end-2021. In August 2023, the company restated to add 23.5 million yuan in impairment, but the CSRC found insufficient accounting basis — allegedly reducing 2021 pre-tax profit by 23.5 million yuan (213.52% of restated 2021 profit). At end-2024, the company reversed the impairment, allegedly inflating 2024 profit by 23.5 million yuan (60.26% of reported profit). Former chairmen Ji Lin and Ji Min, and former CFO Cao Yi, face fines of 2 million, 1 million and 1 million yuan respectively.

UK Fiscal Discipline: PM and Chancellor Memo Tells Ministers to Stick to Spending Limits

The UK government on August 1 published a joint memo from the Prime Minister and the Chancellor of the Exchequer instructing department ministers to strictly respect spending limits. The Chancellor said the Budget on October 28 will "meet our fiscal rules" and give families the "stability" to plan for the future. The memo is a fiscal-discipline signal ahead of the autumn Budget, and markets will watch closely how the government balances taxation and spending.

Global markets remain in a sensitive phase: the S&P 500 logged its first July decline since 2014 and chip stocks suffered their worst month in 24 years, while reports that Fed Chair Warsh is considering fewer scheduled policy meetings continue to ripple through markets. The UK's fiscal-discipline stance adds another note to the "austerity" narrative among major economies.
