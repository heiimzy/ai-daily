⚡ SK Hynix Reports Record Q2 Earnings: Revenue Hits 79.3T Won, Operating Profit Surges 557% YoY, HBM4 Mass Shipments Begin

South Korean memory giant SK Hynix released its second-quarter 2026 earnings on Wednesday morning (July 29), delivering a critical validation point for the AI memory supercycle just one day after the worst global chip selloff in months.

Record-Breaking Results, Slightly Below Lofty Expectations

According to SK Hynix's regulatory filing, the Q2 2026 financial highlights include:

  • Revenue: 79.31 trillion won (~$54.5B), up 256.8% YoY and 51% QoQ — an all-time quarterly record
  • Operating Profit: 60.54 trillion won (~$41.6B), up 557.2% YoY and 61% QoQ — also a historic high
  • Net Profit: 93.92 trillion won (~$64.6B), surging 1,242.5% YoY — another record
  • Operating Margin: 76%, up from 72% in Q1, surpassing TSMC's ~60% for the second consecutive quarter
  • First-half cumulative revenue: 131.9 trillion won, exceeding the 100 trillion won mark for the first time

While the absolute numbers were all record-breaking, operating profit fell slightly short of the market consensus of approximately 64.1 trillion won (per FnGuide), causing after-hours ADR trading to dip over 9% initially. However, shares quickly rebounded as investors digested the details, opening the Korean session up 3.74%.

HBM4 Mass Production: Extending Technology Leadership

SK Hynix disclosed that its fifth-generation High Bandwidth Memory (HBM4) began mass shipments in Q2, with plans to accelerate production ramp-up in the second half of the year. HBM4E (enhanced) samples have already been delivered to customers, developed using a mass-production-optimized process.

According to Counterpoint Research, SK Hynix commanded 58% of the global HBM market by revenue in Q1, far ahead of Samsung Electronics (21%) and Micron Technology (21%). The company has cemented its deep partnership with Nvidia as the primary memory supplier for AI accelerators.

The company also revealed it has completed long-term supply agreement (LTA) negotiations with approximately 10 major customers, including key clients, and continues discussions with other customers to secure stable medium-to-long-term supply.

Asian Chip Stocks Bounce Back After "Black Tuesday"

Following the brutal global semiconductor selloff on Tuesday (July 28), SK Hynix's record earnings injected fresh confidence into Asian markets. Regional semiconductor stocks rebounded broadly in Wednesday morning trading:

  • KOSPI: Opened +1.1%, expanded to +2.76% at 6,190, recovering from yesterday's 10.84% crash that triggered the 8th circuit breaker of 2026
  • Nikkei 225: Reclaimed 63,000, up 1.07% after yesterday's 3.95% plunge
  • SK Hynix: +3.74% to 1,608,000 won (recovering from -14.65% on Tuesday)
  • Samsung Electronics: +4.55% to 230,000 won (recovering from -13.39%)
  • Kioxia: Surged 7.30%, among the top Nikkei 225 gainers

Analysts widely view Tuesday's selloff as an overreaction driven by funding concerns and forced deleveraging rather than deteriorating AI demand fundamentals. SK Hynix's strong results provide tangible evidence that AI memory demand remains robust.

The Earnings "Miss" — A Structural Story

While revenue and profit both set records, the 60.54 trillion won operating profit fell short of the market's optimistic 64.1 trillion won consensus. Analysts attribute this gap to two structural factors:

  1. Shifting HBM product mix: As HBM4 ramps up, its share in the DRAM mix increases. Although HBM margins are exceptional, the initial ASP growth trajectory differs from conventional DRAM ASP spikes during tight supply periods
  2. LTA pricing mechanics: The growing adoption of long-term agreements provides volume certainty but caps price flexibility compared to spot market sales

Korea Investment & Securities had projected Q2 operating profit of approximately 60.4 trillion won, closely matching the actual figure, suggesting that the market's loftier expectations may have been overly optimistic.

Key Forward-Looking Themes

SK Hynix's earnings serve as a critical bellwether for the global AI infrastructure investment cycle. Key areas to monitor going forward:

  • Q3 guidance: Management's outlook for sequential growth trajectory will be the primary stock price driver
  • Nvidia $50B partnership: Specific financial contributions from the recently announced joint investment plan will be crucial for valuation
  • Competitive landscape: China's CXMT and domestic DUV lithography developments are reshaping long-term pricing power expectations
  • Macro events: Tonight's FOMC decision and tomorrow's June PCE data will influence global risk asset sentiment

Samsung Electronics is scheduled to report Q2 results on Thursday (July 30), with market expectations of 170-172 trillion won in revenue and 89.3-89.5 trillion won in operating profit. The back-to-back earnings from Korea's two chip titans will determine whether the KOSPI can sustain its recovery.
