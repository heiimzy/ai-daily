⚡ Aug 4 Tech Update: June AI Emerges From Stealth With $20M, Yellow.ai Goes Public Via $550M SPAC Merger, Siemens and NVIDIA Automate Chip Verification

Late on August 3, the AI world moved fast on three fronts: an enterprise software implementation platform founded by Salesforce alumni stepped out of stealth, an agentic AI platform serving more than 650 global enterprises decided to go public via a blank-check company, and chip EDA giant Siemens pushed "autonomous verification" AI agents into the spotlight. The stories point to enterprise AI adoption, agent commercialization, and AI designing AI — here is the roundup.

1. June AI Exits Stealth With $20M Pre-Seed to Reinvent Enterprise Software Implementation

On August 3, enterprise AI implementation platform June AI emerged from stealth with $20 million in pre-Seed funding. The round was led by TIME Ventures, the investment vehicle of Marc Benioff, with an all-star roster of enterprise software leaders participating as individuals: Dell Technologies founder and CEO Michael Dell, VMware co-founder and former CEO Diane Greene, Box co-founder and CEO Aaron Levie, and CrowdStrike founder and CEO George Kurtz. SV Angel, Conviction Embed, Abstract, A* and Vesey Ventures also joined.

June AI was founded by the team behind Bonobo AI — the conversational intelligence company Salesforce acquired in 2019 — CEO Efrat Rapoport, CTO Idan Tsitiat, President Barak Goldstein and Chief Architect Ohad Hen. After five years inside Salesforce, they watched AI transformation collide with the gritty reality of enterprise software implementation. Their target is one of tech's biggest hidden cost centers: companies spend hundreds of billions of dollars each year configuring, integrating and maintaining systems such as Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday, Oracle, SAP and Microsoft. Grand View Research projects the global system integration market will reach $1.3 trillion by 2033.

June's approach is to let AI do the implementation work itself: it starts with process mining to understand how a business actually runs, then automates migration, change and optimization, running seamlessly within existing data platforms such as Snowflake and Databricks. "Oddly enough, AI has created more demand for professional services, not less," said CEO Rapoport. "The hard part is not the demo — it's changing the systems, workflows and operating models that already exist inside the enterprise."

2. Yellow.ai Goes Public Via $550M SPAC Merger, Targeting the BPO-to-AI Reallocation

On the same day, enterprise agentic AI platform Yellow.ai announced a definitive Business Combination Agreement with Bluerock Acquisition Corp. (Nasdaq: BLRK), a publicly traded SPAC. The combined company will operate as Yellow.ai and trade on the Nasdaq Capital Market under the ticker "YAI." The deal values Yellow.ai at a pre-money valuation of approximately $300 million, implying a pro forma equity value of about $550 million and more than $200 million in gross proceeds (including roughly $175 million of trust cash and $30 million of committed PIPE financing), with closing expected in the second half of 2026.

Founded in 2016 by Raghu Ravinutala, Rashid Khan and Jaya Kishore Reddy, Yellow.ai's Nexus platform runs on multiple AI models rather than being locked to one provider. The company reports it processes 16 billion conversations annually for 650+ enterprise clients across 135+ languages and 85+ countries, with more than $34 million in unaudited revenue last fiscal year. It has raised $100M+ from Lightspeed, Salesforce Ventures, Sapphire Ventures and WestBridge Capital.

The strategic thesis is a bet on one of the largest enterprise reallocations of the coming decade: the shift from human-delivered to AI-delivered customer experience. The BPO market is a $384 billion, labor-intensive category where roughly 85% of customer-service calls are still answered by humans; it is projected to reach $906 billion by 2035, with the AI agent sub-segment compounding from $12 billion to $295 billion at roughly a 43% CAGR. Yellow.ai plans to use the proceeds to accelerate enterprise sales in North America and Europe and to acquire complementary BPO operators, transforming them into AI-native operations.

3. Siemens and NVIDIA Upgrade Fuse EDA With "Autonomous Verification" AI Agents

On the chip design side, Siemens announced on August 3 that it is expanding its partnership with NVIDIA, upgrading the Fuse EDA AI Agent system so AI agents move from automatically executing engineering tasks to continuously checking, verifying and correcting their own decisions. The technology has already been applied to characterization of custom IC cell libraries, shortening the characterization cycle by more than 10x and cutting token costs by 5-10x.

Chip verification is a notorious bottleneck — it can consume up to 70% of total design time. Siemens is bringing NVIDIA's AI infrastructure, inference models and accelerated computing into agentic AI workflows with "autonomous verification" capability: agents can reason, plan and call different EDA tools, while a deterministic, physics-based EDA engine continuously validates their outputs, forming a closed "reason-execute-verify-correct" loop. For custom IC design, the Solido Characterization Suite's AI agents can call Solido LibSPICE, Solido Generator and Solido Analytics to automatically generate and verify Liberty files.

On the digital verification side, Siemens builds on its Questa One Agentic Toolkit and adds NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra, an inference model designed for complex, long-running AI agent tasks. In RTL benchmarks with the ACE-RTL agent, Nemotron 3 Ultra showed strong performance among open models, letting agents evaluate engineering trade-offs and continuously validate results against standard test environments. Abhi Kolpekwar, Siemens EDA's senior vice president and GM of digital verification, said the industry is at an inflection point: the complexity of AI chips, chiplets and 3D ICs is outgrowing traditional verification methods, and agentic AI is the natural evolution — future agents will collaborate across verification flows, reasoning over billions of test scenarios to shorten the path to production-quality results.
