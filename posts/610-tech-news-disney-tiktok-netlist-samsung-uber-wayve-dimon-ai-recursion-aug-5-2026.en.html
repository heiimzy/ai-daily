⚡ Aug 5 Tech Update: Disney-TikTok Content Deal, Samsung-Netlist 5-Year Alliance, Uber-Wayve London Robotaxi Licenses, Dimon's AI Risk Push, Recursion's AI-Discovered Neuro Target

Over the past hour or two, big-company moves, semiconductors, autonomous driving and AI drug discovery all delivered major headlines: Disney and TikTok announced a "first-of-its-kind" global short-form content-sharing deal that brings creator videos to Disney+; Samsung and Netlist buried the hatchet after years of patent battles, signing a five-year strategic alliance with a 10-million-share equity investment; Uber and Wayve won London robotaxi licenses with more than 100,000 Londoners on the waitlist; JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon is rallying corporate leaders into a cross-industry group to tackle AI risks; and Recursion said Genentech exercised the first validated neuroscience target discovered through their AI map. Here is this round's tech roundup.

1. Disney × TikTok: "First-of-its-Kind" Global Content Deal Brings Creator Videos to Disney+

On Aug 5, Disney and TikTok jointly announced a "first-of-its-kind" global short-form content-sharing agreement: opt-in TikTok creators will gain access to Disney's IP asset library (including character photos and videos), and their Disney-fan content will appear on both TikTok and Disney+'s vertical hub "Verts." The companies said a "thoughtfully curated" selection of videos will live on both platforms, and best-in-class creators can unlock special rewards, increased visibility, exclusive events and career development pathways.

Disney's chief marketing and brand officer Asad Ayaz said: "The best storytellers are fans first... This collaboration creates a new bridge between the stories we tell and the creativity they inspire." Dawn Yang, TikTok's global head of entertainment, added that "creators are at the heart of everything we do at TikTok." The deal echoes Disney's short-lived earlier plan with OpenAI to bring Sora videos to Disney+, but without the AI-generated angle; the US launch comes within the next couple of months, with other markets to follow. Vertical video has become a top priority for streamers — Peacock, HBO Max and Netflix have all rolled out enhanced vertical experiences in recent months.

2. Samsung × Netlist: Patent War Ends, Five-Year Alliance Signed with 10M-Share Investment

On the same day, memory technology company Netlist announced a strategic alliance with Samsung, signing five-year agreements covering patent portfolio cross-license, memory product supply and technology cooperation. Under the deal, Samsung will receive access to Netlist's complete patent portfolio, including its server DIMM and High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) technologies, while Samsung will supply Netlist with DRAM and NAND products. Critically, the parties agreed to settle and mutually release all pending legal actions — Netlist had pursued multiple AI-memory patent cases against Samsung, including at the US International Trade Commission (ITC); the two sides have now formally made peace.

In connection with the memory product supply agreement, Samsung will purchase ten million shares of Netlist common stock. Netlist CEO C.K. Hong said the strategic agreements "reflect the companies' shared commitment to innovation in the AI-memory space and validates the value of Netlist's IP." Netlist hosted a conference call at 8:30 a.m. Eastern Time to discuss the transaction. From courtroom adversaries to strategic partners, the deal marks a notable re-rating of the value of AI-memory intellectual property.

3. Uber × Wayve: London Robotaxi Licenses Secured, 100,000+ Londoners Queued Up

Transport for London (TfL) has granted Private Hire Vehicle (PHV) licenses to Wayve's autonomous vehicles, a major step forward for Uber and Wayve's London robotaxi plans. The licensed fleet consists of autonomous all-electric Ford Mustang Mach-E vehicles equipped with the Wayve AI Driver, surround cameras and radar; the vehicles complete the "triple-lock" requirement — operator, driver and vehicle all licensed by the same authority. Initially, a trained, TfL-licensed private hire driver will remain aboard each vehicle to supervise and take control when needed, moving a step closer to fully driverless operations.

Public interest has been strong: more than 100,000 Londoners have signed up to Uber's Interest List in the past eight weeks, and select riders will get access to early trial rides later this summer. Wayve's AI-first approach, known as AV2.0, moves beyond HD maps and hand-coded rules, learning from experience to adapt to new roads, vehicles, weather and cities; its technology was built and trained on UK roads. Annie Dvunjak, Uber's global head of autonomous mobility operations, called the license "a key milestone in bringing autonomous rides to London on Uber" — putting Uber ahead in its race against Waymo and Baidu.

4. JPMorgan CEO Dimon Leads Cross-Industry Effort to Tackle AI Risks

According to a Reuters exclusive, JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon is urging corporate leaders to join a US-focused industry group to address risks posed by AI. Against the backdrop of rapid AI adoption across corporate America, Dimon's initiative signals business-led momentum toward AI governance and risk coordination. Dimon has been outspoken on AI before — warning it will reduce some jobs while insisting AI has "created a lot of jobs" at JPMorgan. This cross-industry push marks a shift from single-company risk management to industry-wide coordination.

5. Recursion × Genentech: First AI-Discovered Neuroscience Target Officially Optioned

AI drug discovery company Recursion announced that Roche's Genentech has exercised the first validated target option in their 4.5-year, up-to-$12 billion AI drug discovery collaboration, with the two companies co-developing a small-molecule early discovery program based on the AI-discovered target. Genentech's acceptance of the validation package triggered a $3 million milestone payment, bringing total cash paid by Roche/Genentech to Recursion to $216 million; per program, Recursion can receive up to $300 million in development, commercialization and net-sales milestones, plus tiered royalties up to high single digits.

The target was discovered and validated using the first whole-genome CRISPR knockout map built by the partners — generated from over one trillion internally manufactured neuronal cells derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), roughly 12 brains' worth of neurons — spanning more than 17,000 genes and thousands of small molecules, with over 46 million cellular images analyzed. Recursion CEO Najat Khan said: "Finding new targets in neuroscience has historically been challenging, and this milestone highlights our ability to uncover novel biology in areas where conventional approaches have struggled." With more than three billion people affected by neurological conditions worldwide but only 1 in 40 neuroscience drugs reaching approval, AI maps could open an entirely new target space.
