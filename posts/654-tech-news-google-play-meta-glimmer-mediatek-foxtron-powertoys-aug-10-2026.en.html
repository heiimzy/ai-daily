A wave of heavyweight tech news hit this Monday evening: Google makes an "epic" concession under Epic Games' legal pressure, allowing third-party app stores onto Google Play as ordinary apps for the first time; Meta open-sources Muse Glimmer, a 30B-parameter model built for local AI agents; MediaTek reports July revenue up 12.16% year on year; Foxconn-built Foxtron Bria heads to Australia as the Mitsubishi ASX VR-e; and Microsoft's PowerToys preview adds on-device Phi Silica support. Here are the details, with all source publication times verified.

1. Google's "Epic" Concession: Rival Store Aptoide Games Is Coming to Google Play

According to ITHome (published Aug 10, 18:39 CST, citing Android Authority), Aptoide's Android game store Aptoide Games is about to launch on Google Play in the US. Users will simply search for Aptoide Games in the Play Store and tap install to get a game-download platform fully independent of Google Play (Source: ITHome).

The change sends a clear signal: Google's grip on Android app distribution is loosening, with Epic Games' years-long legal battle as a key driver. Android has always been more open than iPhone — users could sideload apps and third-party stores have existed for years — but it was never convenient: installing a store not listed on Play meant finding the official site, downloading an APK, allowing unknown sources, and wading through a string of Android security warnings. Once Aptoide Games is on Google Play, installing a competitor's store will feel just like downloading any normal app.

The report adds that Google recently confirmed qualified third-party app stores can now enter the US Play Store through a new registration mechanism, and that its Play Catalog Access program will let eligible third-party marketplaces access Google Play's app catalog.

2. Meta Open-Sources Muse Glimmer: A 30B Model Built for Local Agents

According to ITHome (published Aug 10, 18:52 CST), Meta today released Muse Glimmer, open-sourcing the model weights under the Apache 2.0 license. Muse Glimmer is a 30-billion-parameter (30B) model deeply optimized for always-on, locally running AI agent workflows (Source: ITHome).

Meta says Muse Glimmer uses 4-bit quantization, letting it run locally on Macs or PCs with 24GB/32GB of VRAM, with testing showing the compression has a negligible impact on agent-task performance. The company tested it on MacBook M4 Max, M5 Max and RTX 5090 platforms; results show the model can handle fluid conversation and real-time agent interactions, with 100% of compute done on-device.

Compared with Gemma4-31B and Qwen3.6-27B, Muse Glimmer delivers strong performance for its size class across major LLM benchmarks. The weights are available on Hugging Face (meta-models/Muse-Glimmer-30B) for developers to download and deploy.

3. MediaTek July Revenue Hits NT$48.475B, Up 12.16% YoY

According to ITHome (published Aug 10, 19:09 CST), MediaTek today released its July 2026 revenue report: July revenue reached NT$48.475 billion (about RMB 10.16 billion at current rates), up 12.16% year on year; January-July cumulative revenue stood at NT$349.808 billion (about RMB 73.32 billion), up 0.84% YoY (Source: ITHome).

Month by month, MediaTek's January-June figures were: Jan NT$46.977B (-8.15% YoY), Feb NT$38.954B (-15.63%), Mar NT$63.219B (+12.90%), Apr NT$46.737B (-4.14%), May NT$47.434B (+4.99%), Jun NT$58.012B (+2.80%). July's return to double-digit growth points to stronger consumer-electronics and AI-device demand feeding through the chip supply chain.

4. Foxconn's Foxtron Bria Becomes the Mitsubishi ASX VR-e, Landing in Australia in Q4

According to ITHome (published Aug 10, 19:04 CST, citing Australian outlet EV Central), Mitsubishi's new EV for Australia is officially named ASX VR-e. Built on Foxconn's Foxtron Bria, it will be manufactured in Taiwan and is slated to arrive in Australia in Q4, with Foxconn and its automotive unit Foxtron (Hon Hai) involved in development and manufacturing, and Pininfarina responsible for the platform design (Source: ITHome).

Mitsubishi wants the familiar name to preserve brand heritage: the car keeps the long-running ASX nameplate and adds the VR badge tied to several classic high-performance Mitsubishi models. The ASX VR-e is not a simple rebadge — front and rear styling, wheels and interior trim are redesigned with Mitsubishi's own character, featuring an S-Duct that channels airflow from the front bumper to the hood, plus an active grille that opens and closes automatically.

For reference, the already-on-sale Foxtron Bria uses a 171kW rear motor in entry and mid trims, while the dual-motor AWD Bria Pioneer peaks at 298kW with a 3.9-second 0-100 km/h sprint; both use a 57.5kWh LFP battery, with NEDC range up to 516 km (RWD) and 466 km (AWD flagship), and DC fast charging up to 134kW. The Foxtron Bria measures 4,315mm long with a 2,800mm wheelbase, slotting in as a compact crossover.

5. Microsoft PowerToys Preview: Advanced Paste Gains On-Device Phi Silica

According to ITHome (published Aug 10, 18:50 CST, citing Neowin), Microsoft's Windows 11 productivity tool PowerToys has been updated to preview build 0.101.2211.0. The biggest change: Advanced Paste now supports the on-device model Phi Silica, letting users run this small model locally for free — no third-party API fees, and no need to configure Ollama or Microsoft Foundry (Source: ITHome).

The update also adds an AI model picker and more customization options to Advanced Paste, fixes a series of bugs in the Command Palette, and improves performance. In addition, the team has largely rebuilt Mouse Jump, migrating it from WinForms to WinUI.
