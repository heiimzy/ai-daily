⚡ Aug 1 International Roundup: 60,000 Migrants Storm Ceuta as at Least 57 Die, Italy Suspends Schengen with Spain, Trump Wavers on Patriots for Ukraine, Iran Halts Tankers in Hormuz, Nine Nations Warn on North Korean IT Workers

Ceuta: Around 60,000 Migrants Pour In Within 24 Hours, at Least 57 Die, Italy Suspends Schengen Arrangements with Spain

Around 60,000 migrants crossed from Morocco into the Spanish North African enclave of Ceuta within 24 hours, with local media reporting at least 57 deaths during the surge (BBC, 17:03 GMT; The Guardian, 17:12 GMT). Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, who arrived in Ceuta on Friday (July 31), called the influx "a violation of Spain's territorial integrity" and blamed human-trafficking gangs that deliberately twisted a recent Supreme Court ruling — which held that migrants intercepted at sea while trying to reach Ceuta or the sister enclave of Melilla cannot be summarily returned under special rules. "Trafficking mafias took a self-serving interpretation of a Supreme Court ruling, and it spread like wildfire," Sanchez said. Ceuta's president, Juan Jesus Vivas, said arrivals amounted to around 70% of the city's population of about 83,600, while Spain's Interior Ministry estimated about 50,000 entries since the early hours of Thursday. By 6pm Friday, more than 48,300 people had voluntarily returned to Morocco (AP, 17:16 GMT). Spain has deployed armed forces to reinforce Ceuta and Melilla — which reported 300-400 crossings overnight — and will set up a line of buoys near the breakwater at Ceuta's Tarajal beach. The crisis dwarfs the last mass crossing in May 2021, when about 10,000 people entered over two days. Italy then moved to suspend its free-travel Schengen arrangements with Spain by sea and air, citing the need to safeguard its citizens, in what will amount to "targeted, selective checks" on third-country nationals arriving from Spain (Guardian live blog, 17:43 GMT). Trump, meanwhile, blamed Spain for the migrant chaos (France 24, 16:27 GMT).

Trump: Not Sure About Sending Patriot Missiles to Ukraine, Calling It a "Big Step"

Speaking to reporters during his cabinet meeting at Camp David on Friday, Trump said he was unsure whether to grant Patriot air-defense missiles to Ukraine, calling it a "big step" (Guardian live blog, 17:42 GMT). Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy asked Trump for Patriot interceptors to defend against Russian attacks when the two leaders met earlier this week. "These weapons are incredible," Trump said. "We have to be very careful about letting somebody build them... it's a hard thing to give away that kind of technology, and you have to be very judicious... Those people that you give that technology to, they can someday turn on you. That is possible." The remarks underscore the administration's caution over transferring sensitive weapons technology to Kyiv.

Iran: IRGC Says It Halted Tankers Under US Escort in the Strait of Hormuz as Oil Prices Rise Again

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) said on Friday that two "non-compliant oil tankers" trying to pass through the Strait of Hormuz under US military escort were "struck and brought to a halt," while four other tankers quickly changed course and returned to their previous positions; the ships had tried to use an "undeclared route" under US aerial escort. Oil prices rose again after Iran said it was stopping ships in Hormuz, Reuters reported (17:17 GMT). The strait has been almost completely closed to traffic since the US and Iran returned to fighting two weeks ago, sending energy prices soaring; crude pushed past $90 a barrel on Thursday after US strikes on Iran in retaliation for its targeting of US bases in Jordan. The two sides each claim ships should use competing routes: Iran bombs vessels taking the southern route near Oman's coast, while the US bombs ships violating its blockade of Iranian vessels and ports. The US-Iran war has now dragged on for five months — far beyond Trump's initial promise that it would last "a few weeks" — and on Friday Trump gathered his cabinet at Camp David to chart a path forward; the Senate narrowly rejected a war-powers resolution asking the president to stop the war, 49-50, and a Reuters-Ipsos poll found only one in three Americans support the war, the lowest since it began. Kuwait's defence ministry said it shot down Iranian drones targeting "vital and military facilities," and Israeli forces carried out a huge detonation near the ancient Beaufort Castle in south Lebanon overnight, saying it targeted Hezbollah tunnels; Lebanese officials accused Israel of violating the ceasefire.

Nine Nations, 19 Agencies: Joint Warning That North Korean IT Workers Fund the Nuclear Arsenal with AI-Assisted Remote Work

A coalition of 19 government agencies across nine countries issued a joint statement on Friday warning the private sector against employing North Korean IT workers (Al Jazeera, 17:06 GMT). The signatories include the United States, South Korea, Japan, the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands and New Zealand. The statement accuses Pyongyang of relying on IT operatives deployed in North Korea and abroad who obtain remote work on freelancing and hiring platforms under stolen identities and remit their paychecks directly to state agencies — revenue used to fund its prohibited nuclear weapons and ballistic missile programs. The coalition warned that artificial intelligence is being deployed to expand Pyongyang's network of fraudulent remote IT workers, with operatives using increasingly sophisticated methods to obfuscate their identities. It also detailed the "laptop farm" tactic: overseas facilitators in North Korea, China, Russia and Southeast Asia set up sites housing company-issued devices that North Korean workers access remotely via VPNs to mask their true locations. The workers pose a growing insider threat to global firms through corporate data exfiltration, cryptocurrency theft and espionage. The coalition urged hiring platforms and employers to significantly strengthen identity-verification and hardware-vetting procedures.

Gaza: Trump Says Israel Is "Very Happy" with the Peace Plan as the Board of Peace Publishes Its Roadmap

Speaking after his Camp David cabinet meeting on Friday, Trump said the US has "an understanding" with Israel on the Gaza peace deal: "We do have an understanding with Israel. Israel is very happy about it. They helped us and they've been very good about it." He described the agreement as "a great breakthrough": "Nobody ever thought that would be possible, to disarm Hamas." But he did not answer the key question — whether Hamas disarmament or an IDF withdrawal from Gaza comes first (Guardian live blog, 17:32 GMT). His Board of Peace published a roadmap the same day: all parties reaffirm their commitment to end the violence, secure the "complete" withdrawal of Israeli troops and allow "a credible political path that achieves self-determination and statehood" for Palestinians; Hamas and other factions agreed to hand all civilian governance and security to the newly established National Committee for the Administration of Gaza (NCAG), with Gaza governed under the principle of "One Authority, One Law, One Weapon" and all police personnel undergoing comprehensive vetting. Senior Hamas official Ghazi Hamad told Reuters that the group will hand over its weapons to the NCAG, but that disarmament will be contingent on the withdrawal of Israeli troops, and that the NCAG and an international force must enter Gaza and dismantle Israeli-backed militias. On the Israeli side, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has yet to respond publicly, right-wing members of his government have called the plan "not acceptable," and one Israeli official told AFP there would be no IDF withdrawal from the Yellow Line unless Hamas undergoes "genuine disarmament." A US official said Trump would be "very, very disappointed" if Israel fails to go along with the plan.
