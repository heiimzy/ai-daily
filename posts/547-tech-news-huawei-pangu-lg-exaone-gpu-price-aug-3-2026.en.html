⚡ Aug 3 Tech Update: Huawei Open-Sources 505B Model Trained on Ascend NPUs, LG Debuts Korea's Largest 750B Open Model, GPU Prices Rise for a Third Time

Early on August 3, 2026, several major stories hit the broader tech world: Huawei open-sourced openPangu-2.0-Pro, a 505-billion-parameter model whose entire pretraining ran on Ascend 910B NPUs with zero Nvidia hardware, making it the first frontier-scale open-weight model to publicly claim fully domestic compute; almost simultaneously, LG AI Research released K-EXAONE 2.0, Korea's largest open-source model at 750B parameters under an Apache 2.0 license, far ahead of China's leading models on long-text comprehension; meanwhile, Nvidia and AMD raised graphics card prices for the third time this year as AI demand squeezes supply and memory costs surge; and Microsoft announced that Project Perception, its agentic AI security system, entered public preview on August 3 with a standout first cybersecurity model.

Huawei Open-Sources 505B Model: Pure Ascend Training, Zero Nvidia

On July 31, Huawei released the weights, inference code, and technical report for openPangu-2.0-Pro, live on GitCode's Ascend Tribe community and accessible via Huawei Cloud ModelArts Studio. It is a Mixture-of-Experts model with 505 billion total parameters and roughly 18 billion active per token, a 512K context window, and about 34 trillion training tokens; post-training included supervised fine-tuning, multiple rounds of reinforcement learning, and capability fusion through online distillation.

The headline claim is its training infrastructure: openPangu-2.0-Pro completed its full pretraining run on Huawei Ascend 910B NPUs with no Nvidia hardware involved at any stage. According to TechTimes, no prior frontier-scale open-weight model at 500B parameters or above has credibly made that claim. The model is part of Huawei's broader openPangu 2.0 open-source plan - at HDC 2026 in June, Huawei said it would gradually open-source seven key components starting June 30; a smaller 92B openPangu-2.0-Flash was released first.

"openPangu-2.0-Pro proves the Ascend software and architecture stack can sustain frontier-scale training. The next real test is whether a fully domestic manufacturing chain can do the same." - industry analysis

The supply chain story is more nuanced. TechInsights teardowns of Ascend 910B/910C chips found that essentially every chip contained dies fabricated at TSMC's 7nm node (Huawei acquired roughly 2.9 million such dies through Sophgo, a scheme U.S. authorities determined violated export controls). With that die bank effectively exhausted, future production depends on SMIC's N+2 process and CXMT's domestic HBM. So openPangu-2.0-Pro validates that the Ascend-plus-domestic-software stack can train frontier models, but not yet that a fully domestic hardware chain can do so independently.

LG Debuts Korea's Largest Open Model: K-EXAONE 2.0 at 750B

In the same week, LG AI Research released K-EXAONE 2.0 on Hugging Face on July 31 - 750 billion total parameters with 37 billion active, triple the size of its predecessor and the largest open foundation model ever released in Korea. The model ships under Apache 2.0, free for commercial use and modification.

According to Seoul Economic Daily, K-EXAONE 2.0 scored 94.4 on the OpenAI-MRCR long-text comprehension metric and 89.6 on the Korean-language Ko-LongBench, both significantly ahead of Zhipu AI's GLM-5.1 (71.5 and 83.6). LG says it will unveil an industry-specific foundation model next week and plans a public evaluation service.

K-EXAONE 2.0 is part of South Korea's "Homegrown AI Foundation Model Project" overseen by the Ministry of Science and ICT. Just days earlier, SK Telecom published its 688B-parameter A.X K2 - two chaebol releases within 48 hours have pushed the sovereign AI race into overdrive: the second-phase evaluation from August 8-11 will cut the field from four teams to three, with two winners selected by year-end to anchor the nation's AI infrastructure. Together with Huawei's openPangu, Asia's open-source model ecosystem is rapidly diversifying.

AI Demand Squeezes Supply: Nvidia/AMD GPU Prices Rise a Third Time

Graphics cards are seeing an extraordinary "three-peat" of price increases in 2026. Nvidia informed partners in July of its third hike of the year, with the entire RTX 50-series - from entry-level to flagship - rising up to 30%; AMD announced GPU and memory-kit price increases of at least 10% effective in August, hitting the RX 9000 series - the entry RX 9050 rises 10%, while the high-end RX 9070 XT, with its larger memory, climbs from its $599 launch price into the $700-750 range.

The core driver is AI demand's gravity well: AI startups and compute speculators are competing for consumer graphics cards to expand capacity, keeping supply persistently tight; DRAM prices are soaring (Samsung has raised DRAM quotes by 20%), pushing a 2GB GDDR7 module to roughly $20 and 3GB modules to $60-70; and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) bottlenecks for AI data centers are expected to persist into 2027. Omdia projects global semiconductor sales will surge 94.1% year over year in 2026, with memory chips accounting for more than half of revenue. For ordinary consumers, affordable graphics cards remain a distant hope.

Microsoft Project Perception Enters Public Preview: AI Agents That Patch Vulnerabilities

Microsoft is also accelerating its AI security push. Project Perception entered public preview on August 3, built around MDASH (its multi-agent vulnerability discovery and remediation harness) and MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, Microsoft's first cybersecurity-specialized AI model - a sparse MoE with 137B total and 5B active parameters, a 256K context window, and a focus on vulnerability discovery, validation, triage, prioritization, and remediation.

According to Microsoft, MDASH with MAI-Cyber-1-Flash and GPT-5.4 hit 95.95% on CyberGym, about 12 percentage points above Anthropic's Mythos, at roughly half the cost. The system uses red, blue, and green agents: red exposes weaknesses, blue detects and responds, and green remediates and hardens across the attack lifecycle. Microsoft draws a clear line between Project Perception and Security Copilot - Copilot is the assisted chat interface, while Perception is the system where agents actually act. Due to dual-use concerns, MAI-Cyber-1-Flash is available only through Azure AI Foundry Private Preview and restricted to use within MDASH, not as a general-purpose cyber API.
