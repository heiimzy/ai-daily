⚡ Aug 1 Financial Roundup: Texas Stock Exchange Goes Live Challenging the NYSE, Warsh Weighs Fewer Fed Meetings, KKR Nears $4B Integer Deal, Stocks Rise as Gold Retreats

Texas Stock Exchange (TXSE) Activates All Tickers — the First Major New US Exchange in Decades

On Friday (July 31), the Dallas-headquartered Texas Stock Exchange (TXSE) completed the activation of all National Market System (NMS) symbols, officially becoming the first major new national stock exchange to open in the United States in decades and directly challenging the NYSE and Nasdaq. Texas Governor Greg Abbott formally launched operations at 20:38 GMT (News Radio 1200 WOAI). TXSE has rolled out in carefully phased steps: test symbols went live on July 6, a subset of NMS tickers began real trading on July 10, and by July 31 all 12,000-plus US equity symbols were tradable (CryptoBriefing, 16:44 GMT). The exchange's pedigree is formidable — after receiving SEC approval on September 30, 2025, it raised $250 million from more than 80 institutional investors including BlackRock (which manages roughly $13.5 trillion), Charles Schwab, Citadel Securities and J.P. Morgan. TXSE operates fully electronically with no trading floor, and its core ambition goes beyond competing for existing volume: it wants to attract IPOs, secondary listings and dual listings. With no corporate income tax in Texas and a growing cluster of corporate headquarters, the exchange is a natural lever in the fight with New York over listing business.

Federal Reserve: Warsh Reportedly Considers Reducing the Frequency of Policy Meetings

The New York Times reported at 20:30 GMT that Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh is considering reducing how often the central bank holds its scheduled policy meetings. The Fed has met at least eight times a year for decades; if enacted, the change would be the biggest shift in monetary-policy mechanics since Warsh became chairman (NYT; Bloomberg followed up at 20:52 GMT). Bloomberg, citing the report, said the idea of cutting the number of meetings was raised internally this week (Reuters, 19:47 GMT). At Wednesday's (July 29) meeting, the Fed voted 9-3 to hold rates at 3.50%-3.75%, with three officials dissenting in favor of a 25-basis-point hike (Kitco PM closing report). Analysts note that with inflation still running at 3.7% year over year (June PCE) and geopolitical risk pushing oil higher, fewer meetings would signal a preference for "longer cycles, fewer moves" — and would change how much weight markets assign to each individual FOMC decision.

M&A: KKR Nears Deal Worth More Than $4 Billion for Medical-Device Outsourcer Integer Holdings

Private-equity giant KKR is closing in on a multibillion-dollar healthcare acquisition. The Wall Street Journal reported exclusively at 19:22 GMT that KKR is near a deal to acquire Integer Holdings (NYSE: ITGR), a medical-device outsourcing company, and that an agreement could be finalized soon (Reuters confirmed at 19:21 GMT; the Financial Times reported the deal is valued at more than $4 billion). The FT noted that Integer had recently drawn attention from an activist hedge fund founded by an Elliott Management alum. Integer shares jumped about 20% on the news, with reports putting the offer near $127 per share (Seeking Alpha, 19:19 GMT). Integer Holdings is a leading contract manufacturer for medical devices, supplying critical components for pacemakers, neurostimulators and other implantable equipment, making it a key node in the medical supply chain. If completed, the deal would rank among the largest private-equity take-privates in medical outsourcing this year.

US Stocks: Friday's Close Higher with the S&P 500 Up 0.7% — Europe's STOXX 600 Hits a Record

US stocks closed a turbulent July on a positive note Friday (July 31), with all three major indexes finishing higher: the S&P 500 rose 0.7% to 7,489.72, the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.0% to 25,373.85 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 0.5% to 52,485.03, while the small-cap Russell 2000 slipped 0.5% to 2,931.34 (Kitco PM Report, updated 20:54 GMT). Reuters reported "Wall Street ends higher as Amazon soothes AI jitters" — Amazon's blowout earnings lifted mega-cap tech while Apple weakened on supply concerns (Reuters, 20:01 GMT; WSJ called Amazon and Apple the drama of a turbulent month). European bourses followed suit: the STOXX 600 rose 0.8% to a record high, Germany's DAX added 0.7%, France's CAC 40 rose 0.7% and the FTSE 100 gained 0.6% (Kitco). Still, markets are far from comfortable: the 10-year Treasury yield hovered near 4.7%, and the 30-year yield had earlier hit its highest since 2007, keeping the inflation-versus-hikes debate very much alive.

Commodities: Gold Retreats Toward $4,050 and Silver Slips Nearly 2% as the Dollar and Yields Bite

Precious metals came under pressure on Friday. In late-afternoon US trading, spot gold was near $4,050.70 an ounce, down 1.27% on the session with a range of $4,018.20-$4,112.80, while spot silver traded near $57.75, down 1.93%, within a $56.86-$59.30 range (Kitco PM Report, 20:54 GMT). Kitco's analysts said a firmer US dollar, elevated Treasury yields and rising oil prices outweighed support from Thursday's softer PCE inflation data — even with core PCE cooling to 3.3% year over year, yield pressure leaves gold exposed to real-rate risk. Silver failed to sustain a push through the $59.00-$60.03 resistance band. Net-net, metals markets remain stuck in a tug-of-war between cooling inflation and a hawkish policy stance; most institutions expect gold to keep trading in wide ranges above $4,000 until the Fed's September meeting.
