State Department Issues Middle East Travel Warning: Americans Told to 'Consider Departing,' Fresh Strikes on Iran Could Come as Soon as This Weekend

Tensions in the Middle East escalated over the weekend. Citing the latest State Department advisory, CNN reported that Washington has issued a fresh travel warning to US citizens in the region, advising them to "consider departing, or be prepared to depart should there be escalation". The warning lands as the White House weighs a new round of strikes against Iran: according to multiple US officials, the US military is planning fresh strikes on Iran as soon as this weekend, though the scope, potential targets and timing remain unclear and could still be called off. President Donald Trump signaled the plan on Friday at a Camp David cabinet meeting, saying "we'll be hitting them very hard."

Meanwhile, Tehran has vowed more attacks on any country hosting US bases, and Kuwaiti authorities reported a new round of Iranian drone attacks early Saturday. The Associated Press, summarizing the day's developments, said Kuwait's military responded to fresh Iranian drone attacks while a tanker was struck in the strategic waterway. There is also dissent inside Washington: one lawmaker publicly slammed Trump, saying he "doesn't know what the heck he's doing." Analysts note that the escalation of the travel advisory signals the White House already expects military action to continue for some time - if strikes land over the weekend, oil, shipping and global markets would reprice violently once more.

CNN cited US officials saying a new strike campaign could begin "as soon as this weekend," with officials cautioning the plans could still change; the State Department is urging Americans in the region to "consider departing now" - among the starkest travel warnings of the US-Iran conflict so far.

Israel Strikes Gaza: Five Hamas Weapons Depots and Medicine Warehouses Destroyed, Over 1,222 Killed Since the Ceasefire

Days after Hamas and other factions signed a disarmament agreement, Israeli forces launched multiple rounds of strikes across Gaza on Saturday. Al Jazeera reported that Israeli strikes killed at least four Palestinians on Saturday: a drone attack killed two people in Gaza City's Sheikh Radwan neighborhood, one person was killed in Deir el-Balah, and another Palestinian died in a strike on a tent near a school in al-Mawasi, north of Khan Younis.

The most striking development was the targeting of medical facilities: an overnight strike destroyed medicine warehouses attached to Al-Aqsa Martyrs Hospital in Deir el-Balah - Gaza's Health Ministry said two of four storage facilities were completely destroyed and two badly damaged, calling the attack a "heinous crime" and appealing for international intervention to protect medical facilities. Hospital spokesman Khalil al-Daqran told the Anadolu agency that the strike destroyed intravenous fluids, medicines and supplies used in dialysis and intensive care. Witnesses said tents sheltering displaced families near the hospital caught fire. The Israeli army and Shin Bet, for their part, claimed they destroyed five Hamas weapons depots overnight - one located near Al-Aqsa Hospital - which they said stored assault rifles, explosives and anti-tank launchers.

Gaza's Health Ministry said seven people were killed and 76 wounded across the territory in the past 48 hours, and that at least 1,222 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire took effect on October 10, 2025, with the overall death toll since October 2023 reaching 73,349. The timing is telling: Hamas agreed to Trump's "Board of Peace" disarmament plan announced Thursday, but Israel has yet to officially comment, with unnamed senior officials saying Jerusalem "does not approve" of the plan. Former US deputy national security adviser Mark Pfeifle told Al Jazeera that Netanyahu's continued strikes send a signal that he is "not quite ready" to come to the framework. The Times of Israel cited a Board of Peace official saying Hamas will begin handing over weapons in the coming days, though full disarmament is expected to take between 200 and 350 days.

Munir al-Bursh, director-general of Gaza's Health Ministry: "When the occupation fights people by destroying medicines and preventing medicine from entering, it turns medicine and illness itself into a tool of war."

NBC Exclusive: Russia Is Sharing Electronic Intelligence with Iran, US and European Officials Say It Helps Tehran Pinpoint US Forces

NBC News reported exclusively, citing a US official and a European official, that Russia is providing Iran with electronic intelligence - including satellite surveillance and signals intelligence (SIGINT) - that is likely helping Tehran pinpoint American forces in aerial attacks or jam US-made weapons. According to experts and Western officials cited by NBC, the combination of Russian satellite monitoring, signals intelligence and technical insight into US weapons systems is allowing Iran to better defend against US airstrikes and refine its own attacks on moving targets such as ships and planes.

The report also said China has allowed Iran access to a surveillance satellite and planned to send shoulder-launched missiles to Tehran, though Beijing's assistance is described as smaller in scale than Moscow's. Trump downplayed the claims on July 28 aboard Air Force One, saying any Russian help had been "very unimpactful" and adding, "I'll ask Putin about it." A White House official told NBC: "As the president stated, nothing that any outside actor is doing to help Iran is in any way increasing their ability to target Americans." Neither Russia nor Iran has publicly commented, and the claims remain independently unverified.

In context, Russian-Iranian strategic cooperation has deepened steadily: in January the two countries signed a comprehensive strategic partnership agreement covering defense, trade, energy and security, and the West has long accused Iran of supplying Russia with Shahed drones and their blueprints for use in Ukraine - accusations both sides deny. If the NBC report is accurate, Iran is effectively leveraging Russian space-based reconnaissance and electronic-warfare capabilities to compensate for its own shortfalls in air defense and long-range precision strikes, a dynamic that could prolong the stalemate in the US-Iran conflict.

Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Plant Loses Off-Site Power for the 23rd Time; IAEA Calls for Restraint

On the Ukraine front, the International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA) said Saturday that the Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant lost off-site power for the 23rd time since the conflict began. The latest outage lasted nearly two hours and the cause is not yet known. Europe's largest nuclear plant, occupied by Russian forces since 2022, has repeatedly faced blackout risks; each loss of external power forces the facility to rely on emergency diesel generators to keep reactor cores cool, a serious safety concern. IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi again urged all parties to exercise "maximum restraint" and avoid any action endangering nuclear safety.

Meanwhile, mutual strikes between Russia and Ukraine continue to intensify: Russia's Defense Ministry said it intercepted 274 Ukrainian drones overnight; Ukraine's General Staff announced strikes on the Syvash railway bridge linking occupied Crimea with the Kherson region and another railway bridge, along with targets including a Russian Black Sea Fleet signals-intelligence unit; President Zelensky said Ukrainian forces struck the infrastructure of three oil refineries in the Bashkortostan region and sank the sanctioned Russian container ship Yanina. Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha called on allies to urgently supply more air defense systems: "It is the battle for the skies that will define the trajectory of this war."
