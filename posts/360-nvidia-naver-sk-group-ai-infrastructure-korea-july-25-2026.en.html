⚡ Nvidia Invests $1B in Naver AI Factory, $500B Partnership with SK Group: South Korea Builds Global AI Infrastructure Hub

🇰🇷 Nvidia and Brookfield to Invest $10 Billion in Naver

At the "San Francisco AI Summit" held on July 24 local time, Naver founder Lee Hae-jin announced that Nvidia and Brookfield Asset Management will invest a combined $10 billion (approximately 14.6 trillion won) in Naver's AI data center expansion in South Korea. Nvidia is committing $1 billion, while Brookfield is providing up to $9 billion in financing support.

Nvidia said the funding will more than triple the scale of Naver's AI data center. The project, jointly developed with Brookfield, will be fully equipped with Nvidia AI computing hardware. Naver's AI factory plan starts with a 55MW facility at the GAK Sejong data center, targeting gigawatt-scale capacity by 2029.

Naver, South Korea's dominant internet platform with the country's largest search engine and messaging service Line, has been aggressively expanding its AI infrastructure. In April, it secured a 400 billion won ($270 million) loan specifically for the Sejong data center expansion. In June, Naver announced a partnership with Nvidia to expand its AI infrastructure based on Nvidia's DSX platform, with plans to launch an AI agent platform in the second half of the year.

Brookfield, one of the world's largest infrastructure investors, co-launched a $100 billion global AI infrastructure investment program with Nvidia and the Kuwait Investment Authority last November. The Naver investment represents the program's first major project in the Asia-Pacific region.

🤝 Nvidia-SK Group: A $500 Billion Mega-Partnership

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang announced at the summit that SK Group and Nvidia will establish a comprehensive business partnership valued at over $500 billion — one of the largest cross-border corporate collaborations in Korean history. The partnership covers two major directions: Nvidia's procurement of memory chips and SK Group's procurement of Nvidia's supercomputing equipment.

Under the agreement, Nvidia and SK Group plan to build an AI data center cluster with total computing power exceeding 2 gigawatts in South Korea — enough electricity to power approximately 1.5 million households. SK Telecom will construct the first AI computing factory, set to begin operations next year.

"SK Group and Nvidia will announce a business partnership worth $500 billion," Huang said in an interview. "SK Hynix, Samsung, and Nvidia will collaborate on chip design and memory design." Nvidia will also assist SK Hynix in developing next-generation high-bandwidth memory (HBM) chips to secure a stable supply channel. With global AI data center construction driving insatiable demand, HBM chips remain in critically short supply.

🏭 Jensen Huang: "Korea Is in a Golden Age"

Huang praised South Korea's AI industry prospects at the summit, stating: "Korea is in a golden age. The country's semiconductor industry and industrial sector are both booming, with a complete capability to build AI infrastructure for the world."

Huang also announced plans to establish an "AI Frontier Lab" in Korea, relocating core Nvidia AI researchers from California to work with the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST) to develop Korean-language LLMs. Additionally, Nvidia will collaborate with Hyundai Motor Group to develop autonomous Genesis vehicles and advance robotics, and with LG to develop AI systems and power generation for factories and homes.

Huang is pushing Nvidia to expand beyond major cloud providers to serve a broader enterprise customer base, arguing that every country should build its own sovereign AI computing infrastructure while ensuring small and medium enterprises have access to AI computing resources.

📜 President Lee Jae-myung Unveils "San Francisco AI Declaration"

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung, on a state visit to the United States, delivered the "San Francisco AI Declaration" at the summit. "Korea will leap forward to become an irreplaceable core nation in the global AI supply chain," he said, emphasizing the country's world-class manufacturing competitiveness, digital infrastructure, and vibrant AI ecosystem.

The declaration represents a large-scale AI investment initiative built on extensive consensus among Korean and global companies with a combined market capitalization exceeding 2,000 trillion won. Korean business leaders including Samsung Electronics Chairman Lee Jae-yong, SK Group Chairman Chey Tae-won, Hyundai Motor Group Chairman Chung Euisun, and Naver Chairman Lee Hae-jin attended, alongside global tech executives including Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei, and Broadcom CEO Hock Tan.

President Lee pledged to "enable global enterprises to stably build AI infrastructure and continuously promote technological innovation under any circumstances." He proposed the concept of an "AI Basic Society," positioning Korea as a responsible partner willing to share AI opportunities and benefits globally. South Korea last month announced an $880 billion AI investment plan, with this U.S. visit aimed at converting it into concrete projects.

🌐 Market Impact and Industry Outlook

Nvidia's intensive Korea push aligns with its global expansion strategy. Earlier this week, Nvidia signed a $1.5 billion deal with Amkor Technology to expand its chip packaging factory, following Jensen Huang's Japan trip where he secured partnerships with robotics companies and Toyota.

Analysts note that the center of AI infrastructure investment is accelerating its shift from North America to Asia-Pacific. Naver's announcement of a gigawatt-scale AI data center project — from a non-hardware internet company — is a milestone that signals intensifying strategic competition in the APAC AI infrastructure space. SoftBank, Singapore's GIC, and Australian telecom operators are all advancing similar-scale AI data center plans.

For Nvidia, deepening ties with SK Group secures stable memory chip supply while establishing Korea as a core Asian AI R&D and production base. The $500 billion partnership creates a deep mutual dependency between the two groups: Nvidia secures HBM supply while SK Group gains access to Nvidia's cutting-edge computing hardware. This partnership model is expected to further cement Nvidia's dominance in the global AI computing market.
