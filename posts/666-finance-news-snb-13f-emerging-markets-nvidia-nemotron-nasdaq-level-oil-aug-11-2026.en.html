⚡ Aug 11 Finance Update: SNB's US Stock Holdings Hit a Record, EM Portfolios See $18.8B Net Inflow in July, Nvidia Open-Sources Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, Nasdaq to Acquire LeveL Markets, US-Iran Close to a Hormuz Arrangement

Tuesday evening brought a dense stream of global market news: the Swiss National Bank's latest 13F filing shows its US stock reserves rose more than 10% in Q2 to a record $191.4 billion, with Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft the top three holdings; IIF data show emerging-market fund flows improved sharply in July, with equity outflows slowing dramatically and bonds still drawing inflows, as non-resident investors added a net $18.8 billion and ended two straight months of outflows; Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, an open 30B-parameter model, sending its shares up nearly 2% premarket; Nasdaq announced a definitive agreement to acquire LeveL Markets, advancing its "always-on markets" strategy; and Pakistan's defense minister said the US and Iran are close to "some sort of arrangement" on the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent crude at $87.99. Details below; all source timestamps have been verified.

1. SNB 13F: US Stock Holdings Hit Record $191.4 Billion

According to Bloomberg (12:42 GMT) and Sina Finance (Aug 11, 21:09 CST), the Swiss National Bank's latest 13F filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission, published Tuesday, shows that its foreign-exchange reserves held in US stocks grew more than 10% in the second quarter to an all-time high. As of June 30, the SNB held positions in more than 2,300 US-listed companies worth $191.4 billion, with its three largest holdings being Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft.

As one of the world's largest central banks, the SNB's US equity allocation is often read as an institutional "vote" on American stocks: with the AI-compute rally and big-tech momentum, central banks and sovereign institutions have kept raising their US exposure, making 13F filings a key window into long-term capital flows.

2. IIF: EM Flows Rebound in July as Equity Outflows Slump, Bonds Keep Drawing Cash

According to Sina Finance (Aug 11, 21:03 CST, citing Institute of International Finance data), emerging-market equity outflows slowed to $7.8 billion in July, far below June's $46.1 billion; EM debt attracted $26.7 billion of inflows (versus $28.1 billion in June). Overall, non-resident investors added a net $18.8 billion to EM portfolios in July, ending two consecutive months of outflows. Cumulative EM debt inflows through July reached $214.4 billion, a marked improvement versus the same period of 2025.

Analysts tie the EM rebound to the dollar's path, Fed policy expectations and firmer commodity prices: when Treasury yield pressure eases and markets stop re-pricing "higher for longer," capital tends to flow back into EM bonds and equities where valuations look more attractive.

3. Nvidia Open-Sources Nemotron 3.5 Lightning; Shares Rise Nearly 2% Premarket

According to Sina Finance (Aug 11, 21:03–21:05 CST), Nvidia officially launched Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, an open 30B-parameter mixture-of-experts (MoE) model available through OpenRouter, Nvidia's build.nvidia.com developer platform and cloud partners. The release is backed by vLLM, Ollama, llama.cpp, LM Studio and Unsloth, with Nvidia's NEMO Switchyard said to cut benchmark completion costs to one-third of Opus 4.8.

Market reports also indicate Nvidia is developing the next-generation Nemotron 4 family of open models, aiming to drive chip demand through open-source AI — even as that puts Nvidia in direct competition with its own customers and partners at the model layer. On the news, Nvidia shares rose nearly 2% in premarket trading.

4. Nasdaq to Acquire LeveL Markets, Betting on 24/7 Trading

According to Nasdaq's official press release (Aug 11, 13:00 GMT) and Sina Finance (Aug 11, 21:00 CST), Nasdaq announced a definitive agreement to acquire LeveL Markets, advancing its "Always-On Markets" strategy. LeveL Markets is a provider of after-hours and overnight trading execution and liquidity; it previously announced a connectivity partnership with Nasdaq Canada, partnered with LSEG's Turquoise on block trading, and in June became a strategic investor in bond-trading platform OpenYield, which aims to bring equity-style trading to bonds.

As global exchanges race to extend trading hours and retail demand for 24/7 access grows, the deal is seen as a key step in Nasdaq's push toward round-the-clock market infrastructure.

5. US-Iran Close to a Hormuz Arrangement? Brent Pulls Back to $87.99

According to CNBC (updated 12:37 GMT), Pakistan's Defense Minister Khawaja Asif said Tuesday that the United States and Iran are close to "some sort of arrangement" over the Strait of Hormuz: "The signals in the last two to three days are that we are close to some sort of an arrangement." Earlier, a Qatari foreign ministry spokesperson told Al Jazeera that talks between Iran and Oman about opening a shipping channel through Hormuz were at a "critical juncture."

At the same time, Washington and Tehran appear to be hardening their stances: President Trump said Monday that Iran should pay reparations for "the damage they've done" over the past half-century, instructing his representatives to put the demand "firmly into any, and all, future negotiations." On prices, Brent crude jumped as much as 2.5% early Tuesday to near $90 a barrel before paring gains on Asif's comments, trading 0.3% higher at $87.99 per barrel at 8:27 a.m. ET. The strait carries roughly a fifth of the world's oil, making its reopening prospects the biggest swing factor for crude.

6. Outlook: Central Banks Add, Flows Return, and the AI Arms Race Widens

These five stories sketch three threads: long-term money keeps flowing into US and EM assets — the SNB's 13F shows central-bank-level institutions added more than 10% to US equities in Q2, while IIF data show EM portfolio inflows of $18.8 billion in July ended two months of outflows, a sign global risk appetite is healing; AI capital spending is spilling into models and trading infrastructure — Nvidia is using open-source models to drive chip demand (shares up nearly 2% premarket), while Nasdaq's LeveL Markets deal bets on the 24/7 trading era; and geopolitics remains the oil market's pricing engine — the US and Iran edge toward an arrangement while trading reparations demands, leaving Brent swinging below $90. All five items were published within the past two hours (12:37–13:14 GMT), so timeliness is solid.
