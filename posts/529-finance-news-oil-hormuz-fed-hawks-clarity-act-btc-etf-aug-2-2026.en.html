⚡ Aug 2 Finance News: Brent Surges 24% in July, Hormuz Escalates Over the Weekend; Three Fed Hawks Push for Hikes; CLARITY Act in Limbo as Bitcoin ETFs See $265M Outflow

Oil Caps Best Month Since March: Brent Rises 24% in July, Settles at $90.12 on Friday

Oil prices closed higher on Friday (July 31) as the US-Iran conflict intensified and shipping through the Strait of Hormuz remained disrupted: Brent crude futures settled up 1.2% at $90.12 a barrel, while WTI rose 1.3% to $84.67. For the month of July, Brent gained 24% and WTI 21% — the strongest monthly performance since March for both benchmarks. Prices had fallen more than 5% for the week after a Monday selloff on de-escalation hopes, but the geopolitical risk premium returned as the June 17 US-Iran memorandum of understanding effectively broke down, with both sides accusing each other of violations.

The supply-side catalysts were concrete: Iran's Revolutionary Guards said on Friday they had stopped two tankers trying to exit the Strait of Hormuz, with four others changing course; the Iranian body managing the strait said crossings remained "impossible" due to the "continued aggressive actions by U.S. military forces." Data from the U.S. Energy Information Administration on Friday showed U.S. crude output fell about 2% in May from April's record high, with stockpiles at multi-year lows. A Reuters poll of 31 economists and analysts lifted its 2026 Brent average forecast to $85.22 a barrel from $84.50, while Goldman Sachs warned Brent would stay below $80 unless the strait fully reopens by Q4 2026.

"The market has stopped trading the war and started trading the shipping data," said Ole Hvalbye, market analyst at SEB Research — the clearest summary of what now drives oil: not any single strike, but how many vessels can actually pass through the Strait of Hormuz each day.

Weekend Escalation in Hormuz: Kuwait Intercepts Iranian Drones, Qatari LNG Carrier Struck, Trump Weighs Energy Strikes

Tensions around the Strait of Hormuz flared again over the weekend. Kuwait's Ministry of Defense spokesman, Major General Saud Abdulaziz Al-Otaibi, said Kuwaiti forces intercepted Iranian drone attacks targeting "vital facilities"; falling shrapnel caused "material damage" but no injuries. Separately, the UK Maritime Trade Operations (UKMTO) reported a tanker struck by an "unknown projectile" 11 nautical miles northeast of Oman, damaging the engine room and leaving the vessel not under command, while another tanker reported seeing a splash and explosion nearby. Bloomberg, citing shipping intelligence firms, reported that the Gaslog Shanghai LNG carrier, carrying cargo from Qatar, was hit by a projectile while transiting the strait.

On policy, Axios cited a U.S. official saying President Trump is seriously considering striking Iranian energy targets — refineries and power plants — within days, though no final order has been given; CBS reported such a campaign would mark a major escalation aimed at forcing Iran to accept U.S. terms in ceasefire talks. According to ship-tracking data from Kpler, Strait of Hormuz crossings plunged 77% day-on-day to just five vessels, all concentrated on the Iranian-controlled route. Iran's Supreme National Security Council secretary, Mohammad Bagher Zolghadr, warned on Saturday that a continued U.S. blockade would not only "lock the Strait of Hormuz tighter" but also shut other chokepoints, with the price "paid by the global economy, the energy market, and American voters."

Analysts note the plunge in daily crossings is thinning the market's supply buffer: if a fresh U.S. strike wave lands this weekend or Houthi forces in the Red Sea escalate in tandem, Energy Aspects has warned oil could push back above $100.

Three Fed Hawks Publicly Urge Rate Hikes: Gradual Moves Beat Waiting, 10-Year Yield Tops 4.7%

On Wednesday (July 29), the FOMC voted 9-3 to hold the federal funds rate at 3.5%-3.75% — a fifth consecutive hold this year — with Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan dissenting in favor of a quarter-point hike. It was the first time since September 2016 that three members simultaneously dissented in favor of a hike. On Friday, all three issued statements explaining their positions: Hammack said "now is the time for the FOMC to act to speed the return of PCE inflation to our 2 percent objective," arguing policy is not restrictive enough; Kashkari invoked the 1970s inflation lesson, saying "a potential series of small policy moves would be better than waiting and eventually concluding that even bolder actions were necessary"; Logan warned "the FOMC cannot count on unanticipated shocks" and that without policy restraint inflation would keep trending above target.

The market reaction was immediate: the 10-year Treasury yield topped 4.7% intraday Friday, the highest since January 2025, while the 30-year approached 5.25% (a 2007 high). Citi chief economist Nathan Sheets called it a "vote of no confidence" in Fed Chair Kevin Warsh — a chair who keeps pledging price stability while policy stays on hold. Tim Duy of SGH Macro Advisors wrote that board members have signaled they would push for a September hike if inflation fails to cool meaningfully this summer. Forecasts are sharply split: Goldman Sachs and Barclays expect no move this year; BofA Global Research sees three hikes starting in September; long-time dove Citi maintains calls for cuts in October, December 2026 and January 2027.

CLARITY Act's Make-or-Break Weekend: Polymarket Odds Slide to 27%, Bitcoin ETFs See $265M Outflow

The U.S. crypto market-structure bill, the Digital Asset Market Clarity (CLARITY) Act, faces a decisive weekend. Passed by the House 294-134 in July 2025 and advanced by the Senate Banking Committee 15-9 on May 14, it still needs 60 votes on the Senate floor, which has not yet scheduled a vote before the chamber's August recess begins next week. On August 1, crypto policy journalist Eleanor Terrett reported that Trump is weighing a bipartisan ethics counteroffer from Republican Sen. Thom Tillis and Democratic Sen. Ruben Gallego — a state attorneys general enforcement plan sent to the White House on July 29 — after seven Senate Democrats rejected the latest ethics draft. On Polymarket, the probability of the CLARITY Act becoming law in 2026 has fallen to 27%, down from peaks near 70% earlier this year. SEC Chairman Paul Atkins says the agency is "ready, willing and able" to step in if Congress stalls, working with the CFTC on a joint "Regulation Crypto" framework.

Institutional flows are under pressure too: according to SoSoValue, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs posted $265 million in net outflows on July 31 — led by BlackRock's IBIT (-$123M) and Fidelity's FBTC (-$54.8M) — ending a two-session inflow streak, while spot Ethereum ETFs eked out $9 million in net inflows. Total U.S. spot Bitcoin ETF assets stand at roughly $76.29 billion, about 6.04% of Bitcoin's market value. Separately, Coinbase's Q2 report showed a GAAP net loss of $359.5 million — a third straight losing quarter — with transaction revenue down 21% quarter-over-quarter, even as its derivatives market share hit a record 10.3%. With legislation unresolved and institutions on the sidelines, crypto markets look set to stay under pressure in the near term.

Industry observers broadly agree that whether or not the CLARITY Act clears this week, a joint SEC-CFTC rulebook is already on the way — for crypto, "when the rules arrive" matters more than short-term price action for institutional flows over the next six months.
