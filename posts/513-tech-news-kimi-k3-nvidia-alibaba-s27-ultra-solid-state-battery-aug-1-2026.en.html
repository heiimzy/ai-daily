Bloomberg Exclusive: Kimi K3 Trained on 20,000 Nvidia Chips via Alibaba; Washington Alleges Export-Rule Breach

Bloomberg reported exclusively that Moonshot AI's Kimi models are trained on a cluster of 20,000 Nvidia chips secured through a computing-power agreement with Alibaba — with the much-watched Kimi K3 among the models built on that hardware. Reuters quickly followed up, citing Bloomberg's report that Moonshot obtained access to the Nvidia chip cluster through its Alibaba computing deal. The story complicates the narrative of "China building a top-tier AI model on its own": MarketWatch asked directly whether China really made a frontier model by itself, noting the report suggests Nvidia chips played a central role. The export-control angle is even more sensitive — Inshorts, citing reports, noted Alibaba had previously denied Moonshot access to H200 compute, while Briefs Finance reported that Washington claims the procurement violated export rules.

The news lands against the backdrop of an intensifying AI price war: earlier this week (July 30), OpenAI announced API price cuts of up to 80% on GPT-5.6 Luna and 20% on Terra, widely seen as a direct response to the rise of Chinese open-weight models such as Kimi K3 and DeepSeek. One one side, a Chinese company is accused of routing around US curbs to secure Nvidia compute; on the other, US giants are being forced to cut prices to compete — the twin tensions of the "compute arms race" collided this week. After the news broke, Alibaba's shares jumped to near two-month highs, underscoring how sensitive the market is to the AI compute landscape.

Samsung Galaxy S27 Ultra Leak: 5,700mAh Silicon-Carbon Battery + Redesigned Camera Module

Android Central reported Saturday that tipster Anthony (@TheGalox_) shared fresh Galaxy S27 Ultra specs on X, pointing to a "massive camera and battery overhaul." On battery, the S27 Ultra is tipped to feature a 5,700mAh silicon-carbon cell — a major jump over previous generations that should relieve endurance anxiety for heavy users. The display is said to be a 6.9-inch QHD+ "Privacy Display" designed to keep onlookers from reading sensitive on-screen content. Under the hood, the flagship is expected to run the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 Pro for Galaxy with 12GB or 16GB of RAM. The camera system is also set for a big upgrade: a 16MP selfie shooter with optical image stabilization (OIS) for the first time, plus a 200MP main sensor and three 50MP cameras — an ultrawide, a 5x periscope telephoto, and a second dedicated telephoto for extended zoom.

Design-wise, the leak suggests Samsung will drop the floating camera-ring layout used since the Galaxy S22 Ultra in favor of a unified camera module — one of the biggest visual changes to the Ultra line in years. That said, this remains leak/rumor territory; earlier reports have also suggested the S27 Ultra could miss out on some major upgrades, so final specs will depend on Samsung's official announcement.

Factorial Signs MoU with SK On: Can Existing SK On Plants Make Solid-State Batteries?

US solid-state battery developer Factorial Energy and South Korean battery giant SK On announced they have signed a memorandum of understanding (MoU) to explore solid-state battery manufacturing collaboration. According to Factorial's press release, the two companies will evaluate whether and how SK On's existing manufacturing facilities could produce solid-state batteries, assess whether current lithium-ion production lines are suitable, and conduct a technical evaluation of Factorial's FEST solid-state battery technology. Factorial aims to pursue a capital-light path by leveraging existing manufacturing infrastructure rather than building new plants. SK On has annual global production capacity of more than 200 GWh — including roughly 100 GWh in the US, with facilities in Commerce, Georgia, and operations in Tennessee. For Factorial, this is its first manufacturing-focused partnership with a global battery producer. The companies also plan to explore applications for solid-state batteries in mobility and other high-performance markets. For now, the collaboration remains at the technical-feasibility stage, and whether it leads to commercial-scale production is still uncertain.
