Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro Briefly Appears on Arena, Removed After 30 Minutes

Multiple users on X reported today that Google's latest Gemini 3.5 Pro model briefly appeared on the Arena (formerly LMArena) platform. Screenshots posted by user Harshith (@HarshithLucky3) show that Gemini 3.5 Pro was removed just 30 minutes after going live, having completed only a single SVG test. Other users shared similar screenshots and reports around the same time.

Arena is an AI model evaluation platform based on blind voting by real users, ranking models across text, code and vision tasks via Elo scores. A model appearing there typically signals an imminent public release. Google officially launched the Gemini 3.5 family on May 19, 2026, debuting the 3.5 Flash model and saying 3.5 Pro was already in internal use with a planned launch the following month. It has since been delayed several times, with Google only saying it is testing with partners. The brief appearance and quick removal suggest Google may be running final stability checks before a wider rollout.

ByteDance Officially Launches Seedance 2.5; XCMG, XPeng and Unitree Among First Adopters

ByteDance today officially released its next-generation video generation model, Seedance 2.5. For enterprise customers, Volcano Engine will launch API access in the near future. According to the company, XCMG Group, XPeng, Unitree Robotics, Lingchu Intelligence, Weifen Zhifei, Qiongche Intelligence and Xspark AI have all reached cooperation agreements to be among the first to integrate Seedance 2.5.

From engineering instruction videos precise down to screw-hole positions, to realistic autonomous-driving training environments, to helping robots predict motion trajectories, Seedance is moving into real production workflows across industrial manufacturing, energy and chemicals, automotive, embodied AI and intelligent driving. XCMG is using it to generate industrial operator training and process SOP videos, sharply cutting the cost of traditional live-action filming and 3D animation. XPeng has integrated Seedance into its internal one-stop AI design platform to support visual creation in product design. Qiongche Intelligence is using video generation to expand training data for its embodied AI foundation models, generating operation data adapted to different robot models without collecting it from physical robots one by one.

In addition, several leading autonomous-driving companies are exploring the use of Seedance to generate training data for extreme conditions such as heavy rain, fog, snow and light reflection, as well as corner-case scenarios involving complex interactions and collision risk. Seedance 2.5 extends Seedance 2.0's unified multimodal audio-video joint generation architecture, supporting single generations of up to 30 seconds, with a focus on long-form storytelling, multimodal reference and editing — shifting video generation from "creating a clip" to "completing a piece of creation."

Alibaba's Qwen Reportedly in Deep Testing Inside Tesla China Infotainment

According to a report today from STCN (Securities Times China) citing multiple informed sources, Alibaba's Qwen model has entered deep testing in Tesla China vehicle infotainment systems, with an in-car debut approaching. Qwen has reportedly completed extensive on-vehicle testing in real Tesla environments, with capabilities including "listening and answering, controlling the car, navigation and task execution — all in the plan." What Qwen can offer in Tesla vehicles could go far beyond the definition of a simple "voice assistant."

Notably, leading Chinese automakers announced Qwen integration at the 2026 Beijing Auto Show. Based on the disclosed technical roadmap, Qwen in vehicles offers at least three layers of capability: complex semantic understanding and multi-task route planning; an end-to-end "order–fulfillment–payment" closed loop; and cloud-device collaborative architecture covering content, lifestyle and commerce scenarios. As of press time, neither Alibaba Cloud nor Tesla China had responded to the reports. Earlier, Tesla China released software update 2026.14.13 adding a "Doubao" voice assistant powered by ByteDance's large model, underscoring how foreign automakers are accelerating adoption of Chinese LLM ecosystems in vehicles.

New AI Worm Disclosed: White-On-White Text Silently Alters Word Documents and Self-Replicates

Norwegian data scientist Håkon Måløy has disclosed an AI worm attack targeting Copilot for Word, as reported by tech outlet The Register. In the proof-of-concept attack, when a user works with the Copilot assistant inside Microsoft Word, the AI worm can tamper with documents the user creates or edits through self-replicating malicious prompts, then continue copying itself into new documents.

The attack works in roughly four steps: the attacker embeds hidden instructions — the AI worm — into a document that will later be processed by Copilot for Word; when the user loads the document, Copilot mistakes the hidden instructions for a user request and modifies the document accordingly; Copilot copies the hidden instructions into new documents without the user noticing; once such a document enters another Copilot-assisted workflow, the instructions spread to the next document. This means that once the worm enters a document flow, it can continue replicating beyond the attacker's control. When attacking, the malicious prompt is rendered in white text on a white background, making it extremely hard for users to spot.

Måløy reported the proof of concept to Microsoft on March 6, 2026, and subsequently worked with the company on mitigations. Microsoft deployed several patches, but Måløy found he could still bypass them by modifying the prompts. After a 144-day coordinated disclosure period, during which Microsoft could not find an effective mitigation, Måløy went public. "There is currently no definitive solution for this class of widespread vulnerability," he said.

China's NDRC: AI Industries Growing 30%+; National AI Compute Capacity Now 2.8x Last Year

At its July press conference this morning, China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) detailed progress on high-quality AI development. Jiang Yi, director of the NDRC Policy Research Office and spokesperson, said self-innovation in AI accelerated across the full industrial chain in the first half of the year: first, the computing foundation is being consolidated — the first fully domestic 100,000-GPU AI supercluster is now operational, and as of the end of June, national AI compute capacity reached 2.8 times the level of the same period last year; second, model capabilities are leaping — domestic firms including DeepSeek and Moonshot AI released multiple trillion-parameter open-source models, with global downloads of Chinese open-source LLMs surpassing 10 billion; third, factor flows are active — more than 120,000 high-quality datasets have been built, and AI-related industries are growing at over 30%.

Data released alongside the briefing showed that integrated circuit output of large-scale industrial enterprises grew 23.1% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, setting monthly records for three consecutive months in Q2; IC exports rose 88.7% year-on-year, contributing nearly one-third of the growth in total merchandise exports — a clear sign of the growing synergy between compute and chip industries.
