⚡ Aug 3 Tech Update: China's Big Fund Leads DeepSeek's First Funding Round at $45B Valuation, Zhipu Debuts GLM-5V-Turbo Visual Coding Model, MiniMax H3 Goes Open Source

Three heavyweight developments are reshaping China's AI landscape as Monday, August 3, gets underway: Caijing reports that the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund (the "Big Fund") is leading DeepSeek's first-ever funding round at a $45 billion pre-money valuation, with roughly $10 billion on the table - potentially the largest Series A in Chinese AI history; Zhipu has released GLM-5V-Turbo, a multimodal coding foundation model that fuses vision and text from the pretraining stage, turning design mockups, screenshots and webpages into runnable code; and MiniMax has open-sourced its H3 omni-modal video model on ModelScope at midnight today, putting native 2K video generation with stereo audio in every developer's hands.

China's Big Fund Leads DeepSeek's First Funding Round: $45B Pre-Money, a Record for Chinese AI

According to Caijing, negotiations for DeepSeek's first funding round are in their final stages, with the "Big Fund" leading the round and several market-oriented investors still on the list. A person close to the deal said the roster of Series A investors was basically finalized by the end of last month. Based on earlier reporting from May 22, the round is expected to raise roughly 70 billion yuan (about $10 billion) at a pre-money valuation of $45 billion - up from the roughly $20 billion valuation the market assigned just a month earlier. If completed, it would be the largest first-round raise in the history of Chinese AI model companies.

The significance goes well beyond the headline number. First, "Team National" is stepping in: this is the first time in the Big Fund's 12-year history that it has invested in a pure large-model company. Song Xiangqing, vice president of the China Business Economics Association, says the AI industry is entering a phase of "national-team leadership with market capital following," and that DeepSeek is rising to the status of a national AI strategic platform. Second, industrial capital is circling: media reports say CATL, the battery giant, plans to join, a strategic play on the energy variable behind compute infrastructure, while Tencent and Alibaba were also reported to be in talks (Alibaba has publicly denied interest). Third, the ecosystem angle: DeepSeek-V4, released in late April, was the first model to support Huawei's Ascend 950PR chip, signaling training that does not depend entirely on Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem - part of the backdrop for the state fund's entry. By comparison, Moonshot AI recently closed a $2 billion round at a valuation above $20 billion and is dismantling its VIE structure ahead of a listing. The capital structure of China's leading model labs is being reshaped all at once.

"Everyone wants to touch DeepSeek, but no one can guarantee they're 100% in." - a person close to the deal, speaking to Caijing

Zhipu Releases GLM-5V-Turbo: a Coding Foundation Model That "Sees" From Pretraining

In the early hours of August 3, Zhipu officially released GLM-5V-Turbo, a multimodal coding foundation model built for visual programming. Unlike the common "text-first, vision-later" approach, the model deeply fuses vision and text from the pretraining stage, breaking the text-only input ceiling of programming: it can read design mockups, screenshots and web interfaces, then directly generate runnable code - genuinely "seeing the picture and writing the code."

According to the company, GLM-5V-Turbo is already deployed in "image-as-code" and GUI auto-exploration scenarios: front-end replication (turning a mockup into a page in one shot), autonomous GUI exploration and replication (agents operating interfaces and reproducing functionality), and K-line chart reading with illustrated report generation. For developers, the input modality of programming is expanding from pure text to images: a product manager's wireframe or a designer's mockup can now become the starting point of code. Visual programming is widely seen as one of the next major engineering frontiers for multimodal models, and Zhipu's new line targets exactly this high-frequency "code from images" need.

MiniMax H3 Goes Open Source at Midnight: 2K Video With Native Stereo Audio on ModelScope

MiniMax (Xiyu Technology) has announced that its H3 omni-modal video model is officially open-sourced on ModelScope (Magic Hub) starting at midnight, August 3, free for developers and researchers. H3, launched on July 31, is an omni-modal generation model that treats text, images, video and audio as one unified context, producing native 2K resolution (not upscaled), 4-15 second clips with native stereo audio. It ranks No. 1 in video editing on Artificial Analysis benchmarks, with an API price of about $0.13 per second.

The weights follow a tiered license: non-commercial use is completely free; commercial use under $20 million annual revenue is free with attribution; larger commercial deployments require a separately negotiated license. The open-sourcing gives film, advertising and e-commerce creators a low-cost new tool - yesterday's edition of this daily reported that H3's launch came with a promise of open weights; that promise has now been fulfilled, letting developers self-host the video generation stack instead of routing everything through the official API. One caveat worth tracking: MiniMax remains entangled in a US federal copyright lawsuit (involving its Hailuo platform; a motion to dismiss was denied on May 26 and the case has moved into discovery), so the interplay between its open-source cadence and legal risk deserves attention.

Industry Watch: "Team National" Capital Meets the Open-Source Ecosystem

Read together, the three stories point in the same direction: on the capital side, the Big Fund is directly investing in a pure model company for the first time, and a completed DeepSeek round would reset the valuation anchor of China's AI primary market; on the technology side, Zhipu is turning "visual programming" into a foundation-model capability while MiniMax open-sources its omni-modal video model - differentiation is shifting from parameter counts to engineering scenarios and openness strategy. As open weights, domestic compute and state capital form a triangle, China's AI ecosystem is moving from "chasing closed models" to "defining open source."
